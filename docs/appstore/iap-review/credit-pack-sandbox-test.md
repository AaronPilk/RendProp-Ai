# 100-credit TestFlight offer without public activation

This source change is a **dormant** route for a genuine $39.99 StoreKit offer and signed Sandbox purchase on a named QA account. The migration seeds **zero** test authorities, leaves `credit_products.active=false`, leaves verified retail net proceeds/cash unset, and changes no Production grant or public sale. It does not prove Apple has returned the product in StoreKit on a physical phone.

## Required sequence

1. Review and deploy migration `20261028180000_credit_pack_sandbox_test_authority.sql`, then deploy its matching `me/credits` Edge code. Check readback: `credit_products.active=false`, `verified_net_proceeds_floor_cents IS NULL`, and `credit_pack_sandbox_test_authority` empty. Do not manufacture a net-proceeds figure to make the pack active.
2. Merge the native `sandbox_only` filtering and explicit purchase-intent environment into a **new** TestFlight build. Build 129 predates this client change: it cannot complete the new four-argument intent path. TestFlight `AppTransaction.environment` must verify as Sandbox; Production hides a Sandbox-only product even for the test actor.
3. Once a dedicated, non-anonymous QA owner/admin account and its billing workspace are known, an authorized database operator may insert **one** exact `(actor_id,org_id,product_id='com.rendprop.app.credits.100')` row in `credit_pack_sandbox_test_authority`, with a real private change-record SHA-256 and expiry no later than seven days after authorization. Never use a customer account or give the app role write access. The server checks current owner/admin membership and the expiry. Revoke the row after testing. An expired row closes new offers; an already prepared signed Sandbox purchase remains recoverable to avoid losing a pending Ask-to-Buy transaction.
4. On that account in the exact new TestFlight build, open Account → Settings & plan → Credits. Check that StoreKit loaded the real `com.rendprop.app.credits.100` consumable, the CTA says `Add 100 credits · $39.99` in a US storefront, and the workspace balance is visible. Capture the **native iPhone screenshot**, unedited, for that IAP's private App Review screenshot. TestFlight uses Apple's Sandbox real catalog data; this is not Xcode's `.storekit` fixture. Apple says IAPs need not be approved before Sandbox testing, but the Paid Apps Agreement must be active ([Apple review setup](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/configure-in-app-purchases)).
5. With an Apple Sandbox tester, make one Sandbox purchase and verify the server receives an Apple-signed JWS, pins the intent to the exact actor/org/product and Sandbox, and grants 100 credits to the **Sandbox wallet only**. Verify pending transaction recovery and idempotent replay. Verify a Production wallet and public app do not show or receive that balance. Keep supplier-spend/media funding tests separate; a Sandbox credit grant is not provider cash.
6. After genuine screenshots for this and the luxury website consumable, upload each to its corresponding private App Store Connect IAP review field and read back asset state `COMPLETE`. The first consumables must accompany the app-version review submission. Public activation of the credit pack still requires actual collected-cash/proceeds evidence, a reviewed margin floor, and separate release verification.

## Gates and negative proof

The `me/credits` offer list uses a named operator authority independent of the caller's claimed environment. It marks an inactive product `sandbox_only`. Native code filters that marker against Apple's **verified** AppTransaction environment. Purchase preparation accepts the client's environment only in addition to the server-side operator authority; its prepared intent snapshots the environment. The grant route verifies Apple's signed JWS and passes its **signed** environment, not a body-supplied environment, to the database. A Sandbox intent cannot settle a Production JWS, and Production still requires the existing proceeds evidence and price/storefront checks.

Local tests: `deno test --allow-env --allow-net services/supabase/functions/me/credits.test.ts` covers forged Sandbox query visibility; `python3 -B services/supabase/tests/credit_wallet_pg.py` exercises the disposable PostgreSQL grant/expiry/recovery/Production-denial fixture; an iOS simulator build type-checks the client. None is a live Apple purchase or device screenshot.
