# Rendprop competitor research and delivery priorities — 22 September 2026

## Decision

The immediate competitive objective should be a dependable completed listing: capture or import once, continue on the desktop, review the media, and deliver a coordinated marketing package. Breadth is already common. The opportunity is to make this complete workflow easy enough for the owner and family to use without technical help, while preserving the actual property and their original footage.

This is a current public-source review of **18 relevant products**, not an exhaustive census or an output-quality benchmark. Vendor claims establish advertised features; they do not prove quality, reliability, adoption, increased sales or universal MLS compliance. No account was created, media uploaded, API called, subscription purchased or production setting changed. Research spend: **$0**.

Read before researching: the existing `RendProp-Ai/docs/research/2026-09-market/COMPETITOR-EVIDENCE.md` and `ENGINEERING-BACKLOG.md`, in full. Their source inventory refers to an earlier code snapshot and must not be mistaken for the current implementation. The current user request authorizes delivery work; the historical research files are background, not fresh instructions overriding that request.

## What matters most to a nontechnical agent

The following priorities are product recommendations inferred from the evidence above and the owner's stated workflow. They are **acceptance targets**, not claims that current Rendprop code lacks every item.

1. **One account and one listing.** A photo captured or imported on the phone appears under the same listing in Studio, with the same room, original, selected version, job state and saved draft. Show whether material is saved locally, uploading or available everywhere. Resume should return to the user's actual last step. The phone should never become a compulsory editor for work that is easier on a desktop.
2. **One obvious way to finish.** Start with the listing, show completed work and the next useful action, and offer a prepared listing kit. Keep the app's brand, tool names and thumbnails consistent across devices. Use the larger desktop screen for comparing, arranging and editing; preserve the user's mental model rather than copying the phone's geometry literally.
3. **Batch jobs with review.** Select a set of images, choose the intended result, preview representative changes, run the batch, then accept/reject individual results. Original media stays available. Failed items should be understandable and recoverable without repeating successful paid jobs.
4. **A practical reel editor.** Combine actual clips and stills; arrange scenes; trim; choose a small number of meaningful motion styles; edit captions and narration; adjust music; apply the saved brand; preview horizontal and vertical framing. Save drafts automatically and synchronize them. Generative clips are one ingredient, not the whole editing product.
5. **A coordinated delivery kit.** Produce ordered listing photos, social/landscape videos, copy, a property page, QR code and a compact download package from approved selections. Offer appropriate branded/unbranded outputs. Preserve the original/edited distinction and include the user's staging disclosures in the relevant outputs.
6. **Clear edits that preserve the property.** Keep enhancement, virtual furnishing and hypothetical renovation separate. Support checking before/after, revising a selected area and keeping approved parts of the image. Do not silently replace materials, windows, built-ins, damage or geometry to make marketing footage look better. Multi-angle room consistency is a useful explicit quality target.
7. **A small amount of useful guidance.** Explain what to do next, how long a job is expected to take, what it will cost, and what to do after failure. The owner should not have to know the model name, queue mechanics, codecs or where an export was stored to finish a listing.

The product can support many features without exposing many decisions. The proposed default journey is **Listing → Media → Create → Review → Publish**. Reuse the app's existing structure when it already accomplishes this; do not rebuild stable plumbing to fit the wording of this document.

Do not spend this release building a generic CRM, a photographer marketplace, hardware capture system or unlimited novelty-effect catalog. Those expand the company into adjacent businesses before the current users can reliably finish a property. Keep creative concepts available only where their hypothetical nature is clear; do not present invented construction, scenery or geometry as a faithful record of a listing.

## What can be tested without a camera

Most delivery work does not require new phone capture. With permitted existing media, test authentication, cross-device listing persistence, uploads, background jobs, enhancement, staging, editing, caption timing, audio mixing, reel rendering, downloads, branded/unbranded output, public pages, sharing and failure recovery. A browser and simulator can exercise client behavior with imported files; actual provider outputs and live server/storage behavior still require their own evidence.

Only the owner can provide the remaining physical-device evidence in this environment: real sensor capture, focus/exposure, device motion, interruption/thermal behavior, AR tracking and actual room coverage. Give the owner one short targeted phone checklist after the rest has passed, rather than asking them to discover basic application defects. Do not label physical-camera behavior tested from a simulator.

For competitive output quality, retain a representative fixed set of owned/permitted interiors and exteriors, including mirrors, large windows, blank walls, clutter and poor lighting. Report accepted-output rate, obvious permanent-property changes, time to completion, user revisions, failures and total cost per completed listing. Generative camera changes should not receive misleading pixel-reconstruction scores; the spatial pipeline should retain its separate fixed held-out PSNR/SSIM evaluation.

## Research limitations and corrections worth carrying forward

- “Virtual tour,” “walkthrough” and “3D” cover materially different things across these products: slideshows, generated photo motion, connected panoramas, floor-plan renders and captured spatial scenes. Match the method before making comparisons.
- Current pages contain marketing claims about faster sales, more inquiries, perfect fidelity and MLS compliance. Those claims were not independently established and should not be repeated as measured outcomes.
- Several price pages defaulted to annual billing or localized currency. Virtual Staging AI's $16 and Collov's $19 cards were annual-billing equivalents in the retrieved state. Apply Design's automated staging consumes more than one coin. Per-image headline pricing does not establish video or full-listing cost.
- This review does not establish that the researched features all work, that they are usable on every plan/device, or that Rendprop is currently missing them. Pair this matrix with the current source/deployment audit before opening implementation tasks.
- Nothing here changes the source isolation requirement, authorizes new spending beyond existing user caps, or substitutes for real-phone capture verification.
