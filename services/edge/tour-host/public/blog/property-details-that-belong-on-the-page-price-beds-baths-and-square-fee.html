Property details for the page: price, beds, baths, square feet

Four facts belong above the gallery on every listing page: price, beds, baths and square feet. Match the MLS exactly and leave the rest for the showing.

Four facts belong at the top of a property page, directly under the main photo: price, bedrooms, bathrooms and square feet, matching the MLS entry to the digit. Add the address or neighbourhood and the type of home, and stop there; anything else is detail for lower on the page or for the showing. A buyer scans for those four numbers in the first two seconds and decides whether to keep scrolling.

The page is not a disclosure document. It is the thing that gets the buyer to ask for a showing. The four facts answer the only questions that matter at that moment: can I afford it, is it big enough, and how big exactly.

The four headline facts

Price. The full asking price, formatted the way the MLS shows it. If the price has changed, the page changes the same day. A buyer who sees two different prices in two places trusts neither.

Bedrooms. Count what the MLS counts. If a room is a den on the MLS, it is a den on the page. Do not round a 3-bed plus office up to a 4-bed; the buyer will count at the showing and feel misled.

Bathrooms. Use the same convention as your MLS: 2.5, or 2 full and 1 half. Pick one and be consistent across your listings.

Square feet. State the source (assessor, appraiser, builder plans) if your MLS's current rules call for one. Do not quote the seller's memory. If you are unsure, use the number on the MLS and nothing else.

The two labels that go with them

Location. The full address if the seller is comfortable with it, otherwise the street and town or the neighbourhood. Buyers want to place the home on a map in their head.

Type. Single-family, townhouse, condo, duplex. One or two words. It sets expectations for the gallery that follows.

What can go lower on the page

If you have them and they help, a short block below the gallery can hold lot size, year built, garage spaces, HOA fee, and annual taxes. These matter to a buyer who is already interested and is comparing this home against another. They do not belong at the top, where they dilute the four facts a first-time viewer needs.

Leave off: room-by-room dimensions (the showing covers it), school ratings (they change and you do not control them), and anything that sounds like a legal description.

A worked example: the 1,720-square-foot ranch

MLS entry: $412,000, 3 bed, 2 bath, 1,720 sq ft per assessor, single-family, 0.31 acre lot, built 1998, 2-car garage, no HOA. The page's top block reads: $412,000, 3 bed, 2 bath, 1,720 sq ft, Cedar Court, Fairview, single-family. Below the gallery: 0.31 acre lot, built 1998, 2-car garage, no HOA.

Three weeks in, the price drops to $399,000. You change it in the listing and the page shows the new figure. The MLS and the page now agree.

What you do not add: "4th bedroom possible in basement" or "approximately 1,900 sq ft with the sunroom". Those are showing conversations, not page facts.

Detail errors that cost credibility

  • Page and MLS disagree. The most common error, usually after a price change or a correction to the bed count. Make the listing the single source and update it in one place.
  • Padding the square footage. Including an unfinished basement or a garage in the headline number. Buyers compare price per square foot and feel cheated when the number does not hold up.
  • Hiding the price. Unless the seller insists on price-on-application, a missing price reads as either unaffordable or evasive.
  • Too many numbers at the top. Twelve facts in a grid means none of them register. Four, then the gallery.
  • Dated details left live. "Open house Saturday" still showing two weeks later tells a buyer the page is unattended.

Business listings use different facts

The same principle holds for a venue, a restaurant or a gym, but the four facts change. A venue page leads with capacity and the types of events it hosts. A restaurant page leads with hours and the menu. A gym leads with membership options and class types. The buyer, planner or member still wants the headline facts before the gallery, just different ones.

The check before publishing

Open the MLS entry and the page side by side. Read the four facts aloud from each. If any differ, fix the page before anyone sees it. It takes a minute and it prevents the one question that undermines trust: "Which number is right?"

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should the property page show the full address?

If the seller is comfortable, yes; buyers want to place the home. If the seller prefers privacy, use the street and town or the neighbourhood. Either way, the location label should sit with the four headline facts, not hidden lower down.

What if the square footage on the MLS is different from what the seller told me?

Use the MLS number and its stated source. The page and the MLS must agree, and the seller's recollection is not a source a buyer can rely on. If the seller disputes it, resolve it in the MLS entry first and then update the page.

Should I include HOA fees and taxes on the page?

Below the gallery, if you have accurate figures. They help a buyer who is comparing homes but do not belong in the headline block. If you are not sure the figures are current, leave them for the showing.

How do I keep the page and the MLS in sync after a price change?

Make the listing the single source for the page, so one edit updates it. Then change both the MLS and the listing on the same day, and open the page on your phone to confirm. A page that lags the MLS by a week costs you credibility with every buyer who notices.
