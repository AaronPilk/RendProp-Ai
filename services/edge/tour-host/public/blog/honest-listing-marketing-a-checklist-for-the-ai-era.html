Honest listing marketing: a checklist for the AI era

Twenty checks across photos, video, the property page, social posts and enquiries that keep a listing honest when AI tools are in the workflow.

Honest listing marketing in the AI era comes down to one rule applied everywhere: nothing a buyer sees online should surprise them at the showing. AI tools make it easier to break that rule by accident, because the edits look finished and the video looks filmed. The checklist below applies the rule to photos, video, the property page, social posts and enquiry replies: twenty items, most of them ten seconds each, run once per listing before anything goes live.

Photos

  • 1. Originals kept. Every untouched photo is stored and reachable from your phone, separate from any edit.
  • 2. Every edit compared. Each AI edit was put beside its original and checked: openings counted, fixed surfaces scanned, materials unchanged, view unchanged.
  • 3. Nothing fixed was removed. No fixtures, outlets, vents, bars, cracks, stains or damage taken out. Loose items only.
  • 4. Nothing was added except labelled staging. No invented windows, lights, plants or furniture outside a staged, labelled photo.
  • 5. Staged photos say “Virtually staged.” On the image or caption, and in the remarks.
  • 6. Other alterations say what changed. Sky, lawn, lighting, items removed. Named, not “enhanced.”
  • 7. The surroundings are untouched. Power lines, poles, neighbours, the street: all still there.
  • 8. MLS rules checked. Rules differ by MLS and state. You have read your MLS's current rules on altered images this year, and asked your broker where the office draws the line.

Video

  • 9. Only accepted versions in the video. No rejected edit made it in by mistake.
  • 10. Staged scenes carry a label. On screen or in the caption, and in the voiceover if staging is the point.
  • 11. Every fact in the script matches the details. Beds, baths, square feet, price, features.
  • 12. The video is described as what it is. A photo-based video is not called a tour, a walkthrough or footage. A generated opening is called generated.
  • 13. Both formats watched. 16:9 and 9:16 reviewed separately, since the crop differs.

The property page

  • 14. Details typed and checked. Price, beds, baths, square feet entered by you, matching the MLS.
  • 15. Gallery labels match the MLS labels. Same photo, same disclosure, in both places.
  • 16. The empty original sits near the staged version where a room was staged.
  • 17. Status is current. Sold or archived listings say so on the page.

Social and enquiries

  • 18. Disclosure in the first lines of the caption. Not after the hashtags.
  • 19. The seller knows. Which rooms are staged, what the video is, what the labels say, before the listing goes live.
  • 20. A buyer who asks gets a specific answer. Which photos were edited, how, and where the originals are. Not “just a bit of enhancement.”

A worked example: running it on a Thursday

An agent runs the list on a four-bed before a Friday launch. Items 1 to 8 take six minutes with originals beside edits; she rejects one tidy edit that removed a floor vent and re-runs it. Items 9 to 13 take twelve minutes across two video formats; she catches the script saying “three baths” where it is two and a half. Items 14 to 17 take three minutes on the page. Items 18 to 20: she writes the caption line, calls the seller to confirm the two staged rooms, and saves a two-sentence answer for buyers who ask about edits. Twenty-five minutes. The listing goes live Friday with nothing in it that the Saturday open house will contradict.

What the list is not

It does not make a listing compliant on its own. It is practice and judgement, built around what buyers notice and what brokers ask about afterwards. It does not replace your MLS's current rules, your broker's policy or an attorney's advice. It does make the Saturday conversation with a buyer boring, which is the goal.

How this works in Rendprop

Several items are built into the workflow. Every AI edit is a separate version with a disclosure, staging is labelled “Virtually staged”, other edits carry an AI-alteration disclosure, and the untouched original stays in photo history; comparing each edit with the original is how you choose which versions to use. The property website shows the details you typed, the selected photos and the selected video, which plays on request, and the page says so when a listing is marked sold or archived. The caption line, the seller call and the buyer answer remain yours. Item 2 exists because AI can change or invent details; compare before publishing, and check your own MLS rules before posting altered images. The features page covers the pieces.

Next step: copy the twenty items into a note on your phone and run them on the next listing before it goes live. Time it. Then decide whether twenty-five minutes is worth a listing nobody can call misleading.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What is the single rule behind honest listing marketing?

Nothing a buyer sees online should surprise them at the showing. Every item on the checklist, from comparing edits to labelling staging to checking the script, is that rule applied to one part of the listing.

How long does the full checklist take per listing?

Around twenty-five minutes for a typical listing with a handful of edits and two video formats. Most items take ten seconds; the video passes and the edit comparisons take the rest.

Does the checklist cover MLS compliance?

No. It is practice and judgement, and it does not make a listing compliant on its own. Rules differ by MLS and state, so read your MLS's current rules on altered images, ask your broker about office policy and consult an attorney for anything specific.

Which items on the list do people most often skip?

Comparing each edit with its original, watching the 9:16 version separately, and telling the seller which rooms are staged before launch. Each takes minutes and each prevents a conversation that takes much longer.
