What is a voiceover for a listing video

A voiceover for a listing video is spoken narration over the scenes, recorded by you or read by an AI voice from your script, saying what viewers see.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A voiceover for a listing video is a spoken narration laid over the scenes, either recorded in your own voice or read by an AI voice from a script you wrote. It tells the viewer what they are seeing and why it matters: the price, the layout, the thing about the home that photos alone do not explain. It is separate from music and from captions, which usually repeat it on screen.

A voiceover for a listing video is a spoken narration laid over the scenes, either recorded in your own voice or read by an AI voice from a script you wrote. It tells the viewer what they are looking at and why it matters: the price, the layout, the new roof, the walk to the school. It is separate from the music underneath and from the captions that usually repeat it on screen.

Without a voiceover, a listing video is pictures and a tune. With one, it is a person showing you a house.

How a voiceover is made

It starts with a script, short enough to fit the video. For a 30-second reel that is five or six lines; for a three-minute page video it is a paragraph per section of the home. The script is then either recorded, on a phone in a quiet room, reading at a calm pace, or handed to an AI voice that reads it. The audio is laid over the video so each line lands on the scene it describes. Captions are generated from the same words. Music, if any, is set low enough that the voice sits clearly on top.

The choice between your voice and a synthetic one is about recognition and consistency. Your own voice is you, which matters for a reel your followers will see. An AI voice is even, tireless and the same on listing forty as on listing one, which matters for a photographer delivering for twelve agents.

When to add one

  • Any video that carries facts a viewer needs: price, beds, baths, the open house time, what changed since the last price.
  • Reels, where a voice in the first second is part of the hook.
  • Page videos for homes with a story the photos do not tell: a layout, a renovation, a lot line that runs to the creek.

It is optional on a quiet luxury video where music and slow motion do the work, and on a walkthrough where the footage speaks for itself.

What a voiceover is not

A voiceover is not the on-camera agent talking to the lens; that is presenter video, a different product that needs a tripod, lighting and nerve. It is not background music, and it is not the caption text, although captions are usually built from it. It is also not the listing description read aloud; MLS remarks are written for a database, and read aloud they sound like one.

The nearest confusable thing is an ambient audio track, the sound of the actual rooms. That belongs to a walkthrough. A voiceover is a layer added on purpose, with words chosen for the viewer.

Two videos, two voices, one afternoon

An agent lists a 2-bed cottage near the harbour on a Tuesday and builds a 32-second reel from eight portrait photos. She writes six lines, each tied to a scene: Two beds, a five-minute walk to the harbour. New windows last autumn. The kitchen opens onto a walled garden. The bedroom at the back is the quiet one. Open house Saturday, eleven to one. Link in bio.

She records it in her parked car with the engine off, in one take, reading slower than feels natural. The captions follow the words. For the three-minute page video she writes a longer script and lets an AI voice read it, because her own voice on a slow page video sounds flat to her and the synthetic one is steadier. Two videos, two voices, one afternoon.

Where voiceovers go wrong

Reading the MLS remarks. Talking too fast because the video feels short. Recording in a kitchen with a fridge humming. A script that describes what the viewer can already see, this is the kitchen, instead of what they cannot, the island is new and the window faces west. And a voice drowned by music set too loud. Write for the ear, record somewhere quiet, say what the picture does not, and keep the music low.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should I use my own voice or an AI voice on a listing video?

Your own voice for reels your followers will see, because it is you. An AI voice when consistency matters more, such as a photographer delivering for many agents, or a long page video where an even read suits the pace. Both work; the script matters more than the voice.

How long should a listing video voiceover be?

As long as the video and no longer. Five or six short lines fill a 30-second reel. A page video of three minutes takes a short paragraph per part of the home. Read it aloud with a timer before you record.

Do I need a microphone to record a voiceover?

No. A phone in a quiet room, held close, reading at a calm pace, is enough for a listing video. Avoid rooms with echo and appliances, and record a few takes so you can pick the cleanest one.

Does a voiceover replace captions?

No. Many viewers watch with the sound off, so the captions carry the voiceover's facts to them. Use both: the voice for viewers with sound, the captions for everyone else.
