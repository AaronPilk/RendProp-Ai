Pinterest for listing photos: boards that keep working

Pin the room photos people search for, link every pin to the property page, and organise boards by neighborhood and room so they keep working after a sale.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Pinterest behaves more like a search tool than a feed, so a listing photo pinned well can keep bringing people to the property page for months. Pin the rooms people search for, write a plain description for each, link every pin to the property page, and organise boards by neighborhood and by room rather than by your name.

Pinterest is worth an agent's time because it behaves less like a feed and more like a search tool: a listing photo pinned with a plain description can keep bringing people to the property page long after the week you posted it. Pin the rooms people search for, write a description that says what the room is and where, link every pin to the branded property page, and organise boards by neighborhood and by room so a visitor who finds one pin finds the rest of your work.

What a pin is for

Nobody on Pinterest is scrolling to see what you did today. They are looking for a white kitchen with a wood island, a covered porch idea, a mudroom layout. Your listing has a real one of those, photographed properly. The pin shows the room, the description says it is in a home for sale in a named town, and the link goes to the page where the whole home is.

That means the pin does two jobs at once. It is a useful image for the person searching, and it is a doorway to the listing for the fraction of those people who happen to be in your area and thinking about moving. Test it with your own account; results vary by market and by how specific the description is.

A farmhouse kitchen, pinned

A four-bed farmhouse on three acres with a kitchen that has a 10-foot wood island, open shelving and a window over the sink looking at the barn.

  • The image. The portrait photo of the kitchen, taken from the doorway so the island, the shelving and the window are all in the frame. Vertical photos suit the format; this is one reason to shoot the key rooms in both orientations on the day.
  • The title. Farmhouse kitchen with 10-foot wood island and barn view.
  • The description. Kitchen in a four-bed farmhouse on three acres in Harlan County. Wood island, open shelving, window over the sink. The home is for sale; the full gallery, video and details are at the link. Two or three sentences, plain words, the town named.
  • The link. The branded property page. Not the MLS portal, not your homepage.
  • The boards. This pin goes on two boards: Harlan County homes, and Kitchens. A visitor who found it through kitchens can wander into the county board, and the other way round.

Then the porch, the mudroom and the barn get the same treatment, each on the room board and the county board. Four pins from one listing, each one a separate search result.

How to name boards

Name boards for what someone would search, not for you. Harlan County homes, not Jane's listings. Kitchens, porches, home offices, primary baths. A board per town or neighborhood you work, and a board per room type that photographs well in your market. Keep the number of boards small enough that each one has twenty or more pins; thin boards look abandoned.

After the home sells

The pins stay. If the link still points at a property page that now says sold, the visitor who arrives sees a real home that sold, your contact card, and a way to reach you. That is a quiet, useful outcome. If the link points at a dead page or at a portal listing that has been removed, the pin is a broken door, and it will keep being a broken door for as long as it exists. Keep the page up and marked sold. The reasoning is the same as in marking a listing sold on its property page.

What goes wrong with listing pins

  • Pinning the portal photo with a watermark across it. It looks borrowed. Pin your own clean photo.
  • Linking to a listing portal. The link breaks when the listing is removed, and while it works the page shows other agents. Link to your own property page.
  • Descriptions that are hashtags. Write a sentence. The place name and the room name belong in words.
  • Boards named after you. Nobody searches for your name. They search for a county or a kitchen.
  • Pinning an AI-edited photo without saying so. A virtually staged living room on a board called living rooms is a photo someone may try to copy. Put the disclosure in the description, the same way you would in a caption.
  • Pinning only exteriors. Exteriors are rarely what people search for. Rooms, porches and details are.

Two boards to start

Create two boards: one for the town you list in most, one for the room type that photographs best in your market. Pin the four strongest room photos from your current listing to both, each with a two-sentence description and the property page link.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Is Pinterest worth it for a real estate agent?

It can be, because pins are found by search over a long period rather than seen once in a feed. A listing kitchen pinned with a plain description and a link to the property page can keep bringing visitors for months. Test it on your own account before committing time.

Which listing photos should I pin?

The rooms people search for: kitchens, porches, home offices, baths, mudrooms, barns. Exteriors rarely get searched. Use portrait photos where you have them, since they suit the format.

Where should a listing pin link to?

Your own property page for that listing. Portal links break when the listing is removed and show other agents while they work. A property page that stays up and says sold after the sale keeps old pins useful.

How should I organise real estate boards?

By place and by room, named the way someone would search: Harlan County homes, Kitchens, Porches. Not by your name. Pin each listing photo to one place board and one room board.
