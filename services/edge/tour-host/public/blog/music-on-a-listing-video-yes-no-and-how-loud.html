Music on a listing video: yes, no and how loud

Music helps a social reel and often hurts a page video. When to use it, when to leave it off, and the one rule for volume: the voice stays on top.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Use music on a social reel, usually. Leave it off or keep it very quiet on the property page video, on luxury listings and on rentals, where the voice alone reads as more honest. Whatever you choose, the voice stays clearly on top and the music sits under it; if you have to strain to hear a word, the music is too loud. Check the platform's current rules on which audio you may use before you post.

Yes to music on most social reels, no or very quiet on the property page video, luxury listings and rentals, and in every case the voice sits clearly on top. The test for volume is simple: play the video on a phone speaker in a normal room, and if you miss a single word of the script, the music is too loud. Before posting, check the platform's current rules on which audio you are allowed to use.

When music earns its place

A reel is competing with everything else in the feed. A light track under the voice gives the video energy and tells the viewer, before they have read a word, that this is a bright two-bed near the park and not a probate sale. On a starter home, a condo, a renovated bungalow, music usually helps.

Music also papers over the gaps. A 25-second script has pauses between sentences, and on a reel those pauses can feel like dead air. A track underneath makes a pause feel intentional.

When to leave it off

The property page video. A buyer opened the page on purpose, possibly at a desk, possibly with a colleague nearby. A video that starts with a beat before the voice feels like an advert. The 16:9 tour on the page reads better with the voice alone or with music so low it is barely there.

Luxury listings. Space, silence and a measured voice say expensive. A track says reel. If you use anything on a high-end home, make it slow, sparse and almost inaudible. More on that in listing video for a luxury home.

Rentals. Tenants are looking for the truth about a unit. Music reads as sales pressure. Voice, captions, honest photos.

Anything with a recorded walkthrough. If the viewer can hear the floor, the door, the quiet at the back of the house, that is information. Do not cover it.

How loud, in practice

  • The voice is the signal. Set the music so that every word is clear on a phone speaker, not just on headphones. Phone speakers flatten everything, and what sounded balanced on earbuds buries the voice on a phone.
  • Quieter than you think. Almost every first attempt has the music too loud. If you are unsure, bring it down and listen again.
  • No vocals under a voice. Two voices at once, one singing and one talking, means the listener follows neither. Instrumental only under a voiceover.
  • Match tempo to the home. A quick track on a slow, wide luxury video feels wrong. A slow track on a cheerful condo reel feels flat.

A worked example: three listings, three calls

A renovated two-bed condo near the train, reel for Instagram and TikTok: music yes, light and quick, under a 24-second voice. The property page version of the same listing: voice only.

A four-bed on an acre with a long drive, 16:9 listing video for the page and YouTube: no music, voice and the pauses, let the drive and the trees do the work. The reel for the same home: a slow, sparse track, low.

A one-bed rental above a shop: no music on anything. Voice, captions, the actual kitchen.

Licensing, briefly

What audio you may use depends on where the video is posted and on the source of the track, and both change. Check the current rules for Instagram, TikTok and YouTube before you post, and do not assume a track that was fine on one platform is fine on another or on your own website. If in doubt, no music is the safe answer and often the better one.

Mistakes that make a good reel sound cheap

  • Music that starts before the voice and runs for four seconds on its own. The viewer has scrolled by the time the script begins.
  • A track with a drop or a beat change halfway through a photo. The eye and the ear expect something to happen, and nothing does.
  • The same track on every listing for a year. Followers hear it and know what is coming before the first frame.
  • Music loud enough that the captions are doing the work the voice was supposed to do.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should a listing reel have music?

Usually yes for a social reel on a mid-range home or condo, where a light track gives the video energy and covers the pauses in the script. Leave it off or very quiet on luxury listings, rentals and the property page video.

How loud should background music be under a voiceover?

Low enough that every word of the script is clear through a phone speaker in a normal room. If you miss a single word, bring it down. Use instrumental tracks only; a sung vocal under a spoken voice makes both hard to follow.

Can I use any song on a listing video?

No. Which audio you may use depends on the platform and the source of the track, and both change. Check the current rules for each platform before you post, and treat no music as the safe default when you are unsure.

Does the property page video need music?

Generally not. A buyer opened the page on purpose and the video plays on request, so a voice-only tour reads as calmer and more honest. If you want something under it, keep it slow and barely audible.
