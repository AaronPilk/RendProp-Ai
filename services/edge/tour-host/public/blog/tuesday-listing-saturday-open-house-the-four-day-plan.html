Tuesday listing, Saturday open house: the four-day plan

List Tuesday, open house Saturday: a day-by-day plan for the four days between, from launch to announcement video to the sign-in that follows up itself.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. List on Tuesday and hold the open house Saturday: Tuesday is launch day (page, MLS, first post), Wednesday is the reel and the email, Thursday is the neighbours and the sign, Friday is the open-house announcement video, and on Saturday the property page link does the sign-in follow-up for you.

List on Tuesday, hold the open house on Saturday, and you have four working days in between. Use them in this order: Tuesday is launch day (page, MLS, first post), Wednesday is the reel and the email to your database, Thursday is the street (neighbours, sign rider, flyer), Friday is the open-house announcement video, and on Saturday the property page link on the sign-in sheet does the follow-up. Each day has one job. Here is each one.

Tuesday: go live with everything that exists

By Tuesday the photos are reviewed, the main photo is chosen and the landscape listing video is built and watched once for wrong details. Publish the property page in the morning with the details typed, the gallery in walk-through order, the video selected and the form live. Enter the MLS with the same main photo and the unbranded link in the virtual-tour field. Post the first vertical video at midday with the branded link. Text the seller the branded link so they can send it to their family. Then answer the first enquiries the same hour they arrive; Tuesday evening is when the first ones come.

Wednesday: the reel and the database

Wednesday's post is different from Tuesday's. Tuesday was the house; Wednesday is one thing about it: the kitchen, the deck, the view from the main bedroom. A short vertical cut from three or four photos with a one-line voiceover. The email to your database goes out Wednesday morning, not Tuesday, so it does not land on top of the launch post: the main photo, three lines, the branded link, and the open house date and time. People forward emails with a date in them.

Thursday: the street

Thursday is for the people who will walk past the sign. Add a rider with a QR code pointing at the branded link; a neighbour who scans it on a dog walk sends it to a friend who is looking. Drop a one-page flyer, with the same QR code and the open house time, at the twenty closest homes. Neighbours are the most reliable source of a buyer who already loves the street. If the home has a feature worth a third post, Thursday evening is the slot, but it is optional. Thursday is also the day to check the page once more on your own phone and fix anything the seller or a buyer has mentioned.

Friday: the announcement video

Friday's post is the open-house announcement, built from photos you already have: the front exterior, the main living space, the kitchen, with a voiceover that says the address or the neighbourhood, Saturday, the hours, and one reason to come. Captions on, because this one will be watched on mute in a queue. Post it Friday late morning and send the same clip by text to anyone who enquired this week but has not yet visited. Friday evening, print the sign-in sheet with the branded link and a QR code at the top.

Saturday: the open house

Visitors sign in with a name and a mobile number, and the sheet tells them to scan the code to see the video and the full gallery. Some scan it on the porch; more scan it in the car afterwards, which is the point. Anyone who then enquires through the page arrives in your inbox with the date they want for a private viewing, which is the follow-up you would have had to make yourself. Saturday evening, reply to each of those within the hour, and text the seller the visitor count and any feedback worth repeating.

The four days, worked through

A 3-bed in a cul-de-sac. Tuesday 9 a.m. page published; 9:30 MLS entered; noon reel posted; 6 p.m. two enquiries, both answered by 6:30, one booked for Thursday. Wednesday 8 a.m. the database email with Saturday 1 to 3 in the subject line; 11 a.m. a kitchen reel. Thursday the rider goes on the sign and twenty flyers go out; the Thursday showing goes well; the seller reports a neighbour asked about the price. Friday 11 a.m. the announcement video posts and goes by text to three enquirers who have not visited. Saturday fourteen visitors sign in; six scan the code; two enquire through the page that evening for private viewings on Monday and Tuesday. By Sunday the listing has had a launch, a feature post, a street campaign, an announcement and an open house, and every enquiry sits in one inbox with a date attached.

Where the four days go wrong

  • Launching Tuesday without the video, then posting it Thursday as if it were news.
  • Sending the database email on Tuesday on top of the launch post, so both get half the attention.
  • A QR code on the sign pointing at a portal page where other agents may appear beside your listing.
  • Printing the announcement time wrong. Say it out loud before you record it.
  • No sign-in follow-up until Monday. Saturday evening is when the visitors are still thinking about it.

The pieces in Rendprop

Every video in the plan comes from the same photo set: the landscape listing video on the page, Tuesday's reel, Wednesday's feature cut and Friday's announcement, each a vertical social video with a recorded or AI voiceover and word-by-word captions. The property page carries the gallery, the video, the details, your contact card and an enquiry form that asks for the date the visitor wants, and the branded link from the Share sheet is what goes on the rider, the flyer and the sign-in sheet. Enquiries land in the inbox per listing. See social video, enquiries, and read the open-house announcement video and the sign-in that follows up itself.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Is four days enough between listing and open house?

Yes, if the media is ready on Tuesday. The four days are for distribution, not production: launch, a feature post, the street, the announcement, then the open house. If the photos are not reviewed by Tuesday, move the open house a week rather than launch half-ready.

Should the database email go out on launch day?

The day after. On launch day the post and the MLS entry are already competing for attention. A Wednesday email with the open house date in the subject line gets forwarded; a Tuesday email gets buried under the launch.

What goes on the open house sign-in sheet?

Name, mobile number, and a QR code at the top pointing at the branded property page link, with a line saying the video and full gallery are there. Visitors who scan it later and enquire through the page arrive in your inbox with a date, which is the follow-up done for you.

Do I need a different video for each day?

Different cuts from the same photos, not different shoots. Tuesday's reel is the house, Wednesday's is one feature, Friday's is the announcement with the hours. Each is a short vertical video with captions, built from the set you already have.
