No open house: marketing a listing privately

A listing with no open house is marketed through the page and the people: the link to matched buyers and agents, appointments only, a video first.

The property page does what the open house would have done for the public: the gallery, the video, the details and a way to ask for a visit. “No open house” is not “no marketing.” It is marketing with the front door closed and the page open. Sellers choose it for privacy, for security, because they do not want the street through the house, or because the home shows better one buyer at a time. All of those are fine reasons, and none of them should cost the seller exposure.

Quarry Lane, no Sunday

A 4-bed on Quarry Lane whose owners do not want the neighbours through the house. Listed on a Wednesday. The week:

  • Wednesday. The page is published: twenty-six photos, a listing video with a plain voiceover, the facts, your contact card, the enquiry form. The description says “viewings by appointment.” No hours anywhere.
  • Wednesday afternoon. The link goes by text to the six buyers in your own pipeline who fit: right price, right area, right size. Each text has one line about why this house for them.
  • Thursday. The link goes to the fifteen agents who have sold 4-beds in this part of town in the last year, with “appointments only, call me, happy to fit around your buyer.”
  • Friday. The email to the database: one photo, four facts, “private viewings by appointment,” the link.
  • Saturday and Sunday. Four appointments, each booked after a short call, each forty minutes, each with the seller out. Two enquiries come through the page from people who were forwarded the link; both ask for next week and give a date.

By Monday the house has been seen by six serious parties and nobody who was just curious. The seller's rug is intact and the neighbours know nothing.

The order of the people

Your own buyers first, because they are the warmest and because a sale inside your pipeline is the cleanest. Agents second, because they multiply the reach without a public event. Database third, because it is wide and slow. Social last, if at all, and only with the video and “by appointment,” never with hours or the implication that anyone can turn up.

Each group gets the same link and a different sentence. The sentence is the work.

What the video has to carry

Without an open house the video is the first time anyone walks the house. It should be the full route, in order, two to three minutes, with a voiceover that says the things you would say in the hall: the age of the roof, what is behind the back fence, which way the garden faces. The thirty-second reel is for social, if you use social; the page gets the long one. A buyer who has watched the long one and still calls has done their first visit.

The conversation before every appointment

Five minutes by phone. Who they are, whether they have an agent, what they are looking for, when suits. A private listing with no screening is just an open house spread over three weeks, with more wear on the seller. The call is what makes “by appointment” mean something.

Where private marketing goes quiet

  • Private becomes invisible. No open house and no outreach is a listing nobody knows about. The link has to go to people, by name, in the first forty-eight hours.
  • MLS and office rules. Some listings are marketed outside the MLS, some within it with no open house, and the rules differ by MLS and by brokerage; check the current ones that apply to you before deciding how and where the listing appears.
  • The page gives away what the seller wanted private. If the seller does not want the exact address public, do not type it. The page shows what you enter; a street name and the area is enough for a page that is only sent to people you have chosen.
  • No way to ask. Sending a video file or a portal link instead of the page leaves the buyer with nowhere to request a visit.
  • Slow replies. With no Sunday to funnel people into, the replies are the event. Same day, every time.
  • The seller hears nothing. Without a Sunday there is no obvious moment to report. Send the count every Monday anyway.

Make the list before the page goes live

Write the three lists, your buyers, the agents, the database, and the sentence for each before you publish. Then the day the page goes live is the day the link goes out, and the listing is private without being quiet. For the showing side, read sending the property page before the showing.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Can I market a listing well without an open house?

Yes, as long as the link goes to people by name in the first two days: your own matched buyers, the agents who sell that type of home, then your database. The property page replaces the public event with the gallery, the video and an enquiry form, and every showing is booked after a short call.

Who should get the link first for a private listing?

Your own pipeline buyers who fit the price, area and size, each with one line about why this house suits them. Then the agents who have sold similar homes nearby. Then the database by email. Social last, with the video and the words by appointment, never with hours.

Does a private listing still need a video?

More than a public one. With no open house, the video is the first walk through the house for everyone. Make it the full route in order, two to three minutes, with a voiceover that answers the hallway questions, so each appointment is a second visit by someone who already likes the layout.

What if the seller does not want the address on the page?

Leave it out. The page shows the details you enter, so a street name and the area can stand in for the house number on a page that only goes to people you have chosen. Check your MLS and brokerage rules for how the listing itself must appear, because those vary.
