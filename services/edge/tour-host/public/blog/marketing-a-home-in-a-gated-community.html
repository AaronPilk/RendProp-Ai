Marketing a home in a gated community

Behind a gate buyers cannot drive by, so the page is the drive-by. Show the street, house and amenities with permission, and say how showings get in.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Behind a gate a buyer cannot drive past the house, so the listing page has to do the drive-by: the street, the approach, the house from the curb, the neighbors' upkeep and the amenities, all shot with the association's permission and with residents out of frame. State plainly how showings are admitted, give the fee as the association states it, and point to the association's documents instead of summarizing them.

A gate changes one thing about marketing a home: the buyer cannot drive by. They cannot see the street, the neighbors' lawns, the distance to the clubhouse or how the house sits on its lot without an appointment, so the property page has to do that drive-by for them. Shoot the approach, the street and the house from the curb as carefully as the kitchen, photograph the amenities with the association's permission and nobody in them, and say in plain words how a showing is arranged and admitted. Then give the fee as the association states it and point to its documents rather than paraphrasing them.

What the gate changes for you

Three practical things. Access for the photographer and for every showing goes through the gate's procedure, so find out what it is before you book anyone and put the arrangement in your showing instructions. Photographing the gate, the guardhouse, the clubhouse and the pool may need the association's go-ahead; ask, and ask what they would rather you did not publish. And drive-by traffic is zero, so the yard sign works only inside the gate and the link does all the outside work; the page sent before the showing is how a buyer decides to make the appointment at all.

The shot list that replaces the drive-by

  1. The entrance, gate and guardhouse, from the public side, with the association's agreement. Attendants and residents out of frame.
  2. The drive from the gate to the street, two frames, so the buyer sees the distance and the upkeep.
  3. The street with the house in it, shot from a few doors down so the neighbors' lawns and the streetscape read.
  4. The house from the curb, the frame a buyer would take through a windshield.
  5. Front door and entry.
  6. Interior in the usual order, with any view of the common green, the lake or the course shot toward it.
  7. Back yard and what is beyond its fence: another lot, a berm, open space.
  8. Amenities, empty: clubhouse, pool, courts, trails, the dock if there is one. Early morning, with permission.
  9. The walk or drive from the house to the nearest amenity, one frame, so the buyer knows it is three minutes and not fifteen.

The listing video can open at the gate if the association is comfortable with that; otherwise open on the street with the house in it. Portrait frames of the house from the curb and the pool make the vertical social video. Keep residents and license plates out of everything, including the background of the street frames.

The page: how to get in, what it costs, what it includes

  • How showings are arranged: by appointment through you, with the gate notified, and anything the buyer needs to bring.
  • The fee as the association states it, how often it is paid, and where a buyer reads what it covers. Do not list what the fee covers in your own words.
  • Amenities by name, and whether any carry a separate charge, as the association states.
  • Rules the buyer will ask about, such as rentals, pets and parking, described as the association's current rules with a note to confirm with the association. Never state them as settled fact.
  • Lot position: which street, what the back fence faces, distance to the gate and to the amenities.
  • Price, beds, baths and square feet in the details fields, as on any home.

Worked example: a 4-bed two streets in from the gate

A 4-bed, 3-bath on a cul-de-sac two streets from a staffed gate, backing onto a berm with a walking trail beyond, in a community with a clubhouse, two pools and tennis courts. The agent calls the association's office, confirms the photographer's access for Wednesday morning, and is told the gate and clubhouse may be photographed but not the guard. She shoots at 8 a.m.: the entrance with no one in the booth, two frames of the main drive, the cul-de-sac from its mouth with the house at the end, the house from the curb, the interior in the usual order, the back yard with the berm and the trail gate, the clubhouse and the quieter pool before they open, and the path from the cul-de-sac to the pool. Fifteen landscape frames make the listing video, opening on the cul-de-sac. The page says: showings by appointment through the listing agent with the gate notified in advance, fee as stated by the association with its documents available on request, two pools, clubhouse and courts, rental and pet rules per the association's current documents, back fence onto a berm with trail access. The house from the curb is the main photo.

Where gated listings go wrong

  • No street context. Interiors only, and the buyer has no idea what the neighborhood looks like because they cannot go and look.
  • Residents in the pool frame. They did not agree to it. Shoot early and empty.
  • Summarizing the rules. A sentence about rentals that turns out to be out of date is a problem at closing. Point to the documents.
  • No showing instructions. A buyer's agent who cannot work out how to get in books a different house.
  • A sky or lawn edit on the common areas. The association maintains them; they look how they look. Keep edits to the seller's own lot, with disclosure.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What photos does a gated community listing need that others do not?

The entrance, the drive from the gate, the street with the house in it, the house from the curb and the walk to the nearest amenity. Buyers cannot drive by, so the page does it for them.

Can I photograph the gate, the clubhouse and the pool?

Ask the association first and follow what they say. Shoot early with nobody in frame, and keep residents, attendants and license plates out of every photo.

How should I describe the association's fee and rules?

Give the fee as the association states it and point to its documents for what it covers. Describe rules on rentals, pets and parking as the association's current rules with a note to confirm, never as settled fact in your own words.

What should the page say about showings?

That they are by appointment through you, that the gate is notified in advance, and anything the buyer needs to bring. A buyer's agent who cannot see how to get in will book a different house.
