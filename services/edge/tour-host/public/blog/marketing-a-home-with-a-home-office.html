Marketing a home with a home office

A home office sells on a door, a window, distance from the noise and internet you can name. Stage it, show it early in the video, state the speed test.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

A home office sells on a door that closes, a window, distance from the noisy parts of the house and internet the seller can name and has measured. Those are the four things a remote worker checks, in that order, and most listings mention none of them and show a desk in a corner. Stage the room as an office, shoot it from the doorway with the door and the window both in frame, put it in the first third of the video, and state the internet provider and a dated speed test on the page.

Who is buying the office

Three buyers. The full-time remote worker who takes calls all day and needs the room to be quiet and the backdrop to be presentable. The couple who both work from home and need two separate spaces, not one office and a kitchen table. And the small-business owner who wants clients, deliveries or a second monitor setup to fit without taking over the house. Each is checking whether the office is a real room with a door or a bedroom you have renamed, and whether the internet will hold a video call. Write the page and plan the photos for those three people, and the general buyer loses nothing.

What to state on the page

  • Which room is the office, where it sits in the house, and what is on the other side of its walls.
  • Dimensions, a door, a window, which way the window faces and whether the afternoon sun hits the desk.
  • Internet: provider, connection type as the seller knows it, and a download and upload speed from a test the seller ran, with the date. Say it is the seller's test.
  • Wiring: a hardwired network point, the number of outlets, a dedicated circuit if there is one.
  • A second workspace, if there is one: a nook, a finished basement room, a converted garage, and whether it has a door.
  • Whether the office counts in the bedroom total. If it has a closet and a window and you are counting it as a bedroom, say that it is currently used as an office. If it has no closet, do not count it.
  • Price, beds, baths and square feet in the fields at the top.

Keep the language plain. Remote workers in a tech hub or a commuter suburb have read a hundred listings that promise a perfect office; the one with a speed test and a photo of the door stands out.

Shooting the office so it reads as one

  1. From the doorway, door open and visible at the edge of frame, window in view, desk set up with a monitor, a lamp and nothing else. This is the frame.
  2. From the desk chair, looking toward the door, so the buyer sees what is behind them on a call.
  3. The window view, with the window pull so the garden or street reads.
  4. Built-ins or shelving, tidy, two-thirds full.
  5. The network point or the panel, if there is one. A small frame that answers a big question.
  6. The hallway or stair that leads to the office, so the distance from the kitchen is visible in one frame.
  7. A second workspace, shot the same way.
  8. The rest of the house in the usual order.

In the listing video the office should appear early, close to the exterior and the main living space, for listings where remote workers are the likely buyer. When you review the video, check that the office frames are in it; if they are buried, leave out weaker rooms so the office carries more of the video. For the vertical social video, a portrait frame from the doorway with the door, desk and window in one vertical column is the one that stops a remote worker's scroll.

Worked example: a 4-bed where one bed is the office

A 4-bed, 2.5-bath two-storey where the fourth bedroom, at the front of the upper floor over the garage, has been an office for six years. It has a closet, a north window, a solid door, a hardwired network point and a dedicated circuit the seller added. The seller runs a speed test on a Tuesday morning and screenshots it. The agent stages the room with the seller's desk, a single monitor, a lamp and a plant, clears the shelves to two-thirds, and shoots from the doorway, from the chair, toward the window, the shelves, the network point, and the upstairs hall from the top of the stairs. The listing video is built from fourteen frames with the office frames among them. The page reads: fourth bedroom currently used as an office, over the garage at the front of the upper floor, closet, north window, solid door, hardwired network point and dedicated circuit, fibre from the named provider with the seller's download and upload figures from a test dated that Tuesday. The doorway frame of the office is the second photo in the gallery, after the exterior.

What goes wrong with home office listings

  • A desk in the dining room called an office. It is a desk in the dining room. Say that if it is true, and sell the room that has a door.
  • Internet described as high-speed with no number. Every listing says that. A dated test from the seller is the only version a buyer believes.
  • An add-furniture edit that invents the office. Staging an empty room as an office is fine with the Virtually staged label; the edit should not add a window or a door that is not there. Compare against the original.
  • A tidy edit that removes the cables and the printer. Harmless-looking, but the buyer will see them at the showing. Clear them in person instead.
  • Counting a closet-less room as a bedroom. Describe it as an office or a den.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What does a remote worker check first in a listing?

Whether the office has a door that closes, a window, distance from the kitchen and the children's rooms, and internet with a named provider and a measured speed. Answer those four on the page and in the photos.

How should I describe the internet on the page?

Provider, connection type as the seller knows it, and download and upload figures from a speed test the seller ran, with the date, stated as the seller's test. Vague phrases like high-speed carry no weight.

Can I count the office as a bedroom?

Only if it has what a bedroom needs in your market, usually a closet and a window, and then say it is currently used as an office. If it has no closet, call it an office or a den.

Where should the office appear in the listing video?

Early, close to the exterior and the main living space, when remote workers are the likely buyer. Scene order is planned automatically, so review the video, make sure the office frames are in it, and drop weaker rooms if the office is getting lost.
