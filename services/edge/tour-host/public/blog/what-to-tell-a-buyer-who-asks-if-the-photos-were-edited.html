What to tell a buyer who asks if the photos were edited

Answer in one sentence, name the edits, offer the originals. A script for the question, what not to say, and how to be ready before anyone asks.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Say yes, name the edits, offer the originals: “Yes. The living room is virtually staged and the front photo has a replaced sky. Everything else is as shot, and I can send you the originals of those two now.” Specific, immediate and unapologetic. The answer only works if you know what was edited and have the originals on your phone.

Say yes, name the edits, offer the originals. Something like: “Yes. The living room and primary bedroom are virtually staged, and the front exterior has a replaced sky. Everything else is as shot. I can send you the originals of those three right now.” Specific, immediate, unapologetic, and it only works if you know exactly what was edited on this listing and can reach the originals from your phone, so the preparation happens before the question, not during it.

Why the question is a good sign

A buyer who asks is a buyer who is paying attention and who intends to take the home seriously. They are not accusing you. They are checking whether they can trust the rest of what they see. A clear answer turns that check into confidence. A vague one turns it into a search for what else you are not saying.

The question also arrives at a moment that matters: often at the showing, sometimes on the phone before booking one, occasionally from the buyer's agent in writing. The answer should be the same in all three places.

The script, in three parts

1. Yes, and here is what

Lead with yes if anything was altered. Then list the altered photos and the edit on each. Staging, sky, lawn, twilight, items removed. If nothing was altered beyond exposure and straightening, say that: “No alterations. The photos were colour-corrected and straightened, which every listing photo gets, and nothing was added or removed.”

2. Everything else is as shot

Buyers want the boundary. Knowing that two photos were edited and twenty-six were not is the reassurance. Say it explicitly.

3. Here are the originals

Offer, and mean it. Send the untouched original of each altered photo by text or email while they are standing there. A buyer who has seen the empty living room next to the staged one has nothing left to wonder about.

A worked example: Saturday, at the kitchen island

A couple at an open house. The husband asks, “Were these photos touched up?” The agent says: “Yes. The family room is virtually staged, it is empty as you will see in a minute, and the front photo has a blue sky swapped in because it rained on shoot day. The rest are as photographed, just colour-corrected. Want me to text you the originals of those two?” He says sure. She sends them from her phone before they reach the family room. The wife looks at the empty room on the phone and the empty room in front of her and says “it is bigger than the photo.” The conversation is over and the showing continues on the home's merits.

The same question, answered with “a little enhancement, standard stuff,” would have followed the couple through every room.

What not to say

  • “Just a bit of enhancement.” Vague, and it sounds like a dodge even when it is true.
  • “The photographer handles that.” You published the photos. The answer is yours.
  • “Everyone does it.” True and irrelevant. They asked about this listing.
  • “No,” when staging was used. One label on one photo in the gallery contradicts you, and now the buyer has caught you in it.
  • “I would have to check.” Acceptable once, if you then check within the hour. A second time, it is a pattern.

Being ready before anyone asks

Three things make the script possible:

  1. A per-listing edit list. One line per altered photo: which photo, which edit. Write it when you publish. The provenance post covers keeping this with the originals and labels.
  2. Originals on your phone. Not on a laptop, not in the photographer's inbox. The post on originals explains why.
  3. Labels already in place. If every staged and altered photo already carries its label, the buyer's question is often answered before they ask it, and your answer confirms what they read rather than revealing something new.

When the buyer's agent asks in writing

Same answer, in writing, with the originals attached. Keep it to the facts: which photos, which edits, originals attached, labels as shown in the listing. Do not explain why the edits were made or defend them. If the agent's email uses the word “misrepresented,” forward it to your broker before replying, and keep the reply factual. Rules differ by MLS and state; your broker will know what the office wants on record.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What is the best way to answer a buyer who asks if the photos were edited?

Lead with yes if anything was altered, name each altered photo and the edit, say that everything else is as shot, and offer the untouched originals on the spot. Specific and immediate beats reassuring and vague.

What if only colour correction and straightening were done?

Say so plainly: no alterations, the photos were colour-corrected and straightened as every listing photo is, and nothing was added or removed. Buyers accept that readily when it is stated rather than implied.

Should I send the buyer the original photos?

Yes, when they ask. Text or email the untouched original of each altered photo while they are with you. A buyer who has seen the staged room beside the empty original has nothing left to wonder about.

What if the buyer's agent asks in writing and uses the word misrepresented?

Forward it to your broker before replying, then answer with the facts only: which photos, which edits, originals attached, labels as shown. Rules differ by MLS and state, and your broker will know what the office wants on record.
