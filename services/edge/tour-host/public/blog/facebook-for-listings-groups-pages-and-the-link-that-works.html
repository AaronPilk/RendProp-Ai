Facebook for listings: groups, pages and the link that works

Post the listing video on your business page, share into local groups whose rules allow it, and use the branded page link so the preview shows the home.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Put the listing on your business page first, then share it into local groups whose rules allow listings, with a different first line for each group. Use the branded property page link, not the MLS portal link, and check the preview on your own phone before you post. Reply to every comment in the thread where it was asked.

Facebook works for a listing in three places: your business page, local groups whose rules allow listings, and, sparingly, your personal profile. The post itself is the vertical or landscape video plus a short caption, and the link is always the branded property page, because that is the link that shows the home in the preview and tells the viewer who to call. Check each group's rules before you post, and check the preview on your own phone before anyone else sees it.

The three places and what each is for

Your business page

This is the home for the post. It carries the video, the full caption with price and facts, and the link. Everything you share elsewhere should trace back here, and it is the version you can send to the seller as proof the listing went out.

Local groups

Town groups, neighborhood groups, buy-and-sell groups and the occasional group built around a specific subdivision. Some welcome listings, some tolerate them on a specific day of the week, some ban them outright. The rules are in each group's description, and the admins enforce them differently. Read the rules every time; they change.

Your personal profile

Friends and family are not a buyer pool, but they are a sharing pool. One personal post per listing, written as a person rather than an agent, is fine. Three is a pattern people mute.

A colonial, posted three ways

A four-bed colonial on a half acre in a town with an active community group, listed on a Wednesday.

  • Business page, Wednesday morning. The vertical video. Caption: Half an acre inside the town line is rare and this one has it. Four beds, two and a half baths, 2,300 square feet, built 1994, new furnace in 2024. $639,000. Open Saturday 12 to 2. Full gallery and video at the link. The branded property page link follows.
  • Town community group, Wednesday afternoon. The group's rules allow listings on weekdays with no price in the first line. So: For anyone who has been waiting for a half-acre lot in town, this one is on Maple from today. Four beds, open Saturday 12 to 2. Details at the link. Same link.
  • Personal profile, Thursday. One photo, the back yard. Text: The yard on my new listing is the kind I grew up with. Open Saturday if you know someone looking. Same link.

Three posts, three first lines, one link. Each one reads as if it was written for the people who will see it, because it was.

The link that works

Paste the branded property page link, not the MLS portal link and not the unbranded link. A portal link often opens a page with other agents' names on it and a login prompt. The unbranded link opens a page with no name and no number, which is correct for the MLS and wrong for a post that is supposed to produce a call. The difference is explained in unbranded versus branded on social.

Before the post goes live, paste the link into a message to yourself and look at the preview. You want the main photo and the listing's title. If the preview is blank or shows the wrong image, fix the page's main photo before you post, not after.

Comments are the second post

In groups especially, the comments are where the listing gets sold or sunk. Answer every real question in the thread, with the fact, in the same thread. Price questions get the price. Tax questions get the figure from the listing sheet. A comment about the busy road gets an honest answer about the road. A neighbor who says the basement floods gets a calm reply and a private message asking what they know, because you want that information before a buyer's inspector finds it. See replying to comments for the full pattern.

What goes wrong on Facebook

  • Posting into a group that bans listings. The post is removed, and sometimes you are. Read the rules first.
  • The same text in six groups. Members who belong to several groups see the same paragraph six times. Change the first line for each.
  • A link preview that shows nothing. A bare link with a grey box underneath gets no taps. Check it before posting.
  • Leaving comments unanswered for a day. In a group, silence reads as something to hide.
  • Posting the seller's details. No names, no reasons for selling, no moving dates. Groups are public enough.
  • Describing the ideal buyer. Great for a young family. Ask your broker about fair housing language before any sentence that describes people instead of the home.

Start with the group rules

List the groups you belong to in the towns you work, open each one's rules and note whether listings are allowed and on what terms. That list is the plan for every listing from here on.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should I post listings in Facebook groups?

Only in groups whose rules allow it, and on the terms the rules set. Many town and neighborhood groups welcome listings; some restrict them to a day or ban them. Read the rules each time, because admins change them.

Which link should I use in a Facebook listing post?

The branded property page link. It shows the home in the preview and carries your name and number. The MLS portal link often shows other agents and a login prompt, and the unbranded link shows no contact at all.

Why does my listing link show a blank preview?

Usually the page's main photo was not set or the link was pasted incorrectly. Paste the link into a message to yourself and check the preview before you post. If it is wrong, fix the page's main photo first.

How often should a listing go on my personal profile?

Once per listing, written as a person rather than a brochure, is enough. Friends share; they rarely buy. Repeated listing posts on a personal profile get muted.
