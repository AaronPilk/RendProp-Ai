YouTube for listings: landscape video, titles and descriptions

Upload the 16:9 landscape video, title it the way a buyer searches (area, home type, beds, price) and write a description that leads with the page link.

YouTube is where listing videos get found months later by someone searching the town, so write for that person.

On YouTube, upload the 16:9 landscape video as the main listing video, title it the way a buyer types a search, and write a description that leads with the property page link and then gives the facts in plain lines. A title like 3 bed 2 bath ranch in Maple Grove under $500k, home tour gets found by the person searching the town three months from now. The vertical reel can go up separately as a Short, but the horizontal tour with a searchable title is the one that keeps working.

Why landscape video belongs on YouTube

YouTube is watched on laptops, tablets and televisions as much as on phones, and a buyer who searches for homes in a town is sitting down to look, not scrolling. The 16:9 video shows every room at full width and runs long enough to be a tour, 60 to 120 seconds. It is the same video that sits on the property page, which means one upload serves two homes.

Shorts take the 9:16 reel and are a different audience: feed viewers, not searchers. Post both if you have both. The horizontal upload is the one this post is about.

The title: write the search, not the slogan

Nobody searches for a hidden gem. People search for the town, the home type and the budget. The title should contain those words in roughly the order a person types them.

  • Area or town. The town or the neighbourhood as locals say it. If the street is well known, the street.
  • Home type and size. 3 bed 2 bath ranch. 2 bed condo. 4 bed colonial on half an acre.
  • Price bracket. Under $500k. Around $1.2M. A bracket ages better than an exact figure if the price changes.
  • The format word. Home tour, listing video, walkthrough. The word a searcher adds.

Example: 2 bed condo with park view, Riverside, $385k, listing video. Plain, specific, searchable. Keep the brokerage and your name out of the title; they are in the channel name and the description.

The description: link first, facts second

The first line of the description is visible before anyone taps to expand. Put the property page link there, with four words saying what it is: Full tour, photos and enquiry form. Then:

  • Price, beds, baths, square feet, lot, year built. One per line.
  • Two or three sentences from the script: what the buyer cannot see in a photo.
  • The open house day and time, if there is one.
  • Your name, brokerage and the way to reach you.
  • A line noting any virtually staged or AI-altered photos in the video, in plain words.

Write the description so that someone who reads only it, without watching, knows the home and can get to the page.

A worked example: the Maple Grove ranch

A three-bed, two-bath ranch on a cul-de-sac, listed at $489,000, open house Saturday. The agent uploads the 16:9 page video, 82 seconds, fourteen photos, the agent's own voice.

Title: 3 bed 2 bath ranch on a cul-de-sac, Maple Grove, under $500k, home tour.

Description, first line: Full tour, all photos and the enquiry form: [property page link]. Then the numbers one per line, three sentences on the renovated kitchen, the fenced yard and the morning light, the open house, the agent's contact, and Living room photo is virtually staged; original available on the property page.

The reel for the same listing goes up as a Short with the same first-line link. Twelve minutes of work, and the video is findable by anyone typing Maple Grove ranch into the search box for as long as the listing is live.

Mistakes that keep a listing video from being found

  • A title that is the address only. Buyers do not search addresses they have not heard of. Add the town, the type and the bracket.
  • Uploading the vertical reel as the main video. It plays as a tall column on a laptop with black sides.
  • Burying the link. If the property page link is the fifth line, most people see your brokerage boilerplate and nothing else.
  • Music that is not cleared for the platform. Check YouTube's current rules on audio before you upload rather than finding out after the listing is live.
  • Leaving a sold listing up without saying so. Edit the title to add Sold, or point the description at the page, which can be marked sold.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should I upload the vertical reel or the horizontal video to YouTube?

Both, as different things. The 16:9 landscape video is the main upload with a searchable title and a full description. The 9:16 reel can go up as a Short for feed viewers. Do not upload the vertical video as the main listing video; it plays as a narrow column on a laptop.

What should a listing video title on YouTube include?

The town or neighbourhood, the home type with beds and baths, a price bracket, and a format word such as home tour or listing video. Write it the way a buyer types a search, and leave your name and brokerage for the channel name and description.

Where should the property page link go in the description?

In the first line, with a few words saying what it is, because that line is visible before anyone expands the description. Follow it with the facts one per line, the open house, your contact details and a note about any virtually staged photos.

What do I do with the YouTube video after the home sells?

Either edit the title to add Sold, or leave it and let the description's link do the work: mark the listing sold on its property page so anyone who clicks through sees the status. Removing the video loses a searchable example of your work.
