The listing launch checklist: photos, video, page, links, post

A listing launch checklist in the order it happens: review the photos, make one video, publish the page, place both links, post. With a Tuesday example.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A listing launch is complete when the photos are reviewed and ordered, one video exists in the format you will post first, the property page is published with its details filled in, the branded link is on your channels and the unbranded link is in the MLS, and the first post is up. Work the list in that order.

A listing launch is done when five things are true: the photos are reviewed and ordered, one video exists in the format you will post first, the property page is published with its details filled in, both links are where they belong (branded on your channels, unbranded in the MLS virtual-tour field), and the first post is up. Here is that list in the order it actually happens, with the check that matters at each step.

Before the shoot: three things to settle

  1. Type the address and the details once (price, beds, baths, square feet) in the place you will publish from, so every later piece pulls the same numbers.
  2. Walk the home with the seller and agree what is shown and what is not. The home office with the family photos, the garage with the project car, the room the teenager will not clear: decide now, not at the review.
  3. Note which rooms are empty. That is your staging decision, and it is easier to make before you see the photos than after.

Photos: a ten-minute review, not an hour

  1. Cull to the set a buyer will scroll. Twenty-five to thirty-five photos is enough for most homes.
  2. Order them the way a buyer walks the house: exterior, entry, main living space, kitchen, bedrooms, baths, outdoor space.
  3. Pick the main photo. It is the one that appears on the page, the MLS and the first post, so it should be the same everywhere.
  4. If any photo has an AI edit (blue sky, twilight, tidy the room, staging), compare it with the original before it goes anywhere. Confirm the edit carries its disclosure and check your MLS rules on altered images.

Video: one format first

Decide which video goes live first. A landscape listing video belongs on the property page, in the MLS tour field and on YouTube. A vertical social video belongs on Instagram, TikTok and Facebook. Make the one that matches your first post, then the other. Keep the voiceover under thirty seconds for the vertical cut, turn on word-by-word captions because most people watch with the sound off, and watch the whole thing once looking only for wrong details: a staged room that is not labelled, a bedroom count that does not match the page.

Page: fill in what buyers ask first

  • Price, beds, baths and square feet typed correctly. Buyers check these before they look at a second photo.
  • Gallery in the order you set above, with the main photo first.
  • The listing video selected so it plays on the page when someone taps it.
  • Your contact card with the number you actually answer.
  • The enquiry form live, and you know where its messages land.

Links: two links, two jobs

The branded link goes on the sign rider, the flyer, the email to your database, your social captions and your text messages. The unbranded link, with no name, no contact details and no branding, goes in the MLS virtual-tour field, which is what that field is built for; check your board's current rules before you paste it. Copy both before you leave the website step and paste them into one note so you are not hunting for them on Thursday. See why there are two links.

Post: the first twenty-four hours

  1. Post the vertical video with the branded link in the caption or bio, and a plain line about the home.
  2. Email the database with the main photo and the branded link, not an attachment.
  3. Text the five people who told you they wanted a home like this. A link in a text with a preview beats any post.

A Tuesday launch, worked through

The listing agreement for a 3-bed in a cul-de-sac is signed Thursday. The photographer shoots Monday morning. Monday afternoon you cull, order, pick the main photo and compare the two twilight exteriors with the originals. Monday evening you build the vertical video for Instagram and the landscape video for the page. Tuesday at 9 a.m. the page is published, the unbranded link is in the MLS, the branded link is in the email that goes out at 10, and the reel is posted at noon. By Tuesday evening the first enquiry asks for a Saturday viewing. That is a launch, and nothing on the list waited on a vendor.

Where launches go wrong

  • Publishing the page before the details are typed, then fixing the price after the link has been shared.
  • A different main photo on the MLS, the page and the post. Pick one.
  • Posting the video before the AI edits were compared with the originals.
  • Forgetting the unbranded link, so the MLS tour field carries your contact details. Check your board's current rules on what that field may contain.
  • Launching Friday evening and answering the first enquiry on Monday.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Which video should go live first, the landscape video or the vertical one?

The one that matches your first post. If your first post is an Instagram reel, make the vertical social video first. If the first place the listing appears is the MLS and your email, make the landscape listing video first and put it on the property page.

How many photos should be on the page at launch?

Twenty-five to thirty-five is enough for most homes. More than that and buyers stop scrolling before they reach the backyard. Lead with the exterior and the main living space, and end with outdoor space.

Does the unbranded link really matter?

Yes. The MLS virtual-tour field is built for a link with no agent name, contact details or branding, which is what the unbranded link is. Check your board's current rules on that field, then use the unbranded link there and keep the branded one for your own channels.

What if the listing goes live before the video is ready?

Publish the page with the photos and details, place both links, and add the video when it is reviewed, then publish again. The listing keeps its two links. Do not hold the whole launch for one piece.
