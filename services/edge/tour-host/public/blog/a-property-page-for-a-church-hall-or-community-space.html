A property page for a church, hall or community space

A hall for hire needs one page that shows the room empty, states capacity, lists hours and lets a visitor ask about a date. No designer, no PDF, one link.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A church hall, village hall or community space for hire needs one simple page: the room shown empty and set up, the capacity, the hours it can be used, the kitchen and parking, and a form that asks which date the visitor wants. It replaces the PDF and the phone-tag, and it can be kept up by whoever has the phone that week.

A church hall, village hall or community centre that hires out its rooms needs one page that does four things: shows the room empty and set up, states the capacity honestly, lists the hours it can be booked, and lets a visitor ask about a date without calling. That page replaces the PDF nobody can find, the photo on the noticeboard and the voicemail that gets checked on Thursdays. It can be built from a phone by whoever runs bookings that month, and handed on when the role changes.

Who is looking and what they need

The people enquiring about a hall are planning something specific: a child's birthday party for thirty, a yoga class on Tuesday evenings, a wake for eighty, a craft fair with twenty tables. Each of them has the same first three questions: does it fit, is it free on my date, and what does it cost. The page answers the first two. The third is for the reply, because hall pricing has conditions that do not fit a page.

The photos a hall page needs

  1. The hall empty, from the main door. Chairs stacked at the side, floor clear, lights on. This is the photo that tells a parent whether thirty children can run around.
  2. The hall set for a seated event. Tables and chairs out in the layout you most often get asked for. Shows capacity better than a number.
  3. The stage or the front, if there is one. From the back of the room.
  4. The kitchen. Counters clear, urn and oven visible. Hirers ask whether they can make tea for eighty.
  5. Toilets, including the accessible one. Clean and lit.
  6. The entrance, the ramp and the car park. Access questions come up in every booking.
  7. Any second room. The small meeting room, the vestry, the side hall. Each one from its door.

Shoot on a bright morning with every light on. Push the stacked chairs to one wall for the empty shot. If a tidy edit is used to clear noticeboards or a stray trestle, keep the disclosure and check it against the original; the hirer is coming to this exact room.

Worked example: a village hall with a main room and a side room

A village hall has a main room that seats 120 at tables or holds 180 standing, a side room for 25, a kitchen and a car park for 40 cars. The booking secretary shoots ten photos on a Saturday morning while the room is empty, then sets twelve tables for a second set. The listing video opens on the car park and the entrance, goes through the lobby to the main room empty, cuts to the room set with tables, then the stage, the kitchen, the side room and the accessible toilet. Forty-five seconds, with a voiceover that states the capacities and the fact that the kitchen is included. The page shows capacity as 120 seated or 180 standing in the main room and 25 in the side room, the hours the hall is available, the gallery, the video on request, the secretary's contact details and a form that asks for the date. The first enquiry is a parent asking about a Sunday afternoon in March for a party of forty. The reply takes two minutes because the only question left is the price.

Hours and capacity, stated plainly

Hours are the window the hall can be hired, including the latest finish and any days it is not available. Capacity is the seated number for the layout people usually book, and the standing number separately. If there is a limit on music or a time by which the car park must be clear, put it in the voiceover; it saves an awkward email later.

Handing the page on

Halls are run by volunteers and the role moves. A page built in an app on a phone can be shared with the next secretary through a team, so the photos, the details and the enquiries stay in one place instead of in someone's personal email. The link on the noticeboard and the parish website stays the same when the person changes.

What makes a hall look worse than it is

  • Photos from the last jumble sale. Full of people and clutter. Shoot it empty.
  • Dark photos with the lights off to save electricity. Turn them all on for ten minutes.
  • Capacity from the fire certificate as the only number. Give the seated number too, or hirers over-book the room.
  • No kitchen or toilet photos. The two rooms every hirer asks about.
  • A PDF instead of a page. It does not open well on a phone and it goes out of date the moment the hours change.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What should a church hall or community space page show?

The main room empty and set up, the kitchen, the toilets including the accessible one, the entrance and car park, and any side rooms. Then the capacity, the hours it can be hired, a contact and a form that asks for the date.

Should the hire price be on the page?

Usually not. Hall pricing has conditions, such as member rates, deposits and hourly versus session rates, that do not fit a page and go out of date. State capacity and hours on the page and give the price in the reply.

How do we hand the page to the next booking secretary?

Through a team in the app, so the photos, details and enquiries stay in one place rather than in a personal email account. The link on the noticeboard and the website stays the same when the person changes.
