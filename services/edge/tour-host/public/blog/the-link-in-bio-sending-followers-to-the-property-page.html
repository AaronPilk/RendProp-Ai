The link in bio: sending followers to the property page

While a listing is active, the bio link should be that listing's property page, not your homepage. Change it when the listing changes, and check it weekly.

While a listing is active, the link in your bio should be that listing's property page. Not your homepage, not a portal search, not a page about you. A follower who taps the bio link after seeing a reel wants the house, and the page with the gallery, the video, the price and the form is where they should land. With more than one listing, use a short menu ordered by what you most want to sell, and change it the day anything changes.

Why the bio link matters more than the caption

The phrase link in bio exists because some platforms do not make caption links tappable; check how your own account currently handles them. Where they are not tappable, the bio link is the only path from a post to the property page, and it is a shared path across every post you have up. A reel from today, a grid post from last week and a story from this morning all point at the same place. If that place is wrong, every post is wrong at once.

One listing: the direct link

With a single active listing, the simplest setup is the best: the branded property page link, by itself, in the bio. The follower taps once and is on the page. There is nothing to choose from and nothing to get wrong.

Write the bio text to match. A line like Now listed: three-bed on Maple, open Saturday, tap the link, tells the follower what they will find. Change that line when the listing changes.

Several listings: a short menu

When you have two or more active listings, a single link page with a short list works: one line per listing, each going to its property page, ordered by what you most want to sell this week. Keep the list short enough to read without scrolling. Put the newest or the one with the open house at the top. Leave your general site at the bottom, if at all.

Nothing about this requires a particular tool. Any simple page you control with a few links will do, as long as each link goes straight to a property page and you update it yourself.

A fortnight with two listings and an open house

An agent has a condo listed on the 2nd and a townhouse on the 9th, with the townhouse open house on the 13th.

  • 2nd to 8th. Bio link: the condo's property page, direct. Bio line: New: two-bed condo downtown, link below.
  • 9th. Townhouse goes live. Bio link becomes a two-line menu: townhouse first, condo second. Bio line: Two listings, townhouse open Saturday.
  • 13th, morning. Townhouse stays on top. The open house story points at the bio.
  • 16th. Condo goes under contract. Its menu line is removed or changed to say under contract. The bio link can go back to a direct townhouse link.
  • Weekly, every Monday. Tap the bio link on a phone that is not logged into your account. Confirm it opens, that the page is right, and that the top listing is still the one you want on top.

Three edits in two weeks, each under a minute, and nobody ever tapped through to a home they could not buy.

What breaks

  • The link goes to your homepage. The follower lands on a page about you and has to find the house. Most do not.
  • The link is never updated. Three weeks after the sale, the bio still points at the page, and the page does not say sold. Mark it sold or move the link.
  • The link is a portal search. It opens with a login prompt, other agents' names, or an expired listing. Use your own property page.
  • The bio link is the unbranded link. It opens a page with no name on it. Swap in the branded one; the difference is in unbranded versus branded on social.
  • A caption says link in bio and the bio points at a different home. The reel was about the townhouse; the bio still says condo. Change the bio before the post goes up, not after.
  • A menu with fifteen entries. Past listings, your site, your reviews, a newsletter, a podcast. The follower wanted one house. Cut it to the active listings.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should my bio link go to my website or to the listing?

To the listing's property page while it is active. A follower who taps after a reel wants the house, not a page about you. Put your general site at the bottom of a link menu if you need it at all.

What do I do with the bio link when I have several listings?

Use a short link page with one line per active listing, ordered by what you most want sold this week. Keep it short enough to read without scrolling and update it the day a listing changes.

How often should I check the bio link?

Once a week, on a phone that is not logged into your account. Confirm it opens, that it shows the right listing, and that a sold home is marked sold or removed.

What if the bio link still points at a home that sold?

Mark the listing sold on its property page so anyone who taps sees that clearly, then move the link to the next listing. A page that says sold with your contact card on it is a fine place to land; a page that still says for sale is not.
