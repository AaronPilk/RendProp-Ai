Marketing a ski home

A ski home sells on how you get to the snow and what the house does with wet gear. Show the real route to the lift, shoot after a storm, state the access.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A ski home sells on exactly how a skier gets from the front door to the lift, which the listing must describe without rounding ski-in/ski-out up from a shuttle stop, and on what the house does with wet gear and snow: the boot room, the heated drive, the garage, the hot tub. Shoot it on a clear morning after a storm with the slopes in sun, show the actual route to the lift, and state winter road access and the drive from the airport in plain numbers.

A ski home sells on two things, in this order: exactly how a skier gets from the front door to the lift, and what the house does with wet gear, snow and eight people in boots. State the route to the lift as it is, in minutes and method, and never round a shuttle stop up to ski-in/ski-out. Show the boot room, the gear storage, the heated or plowed drive and the hot tub as the working parts of the house they are. Shoot on a clear morning after a storm, with sun on the slopes, and say how the house is reached in winter and how far the airport is.

Shooting a ski home in two seasons

  1. The route to the lift, winter. From the door, in three or four frames: the ski path, the shuttle stop with its sign, or the drive to the base, ending at the lift. If it is a walk, show the stairs too.
  2. The slopes from the house, from the deck or the living room window, at mid-morning when the sun is on them and the sky is clear.
  3. The house in snow from outside, after the walk and drive are cleared, so a buyer sees what clear looks like.
  4. The boot room or mudroom, with the boot dryer, the racks and the bench, gear on them.
  5. Gear storage and the garage, with room for skis, a sled and a car visible.
  6. The great room toward the fire or the view, with the ceiling height readable.
  7. The hot tub, steaming if you can time it, with the path from the door cleared.
  8. Kitchen, dining for a crowd, bedrooms with bed sizes visible, the bunk room, baths, laundry.
  9. The heated or plowed drive, and the access road at the property line.
  10. Summer frames, from the seller if dated or from a second shoot: the deck with the trail or the bike path, the slopes green, the town.

Skip the storm day; grey sky and falling snow photograph as fog. Shoot the clear morning after, before the sun drops behind the ridge. The listing video opens on the slopes from the deck or the first frame of the route to the lift. Portrait frames of the route, the tub and the boot room make a vertical social video skiers send each other in October.

Ski listings that lose a buyer's trust

  • Ski-in/ski-out that is not. If it needs a shuttle, a drive or a walk across a road, say exactly that. Skiers check the map before they call.
  • No winter access facts. A road that closes, a drive that needs chains, a lot plowed by a neighbor. State what the seller knows.
  • Summer-only photos. The buyer cannot picture the house in the season they are buying it for.
  • A sky edit that adds blue to a storm day. It also changes the snow and often the slopes. Compare against the original and keep the disclosure, or wait for the clear day.
  • A tidy edit on the boot room. The gear is the point; a sanitized boot room looks like a closet.
  • Stated rental rules. Give the seller's current permit or registration as the documents show it and tell the buyer to verify; ask your broker how to word it.

The page, with the numbers a skier wants

  • Route to the lift: method, minutes, and the name of the lift or base.
  • The resort or resorts within a stated drive, in minutes.
  • Winter access: road, plowing arrangement, heated drive, parking count with snow on the ground.
  • Boot room and gear storage, boot dryers, lockers, ski racks that convey.
  • Hot tub, sauna, fireplace, with ages where the seller knows them.
  • Sleeping capacity in the text with bed sizes, because ski homes host groups.
  • Roof, heating and fuel, snow management as the seller describes it.
  • Airport and drive time, and the drive in winter if it differs.
  • Summer use: trails, bike paths, the lake, the town, in distances.
  • Rental history as the seller documents it for a stated period, and the current permit status per the documents, with a note to verify.
  • Association fee and documents, for a condo or townhome, as the association states them.
  • Price, beds, baths and square feet in their fields.

Seasons and access roads are covered in mountain town listing marketing; the remote-buyer side is in marketing a vacation home.

Worked example: a 4-bed a six-minute walk from the lift

A 4-bed, 3-bath timber home on a plowed cul-de-sac, a six-minute walk along a cleared path to a named base lift, with a boot room with two dryers, a heated front walk, a two-car garage with a ski wall, an outdoor hot tub and a bunk room sleeping four. The agent waits out a Tuesday storm and shoots at 9:30 a.m. Wednesday under a clear sky: four frames along the path to the lift, the slopes from the deck, the house in fresh snow, the boot room with gear on the racks, the garage, the great room, the steaming hot tub, the kitchen, the bedrooms and bunk room, the baths, and the cul-de-sac showing the plow line. The seller supplies three dated July photos of the deck and the trailhead. The page reads: six-minute walk to the named lift along a maintained path, not ski-in/ski-out; two resorts within 25 minutes by car; plowed cul-de-sac, heated front walk; sleeps ten with bed sizes listed; airport 90 minutes in summer and allow two hours in winter per the seller; rented through a named manager with the seller's gross for the past two seasons; current registration per the seller's documents, buyer to verify. Seventeen landscape frames make the listing video, opening on the path to the lift. The slopes from the deck is the main photo.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

When should a ski home be photographed?

On a clear morning after a storm, with fresh snow, the walks and drive cleared and the sun on the slopes. Shoot before the sun drops behind the ridge. Add dated summer frames so the buyer sees both seasons.

Can I call a home ski-in/ski-out if it is close to the lift?

No. State the route exactly: ski path, walk, shuttle or drive, with the minutes and the lift's name. Skiers check the map before they call and do not forgive rounding.

What should the page say about rentals?

The seller's actual figures for a stated period, the manager used, and the current permit or registration as the documents show it, with a note to verify. Do not state rental rules or project income.

Which photos do skiers care about that other buyers do not?

The route to the lift, the boot room with dryers and racks, gear storage, the plowed or heated drive and the hot tub. Those answer how the house works in February.
