What one listing costs to market the old way, line by line

A line-by-line tally of what one listing costs to market with separate vendors at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026, and where the time goes.

The line items, one vendor at a time

Here is what each piece typically costs in the U.S. as of August 2026 when you hire it separately.

  • Listing photos: $150–300 per shoot. This is the one line almost every agent pays.
  • Listing video: $250–600. Usually a separate visit or a longer one, and a separate editor.
  • Drone or aerial session: $150–350. Weather, airspace and the pilot's calendar all have to line up.
  • Virtual staging: $5–15 per photo, billed per room.
  • Twilight conversions: $5–25 per photo, usually one or two exteriors.
  • Social cutdowns or a reels edit: $75–150, and often a second round if the first cut misses.
  • Single-property website: $9–150 per month, billed for as long as the listing is live and sometimes after.

Add the low ends or the high ends for your own market; either way it is seven invoices in the first month. A marketing agency on retainer sits on top of that at $2,500–5,000 per month, typical U.S. rates as of August 2026, and the retainer covers your brand, not a single address.

A worked example: a 3-bed in a cul-de-sac

Say you list a 3-bed, 2-bath in a quiet cul-de-sac on a Tuesday. The seller expects photos, a video and a page they can send to their sister. At typical U.S. rates as of August 2026, you book a photographer ($150–300), a videographer ($250–600) because the house has a good back deck, and an aerial pass ($150–350) to show the lot. Three rooms are empty, so you stage them at $5–15 each. Two front exteriors get a twilight conversion at $5–25 each. A reels editor cuts a vertical clip for $75–150. The site is $9–150 for the month.

That is seven invoices from four or five vendors before you have printed a flyer, each with their own delivery day. If the video arrives on Thursday and the staging on Friday, the listing goes live with half its media or waits. Most agents choose waiting, which is why a Tuesday listing so often launches on the weekend.

The costs that are not on any invoice

Three things cost more than the vendors do.

  • Chasing deliveries. Every vendor has a handoff: a download link, a zip file, a request for the address spelled correctly. Each one is a message you send and a message you wait for.
  • Re-shoots and re-edits. The staged photo has a sofa floating an inch off the floor. The video opens on the neighbour's bins. Fixing either means another cycle with the same vendor.
  • Inconsistency. The video has a different color balance from the photos, and the site uses a different main image from the MLS. Buyers notice without being able to say what they noticed.

Where agents overspend and where they underspend

Overspend: a full aerial session on a lot that cannot be seen from the air without three neighbours in frame, and a monthly site fee that keeps billing after the home closes. Underspend: skipping video on a home where the flow between rooms is the selling point, and skipping the site, which means every enquiry goes through a portal that may also show other agents beside your listing.

The honest version of a listing budget is not the lowest number. It is the smallest set of pieces that lets a buyer see the home properly on a phone and reach you directly. For most homes that is good photos, one video, a page with a form, and a clip for social.

What to avoid

Do not sign a monthly site fee without a cancellation date written in your calendar. Do not order an aerial session before you have stood in the street and checked what the lot looks like from above. And do not treat the sum above as a target. The point of the exercise is to see what you are paying for coordination, then decide which pieces you can produce from one shoot instead.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Does every listing need all of these line items?

No. Most homes sell well with good photos, one video, a page with an enquiry form and a clip for social. Aerial sessions and staging earn their cost only on lots and rooms where they change what a buyer understands about the home.

Why are single-property sites billed monthly?

Most site tools charge for hosting and the template for as long as the page is up, at $9–150 per month at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026. The fee often keeps running after the home closes unless you cancel, so put a reminder in your calendar on launch day.

Is it cheaper to shoot the photos yourself?

It saves the $150–300 photo line at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026, but only if the photos are good enough to carry the listing. For most homes above entry level, the photographer is the line to keep and the other lines are where to save.

What is the real cost of using several vendors?

Time. Each vendor adds a delivery day, a handoff and a chance of mismatch between the pieces. A listing that waits three days for its video loses its launch weekend, and that is worth more than any single invoice.
