Green lawn edits: fixing a brown yard honestly

Honest when the grass is dormant, the edit changes colour not coverage, and it is disclosed. Dirt and bare patches are a different problem.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A green lawn edit is honest when the grass is dormant and would be green in season, when the edit changes colour but not coverage, and when it is disclosed. It is misleading when the yard is dirt or dead, or when the edit invents turf where there was mulch or gravel. Fix tone, not terrain.

It is misleading when the yard is dirt, patchy or dead, when the edit invents turf where there was mulch or gravel, or when the buyer will see the difference on the drive up. Fix tone, not terrain.

Dormant is not dead

Lawns go brown for two reasons. In August in a dry region, or in February almost anywhere with a winter, healthy grass goes dormant and turns straw-coloured. It comes back. That is the yard a green lawn edit was made for, because you are showing the buyer the lawn they will own for most of the year.

The other reason is that the lawn is dead or was not there to begin with. Bare patches, weeds, compacted dirt by the gate, a strip where the dog runs. A green edit on that yard does not show the buyer anything they will ever own. It shows them a different yard.

Before you run the edit, walk the lawn. Push a finger into it. If there are roots and a thatch, it is dormant. If it is dust, leave the photo alone or disclose the condition in the remarks.

Colour or coverage

The clean way to think about it is that the edit may change colour and may not change coverage. Straw to green is colour. A bald patch becoming grass is coverage. A gravel bed becoming lawn is coverage. The edge of the lawn moving two feet into the flowerbed is coverage.

Coverage changes are the ones a buyer notices at the showing, because they change the shape of the yard, and the shape is the first thing a buyer sees from the car.

A worked example: the August listing

A 4-bed on a corner lot, listed in the second week of August after six weeks without rain. The front lawn is uniform straw with a healthy maple and a trimmed hedge. The back has a dead circle where a paddling pool sat all summer.

The front exterior gets a green lawn edit. Uniform straw becomes uniform green, the hedge and maple are unchanged, the edge against the drive stays where it is. Disclosed as AI-altered.

The rear yard does not get the edit. The dead circle would either stay dead, which looks worse against a green edit, or get filled in, which is a coverage change. Instead the seller is asked to rake it and put a table over it for the showing, and the photo runs as shot with a line in the remarks about the dry summer.

Three edits that go too far

The golf-course green. The AI picks a saturated green that no residential lawn has in August. If the lawn is brighter than the maple leaves above it, dial it back or run the edit again with a prompt that asks for a natural late-summer green.

The expanded lawn. Mulch beds, a gravel path and the bare strip under the fence become turf. Compare the edit against the original and trace the lawn edge with your eye. It should be in the same place.

The season mismatch. A green lawn in front of a house with bare trees, snow on the roof and a wreath on the door. Buyers know what month it is.

The seller conversation

Tell the seller before you edit the lawn. Most are relieved. A few would rather the listing show the yard as it is, and that is their call. If they agree, ask them to water for the two weeks before the open house so the lawn at the showing moves toward the photo, not away from it.

Walk the yard, then decide

Thirty seconds on the grass tells you whether this is a colour edit or a coverage edit, and only the first one is yours to make. FAQ

Questions on this topic

Is it misleading to make a brown lawn green in listing photos?

Not if the lawn is dormant and would be green in season, and the edit is disclosed. It is misleading if the lawn is dead or bare, because the buyer will not own the green version. Walk the yard and check before you edit.

What is the difference between changing colour and changing coverage?

Colour is straw turning green on grass that is already there. Coverage is grass appearing where there was dirt, gravel, mulch or a bare patch, or the lawn edge moving. Colour edits are defensible. Coverage edits change the shape of the yard and buyers notice at the showing.

Does a green lawn edit need a disclosure?

Yes. It is an altered photo and carries an AI-alteration disclosure. Your MLS may have rules on wording and placement, especially for the main photo, so check them before posting.

What if the AI makes the lawn too bright?

Run it again with a custom prompt that names a natural late-summer or early-spring green, or use the original. A lawn brighter than the tree leaves above it reads as fake even to buyers who cannot say why.
