Open house for a condo: the lobby, the elevator and the unit

A condo open house is three places. Clear the lobby and the elevator with the building a week ahead, and let the page show the amenities.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A condo open house happens in three places: the lobby, where visitors get stuck at the desk; the elevator, where they lose the thread; and the unit. Sort the first two with building management a week ahead, put a sign or a person at each, and let the property page show the gym, the roof and the parking that you cannot walk a group to in twenty minutes.

A condo open house is three places, and the unit is the easy one. The lobby is where visitors get stuck at a desk with a concierge who was not told. The elevator is where a group of four becomes four separate people arriving at different times on the wrong floor. Clear both with building management a week ahead, put something at each that says where to go, and let the property page do the amenity tour you cannot do in person.

Visitors to a house judge the house. Visitors to a condo judge the building first, and the first thing they meet is the lobby. If the lobby goes badly, the unit starts behind.

Harbor Street, Sunday 12 to 2

A 2-bed on the fourteenth floor at 500 Harbor Street, a building with a concierge, fob-access elevators and a rule about signs in the lobby. Open house Sunday 12 to 2.

The Monday before. You email management: the date, the hours, a request that the concierge be told, a request to leave a small sign on the lobby desk, and a question about guest access to the elevator. The answer: the concierge will have a list, no signs in the lobby but a sign on the unit door is fine, and visitors will be buzzed up by the desk.

The Friday before. You send the concierge a one-line note with your mobile number, so when the first visitor says “open house?” the desk has an answer and a number.

Sunday 11:30. You are in the lobby, not the unit, for the first twenty minutes. The first few visitors are the ones who get confused; after that the desk has the pattern. A colleague is in the unit with the door open and the page up on a tablet. At 11:50 you go up.

Sunday 12 to 2. At the unit door, a sign with the unit number, your name and a QR code to the property page. Inside, the usual loop, plus the thing a condo visitor cannot see on their own: the view at that hour, the sound through the window, where the parking space is.

The amenities. You cannot take fourteen groups to the roof terrace, the gym and the parking level. The property page can. The gallery has the roof, the gym and the numbered space, so the visitor sees them in the unit on the tablet and on their own phone on the way down.

The building conversation

Ask management four things, in writing, a week out:

  • Can the concierge be told the date and hours, and may you send them a note with your number?
  • Where may signs go: lobby, elevator, the unit door only?
  • How do visitors get past the elevator fob: buzzed, escorted, or a temporary code?
  • Are there hours or days the building does not allow open houses?

Buildings vary and rules change, so ask every time rather than assuming last year's answer. The question you most regret not asking is the elevator one, because you find out at noon on Sunday with three groups in the lobby.

The page's job in a condo

For a house, the page confirms what the visitor saw. For a condo, the page shows what they could not get to. Put the amenities in the gallery after the unit photos: the lobby in good light, the gym, the roof or courtyard, the parking space with its number, the storage cage, the bike room if it is good. In the video, give the walk from the front door to the unit door ten seconds, because that walk is part of the product and most listings skip it.

Details on the page should include the things condo visitors ask about in the elevator: the monthly fee, what it covers, pet rules, whether the parking is deeded. If you have the answers, put them in the description so the Sunday conversation starts further along.

What goes wrong in buildings

  • The concierge was not told. The first visitor is turned away and does not come back.
  • Lobby signs are banned and you find out Sunday. Ask Monday.
  • Visitors cannot get into the elevator. They stand in the lobby, text you, and you leave the unit to fetch them. Sort the fob question first.
  • No amenity photos. The visitor asks about the gym and you describe it. A photo on the page answers it.
  • Groups scattered by the elevator wait. A busy building on a Sunday has slow lifts. Accept it; the sign at the unit door is what gathers them again.
  • The view at the wrong hour. A west-facing unit shown at noon has blinds down against glare. Know which hours the view works and schedule for them.
  • Hallway noise and smells. You cannot control the neighbour's cooking, but you can open the unit's windows at 11 and close them at 11:55.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What should I ask building management before a condo open house?

Four things, in writing, a week ahead: whether the concierge can be told the date and hours; where signs are allowed; how visitors get past elevator access; and whether the building has hours or days when open houses are not allowed. Ask every time, because rules change and the elevator answer is the one you cannot fix on Sunday.

Where should I be during a condo open house, the lobby or the unit?

The lobby for the first twenty minutes, because the first visitors are the ones who get confused at the desk, then the unit. If you have a colleague, one of you stays at the unit with the door open from the start. The sign on the unit door gathers groups that the elevator split up.

How do I show amenities during a condo open house?

Through the property page rather than on foot. You cannot take every group to the roof, the gym and the parking level. Put those photos in the gallery after the unit, include the walk from the front door to the unit door in the video, and have the page open on a tablet in the unit.

Can I put signs in a condo lobby for an open house?

Only if the building allows it, and many do not. Ask management a week ahead. A sign on the unit door with the unit number, your name and a QR code to the property page is usually fine and does most of the work once visitors are off the elevator.
