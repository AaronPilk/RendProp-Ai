Marketing a vacant listing that shows cold

A vacant home reads cold in photos and in person. Fix the light and the temperature, stage with a label, and let a voiced video add the warmth.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A vacant listing shows cold for three fixable reasons: no lamps, no scale and no temperature. Bring in light before you bring in furniture, keep the heat or cooling at a comfortable setting for showings, and stage the main rooms either physically or virtually with the Virtually staged label and the empty original alongside. The video, with a human voice, does the rest of the warming.

A vacant home shows cold for three reasons, and all three are fixable: there are no lamps, so every room is lit by one overhead fixture or a grey window; there is no furniture, so a buyer cannot judge the size of anything; and the thermostat has been turned down, so the house is physically cold when they walk in. Fix the light first, set the temperature for people rather than for the utility bill, and stage the main rooms physically or virtually with the Virtually staged label and the empty original alongside. Then let the listing video, with a human voice on it, do the rest.

Why empty reads as cold

Look at a photo of an empty bedroom lit from the ceiling. The walls are flat, the corners are dark, the carpet shows every track from the last vacuum, and nothing tells the eye how big the room is. The same room with a lamp on a side table and a bed in it reads as a place.

In person the problem is temperature and sound. A house that has been closed for a month and kept at a low setting feels colder than the number on the thermostat, and every footstep echoes.

Bring in light before you bring in furniture

  • Lamps. Four or five plug-in lamps from the agent's own stock, placed in the living room, the primary bedroom and the kitchen counter, turned on for the shoot and left on timers for showings. Warm bulbs, all the same colour.
  • Blinds and curtains. If the owners took the curtains, bare windows are fine; clean them. If they left blinds, open them fully.
  • Overheads. On for the shoot, and every bulb working. One dead bulb in a five-light fixture is the first thing a buyer sees in an empty room.
  • Temperature. Agree with the owners that the thermostat sits at a comfortable setting during showing hours. The savings from a cold house are smaller than the cost of a buyer who leaves after four minutes.
  • Smell and sound. Air the house the morning of the shoot and before each weekend. A rug in the main room, even a cheap one, takes the echo out of a showing.

Virtual staging: where it helps and the label it needs

Once the rooms are lit and clean, virtual staging answers the scale question for the buyer at home on a phone. Stage the rooms where size is the question: the living room, the primary bedroom, a dining space that could be read two ways. Leave the kitchen and the bathrooms alone; they stage themselves.

Three conditions make it honest. The staged photo carries the Virtually staged label where anyone can see it. The empty original sits in the gallery as well, because the buyer who tours the house will walk into the empty room and should have seen it. And the staged version is compared against the original before it is used, to confirm that the furniture was added and nothing else moved: the window is the same size, the radiator is still there, the door still opens the same way. Shooting the empty rooms so that staging lands well is covered in photographing a vacant home so virtual staging looks right.

Physical staging of the main rooms is the stronger option when the budget allows, because it warms the showing as well as the photo; a sofa and a rug for real with virtual staging for the bedrooms is a common split.

The split-level on Garrison, empty since January

A three-bed split-level, owners relocated in January, house empty and set to a low temperature, listing planned for the second week of February.

The fix took one afternoon and a car boot. Five lamps from her garage placed in the living room, the primary bedroom and on the kitchen counter, all with matching warm bulbs, on timers from nine to seven. The dead bulb replaced. A rug in the living room. The thermostat set to a comfortable level for showing hours and the owners' agreement to leave it there. The shoot was the next morning at eleven: every lamp on, blinds open, landscape photos of each room and portrait frames of the living room and the kitchen.

The living room and the primary were virtually staged, labelled, compared with the originals, and placed in the gallery directly after the empty frames. The listing video was built from the empty set and the staged living room, and the agent recorded a short voiceover describing the house in her own voice, because an empty house with a human voice over it feels occupied in a way that music alone does not. The first buyers through on Saturday said the house felt bigger than they expected and warmer than most empty ones they had seen that month.

Where vacant listings go wrong

Staging without the label or the original. A buyer who arrives to an empty room they thought was furnished trusts nothing else in the listing. Label it, and keep the empty frame in the gallery.

Staging that moves the room. An add furniture edit that also widened the window or removed the baseboard heater is a different room. Compare before you choose.

Photos in overhead light only. The most common reason vacant listings look like rentals. Bring the lamps.

A cold house on a Saturday. The owners saved on the bill; the buyer left early.

Music instead of a voice. A vacant home's video needs a person in it, and the voiceover is the only person there is.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Why do vacant homes look cold in listing photos?

Mostly light. An empty room lit by one overhead fixture or a grey window has flat walls and dark corners, and without furniture nothing gives the eye a sense of scale. A few plug-in lamps with warm bulbs do more than staging to fix the photo.

Should I virtually stage a vacant listing?

Stage the rooms where size is the question, usually the living room and the primary bedroom, with the Virtually staged label on each photo and the empty original in the gallery too. Compare the staged version against the original to confirm only furniture was added. Kitchens and bathrooms rarely need it.

What temperature should a vacant house be kept at for showings?

Comfortable for a person in indoor clothes during showing hours, agreed with the owners in advance. A house that has been kept low feels colder than the thermostat suggests, and buyers who are cold do not linger.

Does a vacant listing need a voiceover on the video?

It helps more than on an occupied home. An empty house with a human voice describing it feels lived in; the same video with music alone feels like a slideshow of empty rooms. A short recorded voiceover in your own voice is enough.
