New agent, first listing: the media plan

A first-listing media plan for a new agent: pay for photos, make the video, page and post yourself, and launch everything the same day.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. For a first listing, pay for the photos, make the rest yourself, and launch everything the same day the listing goes live. You do not need an agency, a videographer or a website person. You need a photo set you are proud of and a fixed sequence for turning it into a video, a page and a post.

For a first listing, the media plan is: pay for the photos, make everything else yourself, and launch all of it the same day the listing goes live. You do not need an agency, a videographer or someone to build a website. You need a photo set you are proud of, a fixed sequence for turning it into a video, a page and a post, and the nerve to answer the first enquiry within the hour. Here is the sequence, step by step, with the mistakes new agents make at each one.

Step one: the photographer is the one thing to pay for

At $150–300 per shoot at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026, a photographer is the best money in the whole plan. Book one who shoots homes, not weddings. Ask for landscape photos for the listing video and the page, plus a few portrait frames for social if they will. Be at the shoot. Walk the house with the seller the day before and send a prep list by text: lights on, counters clear, cars out of the driveway, pet bowls away. Your job at the shoot is to decide the main photo on the spot and to notice if the back garden needs a second pass when the sun moves.

Step two: the review, done once

When the photos arrive, block an hour. Cull to about thirty. Put them in the order a buyer walks the house. Choose the main photo and do not revisit it. If the exterior was overcast, a blue sky edit is reasonable; if a room is empty, virtual staging with the label on is reasonable; compare each edit with its original before keeping it, and check your MLS rules on altered images. Do not edit anything else. First listings go wrong when the new agent spends three evenings on edits nobody asked for.

Step three: one video, thirty seconds

Write six sentences: what the home is, where it is, the one thing a buyer will like most, the next two things, and who it suits. Read it aloud once; cut anything you stumbled on. Record it in a quiet room or let an AI voice read it. Captions on. Build the landscape video for the page and the vertical video for social from the same photos. Watch each once for wrong details, the bedroom count, the staged room without its label, and stop. One clean video beats a week of revisions.

Step four: the page and the two links

Publish the property page with the main photo, the gallery in order, the video, the details typed carefully (price, beds, baths, square feet), your name and business on the contact card and the number you will answer. Copy the branded link and the unbranded link into a note. The branded one goes on your posts, your email and the sign rider. The unbranded one, with no name, contact details or branding, goes in the MLS virtual-tour field; check your board's current rules on that field.

Step five: the first post and the first text

Post the vertical video with the branded link and a plain line about the home. Send the branded link by text to the people who know you are now an agent: family, friends, the former colleagues who said they would help. Ask them to forward it to one person. Then text the seller the branded link so they can send it to their circle. Your first listing's first enquiries usually come from a forwarded text, not from a stranger on a portal.

A first listing: a 2-bed condo

A 2-bed condo near the train, your first listing, signed Friday. Saturday you walk it with the seller and send the prep list. Monday at 9 the photographer shoots twenty-eight landscape photos and four portrait ones. Monday afternoon you cull, order, pick the balcony shot as the main photo, apply one blue sky edit to it and compare, and reject a tidy-the-room edit on the kitchen that removed a cabinet handle. Monday evening you write the six sentences, record them twice, keep the second take, and build both videos. Tuesday at 9 the page is published and the links are in a note; at 9:30 the MLS is entered with the unbranded link; at noon the reel is posted; at 12:15 the branded link goes to twenty-two people by text. By Tuesday evening there are three enquiries, two from forwarded texts and one from the page form asking for Thursday. You reply to each within the hour. That is a first listing marketed properly, and nothing in it required anyone but the photographer.

Beginner mistakes

  • Hiring an agency or a videographer for the first listing to feel safe. The retainer buys brand presence, not this launch.
  • Shooting the photos yourself to save the one line worth paying.
  • Spending the launch evening on edits and music instead of on the page and the links.
  • Different details on the MLS and the page because they were typed twice.
  • Posting the video before the staged room got its label.
  • Answering the first enquiry the next morning.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should a new agent shoot the first listing's photos themselves?

No. The photo shoot is the one line in the plan worth paying for, at $150–300 at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026. Everything else, video, page, post, is built from the photos, so a weak set weakens all of it.

Do I need a videographer for my first listing?

No. A thirty-second listing video built from the photo set, with a short voiceover and captions, covers the page, the MLS and social. Add a handheld walkthrough later only if the home's layout is the story.

Where do first-listing enquiries usually come from?

Forwarded texts. Send the branded link to everyone who knows you are an agent and ask each to forward it to one person. The page form and the portals follow, but the first few usually arrive by text.

How long should the whole media plan take?

One morning for the shoot, one afternoon for the review and the video, one evening for the page, and the next morning for the MLS, the post and the texts. If it is taking a week, something is being redone that did not need to be.
