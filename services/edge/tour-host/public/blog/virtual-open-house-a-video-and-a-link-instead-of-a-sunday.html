Virtual open house: a video and a link instead of a Sunday

A virtual open house is a listing video, a property page with an enquiry form, and a defined window when you answer. Here is when it beats the Sunday.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A virtual open house is three things: a listing video that walks the home in order, a property page where it lives with the gallery and an enquiry form, and a defined window (say Saturday 10 a.m. to Sunday 6 p.m.) when you promote it and answer every question within the hour. It replaces the Sunday when the buyers are far away, the home is occupied, or the weather kills the weekend.

A virtual open house is a listing video, a property page where that video lives alongside the gallery and an enquiry form, and a defined window when you promote the link and answer every question quickly. It is not a posted video with “virtual open house” written on it. The window and the replies are what make it an event instead of a post.

It replaces the physical Sunday in three situations: when the likely buyers live two hours or two states away, when the home is occupied and the sellers cannot clear out for weekends, and when the weather or the season makes the house show badly in person. In each case the video walks the home for people who were not going to walk it that Sunday anyway.

The three parts, and what each does

  • The video. A listing video in 16:9 built from the landscape photos, in a sensible order: street, front, entry, kitchen, living, bedrooms, bathrooms, garden, street again. Two to three minutes. A voiceover that says what the photos cannot: the ceiling height, what is behind the fence, how far the station is.
  • The page. The property page with the video, the full gallery, the price, beds, baths and square feet, your contact card and the enquiry form. This is the room people walk into.
  • The window. A start and an end. Within it, you post, text and email the link, and you reply to every enquiry within the hour. Outside it, the page still works, but the urgency is gone.

A lake cottage, two hours from its buyers

A two-bedroom cottage on Mill Pond, listed in the first week of November. The buyers for a place like this live in the city and come up at weekends in summer. In November nobody is driving two hours to stand in a cold cottage for twenty minutes.

So the open house runs Saturday 10 a.m. to Sunday 6 p.m., online. Thursday: the announcement video goes out, nine scenes, voiceover saying when the window opens and that you will answer anything the same hour. Friday: the email to the database with the main photo, the four facts, the window and the link. Saturday 10: a story with the link and the words “open until Sunday 6.” Through the weekend you keep the phone near you and answer the enquiries as they land. One asks about the dock rights, one asks whether the road is ploughed, one asks for a visit the following Saturday and gives the date in the form. Sunday 6: a final story, “thanks to everyone who came through, private visits by appointment from here.”

Nobody drove anywhere, the seller kept the heating off, and you have a Saturday appointment with a buyer who has already seen every room.

What the video has to do on its own

In a physical open house you are standing there to answer “where is the laundry” and “does the garage have power.” In a virtual one, the video is you. So the script carries the answers to the questions people ask in the hallway, and the scenes include the unglamorous rooms a social reel would skip: the utility room, the garage, the view from the back door toward the neighbour.

Resist cutting it to thirty seconds. The social reel is the invitation. The open house video is the tour. They are different cuts from the same photo set.

Where virtual open houses go flat

  • No window. A video with no start and end is a listing video, which is fine, but nobody feels they have to watch it this weekend.
  • No way to ask. If the link goes to a video platform rather than the property page, the buyer has a comment box and nothing else. The enquiry form is the hallway conversation.
  • Slow replies. If someone asks about the dock at 11 on Saturday and hears back Monday, the event was a post. Replying within the hour is the whole job during the window.
  • A reel instead of a tour. Hooks and music at the start, three rooms, done. The remote buyer learns nothing and books nothing.
  • Calling a livestream an open house with no recording. If you do go live, keep the recorded listing video on the page for the people who missed the hour.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What is a virtual open house?

A listing video that tours the home room by room, a property page where it lives with the gallery and an enquiry form, and a defined window during which you promote the link and reply to every question quickly. The window and the fast replies are what separate it from a video that is simply posted.

When should a virtual open house replace a physical one?

When the likely buyers are far away, when the home is occupied and the sellers cannot clear out for weekends, or when the season or weather makes the home show poorly in person. In those cases the people who would have come were not going to come that Sunday anyway, and the video reaches them where they are.

How long should the video for a virtual open house be?

Two to three minutes in 16:9, covering every room including the utility room and the garage, with a voiceover that answers the questions people ask in the hallway. The thirty-second social reel is the invitation; this longer video is the tour.

Do I need to go live on video for a virtual open house?

No. A recorded listing video on the property page, a defined window and fast replies to the enquiry form do the job. If you choose to go live as well, keep the recorded video on the page for everyone who missed the hour.
