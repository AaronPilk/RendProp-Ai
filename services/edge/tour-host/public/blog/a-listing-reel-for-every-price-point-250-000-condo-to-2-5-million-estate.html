Listing reels by price point: $250,000 condo to $2.5M estate

The job of a reel changes with the price. A $250,000 condo sells on speed and location, a family home on livability, a $2.5 million estate on restraint.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A reel's job changes with the price. A $250,000 condo reel is quick, lively and about location and monthly life. A $600,000 family home reel is about livability: kitchen, yard, bedrooms, schools. A $2.5 million estate reel is slow, quiet and sparse, letting space and light do the talking. The photos, the script, the pace, the music and the voice all shift with the bracket.

The reel for a $250,000 condo and the reel for a $2.5 million estate share a format and nothing else. The condo reel is quick, bright and about location and daily life. The family home reel in the middle is about livability: kitchen, yard, bedroom count, the school route. The estate reel is slow, quiet and sparse, with fewer photos held longer and a voice that does not sell. Pace, music, script, voice and photo choice all move with the price bracket, and getting them wrong for the bracket is the most common way a decent reel misses.

$250,000 condo: speed and the neighbourhood

The buyer is often first-time, often renting now, and comparing this unit to the rent they pay. The reel has to answer: where is it, what does life here look like, and is it in reach.

  • Photos. Six. Lead with the view, the balcony or the living space, not the building. Kitchen, bedroom, bath, the building amenity that matters (gym, pool, lobby), and the street.
  • Script. 60 words, fast. Location first: the train, the park, the walk to coffee. Then two things inside. Then the ask.
  • Pace and music. Quick cuts, light track under the voice. Energy is the point.
  • Voice. Yours, if you are the agent who will meet them. It is a relationship sale.

Example script: One bedroom, a six-minute walk to the light rail, and this balcony looks over the park. Renovated kitchen, washer in the unit, pool on the roof. Open Sunday, link in profile.

$600,000 family home: livability

The buyer has kids or is planning on them, has seen eight houses, and is tired. The reel has to show the three things they are checking every house for and prove this one has them.

  • Photos. Eight. Kitchen first, then the front so they know what it is, main living, yard, main bedroom, a second bedroom to show the count, a bath, the yard again from the deck.
  • Script. 75 words. Street character, kitchen, yard, bedrooms, the thing a photo cannot show: morning light, the quiet, the walk to school.
  • Pace and music. Steady. Music optional and low. The voice does the work.
  • Voice. Yours or an AI voice; consistency across your listings matters more here than personality.

This bracket is where most listings sit, and what to show and what to skip on a starter home covers the lower end of it in more detail.

$1.2 million: the upgrade buyer

Second or third home, moving for space or a specific feature: the office, the pool, the lot. They know what they want and the reel should show it early and without fuss.

  • Photos. Seven. Lead with the feature that justifies the step up. Then kitchen, main living, main bedroom suite, the outdoor space, exterior.
  • Script. 70 words, calmer. Fewer adjectives than the family-home reel. Name the feature, the finish, the lot size in words rather than numbers.
  • Pace and music. Slower. Music sparse or none.

$2.5 million estate: restraint

The buyer is private, has seen the best, and reads effort as desperation. The reel's job is to look like it did not need to try.

  • Photos. Five. The approach, one wide interior, the view or the grounds, one detail, the exterior at dusk. Each held longer than feels comfortable.
  • Script. 40 words or fewer. Location, acreage in words, one line about the house, one line about the setting. No ask beyond the link. No open house.
  • Pace and music. Slow, and either no music or something barely audible with no beat.
  • Voice. Measured, quiet. An AI voice can work here if yours tends to sell.

Example script: Forty acres on the ridge, the last house on the road. Stone and glass, built around a courtyard. The pool faces the valley. Details on the page.

A worked example: one agent, one Tuesday, two reels

Two listings go live the same day: a $280,000 one-bed near the hospital and a $1.9 million house on the lake. The condo reel is six photos, 58 words, quick, light music, the agent's voice recorded in the car, lead frame the balcony, ask is Sunday's open house. The lake house reel is five photos, 38 words, slow, no music, an AI voice reading a quiet script, lead frame the dock at dusk, ask is the link. Same agent, same afternoon, two reels that could not be swapped.

Where price-point reels go wrong

  • Luxury pace on a condo. Five slow frames and a whisper on a one-bed near the train looks pretentious and loses the first-time buyer in three seconds.
  • Condo energy on an estate. Quick cuts and a pop track on a forty-acre property makes it look like it is trying too hard, which at that price reads as a problem.
  • Reading numbers aloud at any price. Square footage and lot size go in the caption and on the property page.
  • The same lead frame logic everywhere. The condo leads with the view; the estate leads with the approach. The strongest frame is different at each bracket.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should a luxury listing reel be shorter than a starter home reel?

Usually, in photo count and word count, though each frame is held longer. Five photos and 40 words with slow motion and no music reads as confident. A luxury reel with ten quick frames and a track reads as effort, which the buyer at that bracket tends to distrust.

What should a condo reel lead with?

The view, the balcony or the main living space, not the building. Condo buildings look alike from outside and the first frame has to stop a scroll. Show the exterior second so the viewer knows what kind of building it is.

Does the voice choice change with price?

It can. On a condo or a family home, your own voice builds the relationship the buyer will continue at the showing. On a high-end listing a measured AI voice can be the better fit if your natural delivery tends to sell.

Can I use the same photo set for the reel at any price?

The set, yes; the selection, no. The condo reel picks six lively frames, the estate reel picks five wide, quiet ones. The photographer's full delivery goes on the property page gallery, and each reel takes the frames that fit its job.
