A week of posts from one listing without repeating yourself

Seven posts from one photo set, each with a different job and photo: the reel, the exterior, the room, the detail, the street, a question, the reminder.

One photo set gives you seven different posts in a week if each post has a different job and a different photo: the launch reel, the exterior, the room that sells the home, a detail, the street, a question post and the open house reminder. Change the caption type each day as well as the picture, keep the property page link the same throughout, and never let the hero photo appear twice.

The rule that stops the repetition

Repetition on a listing comes from two habits: using the best photo every time, and writing the same caption shape every time. Fix both with a simple rule. Each day gets one photo that has not been used yet and one caption shape that was not used yesterday. The shapes are: the fact list, the single observation, the story, the question, the instruction. Five shapes, seven days, so two get reused at most, and never on consecutive days.

Seven days for a Cape Cod with a finished attic

A three-bed Cape Cod, 1,650 square feet, with a finished attic the seller used as a studio, listed on a Monday with an open house the following Sunday.

  1. Monday, the reel. Vertical video from eight portrait photos, opening on the attic studio under the eaves, voiceover with the price and the attic in the first line, word-by-word captions. Caption shape: fact list. Beds, baths, square feet, price, open Sunday 1 to 3, link in bio.
  2. Tuesday, the exterior. Front of the house from the sidewalk, morning light. Caption shape: single observation. Cape Cods on this street were built in 1948 and this one still has the original front door. Link in bio.
  3. Wednesday, the room. The attic, a landscape photo from the top of the stairs. Caption shape: story. What the seller did up here for eleven years, with their permission, and what the next owner could.
  4. Thursday, the detail. The built-in bookcase on the stair landing. Caption shape: single observation, kept to two lines. Details are the posts people save.
  5. Friday, the street. A photo you took on your phone of the block, the sidewalk, the maple trees. Caption shape: fact list about the area, only things you have measured or checked: the walk to the school crossing timed at six minutes, the bus stop at the corner. See neighborhood content for what to include and what to leave out.
  6. Saturday, the question. The kitchen, which has not appeared yet. Caption shape: question. The seller kept the 1990s cabinets and painted them. Paint or replace, which would you have done? The replies are the point.
  7. Sunday morning, the reminder. The back yard from the attic window, the one frame that shows both. Caption shape: instruction. Open today 1 to 3, park on Elm, come up to the attic first. Link in bio.

Seven posts, seven photos, five caption shapes, and the kitchen and the attic each appear once in the grid. A follower who saw every one of them learned something different each day.

Keeping the link and the disclosure constant

The one thing that does not change across the week is the link. Every post points at the property page, through the bio or wherever the platform currently allows a link. The disclosure does not change either: if the exterior in Tuesday's post has a replaced sky, Tuesday's caption says so in one line, and so does any later post that uses the same frame.

What goes wrong

  • The hero photo three times. The exterior in the reel, the exterior on Tuesday, the exterior on Sunday. By Sunday it is wallpaper.
  • Seven fact lists. Beds, baths, price, open house, every single day. The facts have not changed and the follower knows it.
  • Seven posts and nothing else. If the account carries only this listing for a week, the followers who are not buying this home have nothing. Slip in one post that is not a listing, or run the week over ten days.
  • The link goes somewhere different on Thursday. Keep it one page.
  • The question post with no reply from you. Someone answers paint and you say nothing. Answer every one.
  • Posting the street with a stock photo. Take it yourself. A stock photo of a street that is not this street is a lie with trees in it.

Assign seven photos to seven days

Open your current listing's photo set and assign seven photos to seven days, writing the caption shape next to each one. If you cannot find seven different photos worth a post, that is a note for the next shoot, not a reason to repeat the exterior.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Is seven posts in a week about one listing too many?

It is at the upper end. It works when each post uses a different photo and a different caption shape, and it works better if one non-listing post is mixed in or the seven are spread over ten days.

Which photo should I save for the open house reminder?

One that has not appeared yet and that says something about the day: the yard, the view from the best room, the porch. The exterior has usually been used by then.

What caption shapes stop posts from sounding the same?

Five: the fact list, the single observation, the story, the question and the instruction. Rotate them so no two consecutive days use the same shape, and limit the fact list to the reel and the reminder.

Can I reuse photos from the week in later posts?

Yes, after a gap. A detail photo from Thursday can anchor a sold post in two months. Within the same week, each photo appears once.
