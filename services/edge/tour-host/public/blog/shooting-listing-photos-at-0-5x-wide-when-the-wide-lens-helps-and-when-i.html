0.5x wide listing photos: when the lens helps and when it lies

Use 0.5x only when 1x cannot show the room from any corner: small baths, galley kitchens, tight entries. Everywhere else it bends the walls.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Use the 0.5x lens when the 1x lens cannot show the room from any corner, which means small bathrooms, galley kitchens, walk-in closets and tight entries. Use 1x everywhere else. The wide lens helps by fitting the room in. It lies by stretching the foreground, bending the edges and making every room look four feet bigger than it is, which the buyer discovers at the showing.

What the wide lens does to a room

At 0.5x the phone sees roughly twice the width of 1x. To fit that onto a flat image, it stretches whatever is near the edges and near the camera. The floor in the foreground balloons. The wall on the left leans out. A bathtub at the edge of the frame becomes a long trough. A room that is 11 feet across looks 15.

None of this is a defect. It is how wide lenses work, and a photographer with a 16mm on a full-frame camera deals with the same thing. The difference is that the phone makes it one tap, so it gets used where it should not.

When 0.5x is the right call

  • A bathroom where you are standing in the doorway with your back against the far wall and 1x shows the sink and nothing else
  • A galley kitchen shot end-on, where the counters run toward the window and the stretch at the edges is along the counters, which reads as depth rather than distortion
  • A small entry hall where the point is to show the stairs and the door together
  • A tight exterior where there is no room to step back and the only alternative is cutting the roof off

In each case the wide lens is showing a room that 1x cannot. That is the test.

When it lies

Bedrooms. A 0.5x frame from the doorway makes a standard bedroom look like a primary suite. Shoot from the corner at 1x instead, and if you cannot get the whole bed in, show the bed and the window and let the buyer infer the rest.

Living rooms. The wide lens makes a sofa in the foreground enormous and the fireplace on the back wall tiny. The buyer walks in and the room is smaller and the fireplace is bigger than the photo. Step back to the corner at 1x.

Exteriors from the kerb. The garage bulges, the house leans back, the lawn becomes a field. Cross the street and shoot at 1x.

Anything with a vertical line near the edge. Doorframes, cabinet runs, window mullions. At 0.5x they curve, and the buyer reads curved lines as a cheap photo even if they cannot say why.

A worked example: the hall bath and the primary

A 3-bed with a 5-by-8 hall bath. From the doorway at 1x the frame shows the vanity and half the mirror. At 0.5x, level, from the same spot, the frame shows vanity, toilet, tub and the window over it. The tub stretches a little at the right edge. The agent accepts that, because the alternative is a photo of a sink. No label, because nothing in the frame was altered.

The primary bedroom is 13 by 15. At 0.5x from the doorway it looks like a hotel suite. At 1x from the corner it looks like a 13 by 15 bedroom with a window wall and space for a king. The 1x frame is the one that goes out, because the buyer will stand in the room on Saturday.

Keeping 0.5x honest when you do use it

  1. Hold the phone level. Any tilt at 0.5x bends the verticals hard.
  2. Keep the camera at chest height. Low angles at 0.5x turn the floor into a ramp.
  3. Put the important thing in the middle third of the frame, where the stretch is least.
  4. Keep furniture out of the near foreground.
  5. Do not use 0.5x on a frame you plan to stage. Added furniture scaled to a stretched floor comes back the wrong size.

Count the rooms that need it

Before a shoot, walk the house and note which rooms 1x cannot show from a corner. It is usually two or three. Those get 0.5x, level, chest height. The rest get 1x and look like the house the buyer will see. FAQ

Questions on this topic

Does the 0.5x lens make rooms look bigger than they are?

Yes. It stretches the foreground and the edges, so an 11-foot room reads as 15. That is useful in a small bathroom where the alternative is a photo of a sink, and misleading in a bedroom where the buyer will stand with a king bed in mind.

Which rooms should be shot at 0.5x?

Only rooms the 1x lens cannot show from any corner: small bathrooms, galley kitchens shot end-on, walk-in closets and tight entries. Usually two or three rooms per house. Everything else at 1x from the corner.

Does using the wide lens need a disclosure?

Not in the way an edit does: nothing in the photo was altered, so there is no AI label to attach. It can still mislead, which is why the rule is to use it only where 1x cannot show the room, and to keep the phone level and at chest height when you do.

Why does Rendprop use 0.5x for walkthroughs if it distorts stills?

A handheld walkthrough is a different job. The viewer is moving through the space, the stretch is in motion and reads as the room opening up, and the wide view keeps the frame steady. For stills that go into the photo-based video, 1x is still the default.
