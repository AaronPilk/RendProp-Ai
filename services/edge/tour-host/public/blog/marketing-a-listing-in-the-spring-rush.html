Marketing a listing in the spring rush

Spring listings compete for attention. Book the shoot before you sign, launch complete on day one, and skip the wait for the lawn to green up.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Win the spring rush by launching complete on day one: photos, video, property page and both links. Book the photographer before the listing agreement is signed, set the list date from the shoot date, and do not wait weeks for the lawn to green up.

In the spring rush, the listing that gets attention is the one that launches complete: photos, a video, a property page and both links live on day one, while the house two doors down is still waiting on its photographer. That is won in the two weeks before you sign, not the two days after. Book the shoot before the listing agreement is signed and build everything else from that single shoot.

Book the photographer before the paperwork

Spring is when every agent in your market calls the same three photographers in the same week. A photographer with four shoots on a Saturday is not fitting you in on Friday night. The moment a seller says yes in principle, get on the calendar. If the listing falls through you cancel; if it goes ahead, you have the one date everything else hangs on.

Pick the shoot date first and the list date second. The list date is the shoot date plus the time you need to review photos, approve edits, build the video and check the page. For most agents that is two working days. Promising a seller a Thursday launch before the photographer has confirmed Tuesday is how spring launches slip into the following week.

What April asks for that January did not

In January your listing was one of a handful in the neighborhood. In April it is one of many, and buyers compare them in a single scrolling session on a phone. The listing that makes them stop is the one that answers questions without a phone call.

  • Every detail typed in: price, beds, baths, square feet, and the things buyers in your area ask about first.
  • A gallery in walking order, exterior first, kitchen early, no duplicates.
  • A video on the page for the buyer who will not scroll forty photos.
  • The unbranded link in the MLS virtual tour field, the branded link in your posts, texts and emails.
  • An enquiry form you have tested yourself, from your own phone, before the first buyer sees it.

None of this is extra in spring. It is the baseline, because the house across the street has most of it.

The colonial on Maple Court: a two-week plan

A four-bed colonial in a cul-de-sac, sellers ready to go the first week of April. Here is the calendar that worked.

  1. Friday, March 27. Seller meeting. Verbal yes. Photographer texted the same evening and booked for Tuesday, April 7.
  2. The weekend. Sellers get the prep list: clear the counters, cut the lawn once it is dry, move the recycling bins out of the driveway, replace the two dead bulbs in the kitchen.
  3. Tuesday, April 7. Shoot at ten in the morning when the front of the house is lit. Landscape photos of every room for the listing video; a handful of portrait frames of the kitchen, the stair and the back deck for the social cut.
  4. Wednesday. Photos reviewed. One blue sky edit on the front exterior because the sky was flat grey; compared against the original, approved, disclosure kept. Video built and watched twice. Page checked on a phone.
  5. Thursday, April 9. Live on the MLS in the morning with the unbranded link in the tour field. Branded link in the just-listed email and the first reel by lunchtime.
  6. Saturday, April 11. Open house, with the page link on the sign and the flyer.

The compressed version of this schedule is in Tuesday listing, Saturday open house. In spring the difference is that the photographer is booked two weeks out rather than two days.

Where spring launches fall apart

Waiting for the yard. Sellers want to hold until the lawn greens up and the tulips come through. That can cost weeks of strong buyer traffic. Shoot now; if the lawn is dormant and will be green within the season, a green lawn edit with a disclosure is defensible. If it will not be green, leave it alone. Do not paint May onto March.

Shooting a house that is not ready. The flip side. If the sellers have not done the prep, a rushed shoot produces photos you will want to redo, and in spring the photographer cannot come back next week.

Posting before the listing is live. Pre-marketing rules differ by MLS. Check your MLS's current rules and ask your broker before the first reel goes out.

Running four launches without a checklist. In a busy week the thing you forget is the thing that costs you: the tour link left blank, the price typed wrong, the enquiry form untested. Use the launch checklist for every listing, especially the fourth one that week.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should I wait until the yard looks better before listing in spring?

Usually no. Waiting for green grass and flowers can cost weeks of buyer traffic, and the photographer may not have a slot when the yard is finally ready. Shoot now, keep the exterior honest, and if the lawn will be green within the season a labelled green lawn edit is a reasonable bridge.

How far ahead should I book a photographer in spring?

As soon as the seller says yes in principle, even before the listing agreement is signed. In a busy spring the good photographers are booked well ahead, and the shoot date is what sets the launch date. You can cancel if the listing does not go ahead.

What matters most on the listing when there are many competing homes?

Completeness. Buyers compare several homes in one sitting on a phone, and the listing that answers their questions without a call is the one they book a showing on. That means details typed in, a gallery in walking order, a video, and a tested enquiry form.

Can I post the reel before the listing is live on the MLS?

Rules on pre-marketing differ between MLSs, so check your MLS's current rules and ask your broker before posting anything. Having the reel ready on launch morning is the goal; when you release it should follow those rules.
