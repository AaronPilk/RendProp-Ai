How Rendprop labels AI-edited photos and videos

Every AI edit in Rendprop is a separate version with a disclosure: staging is labelled Virtually staged, other edits carry an AI-alteration disclosure.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Rendprop saves every AI edit as a separate version with a disclosure. Staging (add furniture) is labelled Virtually staged; every other edit, such as blue sky, twilight, green lawn, tidy the room or a custom prompt, carries an AI-alteration disclosure. The untouched original stays in photo history. A video built from edited versions shows those versions, so the same disclosures apply to what viewers see in it. Check your own MLS rules before posting altered images.

Rendprop labels AI edits at the version level. Every edit you make in the photo studio is saved as its own version with a disclosure attached: staging is labelled "Virtually staged", and every other edit carries an AI-alteration disclosure. The untouched original stays in photo history.

A photo-based video is built from whichever versions you chose, so what a viewer sees in the video is what those versions show, with the same disclosure applying. The label travels with the version, not with your memory of what you did.

The two labels and which edits get which

  • "Virtually staged" goes on add-furniture edits: an empty room with furniture placed in it by the AI. The phrase is one buyers already recognise.
  • AI-alteration disclosure goes on everything else: blue sky, twilight, green lawn, tidy the room, and any custom prompt. A sky that was grey, a lawn that was brown, a counter that had a toaster on it: all altered, all labelled.

There is no unlabelled AI edit. If a version came out of the photo studio, it has a disclosure. The only unlabelled image is the original, which is the point.

How versions keep the labelling honest

An edit does not overwrite the photo. It creates a version beside it. Try blue sky on a front exterior and you have the original plus a blue-sky version. Try a custom prompt afterwards and you have a third. Each carries its own disclosure, and you choose which saved versions to use in the gallery and the video. Decline a version and it stays in history, labelled and unused. The photo history post explains why the original matters more as time goes on.

What this means for the video

The listing video and the vertical social video are built from the photo versions you select in the video step. If you pick the blue-sky version of the front exterior, that is the sky in the video. If you pick the virtually staged living room, the video shows a staged room. The disclosure that belongs to the version applies to the scene; a label on a photo does not become false because the photo is moving. When you post the video, say what applies in the caption or description, the same way you would for the photo. The social disclosure post has a line you can reuse.

Reading the labels during review, step by step

  1. Open the photo in the photo studio and look at its versions.
  2. Note which label each version carries: "Virtually staged" or AI-alteration.
  3. Compare each version against the original. The label tells you it was altered; the comparison tells you whether the alteration is acceptable.
  4. Choose the versions to use. The ones you decline stay in history.
  5. Before you publish or post, check your own MLS rules on altered images and on staging. Rules vary and change; the label does not replace the rule.

What to check before you rely on a label

That the version you selected is the one you compared. That a "Virtually staged" room has not also gained a window. That an AI-alteration disclosure on a tidy-the-room edit is attached to a version that only removed loose items, not an outlet or a crack. The label says altered; you confirm how.

A worked example: the empty 3-bed

A vacant 3-bed in a cul-de-sac. The living room is empty, the front exterior is grey, the lawn is August brown. In the photo studio: add furniture on the living room (version labelled "Virtually staged"), blue sky on the exterior (AI-alteration disclosure), green lawn on the same exterior (second version, also AI-alteration). The agent compares all three with their originals. The staged room kept its two windows and the fireplace. The blue sky did not change the roof. The green lawn crept onto the driveway edge, so she declines it and keeps the blue-sky-only version.

Gallery and video use the staged living room and the blue-sky exterior. The originals stay in history. In the MLS she follows her MLS's staging rule, and in the social caption she writes one line: "Living room virtually staged; exterior sky edited." Nobody at the Saturday open house is surprised by an empty living room, because the page told them.

Where labelling goes wrong in practice

  • Treating the label as approval. It discloses; it does not vouch. Compare against the original every time.
  • Exporting an edited version and re-adding it elsewhere. A version that leaves the app and comes back as a plain file has lost its history. Keep edits inside the photo studio.
  • Forgetting the video. The scene shows the version; disclose accordingly where you post.
  • Assuming the MLS accepts labelled edits. Some do, some restrict, some require specific wording. Check your own MLS rules.
  • Deleting nothing and choosing nothing. Versions pile up. Choose the ones to use and leave the rest in history.

Why the label sits on the version

Because a label on a filename gets lost and a label in your memory gets fuzzy. Keeping the disclosure attached to the version in the photo studio, next to the original in history, means the question "was this edited?" has an answer a week later and a year later, for you and for anyone who asks.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Which edits are labelled Virtually staged?

Add-furniture edits, where the AI places furniture in a room. Every other edit, including blue sky, twilight, green lawn, tidy the room and custom prompts, carries an AI-alteration disclosure.

Can I remove the label from an edited photo?

The disclosure is attached to the version. If you want an unlabelled image, use the original, which stays untouched in photo history.

Is a video made from edited photos labelled?

The video shows the versions you chose, so the disclosure that belongs to each version applies to what viewers see. Say what applies where you post the video, and check your own MLS rules.

Does the label mean the edit is accurate?

No. It means the image was altered by AI. AI can change or invent details, so compare each edited version against your original before you publish.
