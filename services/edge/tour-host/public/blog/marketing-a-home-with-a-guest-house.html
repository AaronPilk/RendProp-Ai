Marketing a home with a guest house

A guest house sells on separation: its own door, path, bath and kitchen. Show the two buildings together, keep square feet apart, state its permitted use.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A guest house sells on separation and on the relationship between the two buildings: its own door and path from the driveway, its own bath and whatever kitchen it has, and how far and how private it is from the main house. Show the two together from outside, walk the path in the video, keep the guest house square footage out of the main house figure, and describe what it is permitted as only from the seller's documents.

A guest house sells on separation and on how the two buildings sit together. The buyer wants to know that a guest, a parent or a tenant can arrive, park, walk to their own door and live for a week without walking through the main kitchen, and they want to see the distance and the sightlines between the two. Show both buildings in one frame, walk the path in the video, keep the guest house's square footage out of the main house's figure, and describe what the guest house is permitted as only in the words of the seller's documents.

What the page needs to settle

  • What the guest house has: bedroom or studio, full bath, kitchen or kitchenette with what appliances, laundry, heating and cooling, storage.
  • Its own entrance, its own path from the driveway or street, and whether a visitor can reach it without entering the main house or crossing the private part of the yard.
  • Parking for it.
  • Distance from the main house in feet and what sits between: the pool, a courtyard, a hedge, a garage.
  • Utilities: shared meters or separate, and whose bill it is on now.
  • Current use: guests, a family member, a home office, rented at a stated current rent to a tenant on a stated lease, vacant.
  • Permit and status as the seller's documents show it, described as the seller's documents. Whether it may be rented, and how, is not yours to state; ask your broker how to word it.
  • Square footage of the guest house in the text; the main house figure alone in the details field; beds and baths for the main house in the fields, with the guest house's own counted in the text.

If the second dwelling is small or the point of the listing is income, the post on tiny homes and ADUs is the closer fit; this one assumes the guest house is a feature of a larger home.

Mistakes that confuse the buyer

  • Combined square footage. A 2,400-square-foot house with a 500-square-foot guest house is not a 2,900-square-foot house. Buyers who discover the arithmetic feel misled.
  • Guest house photos mixed into the main house gallery. A second kitchen appears with no explanation. Group them and say in the text which building they show.
  • No frame of the two buildings together. The buyer cannot judge privacy or distance from interiors.
  • An add-furniture edit that stages the guest house as something it is not. Staging an empty studio is fine with the Virtually staged label; adding a kitchen that does not exist is not. Compare against the original.
  • Claiming rental income or legality. State the current rent if there is one and what the documents show, and stop.

Shot list: two buildings and the path between them

  1. Both buildings in one frame, from the driveway or the back corner of the lot, with the path between them visible.
  2. The guest house's own entrance, from where a visitor would arrive, with its parking in frame if it is separate.
  3. The path, in two frames, from the driveway to the guest door.
  4. The guest house interior: main room from the door, kitchen or kitchenette close, bathroom with the shower visible, sleeping space, storage, laundry if it has one, the view from its window or patio.
  5. The view from the guest house toward the main house, and the reverse, so privacy reads both ways.
  6. Shared spaces between them: pool, courtyard, fire pit, garden.
  7. The main house, in the usual order, grouped separately.
  8. The utility arrangement, if there is a separate meter or panel worth showing.

The listing video should include the walk: main house, out the back, along the path, into the guest house, and back to the shared space. A short handheld walkthrough of that route lets the viewer scroll the distance. For the vertical social video, a portrait frame of the two buildings with the pool or courtyard between them is the frame people share; the second is the guest house's own door.

Worked example: a 4-bed with a casita across the courtyard

A 4-bed, 3-bath single-storey with a detached 520-square-foot casita across a courtyard: one bedroom, a full bath, a kitchenette with a two-burner cooktop, a bar fridge and a microwave, a mini-split, a gate from the side yard to the street, and a parking spot on the side drive. Electricity is on the main meter. The seller's sister stays four months a year. The agent shoots on a Wednesday at 5 p.m.: the two buildings from the back corner with the courtyard fountain between them, the casita door from the side gate, the two-frame path from the side drive to the door, the casita's main room, kitchenette, bath, bedroom, its patio looking back across the courtyard, the main house looking toward the casita, the courtyard, then the main house in fourteen frames. The page reads: casita 520 square feet, one bedroom, full bath, kitchenette, separate gate and parking, 40 feet across the courtyard from the main house, shared electric meter, currently used by family, permitted as a guest house per the seller's documents, buyer to verify intended use. Main house figures alone in the details fields. Twenty landscape frames make the listing video, with the two-building frame as the main photo.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should the guest house square footage be added to the main house?

No. Put the main house figure in the details field and the guest house figure in the text. Buyers who discover a combined number feel misled.

What is the most important photo of a guest house listing?

Both buildings in one frame with the path between them. It shows the distance, the privacy and how the property works, which no interior photo can.

Can I say the guest house can be rented?

State the current rent if it is rented and what the seller's permit or documents show. Whether and how it may be rented is not yours to state; ask your broker how to word it.

Should the guest house photos be mixed into the main gallery?

Group them and say in the text which building they show, so a second kitchen does not appear without explanation. In the video, include the walk from the main house to the guest house so the sequence explains itself.
