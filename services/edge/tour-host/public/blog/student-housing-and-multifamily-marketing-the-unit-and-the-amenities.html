Student housing and multifamily marketing: unit and amenities

One page per unit type, not one per building. Show the actual unit first, the amenities second, and let the prospect ask for a tour date on the same page.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Market student housing and multifamily by unit type, with one page per unit type that shows the actual unit first and the building's amenities second. A prospect is renting a specific two-bed, not the gym, and a page that leads with the pool makes them hunt for the apartment. Type in beds, baths and square feet and put a form that asks for a tour date on the same page.

Market the unit, then the amenities. A student choosing between three buildings is renting a specific two-bedroom with a desk in each room, not the rooftop lounge, and a page that opens on the pool makes them scroll past it to find the apartment. Build one page per unit type, lead with the actual unit shot from the door, type in beds, baths and square feet, and show the amenities after.

Then give the prospect a form that asks for a tour date on the same page, because that is the message they are about to send anyway.

Why one building page is not enough

A 200-unit building has perhaps six unit types. A prospect wants one of them. If the building page shows the lobby, the gym, the study room and a photo of a model unit that may or may not be the type they want, they end up on the phone asking what a two-bed actually looks like. One page per type, with photos of that type and the details typed in, answers that before the call. The amenities are the same on every page, and that is fine; they are the second half of the decision, not the first.

The unit shot list

  1. The unit door from the corridor. With the number blurred or cropped. It sets the first impression.
  2. The living area from the door, wide, level. Shows the real size.
  3. The kitchen with counters clear. Appliances visible. Students and parents both check the dishwasher.
  4. Each bedroom from its door. Bed in the frame if furnished; the desk and the closet visible.
  5. Each bathroom. Shower in the frame.
  6. The view from the main window. Even if it is the car park; honesty here saves a wasted tour.
  7. Laundry, in-unit or on the floor. One frame.

Shoot the model unit for each type, or a vacant unit of that type, with every light on and the blinds open. If the model is furnished and the leased units are not, say so in the voiceover. Virtual staging of a vacant unit is fine if it is labelled as virtually staged and compared with the original; furniture that does not come with the lease must be clearly an illustration.

Then the amenities, in the order they are used

  • The entrance and the mail room.
  • The study room or the lounge.
  • The gym.
  • Laundry, if shared.
  • Outdoor space, the pool, the roof.
  • Parking and bike storage.
  • The walk to campus or transit, one or two frames.

Worked example: a 180-unit building near a campus with four unit types

A building near a campus has studios, one-beds, two-beds and four-bed shared units. The leasing team builds four pages. The four-bed page opens on the unit door, moves through the shared living area and kitchen, shows each of the four bedrooms from its door with the desk visible, then the two bathrooms, then the view, then cuts to the study room, the gym and the bike store, and ends on the walk to the campus gate. Fifty-five seconds, with a voiceover giving the square footage, the number of bathrooms, that the unit comes furnished with a bed and a desk per room, and the lease start month. The page shows beds, baths and square feet, the gallery, the video on request, the leasing office contact card and a form that asks for the date the visitor wants to tour. The link goes in every reply to an enquiry about a four-bed and on the housing board the student union runs. The first message through it is a group of four asking to tour on Saturday, with a question about whether the desks are included. They are, and the voiceover said so, but the question shows which detail to put higher next time.

Parents are half the audience

For student housing, the person paying is often not the person living there. Parents look for security at the entrance, the laundry, the distance to campus and whether the bathroom is clean. The unit-first order serves them too; they want to see where their kid will sleep before they see the games room. Keep the amenities honest and the walk-to-campus frame real.

What goes wrong with multifamily pages

  • Amenities first. The prospect scrolls past the pool to find the apartment, and some do not get there.
  • One model unit for every type. The two-bed prospect tours and finds a different layout.
  • View photos from the best unit used for all. The tour of a ground-floor unit facing the bins ends badly.
  • Furnished model, unfurnished lease, no mention. Say what comes with the unit in the voiceover.
  • Rent typed and left to go stale. If you quote it, keep it current; otherwise leave it for the reply.
  • Enquiries to a leasing mailbox nobody checks on Saturday. The form only works if someone replies the day the student is looking.

Start with the unit type that leases slowest

Pick the type with the most vacancies, shoot a vacant one with the lights on and the blinds open, and build its page with the unit first. Send that link in every reply about that type for two weeks and compare the tour rate with the building page.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should a multifamily building have one page or one per unit type?

One per unit type. A prospect is renting a specific layout, and a building page makes them hunt for it. Each type gets its own photos, details and link; the amenities repeat on every page.

What should come first, the unit or the amenities?

The unit. Living area from the door, kitchen, each bedroom, each bathroom, the view. Then the entrance, study room, gym, outdoor space and the walk to campus or transit.

Can I use virtual staging on a vacant unit?

Yes, if it is labelled as virtually staged, compared with the original, and the voiceover says what actually comes with the lease. Furniture that is not included must read as an illustration, not a promise.

Which Rendprop mode fits student housing or apartments?

The real estate mode, with one listing per unit type and beds, baths and square feet typed in. The enquiry form asks for the date the visitor wants to tour, and enquiries from all unit types land in one inbox.
