Questions agents ask

27 guides on questions agents ask from Rendprop: practical, specific and written for agents, photographers and business owners.

Questions agents ask Branded vs unbranded tour link: what is the difference A branded link shows the property with your name and contact details. An unbranded link shows the property alone, as an MLS virtual-tour field expects. 3 min read Questions agents ask Can a photographer send me a property website for my listing Yes, if their tool puts your contact details on the page and sends enquiries to you. Ask for both links and a plan for after the sale. 4 min read Questions agents ask Can AI change my listing photos in ways I did not ask for Yes. An AI edit can alter or invent details beyond the request: a missing radiator, a new window, a different floor. Compare before you publish. 4 min read Questions agents ask Can buyers see who the agent is on an unbranded link Not from the page. A proper unbranded link shows the property with no agent name, contact details or app branding. Any leak is in the content you added. 4 min read Questions agents ask Can I make a listing video from photos only Yes. A photo-based listing video uses the stills you already have, adds camera motion, a voiceover and captions, and can be ready the day the photos land. 4 min read Questions agents ask Can I market a rental the same way as a sale Mostly yes: same photos, same video, same page. What changes is the details tenants need, the honesty bar on edits, and what the enquiry is asking for. 4 min read Questions agents ask Can I publish a listing from my phone Yes. Photos, AI edits, the video, the property page and both share links can all be done on an iPhone. What matters is reviewing before you publish. 4 min read Questions agents ask Can I use AI-edited photos in the MLS Often yes, with a disclosure, but your MLS sets the rules. Check them and ask your broker. The edits that cause trouble are the ones that change the home. 4 min read Questions agents ask Can I use one app for homes and for my gym Yes, if the app has modes. A home needs price, beds and baths; a gym needs hours, classes and membership. Photos, video and page work the same. 4 min read Questions agents ask Can I use the same photos for Instagram and the MLS Yes, the same shoot serves both. The MLS wants landscape photos; Instagram favours vertical. Ask for a few portrait frames and carry every AI label across. 3 min read Questions agents ask Do I need a drone to market a listing No. Most homes sell on good ground-level photos, a clear video and a page that answers questions. A drone earns its fee when the setting is the story. 4 min read Questions agents ask Do I need a videographer for a listing video Not for most listings. A photo-based video built from the listing photos covers a standard home. Hire a videographer when the home must be felt in motion. 4 min read Questions agents ask Do I need an account to try a listing marketing app To publish, yes. To look around, usually not. A good app lets you see a finished example and start preparing a listing before you sign in or pay anything. 4 min read Questions agents ask Do listing videos help a home sell faster Nobody can promise that, and distrust anyone who quotes a number. What a video reliably does is get more of the right buyers to the door, sooner. 4 min read Questions agents ask Does a listing page need a contact form Yes, alongside a tappable phone number. The form catches the buyer who will not call, and a date field turns a vague message into a showing request. 3 min read Questions agents ask How many photos should a listing have Enough to show every room and the exterior once, well: usually 20 to 30 for a typical home, fewer for a condo, more for a large house. Order beats count. 3 min read Questions agents ask How much does a listing video cost A filmed listing video runs $250–600 at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026, plus $75–150 for a social cutdown. A photo-based video costs far less. 4 min read Questions agents ask How much does a single-property website cost Dedicated single-property site tools run $9–150 per month at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026. Agencies cost far more. Some apps include the page. 3 min read Questions agents ask Is a photo-based video the same as a virtual tour No. A virtual tour is usually something the viewer steers; a photo-based video is a short film you watch. The MLS tour field often takes a link to either. 4 min read Questions agents ask Is virtual staging allowed in listing photos Often yes, when the photo is labelled and the room is real. Your MLS and brokerage set the rules, so check the current ones before you upload. 4 min read Questions agents ask Should a listing video have a voiceover Yes for most listings. A voiceover carries the facts and reasons pictures cannot, and with captions it works on mute. Skip it only for a pure visual piece. 3 min read Questions agents ask Should a listing video have music Usually yes, kept low under the voiceover. Social reels nearly always want it; the page video can go either way. The wrong track does more harm than none. 4 min read Questions agents ask What does an AI aerial opening actually show Generated footage built from one reviewed front exterior photo: the house as photographed, with motion added. It is not a drone recording of the property. 5 min read Questions agents ask What happens to my listing page after the home sells It should stay at the same link, say sold, stop inviting enquiries about a home that is gone, and keep working as proof of your marketing. 4 min read Questions agents ask What should I check before publishing an AI-edited photo Five things: nothing added or removed beyond the request, the property unchanged, the label present, the original kept, and your MLS's current rules read. 4 min read Questions agents ask Where do enquiries from a listing page go To whoever controls the form. On your own property page that should be you, per listing, with the date the buyer wants. On a portal, whoever it routes to. 3 min read Questions agents ask Who owns the photos and videos I upload to a marketing app You should, and the app's terms should say so plainly. Read the licence clause, the marketing clause and the AI training clause before the first upload. 3 min read
