A photographer's checklist before sending AI edits to an agent

Nine checks to run on every AI-edited listing photo before it leaves your hands: comparison, counts, edges, scale, defects, labels, originals, video, MLS.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Before an AI-edited photo goes to an agent, compare it with the original, count the fixed items, check the edges, judge the scale of anything added, confirm no defect was hidden, confirm the label is on, confirm the original is kept, watch any video it appears in, and remind the agent to check their MLS rules. Nine checks, two minutes a photo, no surprises at the showing.

Run nine checks on every AI-edited listing photo before it goes to an agent. Compare it with the original. Count the fixed items. Check the edges. Judge the scale of anything added. Confirm no defect was covered. Confirm the label is on. Confirm the original is kept. Watch any video the edit appears in. Remind the agent to check their MLS rules. It takes about two minutes per edited photo, and it is the difference between a delivery and a complaint.

AI edits are good enough now that the mistakes are subtle. A window that gained a pane, a fence that lost a post, a rug that covers a stain nobody mentioned. The checklist exists because looking good is not the standard; matching the room is.

The nine checks

  1. Side by side with the original. Not from memory. The edited version next to the untouched frame. Everything else on this list depends on it.
  2. Count the fixed things. Windows, doors, outlets, switches, vents, light fittings, cabinet doors. Same count in both frames. If the count changed, the version is discarded.
  3. Walk the edges. Where the new sky meets the roofline, where the green lawn meets the path, where added furniture meets the floor and the wall, where a removed object used to be. Fringing, smearing or a texture that does not match means the edit failed.
  4. Judge the scale. For staging, does the sofa fit the room the way a real sofa would? A bed that reads as a king in a room that takes a double is a misrepresentation, even though nothing structural moved.
  5. Check that nothing was hidden. A rug over a worn floor, a plant in front of a crack, a sky that happens to remove a power line. If a defect or a fixed feature vanished, discard.
  6. Confirm the label. Staged versions are labelled Virtually staged. Sky, lawn, twilight, tidy and custom edits carry an AI-alteration disclosure. The label is on the version that will be delivered.
  7. Confirm the original is kept. In the listing's photo history and in your own archive.
  8. Watch the video. If the edited frame is in the listing video or the reel, watch that scene with the camera move. Motion can reveal an edge that a still hid.
  9. Remind the agent about the MLS. One line in the delivery: these photos carry AI-edit disclosures; check your MLS rules on altered images before posting.

Where each check lives in Rendprop

Checks one, two, three, four and five are done in the photo studio, where each AI edit is saved as a separate version and you compare it against the original before choosing which saved versions to use. Checks six and seven are built in: staging is labelled Virtually staged, other edits carry an AI-alteration disclosure, and the untouched original stays in photo history. Check eight is the review of the video at the video step before you publish. Check nine is your delivery message. Front exterior, blue sky. Side by side: same house. Count: windows six and four, one door, one garage, same. Edges: clean at the roofline, slight fringe around the top of the maple. Zoom in. The fringe is minor but visible. Second attempt is clean. Nothing hidden: the power line across the top of the frame is still there. Label on. Original kept. Keep.

Living room, staging. Side by side: same walls, same floor. Count: two windows, one door, four outlets, one vent. Same. Edges: sofa legs sit on the floor, shadow direction matches the window. Scale: a three-seat sofa in a room that would take one. Fine. Nothing hidden: the scuffed skirting by the door is still visible. Label Virtually staged. Keep.

Primary bedroom, staging. Side by side: same walls. Count: two windows in the original, two in the edit, but the closet door is gone and the wall is now plain. Discard. Run again with a custom prompt that says to keep the closet door. Second version keeps it. Scale: queen bed in a room that takes one. Keep.

Video: the front exterior opens the landscape video, the camera push is clean. Delivery message includes the MLS line. Six minutes total. The invented detail post covers what to do when the second attempt also fails.

What makes photographers skip the checklist

  • The edit looks good. That is exactly when a changed window count gets through.
  • It is late and the agent is waiting. Do the edits in the car in daylight, one shoot behind, so the review is not at midnight.
  • The agent asked for the edit. They asked for a blue sky, not for the power line to disappear. Your name is on the delivery.
  • Reviewing on a dim screen in bright sun. Brightness up, or wait for shade, or do it on the web Studio.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How long does the checklist take per photo?

About two minutes once it is habit. Most listings have one to three edited photos, so the whole pass is well under ten minutes and fits in the gap between shoots.

What is the most common thing the checklist catches?

A changed count: a window, a door, a vent or a cabinet that the edit added or removed. The side-by-side comparison with the original is what makes it visible.

What should I do when an edit fails a check?

Discard the version and either try again with a more specific custom prompt, or deliver the original. Rendprop keeps every edit as a separate version with the original in photo history, so discarding costs nothing.

Do I need to tell the agent about the disclosures?

Yes, in one line of the delivery message. The disclosures are on the versions already; the agent needs to check their own MLS rules on altered images before posting.
