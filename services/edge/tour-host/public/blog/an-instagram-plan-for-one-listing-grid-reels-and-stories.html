An Instagram plan for one listing: grid, reels and stories

A two-week Instagram plan for a single listing: one launch reel, three grid posts, stories around the open house, all pointing to one property page link.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. One listing is enough for two weeks of Instagram: a vertical reel on launch day, three grid posts spaced across the fortnight, and a short run of stories around the open house. Lead each format with a different photo, send every tap to the property page link, and stop posting about the home once it is under contract.

One listing is enough for a two-week Instagram plan: one vertical reel at launch, three grid posts spaced across the fortnight, and a short run of stories built around the open house. Everything points to the same property page link, and nothing goes up until you have watched it on your own phone.

Divide the photo set before you post anything

Sort the listing photos into three piles the day they arrive from the photographer, or the day you shoot them yourself.

  • Reel photos. The portrait frames: front exterior, kitchen, main living space, primary bedroom, and the one feature that makes the home different (a covered porch, a finished basement, a view). Six to ten portrait photos make a reel.
  • Grid photos. The three strongest frames that hold up as a single still: usually the exterior, the kitchen and one surprise.
  • Story photos. Everything else that is honest and quick: detail shots, the yard, the street, the sign going in.

If the set only has landscape photos, the reel will suffer. Ask the photographer for portrait versions of the key rooms, or shoot them yourself the same afternoon. There is more on that in shooting with the vertical format in mind.

A fortnight for a three-bed on a corner lot

Here is the plan for a specific listing: a three-bed, two-bath ranch on a corner lot, listed on a Tuesday, open house the following Saturday.

  • Tuesday, launch day. The reel goes up first. Twenty to thirty seconds, voiceover, word-by-word captions, the price and the open house date in the caption. The property page link goes in your bio the same hour.
  • Wednesday. Grid post one: the front exterior. The caption gives the area, the price and the three facts that matter here (beds, baths, lot). Link in bio.
  • Thursday. Stories, three frames. The kitchen, the back yard, and a text frame with Saturday's hours and a link wherever Instagram currently lets you place one.
  • Friday. Grid post two: the kitchen. The caption answers the question people ask about corner lots, which is usually about the fence line and the yard.
  • Saturday. Stories from the open house itself: the sign, the street, a frame thanking the people who came. No photos of visitors without asking them.
  • Sunday. Nothing. Rest the account.
  • Second week. Grid post three on Tuesday (the surprise: the covered porch at dusk). A second, shorter reel on Thursday that opens on the porch instead of the exterior. Stories as showings come in.

That is two reels, three grid posts and roughly a dozen story frames from one photo set, and no photo has appeared twice in the grid.

Captions that carry the plan

Each caption does one job. The launch reel caption carries the facts. Grid post one carries the location. Grid post two answers an objection. Grid post three tells a small story about the home. Keep them short, specific and written the way you would text a client. There is a fuller guide in captions that read like a person.

If any photo in a post carries an AI edit, the caption says so in one line. A twilight conversion is not a problem. An unlabelled one that surfaces in a comment thread is.

Where the plan falls apart

  • Posting all of it on launch day. Three posts and a reel in one afternoon means followers see one and miss the rest. Spread it out.
  • Leading every format with the same hero photo on the same day. The feed starts to look like a billboard. Open each format with a different frame.
  • A link that goes to your homepage. Buyers who tap want the house, not a bio page with twelve listings. Send them to the property page itself.
  • Guessing at timing. Nobody can tell you the right hour to post for your followers. Test two different times across two listings and keep the one that got replies.
  • Letting the plan outlive the listing. When the home goes under contract, the stories should say so, and the bio link should move to the next listing or to the page that now says sold.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How many Instagram posts should one listing get?

Two reels, three grid posts and a dozen or so story frames across two weeks is plenty. More than that and followers start skipping the listing. Fewer and the open house arrives before anyone has seen the kitchen.

Should the reel or the grid post go up first on launch day?

The reel. It carries the voiceover, the captions and the price in one piece, and it gives the grid posts something to follow. Put the property page link in your bio at the same time.

What if the photographer only delivered landscape photos?

Ask for portrait versions of the six key rooms, or shoot them yourself on your phone the same day. A reel built by cropping landscape photos loses the edges of every room and looks like it.

When should I stop posting about a listing?

When it goes under contract, post one story saying so and move the bio link to the next listing. A sold post later is fine. Daily posts about a home nobody can buy are not.
