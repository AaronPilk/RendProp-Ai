How buyers share a property page: the link path phone to phone

A listing link travels from you to a buyer to their partner to their parents, by text. Every hop sees the same page, so it must work without you.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

A page that works at hop three is one that was built for a stranger with no context.

A listing link travels by text, in hops: you send it to a buyer, the buyer forwards it to their partner with "what do you think", the partner sends it to a parent with "this one?", and the parent sends it to a friend who lives two streets over. Each hop drops your original message and keeps only the link, so by the third phone the page is all that is left. It has to say what the home is, who is selling it and how to ask for a showing, with no help from you.

Build the page for the person at hop three, a stranger with no context, and it works for everyone before them too.

The four hops and what each person needs

Hop one: the buyer. They have your text for context. They want the photos, the price and the video. They will come back to the page several times over a few days.

Hop two: the partner. They have "what do you think" and the link. They want the same things, plus a sense of whether it is realistic. The price has to be obvious. The gallery has to show every room, because they are looking for the catch.

Hop three: the parent or the friend. They have "this one?" and the link. They do not know the buyer's budget or the area. They want to know what it is, where it is, and whether it looks like a good home. They often scroll to the bottom to see who the agent is, because that is how they judge whether the listing is legitimate.

Hop four: the person who knows the street. They open it to see which house it is. They may know something about it. They may also be thinking of selling their own.

What the page has to carry on its own

  • The main photo as the preview. At every hop, the link renders as a bubble. The house should be in it, not a logo.
  • The price and the location near the top. Hop three does not know either.
  • A complete gallery. Hop two is looking for what is missing.
  • The video, tap to play. Hop one watches it; hops two and three often do, in the evening, with sound.
  • Your name and number. Hop three uses it to judge legitimacy. Hop four may use it to call you about their own home.
  • A form that works for someone who is not the buyer. The partner may be the one who asks for the showing. The form should not assume the person filling it in is the one you texted.

A worked example: Thursday to Sunday

Thursday 6pm: you text the link for a 3-bed to a buyer named Chris. Chris opens it in the car, swipes the gallery, forwards it to Jordan: "what do you think."

Thursday 9pm: Jordan opens it on the sofa, watches the video, scrolls to the bottom, sees your name, and forwards it to her mother: "this one?"

Friday 7am: the mother opens it over coffee. She does not know the town. The page shows the street and the town under the price. She looks at the kitchen twice and texts back "nice kitchen, is it near the school." Jordan asks Chris. Chris texts you. You reply with the answer and a Saturday showing time.

Friday noon: the mother forwards the link to a friend who lives nearby. The friend recognises the house, says the street is quiet, and notes your name because she has been thinking about selling.

Four phones, one page, one showing booked and one future listing conversation. Your original message was read by one of the four.

What breaks the chain

  • A page that needs a login or an app. Hop three will not install anything. The page must open in a browser.
  • A preview that shows nothing. A bare link from an unknown number gets ignored. The main photo in the bubble is what gets the tap.
  • The unbranded link. It forwards fine, but hop three sees no name and cannot judge who is behind it, and hop four cannot call you.
  • A changed link. If you rebuild the page and the address changes, every forwarded copy breaks. Keep the link stable for the life of the listing.
  • A page that autoplays. Hop two opened it at 9pm next to a sleeping child. The page got closed.

Design for the stranger

When you review the page before sharing, pretend you are the mother at hop three. You do not know the buyer, the town or the agent. Does the page tell you what this is, where it is, what it costs, who is selling it, and how to ask about it. If yes, every hop before you is covered as well.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Why does the page need my name if I already texted the buyer?

Because the buyer forwards the link without your text. By the second or third phone, the page is all that is left, and the person looking at it judges whether the listing is real by whether an agent's name and number are on it.

Should I send a shortened link so it is easier to forward?

No. A shortened link hides the destination, and people are warier of tapping one from a number they do not know. Forwarding a normal link is one tap anyway. Send the full branded link and let the preview do the work.

What if the person who fills in the form is not the buyer I texted?

That is common and good. A partner or a parent often asks for the showing on the buyer's behalf. Reply to whoever filled in the form, and ask who will be attending.

Can I see who forwarded the link?

Not reliably. What you can see is the enquiry that comes back, which tells you the link reached someone interested. If you want to know the path, ask on the call; most buyers will tell you who sent it to them.
