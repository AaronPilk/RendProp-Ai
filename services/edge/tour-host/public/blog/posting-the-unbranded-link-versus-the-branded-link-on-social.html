Posting the unbranded link versus the branded link on social

On your own social accounts, post the branded property page link. The unbranded link is for the MLS virtual tour field; check your MLS's current rules.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Post the branded link on your own social accounts, because it carries your name, your number and a form. The unbranded link exists for the MLS virtual tour field; check your MLS's current rules on what may appear there. The only social cases for the unbranded link are when you are posting somewhere that bans agent branding or sharing on behalf of someone else, and both deserve a second look before you paste.

On your own social accounts, post the branded link. It opens a property page with your name, your business and your number on it, and an enquiry form that reaches you. The unbranded link is built for the MLS virtual tour field; check your own MLS's current rules on what may appear there. Paste the unbranded link into an Instagram caption and the person who taps it sees a home with nobody to call.

What each link actually opens

The branded link opens the full property page: the main photo, the gallery, the video, the details, the contact card and the enquiry form. A viewer knows who listed the home and how to reach them without leaving the page.

The unbranded link opens the same home with no name, no contact details and no Rendprop branding. It exists for the MLS virtual tour field, which has its own rules about what may appear on the linked page; check your MLS's current rules. It is the right link for that field and the wrong link for a post whose whole point is that people contact you. The distinction is covered in more depth in why you need both links.

The mistake, as it usually happens

An agent lists a three-bed on a Friday, copies a link from the app to put into the MLS, and then, on the same phone, in the same minute, pastes the same link into a community group post and a caption on a reel. The MLS entry is correct. The group post and the reel now send everyone to a page with no name on it.

Sunday evening, a group member comments: who is the agent on this? Another member answers with the name of a different agent they happen to know in that office. The listing agent sees the thread on Monday. Nobody filled in a form, because the unbranded page is not built to produce a conversation with a named person.

The fix took thirty seconds: edit both posts, swap in the branded link. The lesson is to copy the two links separately and label them in your own notes before you post anything.

When the unbranded link belongs on social

There are a few cases, and each one deserves a pause.

  • A group or forum that bans agent self-promotion. Some community groups allow a home to be shared but not an agent's pitch. The unbranded link can satisfy that rule. Read the group's current rules, and consider whether a post with no way to reach you is worth making at all.
  • A co-listing or referral arrangement. If another agent is sharing the home on their own account under an arrangement you have agreed, the unbranded link avoids putting your details on their page. Agree it in writing first.
  • A photographer or media company showing their work. They may prefer a link without the agent's contact details on a portfolio post. The branded link with the agent's details is still the better credit, and the agent should be asked either way.

Outside those, the answer is the branded link every time.

A quick check before you paste

Open the link on your own phone before it goes into a caption. If you see your name and number, it is the branded one. If you see the home and nothing about who to call, it is the unbranded one, and it belongs in the MLS field, not the post. Thirty seconds, every time, until it is a habit.

Ways this goes wrong beyond the obvious

  • The bio link is the unbranded one. It sits there for weeks sending every follower to an anonymous page. Check your bio link today.
  • A flyer printed with the unbranded link. A QR code on a yard sign that opens a page with no agent is a lost call for every scan. See keeping one link consistent.
  • Sending the branded link to the MLS. The opposite error. The virtual tour field has its own rules about branding on the linked page. Check your MLS's current rules before you paste.
  • Assuming the unbranded page still routes to you. Do not rely on an anonymous page to generate a named enquiry. Use the branded page where enquiries are the goal.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Which link should I put in an Instagram caption or bio?

The branded link. It opens the property page with your contact card and the enquiry form. The unbranded link opens a page with no name on it and is meant for the MLS virtual tour field.

Can I ever post the unbranded link on social media?

In a group that bans agent self-promotion, or when sharing on behalf of another agent under an agreed arrangement. Both cases are worth a pause, because a page with nobody to contact rarely produces a conversation.

How do I tell the two links apart?

Open it on your own phone. If your name and number are on the page, it is branded. If the home shows with no contact details, it is unbranded. In Rendprop the branded link uses the /f/ path and the unbranded link uses the /u/ path.

What happens if I put the branded link in the MLS by mistake?

Check your own MLS's current rules on what may appear on a virtual tour page, and use the unbranded link in that field. If the branded one has already gone in, swap it the same day.
