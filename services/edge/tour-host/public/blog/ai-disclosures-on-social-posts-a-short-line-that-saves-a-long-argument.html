AI disclosures on social posts: one line saves an argument

One plain caption line tells viewers which photos in a listing reel were staged or altered. Wording to copy, where it goes, what happens without it.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Put one plain line in the caption of every listing reel or post that uses AI-edited photos: “Some photos virtually staged” or “Exterior lighting digitally altered.” Put it in the first lines, not after twenty hashtags, and repeat it in the on-screen captions if the reel is mostly staged rooms. It takes five seconds and ends the comment-section argument before it starts.

Put one plain line in the caption of every listing reel or post that uses AI-edited photos: “Some photos virtually staged,” or “Exterior lighting digitally altered,” or both, in the first two lines of the caption rather than after twenty hashtags where nobody reads. If the reel is mostly staged rooms, add it to the on-screen captions as well. Five seconds of typing, and the comment-section argument about whether the furniture is real does not happen.

Why social needs its own disclosure

The MLS photo has a caption field. The property page has a label on the image. The social post has neither by default; it has a cropped, compressed, nine-by-sixteen version of your photo, playing for two seconds, in front of people who have no idea which listing it is or who you are. If the label was on the image, it may have been cropped off. If the disclosure was in the MLS remarks, it is three apps away.

Social is also where the sharpest viewers are. Someone will comment “that sofa is AI.” If your caption already says so, the comment makes you look careful. If it does not, the comment makes you look caught.

Lines you can copy

Keep it short, specific and unapologetic. These work:

  • “Living room and primary bedroom virtually staged.”
  • “Some photos virtually staged; home is vacant.”
  • “Twilight exterior is a digital edit of a daytime photo.”
  • “Sky and lawn digitally altered in the exterior shot.”
  • “Photos include AI edits; originals available on request.”
  • “Video built from listing photos; not a recording.”

Lines to avoid: “Photos enhanced for marketing purposes” (says nothing), “Images may not reflect actual property” (says too much, and sounds like a disclaimer on a cereal box), and anything that apologises for the edit. You are not sorry. You are being clear.

Where the line goes

In the caption, high up

First or second line. Captions fold behind “more” after a couple of lines on many platforms. The disclosure needs to be visible before that fold.

On screen, when the reel is mostly staged

If three of six scenes are staged, a small on-screen caption on each staged scene (“Virtually staged”) is worth the ten seconds it takes. Word-by-word captions for the voiceover do not replace this; they are reading your script, which probably does not say “this sofa is not real.”

In the voiceover, optionally

A single spoken line, “the living room is shown virtually staged,” is the clearest disclosure there is, and it survives every repost because it is baked into the audio. Use it when the staging is the point of the reel.

In the platform's own tools, if offered

Some platforms offer their own AI-content labels. Rules and features change, so check the current rules on each platform you post to and use the label where it applies. It does not replace your caption line; it sits alongside it.

A worked example: the Friday reel

A vacant three-bed goes live on a Friday. The reel runs twenty-eight seconds: exterior (blue sky edit), kitchen (original), living room (staged), primary bedroom (staged), back yard (original), close on the front door. The caption opens:

“Just listed. Living room and primary bedroom virtually staged; sky edited in the exterior. Link in bio for the full gallery with originals.”

On the living room and bedroom scenes, a small “Virtually staged” caption sits in a corner. The property page the link leads to carries the same labels on the same photos, plus the empty originals. Someone comments “is that furniture real?” and three people reply “it says virtually staged” before the agent has even seen the comment.

What goes wrong without it

  • The comment thread becomes the listing. Twenty replies about the sofa, zero about the home.
  • A buyer's agent screenshots it. Now the unlabelled staged photo is in a text thread with the words “misleading” next to it, and your MLS caption is nowhere in that thread.
  • The seller sees it first. Sellers follow their own listing. If the first they hear of staging is a stranger's comment, you have a different conversation ahead; the seller conversation post covers how to avoid it.
  • Reposts strip everything. A colleague shares your reel without the caption. The on-screen label and the spoken line are what survive.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Where in a social caption should the AI disclosure go?

In the first or second line, before the caption folds behind “more.” A disclosure after a block of hashtags is not read by anyone and does not help you when a commenter asks whether the furniture is real.

Do word-by-word captions on a reel count as a disclosure?

Only if the voiceover script actually says the room is staged or the photo is edited. Captions that transcribe the script do not add a disclosure on their own. Put a short on-screen label on staged scenes or say it in the voiceover.

Should I use the platform's own AI-content label?

Use it where it applies and check the current rules on each platform, since features change. It sits alongside your caption line rather than replacing it, because your caption is the part that names which photos were changed.

What if someone reposts my reel without the caption?

That is why on-screen labels and a spoken line matter. Both are baked into the video and survive any repost. The caption is the first layer, not the only one.
