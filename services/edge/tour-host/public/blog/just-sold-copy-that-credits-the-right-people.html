Just sold copy that credits the right people

A just-sold post names the outcome, thanks the people who did the work, and ends with what you do next for the reader. One line about you, no buyer names.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A just-sold post names the outcome, credits the people who did the work (the sellers for preparing the home, the buyer's agent, the photographer, the lender if they earned it), and ends with what you do next for the reader. Keep the bragging to one line, state the price only with the seller's agreement and your MLS's rules in mind, and never name or picture the buyers without asking.

A just-sold post should name the outcome, credit the people who did the work, and end with what you do next for the reader. The sellers who kept the house spotless for three weeks of showings, the buyer's agent who ran a clean deal, the photographer whose main photo did the first job. One line about you. The price only if the seller agrees and your MLS's current rules allow it. And the buyers' names and faces only with their permission, which usually means not at all.

Most just-sold posts fail by being about the agent. A photo of a SOLD sign, a line about how fast it went, a list of hashtags. The reader, who is a future seller, learns nothing except that you like signs. The version that credits others reads as competence, because people who do the work well tend to thank the people they did it with.

Who to credit, and in what order

  1. The sellers. By first name only if they are comfortable, otherwise “the sellers”. What they did: prepared the house, painted the hallway, left for every showing.
  2. The buyer's agent. By name and brokerage. They will see it, remember it, and bring you their next buyer. This is the credit most agents skip and the one that pays back.
  3. The photographer or media team. By name or business. Their main photo and video did the first week's work.
  4. The lender, inspector or attorney, if they did something that saved the deal. Not as a matter of course.
  5. You, in one line, usually implied.

What you can say about the price and the terms

“Sold over asking” and “sold in four days” are the seller's information. Ask before you post either. On the number itself, check your MLS's current rules on reporting sold prices and where they may appear, and ask your broker about office policy; some areas treat it differently. When in doubt, say “sold” and let the record speak. The post loses nothing by leaving the figure out.

The shape

  • The outcome, plainly: “Hollis Road is sold.”
  • The credits, two or three lines.
  • One sentence about what it took or what you learned, if there is something true to say.
  • What you do next for the reader: “If you are thinking about a spring sale, the link in bio shows what a launch looks like.”

The post, the email and the seller’s share

Social post: “Hollis Road is sold. Thank you to the sellers, who repainted the hallway and left the house spotless for every showing, to Dana Reyes at Elm Realty for a clean deal on the buyer side, and to Northside Photo for the main photo that did the first week's work. Thinking about a spring sale? The link in bio shows what a launch looks like.”

Email to the database. Subject: “Hollis Road is sold”. One photo, the same four lines, your name and number.

The seller's share. Give the sellers a version they can post themselves, with the property page link, if they want to. Their friends are your next sellers.

The same sale, two posts

Before: “ANOTHER ONE SOLD. Multiple offers, over asking, under contract in 48 hours. This is what happens when you list with a top producer. Congrats to my amazing buyers Mike and Jess on their dream home. Who's next?”

After: “Hollis Road is sold. Thank you to the sellers for a spotless house through three weeks of showings, to Dana Reyes at Elm Realty on the buyer side, and to Northside Photo for the photos. If you are weighing a sale this spring, the link in bio shows what the launch looked like.”

The first version names buyers who did not agree to be named, discloses terms that were the seller's to share, and is about the agent. The second credits four people and still makes the point.

Where just-sold posts go wrong

  • Naming or picturing the buyers. The keys-on-the-porch photo is theirs to post. Ask; if they say yes, keep the caption to a thank-you.
  • Terms the seller did not clear. Over asking, days on market, number of offers. Ask first, every time.
  • The uncredited buyer's agent. A missed chance to earn a referral and a small discourtesy that gets noticed.
  • The sign photo. Use the home's main photo. The sign is about you; the house is about the sale.
  • “Another one.” It tells the reader their home would be one of many.
  • Forgetting the page. If the property page still says “just listed” while the post says sold, the link undercuts the post.

Ask two people before you post

Text the seller to confirm what they are comfortable sharing about the terms, and text the buyer's agent to say you are going to credit them. Then mark the page sold, write the four lines, and post with the home's main photo.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Can I say a home sold over asking in a just-sold post?

Only with the seller's agreement, since the terms are theirs to share, and after checking your MLS's current rules and your broker's policy on reporting sold prices. Many agents simply write sold and let the public record carry the figure. The post works without it.

Should I tag the buyers in a just-sold post?

Not unless they have asked to be. Buyers did not sign up for a public announcement, and their photo with the keys is theirs to post. If they are happy to be named, keep it to a thank-you. Credit the buyer's agent instead, who will see it and remember.

Why credit the buyer's agent at all?

They are the person most likely to bring you a buyer on your next listing, and a public thank-you costs nothing. It also reads to future sellers as the behaviour of someone who runs clean deals. Name them and their brokerage.

Which photo should a just-sold post use?

The home's main photo, the same one that launched the listing. A photo of the sign is about you; the house is about the sale. If the property page is marked sold, link to it so the reader can see the full gallery and video of what a launch looked like.
