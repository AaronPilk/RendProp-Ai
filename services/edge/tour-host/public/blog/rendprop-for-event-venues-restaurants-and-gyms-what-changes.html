Rendprop for event venues, restaurants and gyms: what changes

Pick a business mode and the page asks for hours, menu, capacity or membership instead of beds and baths. The enquiry form still asks for a date.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Create the listing in the event venues, restaurants and bars, or gyms and studios mode and three things change: the details the page asks for (hours, menu, capacity or membership instead of price, beds, baths and square feet), the words the page uses, and the sample content. The photo studio, the video step, the property page structure, the two links and the inbox work the same. The enquiry form still asks for the date the visitor wants, which for a venue is the question that matters.

Choose event venues, restaurants and bars, or gyms and studios in the mode chooser when you create the listing, and the page asks for business details, such as hours, menu, capacity or membership, instead of price, beds, baths and square feet. The wording on the page and the sample content follow the mode. Everything else is the same app: the photo studio, the video step, the page with a gallery and a video on request, the branded and unbranded links, and the inbox.

The enquiry form still asks the visitor for the date they want, which for a venue is the whole enquiry.

What stays the same

  • Start a listing from Home. Add photos: landscape photos for the landscape video, portrait photos for the vertical social video.
  • Review AI edits in the photo studio as separate labelled versions. Blue sky on the terrace, tidy the room on the bar counter, add furniture in an empty function room (labelled "Virtually staged"). Compare against the original before you use any of them.
  • Build the video in the video step with scene order and camera motion planned for you, a recorded or AI voice, word-by-word captions and optional music.
  • Publish the page from the website step with a connection and a connected workspace. Share the branded link; the unbranded link exists too if a directory or partner wants a page with no contact details on it.
  • Enquiries land in the inbox, per listing and all together.

What changes, mode by mode

Event venues

The page asks for the details a planner needs before they call, such as capacity and hours. The enquiry form's request for a date becomes the main thing a visitor types, because a venue enquiry is "is the 14th free" before it is anything else. Photos: the room set for an event, the room empty, the entrance, parking, the view from the top table. The venue tour post goes through the shot list.

Restaurants and bars

Details such as hours, menu and capacity. The page replaces the PDF menu nobody can read on a phone with the details typed in the app. Photos: the dining room at service time with the lights the way they are at eight, the bar from the door, two dishes, the private room if there is one. The video opens on the room, not the sign. The enquiry date doubles as a private-dining request.

Gyms and studios

Details such as hours and membership. Photos: the floor when it is clean and empty, the floor when a class is on (with permission from the people in it), the changing rooms, the front desk. A vertical social video from portrait photos of the equipment does more here than a long landscape video. The enquiry date is "when can I come in for a trial", and the reply should book it. The gym tour post covers the trial-visit angle.

Retail and other businesses

Retail asks for details such as hours and shows the shop. Other businesses covers offices, studios and workshops, where the page is a tour for a prospective tenant, client or collaborator. Same structure, business wording.

Setting one up, step by step

  1. Start a listing from Home and choose the business mode in the mode chooser. Mode is set at creation; pick the one whose details match what visitors need to know.
  2. Add the photos. Prioritise the space as it is when customers are in it.
  3. Review any edits against originals in the photo studio. A tidied bar counter is fine; a bigger room is not.
  4. Build a landscape video for the page and, if the business lives on social, a vertical one from portrait photos.
  5. In the website step, type the business details the mode asks for. Leave what does not apply empty; the section hides.
  6. Publish, open the page on your phone, and send the owner the branded link.

What to confirm with the owner before publishing

Hours match the door. Capacity matches what the fire certificate allows, not what the owner hopes. The contact card is the person who answers booking enquiries, not the owner's personal cell if the manager handles bookings. The enquiry form is there and asks for a date; try it yourself once.

A worked example: the barn and the Tuesday enquiry

A photographer shoots a 150-seat wedding barn on a Monday for its owner. She starts the listing in event venues, adds 20 landscape photos (barn dressed for a wedding, barn empty, courtyard, the approach at golden hour) and 6 portrait photos (the doors, the chandelier, the aisle). Blue sky on the approach shot, compared with the original, kept. The landscape video opens on the approach with an AI voice reading the owner's forty words. In the website step she types capacity 150 and the hours the office answers the phone, and presents the owner's contact details on the listing with the owner's email verified for forwarding.

Tuesday a couple opens the branded link from the barn's social profile, watches the video, and uses the form to ask for a Saturday the following June. The enquiry, with the date, lands in the inbox under the barn and is forwarded to the owner, who replies with availability in an hour. The owner's old PDF brochure could not do that.

Where business listings go wrong

  • Choosing real estate for a venue. The page then asks for price and beds. Use the mode that matches the questions visitors ask.
  • Photos with nobody in them, for a place that is about people. A bar at 11am reads as closed. Shoot the room as it is used, with permission.
  • Staging a function room without saying so. Add furniture is labelled "Virtually staged" for a reason, and the label stays with the version.
  • Nobody watching the inbox. A venue enquiry with a date is time-sensitive. Decide who replies.
  • Treating the page as a website replacement. It is the page for the space: a tour, the details and a form. Link to it from the main site, the social profiles and the directory listings.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What changes when I pick a business mode?

The details the page asks for (hours, menu, capacity or membership instead of price, beds, baths and square feet), the wording on the page, and the sample content. The photo studio, the video step, the page structure, the two links and the inbox are the same.

Does the enquiry form work for venue bookings?

It asks the visitor for the date they want, which is the first question in any venue enquiry. The enquiry lands in the inbox under that listing and can be forwarded to a client whose email is verified.

Can a photographer run venue and home listings in one account?

Yes. The mode is chosen per listing or per workspace, so a home goes in as real estate and a barn goes in as event venues from the same phone.

Which mode is right for a salon or a coworking space?

Pick the mode whose details match what visitors need: gyms and studios if membership matters, other businesses for offices, studios and workshops. Fill in only what applies; empty sections hide.
