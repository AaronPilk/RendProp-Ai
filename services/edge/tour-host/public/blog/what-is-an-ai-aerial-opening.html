What is an AI aerial opening

An AI aerial opening is a generated clip made from a reviewed front exterior photo, not a drone recording. Paid, permission-gated; results vary; review it.

The honest way to think about it is as an establishing shot that was imagined from a photo rather than flown. That framing matters for sellers, for buyers and for your MLS, so keep it in every conversation about the clip.

How a generated aerial clip is made

Generated aerial footage, as a category, starts from a single still of the front of the home. A model infers what the scene would look like if the camera rose and moved, and renders a few seconds of motion from that guess. It is not a recording of anything; it is a plausible animation anchored to one real photo, and results vary from one attempt to the next. The parts of the home that were visible in the photo are the parts it knows about. Everything beyond the frame, the roof, the back yard, the neighbours, the street behind, is inferred, which is why the clip must be reviewed before use.

That is also why the input photo matters. A sharp, level, well-lit front exterior gives the model more to work from than a tilted phone snap through a car window.

When a generated opening makes sense

  • As the opening seconds of a landscape video, before the cut to the front door, to set the scene.
  • For a home with a strong front elevation and a setting that benefits from a wider establishing view.
  • When a real drone session is not practical for schedule, airspace or budget reasons and you have explained to the seller what the clip is and is not.

It adds less for a condo in a tower, a home in dense trees, or any property where the front photo shows little context for the model to extend.

What an aerial opening is not

A generated aerial clip is not drone footage and should not be described as drone footage, filmed, flown or captured. A real drone session is a pilot, a flight and a recording of the actual roof, lot and surroundings, typically $150 to $350 at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026, and it remains the right choice when the lot, the roof condition or the setting is part of the value. The generated clip is a stylistic opening, and because results vary it needs a review before it goes anywhere.

The nearest confusable thing is a slow camera move on the exterior photo inside a photo-based video. That is a real photo with motion added to it. The generated clip invents new angles the photo did not contain.

A worked example, with the conversation that goes with it

An agent lists a 4-bed on a corner lot with a wide front lawn and mature maples. The photographer delivers a clean, straight-on front exterior shot taken at ten in the morning. The agent wants a landscape video that opens wider than the photo allows. She tells the seller, before anything is made, that the opening will be generated from the front photo, is not a drone recording, that results vary, and that she will review it and drop it if it shows anything the real property does not have.

The clip comes back. She watches it twice at full size. The roofline matches the photo, the maples are where they should be, and the side yard the model invented is a plain lawn, which is what the side yard is. She uses it as the opening of the video and cuts to the front door. The seller, forewarned, is pleased rather than confused.

Where generated aerials go wrong

The failure is always language. A caption that says drone tour. A seller who heard aerial and expected a flight. A buyer who saw the clip, drove past, and found a different roofline. Because the clip is generated, results vary, and a version that invents a pool, a second storey or a neighbour's house that is not there must be rejected on review. Check your own MLS rules before posting generated footage in any listing field, and label it as generated wherever it appears.

Next step: before you make any opening clip, write the one sentence you will say to the seller about what it is. If you cannot say generated, not a drone recording, and results vary, in that sentence, do not make the clip.
