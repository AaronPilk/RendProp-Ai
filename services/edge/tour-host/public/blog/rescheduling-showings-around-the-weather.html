Rescheduling showings around the weather

Decide by Thursday noon for a weekend open house: move it, shorten it or switch to the page and video. Tell everyone the same thing with the same link.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. When the forecast turns, decide by Thursday noon for a weekend open house: move it to the next weekend, shorten it, or replace it with the page and the video for that weekend. Then tell the seller, the booked buyers, their agents and the public, in that order, with one message that includes the property page link, so the weekend is not lost even if the open house is.

Decide by Thursday noon. If the forecast for Saturday or Sunday makes the open house unsafe to reach, unpleasant to attend or bad for how the house shows, you have three options: move it to the following weekend, shorten it to the better of the two days, or replace it for this weekend with the property page and the video. Pick one by Thursday noon and tell everyone the same thing with the same link. A decision made on Saturday morning reaches half the people and annoys all of them.

Weather does not cancel the weekend's interest. The people who were going to come are still at home, now with nothing to do. The page and the video are how you reach them while the open house waits for better light.

Brookside Lane and the Saturday snow

A 4-bed on Brookside Lane with a long, steep drive. Open house planned for Sunday 1 to 3; three private showings booked for Saturday afternoon. On Wednesday the forecast firms up: heavy snow from Friday night, clearing Sunday morning, roads a mess until Monday.

Thursday 10 a.m. You decide. The Saturday showings move to Monday and Tuesday evenings; the drive will be cleared by then and the house shows well lit at 6. The Sunday open house moves to the following Sunday, same hours; snow on the roof and a cleared drive will look fine, slush and a blocked road will not.

Thursday 10:30. The seller first: the decision, the new dates, what you need from them (the drive cleared by Monday 4 p.m., the path salted). Then the three booked buyers' agents by text: new times offered, the page link, “the video shows the garden in October if your buyers want to see it without the snow.” All three confirm by lunchtime.

Thursday afternoon. The public: the social post with the open house date is edited; a new story says “Sunday's open house moves to the 15th, same hours. In the meantime the full gallery and video are on the page, and the form takes a request for a private visit.” The email that was due Friday goes out with the new date. The sign rider stays in the garage until next Saturday.

The weekend. Two enquiries come through the page, both with preferred dates in the following week. Nobody drove up an icy hill.

The decision rules

  • Can people get there safely? Ice, flooding, high wind. If the answer is doubtful, the answer is no.
  • Can they get in? A long drive under snow, a street that floods, a car park that becomes a lake.
  • How does the house show? Some houses are fine in rain and some look miserable: dark interiors, a garden that becomes mud, a flat roof that puddles. Be honest about which this is.
  • Can the seller prepare? Clearing a drive and keeping the floors clean through a wet open house is work. If they cannot, move it.
  • Is it just rain? Rain is not a reason. Lights on, mats at the door, towels for umbrellas, and the house gets the serious buyers only, which is not a bad Sunday.

Who to tell, in order, and with what

  1. The seller. By phone. The decision, the dates, what you need from them.
  2. Booked buyers and their agents. By text, each with two or three alternative times, the page link and one line about what the video shows that the weather will hide.
  3. Anyone who enquired this week. Same text, since they may have been planning to come.
  4. The public. Edit the original posts, add a story, send the email with the new date. The old date must not survive anywhere.
  5. The sign. Rider off, or not put on. A sign that says Sunday 1 to 3 in a blizzard is a sign that says you do not check.

Filling the gap with the page

The weekend's attention is still there. Use it. The message to everyone includes the link because the page and the video are the open house for this weekend: the gallery, the rooms in order, the garden in whatever season it was shot, the details, and the enquiry form for a visit next week. People who are housebound by weather look at houses on their phones. Give them yours.

Where weather weekends go wrong

  • Deciding Saturday morning. The agents have already left, the seller has already cleaned, the post still says Sunday.
  • Cancelling rain. You lose a Sunday for weather that would have thinned the crowd to the people who mattered.
  • Different messages to different people. The agent hears Monday, the buyer hears Tuesday, the post says the 15th. One message, one link, one set of dates.
  • Forgetting the public posts. Someone drives over on Sunday because the Wednesday post was never edited.
  • No link in the message. You have told people not to come and given them nothing to do instead.
  • The sign left out. See above.

How this works in Rendprop

The property page is the fallback for the lost weekend: the gallery, the details, the chosen listing video (it plays when the visitor taps it) and the enquiry form that asks for the date the visitor wants. Because the form asks for a date, a buyer who was going to come Sunday can propose Tuesday instead, and that request lands in the app's inbox for the listing with the date attached. The branded link from the Share sheet is the one that goes into every rescheduling message, so the seller, the agents and the public all land on the same page. Rendprop does not manage the showing calendar; the new times live wherever your diary lives. Details at the property website and enquiries.

Put Thursday noon in the diary

For every weekend open house, set a Thursday noon reminder to check the forecast and decide. Write the rescheduling text once, with the link, and keep it as a template. For what to do when the weather is the whole week rather than a day, read marketing a listing during a storm week.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

When should I decide whether to cancel an open house for weather?

By Thursday noon for a weekend open house. That leaves time to tell the seller, move booked showings, edit every public post and send the email with the new date. A Saturday morning decision reaches half the people and leaves the old date on posts that someone will act on.

Should I cancel an open house for rain?

Usually not. Rain thins the crowd to the serious buyers, which is not a bad Sunday. Put lights on, mats at the door and towels for umbrellas. Cancel or move for conditions that make the house unsafe to reach or impossible to enter, or that make it show badly, such as a garden that turns to mud.

What should the rescheduling message say?

The new date or alternative times, the property page link, and one line about what the video shows that the weather will hide, such as the garden in October. Send the same message to the seller, the booked buyers and their agents, and anyone who enquired that week, then edit every public post so the old date disappears.

How do I keep the weekend from being wasted if the open house moves?

Send everyone the property page link and treat the page and the video as the open house for that weekend. People kept indoors by weather look at houses on their phones. The enquiry form asks for a preferred date, so a buyer who was coming Sunday can ask for Tuesday without a phone call.
