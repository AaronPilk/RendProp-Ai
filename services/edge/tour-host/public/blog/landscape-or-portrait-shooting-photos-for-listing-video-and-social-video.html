Landscape photos or portrait: listing video and social video

Shoot both. Horizontal frames feed the listing video and the MLS; upright frames feed the vertical social video. Which rooms suit each.

Shoot both. Landscape photos go into the listing video, the MLS gallery and anything that plays on a TV or laptop. Portrait photos go into the vertical social video for Instagram, TikTok and the phone-first viewer. The two sets are not crops of each other. A room framed well in landscape orientation is usually framed badly when you simply turn the phone, so plan a short second pass upright for the rooms that suit it.

Why one orientation does not cover both

A horizontal frame of a living room shows the width: the window wall, the fireplace, the sofa and the opening to the kitchen. Crop that to 9:16 and you keep a strip down the middle, which is usually the back of the sofa and a bit of wall. The things that made it a good frame are on the sides you just cut off.

A portrait frame of the same room has to be composed differently. You stand further back, you find a vertical subject, and you let the ceiling and the floor into the shot. Tall windows, a staircase, a hallway, a doorway through to the next room, a kitchen island shot end-on. Those read well upright. A wide, low room does not.

Which rooms suit portrait

  • Entry halls and stairs, where height is the point
  • Kitchens shot down the island, so the counter leads the eye to the window
  • Bathrooms with a tall shower or a window over the tub
  • Any room with floor-to-ceiling glass
  • Exteriors of tall or narrow houses, townhomes, and anything with a tree beside it
  • Details that stand up: a range and hood, a fireplace, a built-in bookcase

Rooms that fight portrait: long living rooms, wide kitchens with the island side-on, open-plan spaces where the width is the feature. Shoot those in landscape orientation only and let the vertical video use the rooms that suit it.

A worked example: the Saturday shoot with two passes

A photographer has a 4-bed to shoot at 9am and three more after it. The horizontal pass takes 40 minutes and produces 34 frames. The upright pass takes 10 minutes and produces 9: the front door with the porch columns, the staircase from the hall, the kitchen island end-on toward the window, the primary bath shower, the deck with the tree beside it, and four details.

The horizontal set goes to the MLS and makes a listing video with 14 scenes. The upright set makes a vertical social video with 8 scenes, opening on the front door and ending on the deck. Same house, same morning, two videos that each look like they were shot for their format rather than cropped into it.

Mistakes that show in the final video

Mixing orientations in one video. A portrait frame in a landscape video gets cropped or gets black bars. Either way the buyer sees the seam. Keep the sets separate.

Shooting portrait from the same spot as the horizontal frame. The composition that worked wide does not work tall. Move your feet.

Tilting the phone up in portrait to get the ceiling. Vertical lines bend outward and the room looks like it is falling over. Keep the phone level and step back instead.

Forgetting the exterior in portrait. The social video needs an opening frame too. A tall front elevation with the door centred does the job.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Can I crop landscape photos to make a vertical social video?

You can, but the result is usually a strip of wall. The features that made the horizontal frame work are on the sides that get cropped. A short upright pass of the rooms that suit portrait gives a far better vertical video for ten extra minutes.

Which rooms look good in portrait orientation?

Stairs and entry halls, kitchens shot down the island, tall showers, rooms with floor-to-ceiling glass, narrow exteriors and vertical details like a range and hood. Wide living rooms and side-on kitchens do not, so leave those horizontal.

How does Rendprop know which photos go in which video?

Landscape photos feed the listing video and portrait photos feed the vertical social video. You add the whole shoot to the listing once and choose the set for each video at the video step.

How many portrait photos does a social video need?

Six to ten. One opening frame, usually the exterior or the front door, a handful of the strongest vertical interiors, and a closing frame such as the deck or the view. More than that runs past the point where a feed viewer is still watching.
