Captions for a listing reel: short, specific, one call to action

A reel caption needs three lines: the fact that made you post, one detail the video shows, and one thing to do. Under forty words, hashtags after a break.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. The written caption under a listing reel needs three lines: the fact that made you post (type, town, price if you share it), one detail the video shows, and one thing to do, usually the link in bio to the property page. Keep it under forty words, put hashtags after a line break, and never ask for three actions at once.

The caption under a listing reel needs three lines: the fact that made you post, one detail the video actually shows, and one thing to do. “Just listed: 3-bed bungalow in Ashby. The kitchen window looks straight down the garden. Link in bio for the gallery and Saturday's open house time.” Under forty words. Hashtags after a line break. One call to action, not three.

To be clear about which caption this is: the written text under the post, which you type when you publish. The on-screen captions that show the voiceover word by word are a different thing and are built into the video. This post is about the words beneath it.

Why three lines and not a paragraph

People watching a reel see the first line or two of the caption and a “more” link. Most never tap it. So the caption has to work as two lines and still be fine if someone reads all of it. A paragraph about the home, its history and the neighborhood reads as one grey block under the video and gets skipped. Three short lines, each doing one job, get read.

The video has already done the showing. The caption's job is to anchor what the viewer saw (which house, where, how much) and give them the one next step.

Line one: the fact that made you post

State what the reel is about in words a stranger scrolling past would understand without sound: just listed, open house Sunday, price change, sold. Then the type and the town, and the price if you and the seller are sharing it on social. “Open house Sunday 1 to 3: 4-bed colonial in Kirkwood, $629,000.” That line alone is enough for a lot of viewers.

Line two: one detail the video shows

Pick one thing that is in the video and is specific. Not “gorgeous kitchen” but “the kitchen window looks straight down the garden”. Not “amazing yard” but “the back fence is the town conservation line”. This line is where the caption earns a second read, and it has to match a scene the viewer just watched. If the reel skipped the basement, the caption does not mention the basement.

Line three: one thing to do

One. Not “DM me, comment below, link in bio, call now”. A viewer given four options takes none. Pick the action that gets them to the property page, since that is where the gallery, the video and the enquiry form live. “Link in bio for the full gallery and the open house time.” If the platform lets you put a link in the post, say what it opens.

The link should be the branded property page, so the viewer lands on your name and your form rather than a portal that routes the enquiry elsewhere. The post on sending followers to the property page covers the mechanics.

Six captions for six reels

  • Just listed: “Just listed: 3-bed bungalow in Ashby. The kitchen window looks straight down the garden. Link in bio for the gallery.”
  • Open house: “Open Sunday 1 to 3: 4-bed colonial in Kirkwood. Four bedrooms on one floor. Link in bio for the address and the full tour.”
  • Price change: “New price on the Pratt Street cape: $439,000. The 2019 boiler and the new windows are still the reason to see it. Link in bio.”
  • Sold: “Sold on Quarry Hill Road. Thank you to the sellers for a spotless house and to the buyer's agent for a smooth close. Thinking of selling this spring? The link in bio has what a launch looks like.”
  • One-room spotlight: “This is the primary suite at the Linden Court listing: 14 by 18 with a window on each side. Full gallery in the link in bio.”
  • Neighborhood: “Six minutes on foot from the Ashby listing to Mill Pond Park. The rest of the walk is on the property page, link in bio.”

The caption, rewritten

Before: “Absolutely gorgeous 3 bed 2 bath home in a highly desirable neighborhood close to everything you need. Updated kitchen, spa bath, huge yard, and so much more. DM me for details, comment LINK for the tour, call or text anytime, and don't forget to follow for more listings. #realestate #dreamhome #justlisted #home #house #realtor #luxury #forsale #househunting #property #sold #newlisting”

After: “Just listed: 3-bed on Linden Court, $472,000. The back fence is the town conservation line. Link in bio for the gallery and Saturday's open house time.

#ashbyrealestate #kirkwoodhomes #lindencourt”

What goes wrong in reel captions

  • The paragraph. Nobody taps “more” to read about granite.
  • Four calls to action. Each one halves the chance of any.
  • Hashtags in the body. They break the sentence and read as noise. Put a short local set after a blank line; see hashtags for listing reels.
  • A detail the video did not show. The viewer just watched it; they notice.
  • No disclosure when the reel uses edited photos. If a scene is virtually staged or the sky was replaced, one short line in the caption says so. Check your MLS's current rules on where disclosures must appear, and ask your broker about brokerage policy.
  • Describing the buyer. “Perfect for a young family” describes people, not the house. Describe the house.

Write the caption last

Make the reel, watch it once, and then write the three lines from what you just saw. The first line names the post, the second names one scene, the third names one action. Then add the local hashtags under a blank line and publish.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How long should a listing reel caption be?

Under about forty words in the body, three lines: the fact that made you post, one detail the video shows, and one call to action. Most viewers read only the first line or two before the fold, so the first line has to stand alone. Hashtags go after a line break and do not count.

Should I put the price in the caption?

If you and the seller are comfortable sharing it on social, yes, in the first line, because it is the fact viewers most want. If not, give the type and the town and let the property page carry the price. Check your MLS's current rules on advertising details off-platform.

What is the best call to action for a listing reel?

One that leads to the property page: the link in bio, or the link in the post where the platform allows one. The page has the gallery, the video and the enquiry form. Asking for a DM, a comment and a call in the same caption splits attention and usually gets none of them.

Do I need an AI disclosure in the caption?

If the reel uses photos that were virtually staged or otherwise altered, a short line in the caption saying so is the honest move. Check your MLS's current rules and your broker's policy for exact wording and placement.
