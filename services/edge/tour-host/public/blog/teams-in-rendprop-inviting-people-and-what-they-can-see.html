Teams in Rendprop: inviting people and what they can see

Rendprop teams are built around a shared workspace. Bring in the people who work on listings, keep other work apart, and decide who publishes and replies.

Think in workspaces, not in people

A workspace is where listings live, and a team is the set of people who share one. That gives you a simple way to decide what gets shared: put the listings you want worked on together in the shared workspace, and keep anything else elsewhere.

  • A brokerage team of three agents and a coordinator: one shared workspace for the team's listings. Everyone works on the same listings; the coordinator does the publishing.
  • A photography business with two shooters and an editor: one shared workspace for client listings. Shooters add photos; the editor reviews edits and builds the videos.
  • A solo agent who occasionally hires a photographer: the agent's own workspace, with the photographer brought in only when there is a shoot, or the photographer presenting the agent's details from the photographer's side. The client details post covers that second option.

Setting a team up without regret

  1. Decide the business Apple ID. Sign in with Apple creates the account, and the account that creates the workspace and publishes the listings is the one the pages and enquiries belong to. Use a business Apple ID, not whoever's personal phone was nearest.
  2. Create the workspace and choose its mode in the mode chooser. A workspace for homes is real estate; one for venue clients is event venues.
  3. Bring in the people who will touch listings. Not the whole office. Someone who only needs to see the finished page can have the branded link.
  4. Agree the handoffs in writing: who adds photos, who reviews edits against originals, who builds the video, who publishes, who watches the inbox and replies.
  5. Run one listing through the team before a live one. A past listing is a fine rehearsal.

What to check before you bring someone in

Whether they need to work on listings or only to see results. Whether the listings they would see include anything the client would not want shared, such as a pocket listing or a client's contact details. Whether the person has an iPhone on iOS 16 or later; check the App Store listing for anything beyond that. And whether the workspace is in the right mode for the work they will do.

A worked example: a four-person listing team

Two agents, one transaction coordinator, one part-time photographer. They create a shared workspace in real estate on the team's business account and bring all four in. The photographer shoots a 3-bed on Tuesday morning and adds the photos to a new listing in the shared workspace from the car. The coordinator reviews the edits against the originals in the afternoon, declines a tidy-the-room edit that removed an outlet, keeps two blue sky edits, and builds the landscape video with the listing agent's recorded voiceover. The listing agent types the details from the agreement and publishes Wednesday morning.

Enquiries land in the inbox under that listing; the coordinator sweeps the inbox all together each evening and the listing agent replies. Nobody emailed a zip file. Nobody asked which version was final. The who-does-what post lays out the roles in more detail.

Where team setups go wrong

  • Building the team on a personal account. Everything the team publishes then belongs to one person's Apple ID, and untangling that after they leave is slow. Use a business Apple ID.
  • Bringing in everyone. More people in a workspace is more people who can change a listing. Invite the ones who do the work.
  • Mixing clients in one workspace when they should be apart. If two agents at different brokerages use the same photographer, their listings do not belong in one shared workspace they both work in.
  • No agreed publisher. Two people editing the website step at once is how a page goes live with yesterday's price.
  • No agreed inbox owner. An enquiry everyone can see is an enquiry nobody answers. The inbox post is blunt about this.

What a team member does and does not get

The people in the team work on the listings in the shared workspace. Buyers and sellers are not part of it; they get links. A client whose contact details a photographer presents on a listing is not part of it either; they get enquiries forwarded once their email is verified. For exactly what each team member can see and do, check the current team options in the app and the support page; the specifics may change as teams develop.

Next step: write down who on your team touches a listing and at which step. If the list is longer than four names, the workspace is probably too wide. Then create the workspace on the business account and bring those people in.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What is the difference between a team and a workspace?

The mode is set when you create a listing or a workspace and publishing needs a connected workspace, so a workspace is best thought of as where a set of listings lives and a team as the people who share one. Put listings you want worked on together in a shared workspace and keep anything else elsewhere; check the current team options in the app for the specifics.

Can a buyer or seller be on my team?

They do not need to be. Buyers and sellers open the published page in a browser through the branded link. Team membership is for the people who work on listings.

Whose account should the team be created on?

A business Apple ID that will outlast any one person. Sign in with Apple creates the account, and the account that creates the workspace and publishes the listings is the one the pages and enquiries belong to.

I shoot for several agents. Should they all be in one workspace?

Not if they should not see each other's listings. Use separate workspaces per client, or present each agent's contact details on their listings from your own side and forward enquiries once their email is verified.
