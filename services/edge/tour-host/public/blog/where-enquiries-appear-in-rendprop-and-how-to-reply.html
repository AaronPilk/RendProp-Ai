Where enquiries appear in Rendprop and how to reply

Enquiries from a property page land in the Rendprop inbox, per listing and all together, with the date the visitor asked for. Reply fast with that date.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. When a visitor fills in the enquiry form on a property page, the enquiry lands in the Rendprop inbox, where you can see it under that listing or in the view of all enquiries together. Each one includes the date the visitor wants. Reply from your own phone or email using the details they left, the same day, and lead with that date.

Enquiries land in the inbox in Rendprop, where you can look at them per listing or all together across every listing you have published. Each one comes from the enquiry form on the property page and includes the date the visitor asked for. Replying is you, on your own phone or email, using the details the visitor left, as soon as you can, starting with that date.

How an enquiry gets to you

A buyer opens the branded link, scrolls the gallery, maybe plays the video, and fills in the enquiry form. The unbranded MLS link has no form, so enquiries come through the branded page. The form asks for the date they want. When they send it, the enquiry appears in the inbox under that listing. If you are a photographer presenting a client's contact details on the listing, enquiries can be forwarded to the client once the client's email is verified; the forwarding post covers that setup.

However the visitor reached the page, the enquiry lands in the same place: under the listing it belongs to, in the one inbox.

Two ways to look at the inbox

  • Per listing. Open the listing and look at its enquiries. Useful when you are preparing for one open house and want to know who asked about Saturday.
  • All together. Open the inbox and see enquiries across every listing. Useful at the close of the day when you want to make sure nothing from any listing has gone unanswered.

Use both. Per listing when you are working a property; all together once a day as a sweep.

Replying: a routine that holds up

  1. Read the enquiry. Note the date they asked for and anything else they wrote.
  2. Open the listing and confirm the date works: is there a showing slot, is the open house that day, is the seller away.
  3. Reply from your own phone or email using the details the visitor left. Lead with the date: "Saturday at two works. The address is on the page you opened; I will be at the door."
  4. If the date does not work, offer two alternatives in the same message.
  5. Put the showing in your calendar before you move on. The follow-up post covers the first call that comes after the first reply.

What to check before you hit send

Right listing. With several listings live, confirm the enquiry sits under the property you are about to describe. Right date. Repeat the one they asked for so there is no confusion. Right link. If you include a link in your reply, it is the branded one, so they can forward it with your details attached.

A worked example: three enquiries, one Thursday

An agent has two listings live: a 3-bed in a cul-de-sac with an open house Saturday, and a condo that has been on for three weeks. Thursday evening the inbox, all together, shows three new enquiries. Two are under the 3-bed, both asking for Saturday. One is under the condo, asking for Sunday morning.

She replies to the two Saturday enquiries first with the open house hours and a line that she will look out for them. Then the condo: the seller is away Sunday, so she offers Saturday late afternoon after the open house, or Monday evening. All three replies sent in under ten minutes, from her own phone, using the contact details each visitor left. The Saturday pair show up; one of them makes an offer the following week. The condo enquiry takes Monday and turns out to be an agent with a client, which is also fine.

Where enquiry handling goes wrong

  • Checking only per listing. The listing you are not thinking about is the one with the unanswered enquiry. Do the all-together sweep daily.
  • Replying without the date. The visitor told you when they want to come. Answer that first.
  • Replying from a shared mailbox three agents watch. One person owns the reply. The inbox ownership post explains why.
  • Leaving it until morning. A buyer who filled in a form at 9pm is comparing homes at 9pm. A short reply tonight beats a long one tomorrow.
  • Marking the listing sold and forgetting the open enquiries. Reply to them before you change the status; some of them want your next listing.

What the inbox does and does not do

It collects and organises enquiries so you can see them per listing and all together. It does not call anyone for you. The reply is yours, in your voice, from your own phone or email. That is the right division: the page and the form do the collecting, and the first human contact is you. The leads section describes the form and the inbox.

Next step: open the inbox now, all together, and make sure every enquiry from this week has a reply. Then make the daily sweep a habit at the same time each evening.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Where do enquiries from the property page go?

Into the inbox in Rendprop. You can see them under the listing they came from, or all together across every listing you have published.

What does the enquiry form ask the visitor for?

It asks for the date the visitor wants, along with the details you need to reply. Lead your reply with that date.

How do I reply to an enquiry?

From your own phone or email, using the details the visitor left. Confirm or offer alternatives for the date they asked for, and put the showing in your calendar.

Do enquiries from the MLS link and from my own marketing land in different places?

No. Enquiries land in the inbox under the listing they belong to, however the visitor arrived at the page. Look per listing when you are working one property and all together for the daily sweep.

I shot the listing for an agent. Who gets the enquiry?

If you present the agent's contact details on the listing, enquiries can be forwarded to the agent once their email is verified. Set that up before the page goes live.
