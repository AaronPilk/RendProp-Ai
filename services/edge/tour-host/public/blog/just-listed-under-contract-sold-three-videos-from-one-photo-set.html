Just listed, under contract, sold: three videos, one photo set

One photo shoot gives you three videos: a just-listed tour for buyers, a short under-contract update, and a sold video aimed at the next seller.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. The same photo set makes three videos with three audiences. Just listed is a 25 to 30 second tour for buyers. Under contract is a 10 to 15 second update for the neighbours and your database, with a different lead frame. Sold is a 15 to 20 second result aimed at the next seller on that street, with the ask pointed at a valuation, not a showing. Three scripts, one shoot.

One photo set makes three videos because each stage of a listing has a different audience. Just listed is a 25 to 30 second tour for buyers. Under contract is a 10 to 15 second update for the neighbours and your database, built from a different lead frame and a different line. Sold is a 15 to 20 second result aimed at the next seller on the street, with the ask pointed at them, not at buyers. Three scripts, three photo selections, no new shoot.

Just listed: the tour for buyers

This is the reel you already know. Six to ten portrait photos, lead with the strongest frame, a script of 65 to 80 words that says where the home is, three things a buyer cannot see in a photo, and one ask. The audience is anyone who might buy or knows someone who might.

The recipe is in vertical social video for a listing. The point here is that when you make it, keep the rest of the photo set in mind, because two more videos are coming from it.

Under contract: the update for everyone watching

The audience changes. Buyers are mostly done with this one. The people watching now are the neighbours who saw the sign, the agents who showed it, and your past clients, who are quietly keeping score. The video tells them it moved, and how fast.

  • Photos. Three. A different lead frame from the just-listed reel, so it does not look like a repost. If the reel led with the kitchen, lead with the exterior or the yard. Then one interior, then the front.
  • Script. 30 words. Under contract on Ashbury Court, nine days on the market. Thank you to everyone who came through on Saturday. More coming in this neighbourhood soon.
  • Ask. Soft. Thinking of selling nearby, send a message. Not a hard pitch.
  • Music and voice. Same as the just-listed reel, so the three read as a series.

Keep the facts honest. Days on market is the number you quote, and only if it is a good one. If it sat for 60 days, skip the number and keep the thank-you.

Sold: the result for the next seller

Now the audience is one person: the owner three doors down who has been thinking about selling since the sign went up. The video exists to show that you got this one done and that you are the obvious call.

  • Photos. Three or four. Exterior first, this time, because the neighbour recognises the house. Then the two best interiors.
  • Script. 40 words. Where, the result in plain terms, what you did, and the ask. Sold on Ashbury Court. Listed on a Tuesday, open house Saturday, multiple offers by Monday. The marketing is the same for every home I list. If you are on this street and curious what yours would bring, send me a message.
  • Ask. Pointed at sellers. A valuation, a conversation, a message. The property page link still works, because it now says Sold and shows the marketing.

Check your MLS and brokerage rules on what you may say about a sale price and terms before you include either. Many agents say sold and the days on market and leave the price to the public record.

A worked example: Ashbury Court, start to finish

Tuesday, week one: just-listed reel, kitchen lead, eight photos, 28 seconds, the agent's own voice, light music. Posted with the branded page link.

Thursday, week two: under contract. Exterior lead, yard, kitchen, 12 seconds, same voice, same music. Caption thanks the neighbours and the agents who showed it.

Friday, week six: sold. Exterior at dusk, living room, yard, 18 seconds. Script names the street, the timeline, the process. Caption asks neighbours what theirs is worth. The property page is marked sold, so anyone who taps the link sees the result and the marketing that produced it.

Three videos. One shoot in week one. About thirty minutes of total production across six weeks.

Where the three-video series breaks

  • Same lead frame three times. Followers see the kitchen island and assume it is the old reel. Change the opener each time.
  • An under-contract video that is a second tour. Buyers are gone. Ten seconds, not thirty.
  • A sold video aimed at buyers. There is nothing to buy. Aim it at sellers.
  • Quoting a price or terms your rules do not allow. Check before you say it on camera.
  • A link that still says for sale. If the sold video sends people to a page that says nothing has changed, the video and the page contradict each other.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Do I need new photos for an under contract or sold video?

No. Both come from the original shoot. Change the lead frame so the video does not look like a repost of the just-listed reel, use two or three photos, and write a new short script for the new audience.

Who is the sold video for?

The next seller, usually a neighbour who has been thinking about listing since the sign went up. Lead with the exterior they recognise, state the result in plain terms, say what you did, and point the ask at a valuation or a conversation rather than at buyers.

Should I say the sale price in a sold video?

Check your MLS and brokerage rules first. Many agents say sold and the days on market and leave the price to the public record. If you do include it, be sure the figure is final and that the rules allow it.

How long should an under contract video be?

Ten to fifteen seconds. Three photos and about thirty words that say it moved, thank the people who came through, and hint that more is coming nearby. It is an update, not a tour.
