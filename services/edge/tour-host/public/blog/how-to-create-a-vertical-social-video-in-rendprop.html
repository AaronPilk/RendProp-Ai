How to create a vertical social video in Rendprop

In the video step, choose the vertical video, pick portrait photos, add a short voiceover with captions, review it and export 9:16 for Instagram or TikTok.

Open the listing, go to the video step and choose the vertical video. Pick the portrait photos you want in it, add a short voiceover with word-by-word captions, choose whether you want music, and let Rendprop plan the scene order and camera motion. Watch it, export 9:16, and post it.

The difference from the landscape video is the photos you feed it and how much you say: portrait photos, fewer of them, and a script that gets to the point in the first line.

Portrait photos are the whole trick

A vertical video is 9:16. A landscape photo in that frame shows a strip of the room with the sides cut off. A portrait photo fills it. So the social video is built from portrait photos: the front door shot tall, the kitchen island from the end so the counter runs away from the lens, the staircase, the view out of a tall window, the bath with the freestanding tub. If the photographer shot landscape photos only, you can still make the listing video; ask for portrait photos next time, or shoot six yourself on the walk-through. The orientation post covers which rooms to shoot tall.

The build, start to post

  1. Open the listing and go to the video step.
  2. Choose the vertical video.
  3. Choose the portrait photos you want in it. Six to ten is plenty. Put the one that would stop a thumb first; the hook post explains why that shot and that line matter most.
  4. Add the voice. Record it on the phone or type a script for an AI voice. Keep the script short: the first line says what and where, the rest says why it matters, the last line says what to do.
  5. Read the word-by-word captions through. Most people watch social video with the sound off, and the captions carry the message when the voice cannot.
  6. Decide on music. Optional; if you add it, keep it under the voice.
  7. Leave the scene order and camera motion to Rendprop; it plans both.
  8. Watch it on the phone, held upright, with the sound off and then on.
  9. Export 9:16 and post it on the channel it was made for.

Four checks before the 9:16 export

  • The first two seconds show the best thing about the home, not the front door of a home that is all about the kitchen.
  • The captions are readable against the photos. If a line lands on a bright window, read it twice.
  • Nothing in the captions or voice contradicts the MLS: price, beds, the open house time.
  • Any edited photo you used is one you compared against the original and kept on purpose. Staging is labelled "Virtually staged" and other edits carry an AI-alteration disclosure; check your own MLS rules and the current rules of the channel you are posting to.

A worked example: the condo with the view

A one-bed condo on the 14th floor, nothing remarkable inside, a view that sells it. The agent shoots eight portrait photos on the phone: the view from the sofa, the window wall, the balcony looking down the street, the kitchen from the end of the counter, the bedroom with the same view, the lobby, the building from across the road, the bath. In the video step the agent chooses the vertical video and puts the view first.

Script, read into the phone in the bedroom with the door shut: "Fourteenth floor, facing the park. One bedroom, one bath, and this is what you wake up to. Open Saturday, link in the bio." Twelve seconds. Captions following the words, music off, scene order and motion planned by the app. The agent watches it upright with the sound off, confirms the captions read, exports 9:16, and posts it with the branded link in the profile. The landscape video for the property page is a separate build from the landscape photos and gets the longer script.

Mistakes that make a social video look like a listing video

  • Landscape photos. Bars at the top and bottom, or a crop that loses the room. Use portrait photos.
  • Twenty-five scenes. That is the listing video. The social video is the trailer.
  • A forty-second script. Twelve to twenty seconds of words is usually enough. Say the one thing.
  • Opening on the front door. Unless the front door is the point. Open on the view, the kitchen, the pool.
  • Ignoring the captions. The sound is off for most viewers; read the captions through, because the words have to land on their own.

Where the vertical video lives afterwards

On the social channels it was made for, with the branded link in the profile or the caption. The property page shows the listing video you selected in the website step; the vertical cut is for the feed, the landscape video is for the page and the MLS. FAQ

Questions on this topic

Can I make a vertical video from landscape photos?

You can, but the frame will crop the sides of each room or leave bars. The vertical video is built for portrait photos. Shoot a few tall frames on the walk-through if the photographer delivered landscape photos only.

How long should the social video be?

Short. A script of twelve to twenty seconds and six to ten portrait photos is a good starting point. The landscape video for the page can be longer.

Do I need captions if I have a voiceover?

Yes. Most viewers have the sound off on social. Rendprop's word-by-word captions follow the voice so the message still lands silently.

Is the vertical video the one that goes on the property page?

The property page shows the listing video you select in the website step, usually the landscape video. The vertical video is for social channels. Both are exports from the same listing.
