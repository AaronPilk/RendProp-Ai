Hotel conference rooms: a video for the sales team

Give the hotel sales team one link per meeting room: a short video in two layouts, capacity for each, hours and a form that asks the planner for a date.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A hotel sales team needs one link per meeting room that they can send in the first reply to an RFP: a short video of the room in its two most-booked layouts, the capacity for each layout, the hours, and a form that asks the planner for a date. It replaces the capacity chart PDF and the site visit for the first round, and gets the planner to the second round faster.

Give the hotel sales team one link per meeting room. Each link opens a page with a short video of the room in its two most-booked layouts, the capacity for each layout typed in, the hours the room can be used, and a form that asks the planner for a date. The sales manager sends it in the first reply to an enquiry, before the capacity chart PDF and before anyone offers a site visit.

The planner sees the room, checks the number, and replies with the date. That is the first round of a corporate booking done in one email.

What a planner is doing with the first reply

A corporate planner sourcing a two-day training for 60 people sends the same enquiry to four hotels. She is not reading the brochure. She is checking three things per hotel: does the room take 60 in classroom layout, is it available on her dates, and does it look like somewhere her VP will be happy to stand at the front of. A link that answers all three in a minute beats a PDF attachment and a 'let us know when you would like to visit' every time.

The video: two layouts, one minute

Most meeting rooms get booked in two layouts: classroom and rounds, or theatre and boardroom. Shoot the room set in each, on different days if you have to, and let the video move from one to the other. Add the pre-function space outside the doors, the view from the room if it has one, the AV set-up from the back, and the route from the lobby. Keep it under a minute. The voiceover gives the room name, the capacity in each layout, the ceiling height if it matters for staging, and the natural light. No music, or very low; the sales team will play it on a laptop in a quiet office.

The shot list per room

  1. The room set in layout one, from the entrance.
  2. The room set in layout two, from the same position.
  3. The room from the front looking back, so the planner sees what the speaker sees.
  4. The windows and the view, blinds up.
  5. The AV wall or the screen, lit, with a slide up.
  6. The pre-function or break area outside the doors, set with coffee.
  7. The route from the lobby or the lift, one or two frames.
  8. The loading route, if the room takes exhibitions or staging.

Shoot with every light on and the blinds up, mid-morning for rooms with daylight. Keep AI edits off meeting-room photos; a planner who books from a video showing twenty rounds and arrives to a room that holds fourteen has a problem the hotel pays for. If a tidy edit is used to clear a cable or a stacked chair, keep the disclosure and compare it against the original.

Worked example: a hotel with four meeting rooms and a ballroom

A city hotel has four meeting rooms of 30 to 80 capacity and a ballroom that takes 300. The sales team builds five pages. For the 80-capacity room, the video opens on the pre-function area with coffee set, moves through the doors to the room in classroom for 60, cuts to the same room in rounds for 72, holds on the windows, shows the screen from the back, and ends on the view from the lectern. Fifty seconds, with the sales manager's own voice giving the name, the two capacities and the hours. The page shows capacity typed as classroom 60, rounds 72, theatre 80, the hours including earliest set-up, a gallery, the video on request, the sales manager's contact card and a form that asks for the date. The training planner gets that link in the first reply to her RFP, two hours after sending it. Her response has her dates in it and a request for the ballroom link, because she has a dinner on the second night.

Capacity per layout, stated once

Type the capacity for each layout the room is actually booked in, and nothing else. A planner comparing four hotels wants to put your classroom number in a cell next to the others. A range, or a single maximum, makes her ask, and asking is a day lost. If a layout is possible but rarely booked, leave it for the conversation.

How the sales team uses the links

  • First reply to any enquiry. The room link, the capacity in the email body, and a question about dates.
  • RFP responses. One link per room proposed. The planner's committee can see the rooms without logging in to anything.
  • Site visit confirmation. Send the links again so the planner arrives knowing which rooms to focus on.
  • Post-visit follow-up. The links are the thing she forwards to her boss.

Enquiries that come through the forms land in one inbox, per room and all together, so whoever is on the desk that day sees the planner's date without digging through a shared mailbox.

What goes wrong with meeting-room marketing

  • One video of the ballroom used for every enquiry. The 30-person meeting does not want the ballroom.
  • Capacity charts as a PDF. They do not open well on a phone and the planner is on a phone between meetings.
  • Rooms shot empty, chairs stacked. The planner cannot judge capacity from an empty carpet.
  • Music on the video. It plays in an open-plan office and gets closed.
  • No pre-function shot. Coffee breaks are half the planner's worry.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should a hotel have one video for all its meeting rooms?

No. One page and one video per room, so the sales team sends a 30-person planner the 30-person room and not the ballroom. The ballroom gets its own link.

What layouts should the video show?

The two the room is most often booked in, usually classroom and rounds or theatre and boardroom. Shoot each set, from the same position, and state the capacity for each layout on the page.

Should the meeting room video have music?

No, or very low. A planner plays it on a laptop in an open-plan office, and music gets the tab closed. A plain voiceover with the room name, capacities and hours is what the sales team needs.

When should the sales team send the link?

In the first reply to any enquiry, before the capacity PDF and before offering a site visit. The planner sees the room, checks the number and replies with dates, which moves the booking to the second round in one email.
