Suburban listing marketing: schools, commute and the yard

Suburban buyers decide on the school district as a fact, the commute as a time and the yard as a photo. Give them all three before the kitchen.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Suburban buyers decide on three things that the interior photos cannot show: which school district the home sits in, stated as a fact they can verify; how long the commute takes, stated as a time to a named place; and what the yard is like, shown as a photo good enough to be the second main photo. Interiors still matter, but they are the tiebreaker, not the reason.

Market a suburban listing by answering those three questions early and shooting the yard as seriously as the living room. The interiors are the tiebreaker between two homes that already passed the three tests.

The four-bed on the cul-de-sac

A four-bed, two-and-a-half bath on a quarter acre at the end of a cul-de-sac, twenty-two minutes from the train station in morning traffic and a short walk to a park. The photographer shoots the front at 9am when the sun is on it and the street is quiet, the interiors through the late morning, and the backyard at 4pm from the deck looking out and from the back fence looking at the house. Portrait frames of the walk from the driveway, through the gate, to the patio and the lawn for the social video.

The page names the school district in one line, with no adjectives. The description gives the drive to the station at 7:30am and the walk to the park in minutes, measured, not guessed. The gallery runs front exterior, backyard, kitchen, living room, primary suite, the other bedrooms, baths, garage, the cul-de-sac looking back at the house. The listing video opens on the front, cuts to the yard, then goes inside. The main photo is the front; the yard is second, because a buyer on a phone sees two photos before they decide to keep going.

Schools and commute: facts, not adjectives

Name the district and the assigned schools if your MLS and brokerage allow it, as facts, and leave the judgement to the buyer. Do not describe a school as good, top-rated or sought-after; those words raise fair housing concerns, they go stale, and the buyer will check the ratings themselves anyway. The useful service you provide is accuracy: the correct district, the correct assignment, and a note to verify with the district because boundaries change.

For the commute, give times rather than distances. Eleven miles to downtown means nothing; thirty-five minutes at 8am and twenty at 10am means everything. Measure it on a weekday, give the route and the hour, and name the station, the highway on-ramp and the nearest grocery store with their walk or drive times. A buyer who is comparing three suburbs is comparing exactly these numbers.

What goes wrong in suburban listings

  • The yard is an afterthought. One wide photo from the deck at noon, grass bleached, hose in frame. The yard is the reason many buyers left the city. Mow it, move the trampoline, shoot it at 4pm from two angles.
  • Green lawn edits on a lawn that is not green. If the shoot is in August and the lawn is brown, the buyer will see brown at the showing. Either water it for two weeks before the shoot or leave it honest and lead with the deck. If you do use the edit, keep the disclosure on it.
  • Adjectives about schools and neighbours. Describe the home and the measurable location facts. Leave out who lives nearby and who the home suits.
  • A commute described as easy. Easy for whom, at what hour, to where. Replace it with a time.
  • No photo of the street. Buyers want to see the cul-de-sac, the sidewalks, the neighbouring houses. Shoot it from the end of the street looking back.

The yard as the second main photo

Treat the yard like a room. Clear it of toys, bins and the hose. Shoot from the back door at the height of a standing adult so the depth of the lot reads, and again from the back fence so the house and deck read. If there is a tree, a patio, a vegetable bed or a play area, give each a frame. Late afternoon light from the side shows texture in the grass and shape in the planting; noon flattens it. For the social video, walk the yard in portrait from the gate to the back fence so the viewer feels the size.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How should I mention schools in a suburban listing?

Name the district and the assigned schools as facts, if your MLS and brokerage allow it, with a note to verify with the district. Avoid adjectives like good or top-rated; they raise fair housing concerns, go stale, and the buyer will check ratings themselves.

What is the best way to describe the commute in a listing?

Give a time to a named place at a named hour, measured on a weekday: thirty-five minutes to downtown at 8am, six minutes to the station. Distances in miles tell the buyer very little; times are what they are comparing between suburbs.

How important is the backyard photo for a suburban home?

Very. Many suburban buyers moved out of the city for the yard, so it should be the second photo in the gallery and the second scene in the video. Clear it, shoot it from the back door and from the back fence in late afternoon light, and give features like a patio or a tree their own frame.

Should I use a green lawn edit if the lawn is brown at the shoot?

Prefer watering for two weeks before the shoot or leading with the deck. If you use the edit, keep the disclosure, compare it with the original, and expect the buyer to see the real lawn at the showing.
