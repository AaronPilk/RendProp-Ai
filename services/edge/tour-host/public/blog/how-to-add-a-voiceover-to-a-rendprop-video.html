How to add a voiceover to a Rendprop video

In the video step, record your own voiceover on the iPhone or type a script for an AI voice. Word-by-word captions follow either. How to do both well.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. In the video step you can record a voiceover on the iPhone or type a script and have an AI voice read it. Word-by-word captions follow the words either way, and music is optional. Write the script first, keep it short, record in a quiet room, and watch the video back with the sound on and off before you publish.

In the video step, add the voice one of two ways: record a voiceover on the iPhone, or type a script and let an AI voice read it. Word-by-word captions follow the words in both cases, and music is optional underneath. The choice between your voice and an AI voice is about who the video is for; the work that makes either one good is the script and the quiet room.

Write the script before you open the video step

A voiceover that is improvised over the pictures wanders. A voiceover read from a script fits the video. Write it in a notes app first, in the order the home will appear: exterior, main living space, kitchen, bedrooms, yard, the close. For a landscape video that is 30 to 50 seconds of words. For a vertical social video it is 12 to 20. The script post has a structure you can copy.

Read it out loud once with a timer. If you are rushing, cut a sentence. Cut the adjectives before you cut the facts.

Option one: record your own voice

  1. Find the quietest room in the home or the car with the engine off. Walk-in closets and pantries are good; the clothes soak up echo.
  2. Open the listing, go to the video step, and choose to record the voiceover.
  3. Hold the phone a hand's width from your mouth, slightly to the side so your breath does not hit the microphone.
  4. Read the script at the speed you would talk to a buyer at the door. Slower than feels natural.
  5. Listen back with headphones if you have them. Re-record if there is a dog, a fridge hum, a plane or a stumble.
  6. Continue; the word-by-word captions follow your words.

What to check before you continue

Street name pronounced correctly. Price and open house time match the MLS. No gap longer than a breath. If the recording has a noise in it you could not avoid, record it again; a video gets watched more times than it takes to redo a take.

Option two: type a script for an AI voice

  1. Paste the script into the video step where the AI voice reads it.
  2. Write numbers the way you want them said. "Three bed, two bath" reads cleaner than "3/2". Spell out the street if it is unusual.
  3. Run it and listen to the whole thing.
  4. If a word is mispronounced, change the spelling in the script to match how it should sound, and run it again.
  5. Continue; the captions follow the AI voice word by word.

An AI voice suits a photographer delivering video for an agent who will not record one, a listing you want out the door on a Tuesday night, or a team that wants the same voice across every listing. The voice comparison goes through when each one is the better choice.

A worked example: the agent who hates her own voice

An agent with a 3-bed in a cul-de-sac wants the landscape video published tonight and does not want to record. She writes an 80-word script in the car, pastes it in the video step, picks the AI voice, and listens. The AI says the street, "Beaulieu Drive", as spelled. She changes the script to "Bewley Drive", runs it again, and now it sounds right. Captions following the words, music off. She watches the whole video once with sound and once muted to make sure the captions carry it. Published by nine.

Two weeks later she records her own voice for the "just sold" version, because by then the video is about her as much as the home. Same script structure, same quiet pantry, same captions.

Captions and music: the two settings that decide how it sounds

Word-by-word captions follow the voice. They matter more than they look: most social viewers have the sound off and the captions are how the words reach them. Music is optional. If you add it, it sits under the voice, and the test is whether you can still hear every word with the phone at normal volume. If not, the music is too loud or you do not need it. The music post covers the yes, no and how loud.

Where voiceovers go wrong

  • Recording in the kitchen. Hard surfaces echo. Go to the closet.
  • Reading too fast. You know the home; the viewer does not. Slow down.
  • Describing what is on screen. "This is the kitchen." They can see it is the kitchen. Say what matters about it.
  • Not listening to the AI voice all the way through. One mispronounced street name undoes the rest.
  • Music that fights the voice. Turn it down or leave it out.
  • A script that says things the MLS does not. The video and the listing have to agree.

Where the voice ends up

In the exported video, 16:9 for the landscape video and 9:16 for the vertical one, and on the property page if you select that video in the website step, where it plays on request. The video section describes the options.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Can I record the voiceover with the iPhone's own microphone?

Yes. A quiet room, the phone a hand's width from your mouth and a slower reading speed matter more than the microphone. Play it back before you continue.

What if the AI voice mispronounces a street name?

Change the spelling in the script to match how it should sound and run it again. Spell out numbers and unusual names the way you want them said.

Do the captions follow the AI voice as well as my own?

Yes. Word-by-word captions follow the words whichever voice you use. Read them through before you publish; many viewers watch with the sound off.

Can I add music and a voiceover?

Yes. Music is optional and sits under the voice. If you cannot hear every word at normal volume, lower the music or leave it out.
