Custom AI prompts: guardrails that keep edits inside the room

A custom prompt for a listing photo should name the one change, what to leave alone and what not to add. Patterns that work, prompts that wander.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A good custom prompt for a property photo has three parts: the one change you want, the list of things that must not change, and the things that must not be added. “Remove the loose items from the counter. Keep all fixtures, outlets, cabinets and the window. Do not add any objects.” Vague prompts like “make it look better” are where invented windows come from.

Keep all fixtures, outlets, cabinets, the floor and the window exactly as they are. Do not add any objects.” That prompt stays inside the room; “Make the kitchen look better” does not, and it is where invented pendant lights and second windows come from.

Why the guardrails matter more than the request

An image model fills gaps with whatever is typical. Ask it to tidy a counter and it may also decide a tidy kitchen has a fruit bowl. Ask it to brighten a room and it may add a window because bright rooms have windows. The request is a small part of what the model does; the rest is assumption. Guardrails replace assumption with instruction. The more you tell it to leave alone, the less it has to guess.

The three-part pattern

  1. One change. Name it precisely. Not “clean up” but “remove the towels and the bottles from the edge of the bath.”
  2. Keep list. Everything fixed that the model might be tempted to touch. Walls, floor, ceiling, windows, doors, fixtures, outlets, vents, radiators, cabinets, counters, appliances.
  3. Do-not-add list. Objects, furniture, lights, plants, art, people, text, reflections. Whatever a “nice” version of this room might contain that this room does not.

One change per prompt. If you want the counter cleared and the light warmer, that is two prompts, two versions, two comparisons. Stacking changes in one prompt gives the model more room to wander and you less ability to see which part went wrong.

Prompts that work, by edit type

Decluttering

“Remove the mail, the charger cable and the paper towel roll from the counter. Keep every cabinet, appliance, outlet, the sink, the faucet and the window unchanged. Do not add anything to the counter.”

Staging a vacant room

“Add a three-seat sofa against the left wall, a rectangular rug and one floor lamp. Keep the walls, floor, ceiling, windows, door, outlets and vent exactly as they are. Do not add art, photos, text, plants, people or additional furniture.”

Light

“Brighten the room to a natural daylight level. Do not add or move any windows or light fixtures. Keep all surfaces, colours and objects unchanged.”

Exterior

“Replace the overcast sky with a clear blue sky. Keep the house, roof, trees, driveway, lawn, fence, power lines and neighbouring buildings unchanged. Do not add anything.”

Note the last one keeps the power lines. Removing them is an alteration to the surroundings, and the buyer who stands in the driveway will see them.

Prompts that wander

  • “Make this room look amazing.” No change named, no guardrails. The model will redecorate.
  • “Stage for a young couple.” Describes people, not furniture. Invites cues about who lives there; the fair housing post explains why to avoid this.
  • “Fix the lighting and tidy up.” Two changes, both vague.
  • “Modernise the kitchen.” This is asking for a different kitchen. Do not run it.
  • “Remove the clutter.” Clutter to a model might include the thermostat.

A worked example: the bathroom that lost its towel bar

An agent prompts “tidy this bathroom” on a photo with towels on the bar, bottles on the bath edge and a bathmat on the floor. The result is clean. It also has no towel bar, because the model removed the towels and the thing under them together.

Second attempt: “Remove the towels, the bottles and the bathmat. Keep the towel bar, the mirror, the vanity, the tap, the toilet, the tiles and the window exactly as they are. Do not add anything.” The towel bar stays. The comparison with the original takes five seconds and passes.

The difference between the two prompts is eleven words of keep list. That is the whole technique.

Check the result anyway

Guardrails reduce wandering; they do not remove it. After every prompt, compare the result with the original: count the openings, scan the fixed surfaces, check the materials, look out the window, confirm only the named change happened. The review habit post lays this out. If the edit wandered, tighten the keep list and run again. Do not keep an edit that added something and plan to explain it in a caption.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What should a custom AI prompt for a listing photo include?

Three parts: the one change you want, a list of everything fixed that must not change, and a list of things that must not be added. One change per prompt, so you can see exactly what the edit did when you compare it with the original.

Why do vague prompts cause problems?

Because the model fills gaps with whatever is typical. “Make it look better” lets it add windows, lights, furniture or plants that are not in the room. Naming what to keep and what not to add replaces that guessing with instruction.

Should I combine several changes in one prompt to save time?

No. Two changes in one prompt give the model more room to wander and make it harder to tell which part went wrong. Run one change, compare, then run the next from the version you accepted.

Do good prompts remove the need to check the result?

No. Guardrails reduce wandering but do not remove it. Compare every result with the original: count openings, scan surfaces, check materials, look out the window. If something changed that should not have, tighten the prompt and run again.
