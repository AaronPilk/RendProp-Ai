Listing marketing for investors and landlords

Investors buy facts and condition. Put lease terms, systems and unit count on the page, shoot the mechanical rooms and respect the tenants in place.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Investors and landlords buy facts and condition rather than feeling, so the marketing shifts from mood to documentation: the current lease terms as stated, the unit count, the age of the roof and the systems, the separate meters, the parking. Shoot the mechanical rooms and the exterior as carefully as the kitchens, respect any tenants in place, and skip the edits that would change what an inspector will find.

Investors and landlords buy facts and condition. The feeling that sells a family home (the light in the kitchen, the porch at dusk) is worth almost nothing to a buyer who will not live there, and the facts most listings leave out (lease terms, meters, roof age, parking count) are the whole decision. Market the listing by documenting it: put the current lease terms as the seller states them, the unit count, the systems and their ages, and the exterior condition on the page; shoot the mechanical rooms and the roofline as carefully as the living rooms; respect any tenants in place; and leave out the edits that would change what the inspector finds.

What an investor wants on the page before they call

In roughly this order: the number of units and their layouts, the current rents and lease end dates as stated by the seller, whether utilities are separately metered, the age of the roof, the heating, the water heaters and the electrical, the parking and laundry arrangement, any known deferred maintenance, and the zoning or permitted use with a note to verify. An investor who reads that list and still asks for a date is a serious buyer. An investor who has to ask for the list by email is comparing you unfavourably to a listing that already gave it.

Do not dress the facts up. A sentence that says the roof was replaced in a named year does more work than a paragraph about the charming brick. If the seller cannot document something, say it is unknown rather than guessing; an investor's due diligence will find the truth either way, and the listing that was straight with them is the one they trust at the negotiating table.

A duplex with tenants in place

A side-by-side duplex, two two-bed units, both occupied, separate electric meters, shared water, off-street parking for three. The seller has leases ending in May and September. The agent gives the tenants a week's notice, books the shoot for a weekday morning, and asks each tenant to tidy but not to stage. The photographer shoots the exterior from the street and both side yards, the roofline, the basement with both furnaces and water heaters visible, the panel, the parking, then each unit from the doorway of each room with the tenants' belongings as they are. No tidy-the-room edits; an investor wants to see how the units are actually lived in.

The page lists the units, the lease end dates, the rents as the seller states them, the meters, the ages of the roof and the furnaces, and the parking. The listing video walks the exterior, the basement and one unit. The social video is skipped; this listing is not for a feed, it is for an email to a list of buyers who already own in the area. The branded link goes to that list and the unbranded link goes to the MLS. Enquiries come in with a requested date, and showings are grouped into two slots to minimise disruption to the tenants.

Video that respects the tenants

Occupied units are someone's home. Give notice, shoot at a time the tenants agreed to, and leave their belongings alone in the edit. A video for an investor does not need a voiceover about lifestyle; it needs a calm walk through the exterior, the mechanical spaces and the units in order, with captions naming each unit and its layout. If the tenants would rather not have the interior filmed, photograph it and let the video cover the exterior and the systems. A buyer who will be their landlord next month prefers a seller who treated the tenants well, because it predicts what the tenants will be like to inherit.

What goes wrong with investor listings

  • Selling the feeling. Sunset exteriors, lifestyle voiceover, twilight edits. The investor skips all of it looking for the roof age.
  • Facts missing from the page. If the leases, meters and systems are not there, the first enquiry is a request for them and the second enquiry goes to a listing that had them.
  • Edits that change condition. A tidy-the-room edit that removes a stain, a green lawn over bare dirt, a sky that hides a tired roofline. The inspector finds it, and the buyer wonders what else was improved.
  • Disrespecting tenants. Short notice, showings at all hours, their belongings in the marketing without a word. The buyer who inherits those tenants will hear about it.
  • A social reel for a buyer who is not on social. Spend the effort on the facts list and the email to the buyers who already own nearby.

Next step: before the shoot, build the facts list with the seller (units, leases, meters, roof, systems, parking, known issues) and put it on the page above the description. For this buyer the list is the listing.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What facts should a listing aimed at investors include?

Unit count and layouts, plus the current rents and lease end dates as the seller states them. Whether utilities are separately metered, the ages of the roof, heating, water heaters and electrical, and the parking and laundry arrangements. Any known deferred maintenance, and the permitted use with a note to verify.

Should I use AI photo edits on an investment property listing?

Rarely. An investor is buying condition, and an edit that removes a stain, greens a bare yard or hides a tired roof will be found by the inspector and will make the buyer wonder what else was improved. If you use an edit, keep the disclosure and compare it with the original.

How do I photograph a rental with tenants in place?

Give notice, agree a time, ask them to tidy but not stage, and shoot each room from the doorway with their belongings as they are. If a tenant would rather not have the interior filmed, photograph it and let the video cover the exterior and the mechanical spaces.

Does an investor listing need a social video?

Usually not. The buyer is more likely on an email list of people who already own in the area than on a feed. Spend the effort on the facts list, the mechanical room photos and the email with the branded link.
