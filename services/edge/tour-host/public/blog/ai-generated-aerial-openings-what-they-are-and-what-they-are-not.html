AI-generated aerial openings: what they are and are not

An AI aerial opening is generated footage built from a front exterior photo, not a drone recording. What it shows, what it cannot, and how to label it.

What it is

Think of it as the front exterior photo given a sense of approach. The opening frames of a listing video often want movement toward the home, and a still photo with a push-in only does so much. A generated aerial opening produces a short sequence that reads as a descent or approach toward the front of the house, derived from that single photo. It is a visual device for the first two or three seconds of a video.

Because it is derived from one photo, everything it shows is an extrapolation of that photo. The roofline, the sides of the house, the trees at the edges: the model is inferring them. That inference may be close, or it may be wrong in ways that are obvious to anyone who knows the house. Most listings do not need one.

What it is not

  • Not a drone recording. No aircraft, no camera above the property, no footage of the actual roof or lot.
  • Not a survey. It says nothing about boundaries, setbacks, the condition of the roof or what is behind the house.
  • Not a view. If the home is sold on its view, a generated opening cannot show the view; it has never seen it.
  • Not a substitute for an aerial session. A drone session at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026 runs $150–350 and produces real footage of the real property. If the lot, the roof or the surroundings matter to the sale, book one.

How to describe it

To a seller: “The video opens with a short generated approach to the front of your home, built from the front photo. It is not a drone shot. If you want real aerial footage of the lot, that is a separate booking.”

To a buyer, in the caption or the voiceover: “Opening sequence is AI-generated from a photo.” Short, plain, said once. The post on generated video covers the broader conversation about photo-based video with sellers.

Do not call it a drone shot, a flyover, aerial footage, or “the drone.” Each of those tells the viewer a camera was in the air.

A worked example: the ranch on the corner lot

A single-storey ranch on a corner lot with a detached garage behind the house. The agent generates an aerial opening from the front photo. On review, the generated sequence shows the house continuing to the left where in reality the driveway and the detached garage sit. A buyer who drove past would notice. The agent rejects it, and instead opens the video with a slow push on the front photo, which is honest and looks fine.

The same agent, a month later, lists a colonial on a flat suburban street with nothing unusual on either side. The generated opening is a plausible approach to the front, nothing invented that a buyer would trip over. She keeps it, adds “opening sequence AI-generated from a photo” to the caption, and moves on. Same tool, two different right answers, decided by the review.

When to skip it

  • The lot, the roof, the pool or the surroundings are part of the pitch. Book a drone session instead.
  • The house has an unusual footprint, an attached structure, or a neighbour very close on one side. The extrapolation is likely to be wrong.
  • The front photo itself is weak. The opening inherits the photo's problems.
  • The seller is uneasy about generated content. A push-in on the real photo costs nothing in trust.

The review it needs

Watch it twice. Once for whether it looks good, once for whether it looks true. Check that the roofline matches the house, that the sides look like the sides of this house, and that nothing was added: a chimney, a dormer, a second storey, a tree, a fence. If anything a neighbour would notice is wrong, reject it. The video review post has the full checklist for the rest of the video.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Is an AI aerial opening the same as a drone shot?

No. It is generated footage built from a front exterior photo, not a recording from a camera in the air. It cannot show the roof, lot or surroundings, because no camera saw them. Describe it as generated, and book a drone session if real aerial footage matters.

How should I disclose a generated opening to buyers?

One plain line in the caption or voiceover: “Opening sequence is AI-generated from a photo.” Avoid calling it a drone shot, a flyover or aerial footage.
