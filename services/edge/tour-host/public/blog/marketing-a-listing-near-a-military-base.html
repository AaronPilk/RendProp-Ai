Marketing a listing near a military base

Buyers near a base arrive on orders with a report date and one short trip. The page does the first showing; the drive to the gate is the first fact.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Near a base, many buyers arrive on orders with a report date, shop from their current posting, and make one short house-hunting trip to decide. The listing has to do the first showing before they land, state the drive to the gate at shift-change hours as a measured fact, answer the practical questions a buyer who will move again in a few years asks, and make it easy to book a showing inside the trip. Describe the house and the location, not the buyer.

Near a base, a large share of buyers arrive on orders: a fixed report date, a search run from wherever they are posted now, and one short trip of a few days to choose a house. The listing has to do the first showing before they land, with a video that walks the house in order, a page that states the drive to the gate at the hours that matter and the practical facts a buyer who expects to move again asks about, and an enquiry form that lets them name the days of their trip. Describe the house and the location in facts, and leave the buyer out of the copy.

This buyer cannot drive past on a Sunday and will not come back for a second look, which is why the page carries more of the decision than it would in a market where buyers can wander.

The buyer on orders

This buyer's calendar is fixed by someone else. The report date does not move, the trip is short, and the decision is made under time pressure by people who have done this before and know what they need. They have often lived in several places and have a clear list: the commute to the gate at 6am and at shift change, the schools named as a fact, the yard, the garage, whether the house will be easy to rent or sell when the next orders come. They shop in the evenings from another time zone and they forward listings to a spouse who may be deployed or at the current posting. A video that can be watched on a phone, on mute, in a different country, is not a nice extra for this buyer. It is how the decision gets made.

A buyer with ten days to decide

A family with orders to arrive in six weeks and a four-day house-hunting trip booked in two. They have shortlisted ten homes from a portal. On a Tuesday night they open the tour links. The three-bed on the east side has a video that opens on the street, walks the house, and ends in the fenced yard; a page that gives the drive to the main gate at 6am and the back gate at 4pm, measured; the names of the assigned schools as a fact with a note to verify; the roof and furnace ages; and the fence, the garage and the laundry in the gallery. They fill in the enquiry form with the dates of the trip. The agent replies that morning with a Saturday time and a line about the gate traffic.

On the trip they see six homes in two days. The three-bed is the one they already understood, and the showing confirms it: the yard is the yard in the video, the garage fits the truck, the drive to the gate is what the page said. They offer on Sunday from the hotel, with a closing date that matches the report date, which the page had said the seller could accommodate.

What the page must answer

  • The drive to the gate. Measured, at 6am and at shift change, to the gate they will actually use. Name the gate.
  • Closing flexibility. Whether the seller can close in a window that matches a report date, and when the house can be empty.
  • The practical rooms. Garage with a truck in it for scale, laundry, storage, the yard with the fence visible. Buyers who move often know exactly what fits.
  • Schools as a fact. Named, with a note to verify with the district, no adjectives.
  • Systems and condition. Roof, furnace, water heater, windows, with years. A buyer who may be a landlord in three years reads these first.
  • The neighbourhood for a newcomer. Grocery, pharmacy, the main road, the drive to the airport, in plain facts.

What goes wrong near a base

  • Describing the buyer. Military families welcome, perfect for service members. The phrase describes a buyer rather than the house, raises fair housing questions you do not need, and adds nothing the facts do not. Say the drive to the gate; the buyer will do the rest.
  • Slow replies. The trip has four days and the other nine agents are also replying. A Monday answer to a Tuesday-night form misses the trip entirely.
  • No video, or a video that needs sound. It is being watched on mute in a barracks or on a base in another time zone. Caption it.
  • Vague commute language. Close to base means nothing. Minutes to a named gate at a named hour means everything.
  • Edits that will not survive the one visit. This buyer sees the house once and decides. A lawn that is greener online, a room that is bigger online, costs you the offer. Compare each edit with its original before publishing and keep the disclosure.
  • Ignoring closing dates. A seller who cannot close near the report date is out of the running whatever the house is like. Say what the seller can do.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What do buyers relocating to a base need from a listing?

A captioned video that does the first showing before they land, and the measured drive to the named gate at the hours that matter. The schools as a fact with a verification note, the practical rooms shown in the gallery, and the system ages. A clear statement of closing flexibility against a report date, because that decides whether the house is in the running at all.

Can I say a home is perfect for military families?

Avoid it. The phrase describes a buyer rather than the house, raises fair housing questions you do not need, and adds nothing the facts do not. State the drive to the gate, the garage size and the closing flexibility, and let every buyer decide whether the house suits them.

How quickly should I reply to an enquiry from a buyer on a house-hunting trip?

The same day, with a showing time inside the dates they named. The trip is short and several other agents received the same form; the first reply with a slot is the one that gets on the schedule.

Why do closing dates matter so much for buyers on orders?

Because the report date does not move. A seller who can close in a window that matches it is in the running; one who cannot is out regardless of the house. Say on the page what the seller can accommodate.
