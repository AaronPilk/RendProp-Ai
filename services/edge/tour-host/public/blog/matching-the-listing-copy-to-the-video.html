Matching the listing copy to the video

The description, the voiceover script and the caption should agree on facts and order. Write the description first and cut the script and caption from it.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. The description, the voiceover script and the post caption should agree on the facts and on the order: same price, same room names, same first impression. Write the description first at 150 to 250 words, cut the script from it at 60 to 200 words depending on the video length, and cut the caption from the script at under 40. If the video opens on the kitchen, the description's first line should earn that choice.

The listing description, the voiceover script and the caption under the post should agree on three things: the facts, the names and the order. Same price, same room names, same first impression. The way to get there is to write them in sequence from one source: the description first, at 150 to 250 words; the script cut from the description; the caption cut from the script. When the video opens on the kitchen, the description's first line should be about the kitchen too, or the buyer who meets the home in a reel and then reads the page feels they have opened two different listings.

Mismatches creep in because the three pieces get written at different times by different hands: the remarks on Tuesday, the script on Thursday, the caption on Saturday morning on the phone. Together they disagree about whether the den is a den or a family room, whether the price has a nine in it, and which room is the reason to come.

Three layers from one source

  1. The description, 150 to 250 words. Written from the photos in gallery order, with every claim backed by a photo. This is the source of truth for the other two.
  2. The script, cut from the description. Take the sentences that match the scenes in the video, in scene order, and shorten them for speech: contractions, numbers as said, one idea per scene. Roughly two to three words per second of video, so about 60 to 80 words for a 30-second reel and 180 to 250 for a 90-second landscape video. Nothing in the script that is not in the description.
  3. The caption, cut from the script. The first line of the script becomes the fact that made you post; one scene's line becomes the detail; add the call to action. Under 40 words.

Because each layer is cut from the one above, the facts cannot drift. The only thing that changes is length.

Scene order and paragraph order

The video has an order: the scenes run exterior, living, kitchen, primary, yard, or whichever sequence the photos were given. The description should run in the same order, or close to it, so the buyer who watches then reads moves through the house the same way twice. If the video was built to open on the kitchen because the kitchen is the reason to see the house, the description's first sentence should be about the kitchen. The post on scene order for a listing video covers how to choose; whichever you choose, the text follows.

One name per room

Pick a name for each room and use it everywhere. The room off the kitchen is a den, or a family room, or a sitting room; it is not all three across the remarks, the script and the caption. The same goes for “deck” versus “patio” and “bonus room” versus “loft”. Write the list of names on the source document and check the script and caption against it.

Numbers that are said and numbers that are read

The description says $512,000 and 1,920 square feet. The script says “five twelve” and “nineteen twenty square feet”, because that is how a person says them, and because an AI voice reads what is on the page. Both must be the same number. After a price change, the description, the page details, the script and the caption all need the new figure; the script is the one most often forgotten, and the old price keeps playing in a reel for weeks.

What the video shows that the text should not oversell

If the reel uses a twilight edit as its opening frame, the description does not get to say “evening light bathes the front”. If a scene is virtually staged, the script does not say “imagine coming home to this”, and the caption carries the one-line note. The video is a presentation; the text describes the house. The two agree on the facts and differ only in that the text says plainly which photos were edited.

One listing, three pieces, before and after

Before: the reel opens on the kitchen with a script that says “a chef's kitchen with a 36-inch range opens to the family room”; the MLS remarks begin “Welcome to this lovely colonial on a quiet street” and later mention “an updated kitchen and a den”; the caption says “Gorgeous updated home, DM for details”. Three pieces, three first impressions, two names for one room.

After: the description opens “The kitchen is the reason to see this house: 14 feet of counter, a 36-inch gas range, and a pass-through to the family room.” The script's first line is “The kitchen's the reason to see this one: fourteen feet of counter, a thirty-six-inch gas range, open to the family room.” The caption's first line is “Just listed in Westbrook: the kitchen is the reason to see this one.” One source, one room name, one first impression.

Where the three pieces drift

  • The script written separately. It picks up features that are not in the description, or leaves out the one the description leads with.
  • The description mentions what the video skipped. The pool is in paragraph two and nowhere in the reel. Either add the scene or move the pool down.
  • The caption promises a scene that is not there. “See the finished basement” over a reel with no basement.
  • The sold or price-changed page under a reel that still says just listed. Update the script and caption with the page.
  • Different disclosures. The page notes the staging; the caption does not. One line, everywhere.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Which should I write first, the listing description or the video script?

The description, from the photos in gallery order. Then cut the script from it, keeping only the sentences that match the video's scenes and shortening them for speech. Then cut the caption from the script. Writing them in that order keeps the facts and the names identical across all three.

Does the description have to follow the same order as the video?

As closely as the writing allows. A buyer who watches the reel and then reads the page should move through the house in the same sequence. If the video opens on the kitchen, the description's first sentence should be about the kitchen, or the two feel like different listings.

How do I keep the numbers consistent after a price change?

Change the source description first, then the page details, the script and the caption, in that order, and check each. The script is the one most often missed, and an old price can keep playing in a reel for weeks. If the voiceover was recorded, re-record the line or rebuild the video with the new script.

Should the caption repeat the first line of the script?

The caption's first line should carry the same fact as the script's first line, shortened and with the context a scroller needs: just listed, the town, the one reason. Repeating the idea is the point; it is the same first impression in a third place.
