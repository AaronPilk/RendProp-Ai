Preparing a seller for an open house weekend

Give the seller a one-page plan on Tuesday: what to remove, what to leave on, when to be out, and what buyers have already seen on the page.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Give the seller one page on the Tuesday before: what to remove, what to switch on, what temperature to set, when to be out and for how long, and a reminder that buyers have already seen the photos and the video, so the house on Sunday needs to look like the house on the page. Then walk the house together on Saturday evening.

Prepare a seller for an open house weekend with one page, handed over on Tuesday, and one walk through the house together on Saturday evening. The page says what to remove, what to switch on, what to set the thermostat to, when to leave and when it is safe to come back. The walk is where you check it against the photos, because the buyers coming Sunday have already seen the photos and the video, and the house has to match.

That last point is the one sellers do not expect. They think of the open house as the first look. For most visitors it is the second look; the first was on their phone on Thursday. If the kitchen counter that was clear in the photo is covered with a week of post on Sunday, the visitor notices the gap before they notice the kitchen.

The Willow Street weekend

A family of four in a 4-bed on Willow Street, two children under ten, a dog. Open house Saturday 12 to 2 and Sunday 1 to 3. The photos were taken three weeks earlier, after a weekend of tidying that the house has not seen since. The Tuesday page for them looks like this.

  • Remove by Friday night. The fridge magnets and drawings, the shoe pile by the back door, the dog bed, the bathroom counters down to soap, the stack on the stairs, the bikes from the hall. Everything that was not in the photos.
  • Put away, not just hide. Post, passports, prescriptions, laptops, keys, anything with a name and address on it. A drawer is not away; a locked car boot or a neighbour's house is.
  • Leave on. Every lamp that was on in the photos. Blinds open at the angle in the kitchen photo. Thermostat at a temperature a visitor in a coat will not notice.
  • Out by. 11:15 Saturday, 12:15 Sunday. Back no earlier than 2:30 and 3:30. Take the dog. Park somewhere else so the drive is clear for visitors.
  • The screen. A laptop on the island with the property page open; you will set it up.
  • What buyers have seen. The page link, so they can look at the house the way a buyer will. “Sunday needs to look like this.”

Saturday at 6 p.m. you walk it with them, the page open on your phone, room by room, and fix the three things they forgot. Sunday morning you arrive at 12:15 to an empty house that looks like the gallery.

The conversation about the edited photo

If any photo was virtually staged or had an AI edit, say it again on Tuesday. The seller already agreed to it, but they need to know what will happen on Sunday. “The living room is staged in the photos; it carries a label, and I will tell visitors at the door. The real room will be empty and that is fine.” Sellers who are not warned sometimes rush out and buy furniture, or worse, assume the photo was a promise they have to keep.

The same goes for a decluttering edit. If the tidy-the-room version of the study is on the page, the real study on Sunday needs to be within reach of it. That is a Friday job, and it belongs on the page.

Where the weekend falls apart

  • The seller stays. “I will just sit in the garden.” Visitors talk differently when the owner is in earshot, and they leave sooner. Out means out of the property.
  • Re-cluttering between Saturday and Sunday. Saturday evening the family comes home, eats, lives. Sunday at noon the kitchen is Saturday night's kitchen. The Sunday reset is on the page as its own line.
  • Fresh paint on Saturday morning. The smell says “something is being covered.” Painting is a Tuesday job or not at all.
  • Surprise repairs. The dripping tap the seller meant to fix becomes the thing every visitor mentions. Ask on Tuesday what has broken since the photos.
  • The dog in the utility room. It barks for two hours and nobody opens that door. Dog goes with the seller.
  • Valuables in a drawer. Drawers get opened. Out of the house is the only safe place.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

When should I give the seller the open house instructions?

The Tuesday before, as a single page, so there is time to do the Friday jobs without a panic. Then walk the house together on Saturday evening with the property page open on your phone and fix what was missed. Sunday morning should be a reset, not a clean.

Should the seller be home during the open house?

No, and not in the garden either. Visitors speak more freely and stay longer when the owner is out of the property. Give them a specific time to leave and a specific time to return, and ask them to take pets and park elsewhere so the drive is clear.

What should I tell the seller about virtually staged photos before the open house?

Remind them that the staged room will be empty on Sunday and that this is fine: the photo carries a label on the page and you will mention it at the door. Sellers who are not reminded sometimes think the photo is a promise and try to furnish the room in a weekend.

What is the most common thing sellers forget before an open house?

The Sunday reset. The house is cleaned for Saturday, the family lives in it Saturday night, and Sunday at noon the kitchen shows it. Put the Sunday reset on the page as its own line with its own time, and plan to arrive early enough to catch what was missed.
