Showings for an occupied home: respecting the owner

Fewer, better showings. Buyers arrive having watched the video, the window is tight and honoured, and you lead a route that keeps private corners private.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Showings in an occupied home work when three things hold: the buyer has watched the listing video before they book, so only real candidates ask; the showing windows are fixed and honoured to the minute; and you lead a route through the house that answers the buyer's questions without opening the owner's life. Fewer, better showings beat open access.

Showings in an occupied home work on three rules. Buyers see the video and the gallery before they book, so the people who come are the ones who already like it. Showing windows are fixed, short and honoured to the minute. And you lead the route, so the buyer sees the house and not the owner's life. Fewer, better showings beat open access every time a family is living in the listing.

Sellers agree to list because they want to move. They start resenting the process when a 6 p.m. showing arrives at 6:25, opens the wardrobe, and leaves without a word. The route and the windows are how you keep the seller on your side for the six weeks it takes.

The bungalow on Pine Street

A couple with a newborn in a 2-bed bungalow on Pine Street. They cannot leave the house for hours at a time, and the baby sleeps from 1 to 3. The arrangement:

  • Windows. Tuesday and Thursday 5 to 7, Saturday 10 to 1. Thirty-minute slots. Nothing outside those hours, no exceptions for “my buyer is in town today,” because one exception ends the arrangement.
  • The ask before the booking. Every agent who requests a slot gets the property page link and one line: “please have your buyer watch the video first; it shows the whole house, and the sellers have a newborn, so visits are kept to real candidates.” Most agents respect it. Some buyers drop out after watching, which is the point.
  • The route. Front door, living room, kitchen, back garden through the kitchen door, then the two bedrooms and the bathroom, then out. The nursery is seen from the doorway unless the buyer asks. The study, which is the owner's desk and papers, is seen from the doorway full stop.
  • The sellers' part. They take the baby for a walk or sit in the car. Ten minutes before each slot, the kitchen counter is cleared, the nursery door is ajar, the study door is pulled to.

Over three weeks that produces fewer showings than open access would, and every one of them is a buyer who has already seen the house twice on their phone and wants to confirm it.

The route is the showing

In an occupied house you walk ahead, not behind. Walking ahead lets you open the doors you want opened and stand in the doorways you want seen from. It also lets you answer the question before the buyer looks for the answer in a drawer. “The boiler is in that cupboard, it was replaced two years ago” means nobody opens the cupboard to check.

Say what is off the route and why, once, lightly. “That is the owner's study; same size as the second bedroom; you can see it from here.” Buyers do not mind. They mind being watched, and they mind being surprised.

The video does the first visit

The listing video is the first showing, done without anyone in the house. Sent with the booking link, it shows the whole route in order, and a buyer who watches it and still books has decided the layout works. The ones who do not book after watching were going to leave in four minutes anyway; you have saved the seller a cleared counter and a walk round the block.

If the photos include a tidy-the-room edit, the page shows the disclosure. Tell the agent so the buyer does not expect the study to look like the photo. A labelled edit is a courtesy; an unlabelled one is a problem the seller has to answer for in their own hallway.

Where it breaks

  • Late arrivals. A 6 p.m. slot that starts at 6:20 runs into the next one and into the baby's bath. Confirm the slot with “please text when you are two minutes away,” and start the clock at the booked time.
  • Agents letting themselves in early. The lockbox is for your use during windows. If the seller finds an agent in the kitchen at 4:45, the arrangement is over.
  • Open drawers and cupboards. Usually curiosity, sometimes checking for damp. Answer the question before it is asked and stand near the drawers.
  • The recorded walkthrough that shows the owner's life. Family photos, a prescription on the nightstand, post with the name on it. If you film a walkthrough of an occupied home, clear those first or do not film those rooms.
  • Feedback that embarrasses the owner, passed on raw. “Smelled of dog” goes to the seller as “a couple of buyers noticed the dog; could the bed go in the garage during windows.”
  • Saying yes to one off-window showing. The next request cites it.

Agree the windows this week

Sit down with the seller, fix the windows, write the route, and put the video link into the showing request reply. Then hold the line on the first off-window request, because that is the one that sets the pattern. FAQ

Questions on this topic

How many showing windows should an occupied home have?

Enough that real buyers can get in within a few days, and few enough that the seller can keep the house ready for them. Two weekday evenings and a Saturday morning, in thirty-minute slots, works for most families. Fix them in writing and hold them; one exception becomes the new rule.

Should I walk ahead of or behind buyers in an occupied home?

Ahead. Walking ahead lets you open the doors you want opened, stand in the doorways of rooms that are seen from the threshold, and answer the question before the buyer goes looking in a cupboard. Say once, lightly, what is off the route and why; buyers do not mind as long as they are not surprised.

How does the listing video reduce showings in an occupied home?

Send it with the booking link and ask that the buyer watch it first. It shows the whole house in order, so the people who book after watching have already accepted the layout. The ones who would have left in four minutes drop out before the seller has to clear the counter.

Should I record a walkthrough of an occupied home?

Only if the rooms can be cleared of anything personal: photos, post, medication, documents. If they cannot, use the photo-based video and leave the walkthrough unmade. A walkthrough that shows the owner's life is a privacy problem the seller did not sign up for.
