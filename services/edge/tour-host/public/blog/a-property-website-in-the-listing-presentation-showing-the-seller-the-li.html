A property website in the listing presentation: show the link

Bring a live property page from a past listing to the presentation and hand the seller your phone. A real link beats a slide about marketing every time.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. The strongest marketing moment in a listing presentation is handing the seller your phone with a live property page from a home you already sold. They scroll the gallery, tap the video, see the enquiry form, and understand in thirty seconds what their home will get. A slide that says "custom property website" does not do that.

Open a live property page from a home you have already sold, hand the seller your phone, and let them scroll. That one moment does more for a listing presentation than any slide about your marketing plan: they see the main photo, the price and facts, the gallery, a video that plays when they tap it, and a form where buyers ask for showings. In thirty seconds they understand what their home will get, and they can picture their own address on it.

Sellers do not want to be told you do marketing. Every agent says that. They want to see it, on a phone, the way a buyer would.

Why the phone beats the laptop

Buyers open listing links on phones, from texts and social posts. If you show the page on a laptop or a printout, the seller sees a version buyers will not. On a phone, the seller taps the call button and it dials. They swipe the gallery with a thumb. They notice the video does not blast audio at them. These are the details that make the page feel real rather than demonstrated.

Hand the phone over rather than holding it. A seller who is in control of the scrolling is evaluating; a seller watching you scroll is being pitched.

A worked example: the kitchen table on a Wednesday evening

You are at a kitchen table with a couple selling a 3-bed ranch. You have talked through comps and pricing. Before the marketing section, you say: "Here is what the last ranch I sold, over on Alder Street, looked like to buyers." You hand over your phone with that listing's page open.

The wife scrolls the gallery. The husband taps the video and watches twenty seconds. She scrolls to the form and asks: "So buyers fill this in and it comes to you?" You say yes, with the date they want, and you show the inbox where that listing's enquiries landed. He asks about the link on the sign. You tell them the QR code on the sign rider goes to this exact page. Then you say: "Yours goes live as soon as the photos are in and you have approved the page."

That is the marketing section. It took three minutes and they did the talking.

Which past listing to show

Pick one that resembles the home in front of you: similar price, similar type, ideally the same neighbourhood. A seller of a $400,000 townhouse learns little from a $2 million estate page. If you have nothing close, pick the one with the best photos, and say so: "Different home, same page."

Make sure the page you show is in good shape. The video plays, the gallery is complete, the status is sold and says so. A page with a broken video or an outdated price undoes the whole demonstration.

Presentation mistakes that undo the demo

  • Showing a demo page that is not yours. A seller can tell a stock example from a real listing. Use your own. If you are new and have none, say that honestly and show what their page would look like using a demo, with the caveat stated.
  • Narrating every feature. Let them find things. Answer questions when asked.
  • Promising numbers. Do not say how many views or enquiries the page will produce. You do not know. Say what the page does and let the seller judge.
  • Skipping the unbranded link. Sellers who have sold before often ask about the MLS. Mention that the page has a second, unbranded link for the MLS tour field so the home shows up properly on portals too.
  • Leaving without a next step. End with the sequence: photos first, then the page and video once you have reviewed them, the link to the seller for approval, then everywhere else.

Make it a fixed part of the presentation

Put a line in your listing presentation outline: "Hand over phone with sold listing page." Keep two or three past pages bookmarked on your phone, one per price tier. Update the bookmarks each time you close a listing with a better set of photos. The three minutes this takes are the three minutes sellers remember.

The next step: before your next presentation, pick the sold listing you will show, open its page on your phone, and check the video, the gallery and the sold status. FAQ

Questions on this topic

I have no past listings with a property page. What do I show?

Show a demo page and say plainly that it is a demo. Then describe the sequence for their home: photos, then the page and video once reviewed, and the link to them for approval before anyone else sees it. Honesty about being new lands better than a borrowed example presented as yours.

Should I send the seller the link before the meeting?

You can, but the in-person moment is stronger. If you send it ahead, they look at it alone and form questions; if you hand it over at the table, they ask those questions while you are there to answer them.

What if the seller asks how many enquiries a page gets?

Tell them it depends on the home, the price and the market, and that you do not quote numbers you cannot stand behind. Show them where enquiries land and what the reply looks like instead. Sellers trust a specific process more than a vague statistic.

Should the page I show be branded or unbranded?

Branded. The seller is evaluating you and wants to see your name and contact card on the home. Mention that an unbranded version exists for the MLS, since experienced sellers often ask about portal visibility.
