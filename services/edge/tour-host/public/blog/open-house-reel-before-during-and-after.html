Open house reel: before, during and after

Three short reels for one open house: the invitation from the listing photos, a short clip from the house that morning, and a thank-you with the next step.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. An open house earns three short reels: a before reel built from the listing photos with the date and time in the first line, a during reel made from a short clip or a few frames on the morning itself, and an after reel that thanks people and gives the next step. Each one under thirty seconds, each one with the property page link, none of them showing visitors.

One open house earns three short reels: a before reel built from the listing photos with the date and time in the first line, a during reel made from a short clip or a few frames you shoot in the house that morning, and an after reel that thanks the people who came and tells everyone else what happens next. Each one runs under thirty seconds, each one carries the property page link, and none of them shows a visitor's face.

Three reels, three different jobs

The before reel is an invitation. The during reel is proof that the day is real and the home is open right now. The after reel is a bridge to the next showing for the people who did not come. They share a home and a link, and nothing else: different first lines, different footage, different asks.

A Sunday open house at a two-bed condo

A two-bed, two-bath condo on the sixth floor with a balcony facing the park, open Sunday 12 to 2.

Before: posted Thursday evening

Built from eight portrait photos of the listing. First line of the voiceover: Sunday noon to two, sixth floor, balcony over the park. Then the kitchen, the living room with the balcony door open, the primary bedroom, the bath, the lobby, and the balcony view to close. Word-by-word captions put the date and time on screen as that first line is spoken. The caption repeats the time, gives the parking situation and the building entrance to use, and points to the link in bio. Twenty-five seconds.

During: posted Sunday at 12:30

Shot on your phone in the first half hour. The balcony door open with the park beyond, the light in the living room at noon, the sign in the lobby, the plate of cookies if there is one. Fifteen seconds, three or four clips or frames, your own voice over it: Open until two, come up to six, the balcony is the reason. No visitors in frame. No sign-in sheet. The caption is two lines and the link.

After: posted Sunday evening or Monday morning

Built from three or four photos that were not in the before reel, or one new frame of the balcony at dusk if you stayed. First line: Thanks to everyone who came up today. Then: Private showings this week, Tuesday and Thursday evenings, pick a day at the link. Twenty seconds. The caption says the same thing. The people who watch this one are the people who meant to come and did not, and the ask is written for them.

Three reels, maybe forty minutes of total work across four days, and the home has been in front of followers at the moment they could act on it three separate times without the same video appearing twice.

What each reel must not do

  • Before: posting it the morning of. Thursday or Friday gives people time to plan. Sunday at 10 is a reel for people who were already coming.
  • During: filming visitors. Not their faces, not their backs, not their children, not their cars. The home and the light, and you.
  • During: the sign-in sheet. Names and phone numbers on a clipboard on a reel is a privacy problem you created on purpose.
  • During: silence. A fifteen-second clip of a room with music and no words tells nobody that the home is open now. Say it.
  • After: pretending it was packed. If six people came, do not frame it as a crowd. The people who were there know, and the ones who were not can tell.
  • After: no next step. Thanks for coming is a dead end. Showings this week, pick a day at the link, is a door.
  • All three: a different link in each. One property page, all three reels, through the bio or wherever the platform currently allows.

Stories alongside the reels

The reels are the three posts. The stories are the connective tissue: the reminder frame on Sunday morning with the link, a question frame on Friday, a thank-you frame on Sunday afternoon. The five-frame story sequence runs alongside these three reels without overlapping them, as long as the story frames use photos the reels did not.

How this works in Rendprop

The before reel and the after reel are vertical social videos Rendprop builds from the listing's portrait photos, each with its own script: the scene order and camera motion are planned, the voiceover is recorded by you or read by an AI voice, and word-by-word captions put the date and time on screen as the first line is spoken. Three first lines

For your next open house, write the three first lines now: the invitation with the date and time, the during line that says open until when and why to come, and the after line with the next showing days. Three sentences, and the three reels are mostly planned.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

When should the before reel for an open house go up?

Two or three days ahead, Thursday or Friday for a Sunday open house. It gives people time to plan. A reel posted the morning of reaches only people who were already coming.

Can I film visitors in the during reel?

No. Keep visitors, their children and their cars out of every frame, and never show the sign-in sheet. Film the rooms, the light, the sign in the lobby and yourself.

What should the after reel say if turnout was low?

Thank the people who came without describing the crowd, then give the next step: private showings on specific days and the link to pick one. Honesty about turnout costs nothing; overstating it costs trust.

Do the three reels need different photos?

Yes where possible. The before reel uses the main listing photos, the during reel uses that morning's footage, and the after reel uses photos the before reel did not or a new frame from the end of the day. Repeating the same frames makes the three feel like one.
