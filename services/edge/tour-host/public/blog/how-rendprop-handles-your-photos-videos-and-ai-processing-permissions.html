How Rendprop handles your photos, videos and AI permissions

You own your photos and videos. Rendprop does not use them for marketing or AI training without written permission; optional outside processing asks first.

You own your photos and videos. Rendprop does not use your content for its own marketing or AI training without written permission from you. Optional Kie photo-to-video processing asks for separate permission before any photos are sent; nothing goes to that processor on a tap you did not make.

Your untouched original stays in photo history, and every AI edit is a separate labelled version you can use or decline.

The three rules, in plain words

  1. Ownership stays with you. Photos you add, videos you build, walkthroughs you record. Rendprop hosts the property page; it does not take the content.
  2. No marketing or training use without written permission. Your listing does not become an advert for Rendprop and your photos do not become training material unless you have agreed in writing. Silence is not agreement.
  3. Separate permission before optional outside processing. Optional Kie photo-to-video processing asks for that specific permission before any photos are sent, separately from anything you agreed to at sign-in.

Where your photos go, step by step

  • Adding photos to a listing. Content can be prepared locally before you sign in. The photos are yours, in your listing.
  • AI edits in the photo studio. Blue sky, twilight, green lawn, tidy the room, add furniture, custom prompts. Each result is a separate version with a disclosure; the original is untouched in photo history. You choose which saved versions to use.
  • Optional Kie photo-to-video processing. This is optional, and the app asks for separate permission before any photos are sent. Decline it and nothing is sent.
  • Optional paid AI Aerial opening. It starts from a reviewed original front exterior photo and produces generated footage, not a drone recording. Creation requires eligible paid access, a remaining Aerial allowance, available processing capacity and separate processor permission. Results vary, and you review the result before use. Most listings do not need it.
  • Publishing. The page shows what you selected: main photo, details, gallery, the selected video on request. Publishing needs a connection and a connected workspace.

What to check before you grant a permission

What is being sent (which photos), where it is going (the named processor), and what you get back (a video, a generated opening). If the prompt in the app is asking for something you did not expect, decline and read the support page before you tap again. A permission you can decline is only useful if you actually read it.

A worked example: the photographer's client asks

A photographer delivers a 3-bed in a cul-de-sac with a landscape video and a property page. The agent's broker emails: "Who owns these images, and is the app training on them?" The photographer answers from the facts: the photographer owns the photos and videos, Rendprop does not use the content for its own marketing or AI training without written permission, and the optional Kie photo-to-video prompt was declined on this listing, so no photos were sent for that. The untouched originals are in photo history and the two blue-sky versions carry an AI-alteration disclosure.

The broker asks for the originals as well; the photographer exports them from her own library and sends them. Whole exchange: two emails. The questions-to-ask post is a checklist for the next time a client asks about any app.

What the app does not do with your content

  • It does not put your listing in Rendprop's own marketing without written permission.
  • It does not use your photos or videos to train AI without written permission.
  • It does not send photos for optional Kie photo-to-video processing without asking you first, separately.
  • It does not overwrite your original when you make an edit. The original stays in photo history.

Where people get caught out

  • Tapping through a permission prompt. The prompt is the moment you decide. Read it.
  • Confusing a disclosure with a permission. A disclosure labels an edit for buyers. A permission lets a feature send photos out. Different things.
  • Assuming the Aerial opening is drone footage. It is generated from a reviewed original photo, needs eligible paid access, an Aerial allowance, processing capacity and separate processor permission, and results vary. Review before use and describe it as generated when you share it.
  • Keeping originals only inside the app. You own them; keep a copy in your own library too. The originals post explains why.
  • Publishing an edit you did not compare. Ownership and permission do not check the photo for you. Compare against the original, and check your own MLS rules before posting altered images.

Why the permissions are separate

Because one tick at sign-in should not cover everything an app might ever do with a client's listing. Sign in with Apple creates the account. Outside processing is a different decision with a different prompt, each time a feature needs it. That is the arrangement a photographer can explain to a broker and an agent can explain to a seller, and the photo studio section describes the edits it applies to.

Next step: open your most recent listing and look at which versions carry a disclosure and whether any optional processing prompt was accepted. If you cannot remember, that is the answer to tighten: read the next prompt before you tap it.
