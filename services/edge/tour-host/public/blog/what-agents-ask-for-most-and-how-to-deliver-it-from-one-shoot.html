What agents ask for most, and how to deliver it from one shoot

The six requests agents make after a shoot, in the order they come, and how one well-planned visit answers all of them without a return trip.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Agents ask for the same six things after a shoot: a faster delivery, a video, a vertical version for social, a twilight, somewhere to put it all, and the lead when a buyer enquires. One visit answers all six if you shoot the landscape photos along the path, add a portrait pass, convert one exterior, and deliver a property page with two links and the agent's contact card.

After a shoot, agents ask for six things, in roughly this order: can it be quicker, can there be a video, can there be a vertical one for Instagram, can the front be twilight, where do I put all this, and did anyone enquire. One visit answers all six when you shoot the landscape photos along the path through the home, add a short portrait pass, convert one front exterior, and deliver a property page with the gallery, the video, the agent's contact card and two links. No return trip. No re-export.

The requests are predictable, which means the shoot can be planned for them. Photographers who plan for them stop getting the follow-up email.

Request one: can it be quicker

What the agent means is: I have a launch date. The answer is a sequence they can plan around, not a promise of a clock time. Start the listing before the shoot with the details collected at booking, add photos and run edits in the car, review in the gaps, publish from one sitting. The fast turnarounds post goes through where the time really goes.

Request two: can there be a video

Typical U.S. rates as of August 2026 put a filmed listing video at $250–600, which is why most listings do not get one. A photo-based listing video is built from the stills you already shot, if the set follows the path: exterior, entry, living, kitchen, primary suite, yard, with the doorways between. Shoot that way on every job and the video is an add-on with no extra visit. The adding video post in the related links covers it.

Request three: a vertical one for social

Five portrait frames taken at the end of the shoot. Not crops. The exterior, the best room, the primary bedroom, a feature, an ending. The vertical video is built from those with captions and a short voiceover, and the agent posts it the day the listing goes live.

Request four: can the front be twilight

One conversion on the front exterior, shot in daylight with the interior lights on, compared against the original at the sky, the windows, the structure and the ground, and labelled with its AI-alteration disclosure. Typical U.S. rates as of August 2026 for a bought-in twilight are $5–25 per photo; from the photographer who shot the frame it is a tick box on the booking form. The twilight at scale post has the review order.

Request five: where do I put all this

This is the one that costs agents the most time and the one most photographers do not answer. The answer is a published property page: the main photo, the price, beds, baths and square feet, the gallery, the video that plays when tapped, the agent's contact card and an enquiry form. Two links: the branded page for their email, social and the seller, and the unbranded page for the MLS virtual-tour field. The agent shares it in a minute and stops asking for re-exports. The property website page shows what is on it.

Request six: did anyone enquire

The enquiry form on the branded page asks the visitor for the date they want to see the home. The enquiry lands in the Rendprop inbox and, when the listing presents the agent's contact details and their email is verified, is forwarded to the agent. Set the verification up on the first listing with each client so the lead goes straight to them and not to you.

The one shoot that answers all six

A four-bed on a Thursday, agent launching Saturday:

  1. 09:55, driveway: start the listing, type the details, set the agent's contact card.
  2. 10:00 to 11:10: landscape photos along the path, front exterior with the lights on, doorway transitions.
  3. 11:10 to 11:18: five portrait frames.
  4. 11:25, car: both sets added, twilight conversion run on the front.
  5. 11:50, next driveway: twilight compared with the original, kept; landscape video built; vertical video built; voiceover recorded.
  6. 16:30, desk: both videos watched through, contact card checked on the live page, published, one message to the agent with the branded link, the unbranded link, the 9:16 export and a caption.

Saturday the listing goes live with the unbranded link in the MLS, the reel posted, the page in the listing email. Sunday a buyer enquires through the page; the agent gets it. All six requests answered, none of them asked.

Where single-shoot delivery breaks

  • No path through the home. Thirty angles, no video.
  • No portrait pass. A cropped reel the agent does not post.
  • Lights off for the exterior. A twilight with dead windows.
  • Details missing at booking. A page without a price and a message that waits.
  • Email not verified. A lead in your inbox on a Sunday.
  • Edits not compared. AI can change or invent details. Two minutes per edit against the original, in daylight.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What do agents ask for most after a shoot?

Faster delivery, a video, a vertical social version, a twilight on the front, somewhere to put it all, and the lead when a buyer enquires. One planned visit answers all six.

How do I deliver a video without a second visit?

Shoot the landscape photos along the path through the home with doorway transitions, and the video is built from those stills with planned camera motion and a voiceover. No filming, no second crew.

What should I collect at booking?

The price, beds, baths and square feet, the agent's name, business, mobile and enquiry email, and which add-ons they want. With those, the page publishes complete and the links go out the same day.

Where should the agent put the photos and video?

On a published property page with the gallery, video, details and their contact card, shared through a branded link for their own channels and an unbranded link for the MLS virtual-tour field. Buyers open it in a browser, so nobody needs an app to see it.
