Showings for a vacant home: light, temperature and the video

A vacant home shows cold and dark unless you plan for it: lamps on timers, heat set two hours ahead, blinds at the photo angle, and the video sent ahead.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A vacant home shows cold, dark and small unless you set it up: lamps on timers, the thermostat up two hours before each showing, blinds opened at the angle in the photos, and the listing video sent to the buyer before they arrive so they walk in with the warmth and scale the empty rooms cannot give. If any room was virtually staged, say so before they open the door.

A vacant home shows cold, dark and smaller than it is. The fix is three physical things and one digital one. Lamps on timers so the rooms are lit when the buyer walks in. The thermostat set two hours before each showing so the air is not the first thing they notice. Blinds and curtains opened at the angle in the photos. And the listing video sent to the buyer the day before, so they arrive with the house already warm and furnished in their head.

The empty house has one advantage: nobody has to be out. It can be shown at 7 a.m. and 8 p.m. Use that, and spend the effort you would have spent on scheduling around a family on making the rooms feel like rooms.

Linden Court, Sunday at 2

A vacant 3-bed on Linden Court in November, owners already moved, showing booked for Sunday at 2 p.m. The house faces north; the sun reaches the back rooms at about 3. Here is the setup.

  • Saturday. Three lamps bought for the price of a dinner, placed in the living room, the main bedroom and the hall, on plug-in timers set for 1:30 to 4:30 daily. The overhead lights stay on their switches; lamps make a room, overheads make an office.
  • Sunday 11:30. You stop by, turn the thermostat from its holding temperature to a comfortable one, open every blind, open the kitchen blind to the angle in the photo where the light hits the counter, check the toilet has paper and the kitchen has soap. These three details are the difference between “vacant” and “abandoned.”
  • Sunday 1:45. A text to the buyer's agent: “14 Linden Court at 2. The back garden gets sun from about 3 if you are running late, no problem. The video on the page shows the living room staged so you can see the scale; the room is empty.”
  • Sunday 2. They walk into a lit, warm hall. The living room is empty but they have seen it furnished on the page, and the label told them that was a staging, so there is no gap to explain.

Nothing in that costs more than an afternoon. All of it is undone by a smart thermostat left in eco mode.

Light: the thing buyers blame on the house

A dark room is read as a small room and a sad one. In a vacant house the darkness is the default, because nobody is there to open blinds at nine and switch on lamps at four. Timers fix the lamps. The blinds you have to do yourself or ask the neighbour, and the angle matters: the photo was taken with the blind at a certain tilt, and the buyer who has seen the photo is half-consciously checking for that light.

Showings in winter should be booked for the hours the house has sun, which you know from the shoot. If the kitchen was photographed at 10 a.m. for a reason, say “mornings are best for this one” when the showing is booked.

Temperature and the other senses

Cold air is the first thing a body registers at the door. Warm air is registered as nothing, which is the goal. Set it two hours before; an empty house takes longer to warm than an occupied one because nothing in it holds heat.

Smell is the second thing. A house with no cooking, no people and no open windows for three weeks smells of nothing, which reads as damp even when it is not. Open two windows for twenty minutes before you leave at noon. Not a plug-in scent; that reads as cover.

Sound is the third. An empty house echoes, and the echo makes rooms feel like warehouses. A rug in the living room and a runner in the hall deaden it for very little money, and they can move to the next vacant listing.

The staged photo and the empty room

Virtual staging exists for vacant homes. It is also the thing most likely to cause a problem at the door, because the buyer saw a furnished living room on Thursday and walks into an empty one on Sunday. The answer is not to avoid staging; it is to label it and say it. The label on the page, and one line in the confirmation text. A buyer who was told arrives curious about the scale. A buyer who was not told arrives feeling tricked.

What goes wrong in empty houses

  • Eco mode. The smart thermostat holds at a cold setting and the showing is in coats.
  • Overheads only. Bright, flat, every mark on the wall visible. Lamps.
  • No paper, no soap. Small, and remembered.
  • Timers set for the wrong hours. Lamps on at 8 p.m. in an empty house are a security light, not a welcome.
  • The staged photo with no label and no mention. The first thing the buyer says is “where is the furniture,” and the second thing they think is “what else was changed.”
  • Showing at the house's worst hour. If the front is in shade until 2 and the back is dark by 4, a 4:30 showing in November sells nothing.

Before the next showing

Buy three lamps and three timers and leave them in the car. Set the thermostat rule, write the one-line note about staging into your confirmation text, and book winter showings for the house's sunny hours. FAQ

Questions on this topic

How do I make a vacant house feel warm at a showing?

Set the thermostat to a comfortable temperature two hours before, not at the door; an empty house takes longer to warm because nothing in it holds heat. Put lamps on plug-in timers in the living room, main bedroom and hall. Open two windows for twenty minutes beforehand so the air does not read as stale.

Should a vacant listing use virtual staging?

It usually helps buyers see scale, as long as the staged photos carry their label on the page and you mention it in the showing confirmation. A buyer who was told arrives curious; one who was not told walks into the empty room feeling tricked and starts wondering what else was changed.

What time of day should I show a vacant home in winter?

The hours the house has sun, which you know from the photo shoot. If the kitchen was photographed at 10 a.m. because that is when the light hits the counter, say mornings are best when the showing is booked. A 4:30 showing in November in an empty, north-facing house sells nothing.

What small things do buyers notice in an empty house?

No toilet paper, no soap, an echo in the living room, a plug-in air freshener and a thermostat left in eco mode. Each one reads as neglect. A rug and a hall runner deaden the echo, and the rest is a Saturday errand.
