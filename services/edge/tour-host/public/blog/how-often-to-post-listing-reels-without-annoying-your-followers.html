How often to post listing reels without annoying your followers

Post a listing reel when the listing has news: launch, open house, a status change, sold. Two to four reels per listing, with other content in between.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Post a reel about a listing when there is something new to say about it: launch, the open house, a price or status change, and sold. For most homes that is two to four reels across the life of the listing. Between those moments your account should carry other content, because a feed that is only listings gets skipped.

Post a listing reel when the listing has news, and not otherwise. For most homes that means two to four reels across its whole life: the launch, the open house, a status or price change, and the sale. Between those moments your account needs other content, because followers who see nothing but homes for sale stop watching all of them, including the one you need them to see.

Frequency is a per-listing question, not a per-week question

The advice to post every day comes from accounts whose product is the account. Your product is the home. So the question is not how many reels a week, it is how many reels this listing has earned, and the answer depends on how much happens to it.

A home that lists on Tuesday, holds an open house on Saturday and goes under contract the following Wednesday has earned three reels: launch, open house, under contract. A home that sits for ninety days has earned the launch reel, an open house reel, a price change reel if there is one, and then one more only when something real changes, such as a renovation finishing or a second open house. Sitting is not news.

A month with three active listings

Take an agent with three listings live in the same month: a two-bed condo listed on the 3rd, a four-bed colonial listed on the 10th, and a townhouse that has been on the market since last month.

  • Week one. Condo launch reel. One non-listing post (a short clip answering a buyer question about inspection timelines).
  • Week two. Colonial launch reel. Condo open house reel two days before the Saturday. One non-listing post.
  • Week three. Townhouse price change reel, framed around what the new price buys. Colonial open house reel. One non-listing post.
  • Week four. Condo under contract reel. One non-listing post. Nothing about the colonial or the townhouse unless something happened.

That is seven listing reels in a month across three homes, and a follower sees a different home or a different moment every time. Nobody sees the same colonial three days running.

What fills the gaps

The gaps are where you keep the audience that the listing reels will eventually need. Short answers to real questions you got this week. A walk down a street you know well. A look at what a specific repair cost on a recent sale, with the seller's permission. The neighborhood content that makes your listing reels land better when they arrive.

One non-listing post for every listing reel is a workable ratio to start. Test it against your own account, because your followers are the only data that matters here.

Signs you are posting too much

  • The same home leads three reels in a row. Even with different scripts, followers read it as the same post.
  • Your replies dry up. If the launch reel got six comments and the fourth reel about the same home got none, the audience has told you something.
  • You are re-posting the identical reel. A second cut with a new opening frame and a new first line is a new post. The same file uploaded again is not.
  • You are posting because the seller asked. A seller who wants to see activity is better served by a weekly update from you than by a feed that has trained buyers to scroll past.
  • Every caption starts the same way. If each one opens with the address and the price, the audience has already read it.

The opposite failure is real too. Agents who post the launch reel and then go quiet until the sale have left the open house and the price change unannounced. Both are moments that bring people back. There is a fuller timeline in the posting schedule from launch to sold.

What to do this week

Open your account and look at your last twelve posts. Count how many are listing reels and how many of those are the same home. If the answer is more than half, and the same home more than twice, your next three posts should be about anything other than a listing.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Is one listing reel a week too few?

Not if you only have one listing and nothing has changed. The listing earns a reel when there is news: launch, open house, a status change, sold. Fill the rest of the week with content that is not a home for sale.

Can I post the same listing reel twice?

Post a new cut instead. A different opening photo, a different first line and a shorter script make it a new post. Uploading the same file again trains followers to scroll past the home.

What should I post between listing reels?

Short answers to questions buyers and sellers asked you this week, a street you know well, the neighborhood around a listing, or what a recent repair cost with the seller's permission. One non-listing post per listing reel is a fair starting ratio.

My seller wants to see the home posted every day. What do I tell them?

Show them the plan: launch, open house, status change, sold, plus stories around each. Explain that daily posts about one home teach followers to skip it. Offer a short weekly update to the seller instead.
