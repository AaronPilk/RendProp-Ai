Listing media for FSBO sellers who hire an agent late

When a for-sale-by-owner seller hires you after weeks or months of trying alone, start the media over: new photos, a new video, a new property page and new links. Their phone photos and their listing text carry their phone number, their framing and sometimes their mistakes, and every buyer who already saw the FSBO listing needs a reason to look at the house again. Keep what the seller knows about the home. Replace everything buyers have already seen.

The first conversation: what to keep and what to replace

A FSBO seller who hires an agent late has usually done a lot of work and had a bad time. Start by asking what happened: how many enquiries, how many showings, what buyers said. That information is worth more than their photos. Then say plainly that the listing will be rebuilt from the ground up, and why: the photos buyers saw did not work, the text had their number on it, and the market needs to see a different listing, not the same one with a new name on the sign. Most sellers are relieved. A few are attached to their photos, and the next section is how to handle that.

Why their photos cannot move to the MLS

Three reasons, and you can give all three without insulting anyone. First, buyers who scrolled past those photos for six weeks will scroll past them again under your name; the main photo is the one thing that has to change. Second, phone photos shot at eye level in afternoon light are what made the house look like a rental; a photographer with lamps on and the right hour makes it look like a home. Third, if any of their photos came from a previous listing or a previous agent's photographer, the seller may not own them, and your name cannot go on photos you are not licensed to use. If the seller has a genuinely good photo, the garden in bloom last June, it can go on the page labelled with the month as a supplementary image, with their permission.

The reshoot: what changes with a professional set

Book the photographer for the hour the house looks best, walk the house with the seller the day before, and send the prep list. Ask the seller to put away the FSBO sign and the printed flyers so the photographer's exterior does not show the previous attempt. Shoot landscape photos for the listing video and the page, a few portrait frames for social. If rooms have emptied since the seller started, virtual staging with the label on and the original compared. The new main photo should be a room, not the front, if the front is what buyers saw on the FSBO listing for two months.

Relaunch, not repost

Treat it as a new listing: a new property page with your contact card, the unbranded link in the MLS tour field, a fresh landscape video on the page and a vertical cut for social with a first line that does not mention FSBO. Email your database as if the home were new to the market, because to them it is. Ask the seller to send the branded link to everyone they told about the house when they were selling alone; those people saw the old photos and should see the new ones. The FSBO portal listing comes down the day yours goes up, so there are not two listings with two prices.

A FSBO ranch after ninety days

A 3-bed ranch on a half-acre, FSBO for ninety days with phone photos, a hand-painted sign and a listing text that opened with the seller's cell number. Four showings, no offers. The seller hires you in late April. First conversation: the buyers who came said the house felt dark and the lot was bigger than the photos suggested. You explain the rebuild. The photographer shoots on a bright evening the following Tuesday, lamps on in every room, two frames of the lot from the back corner so the half-acre reads. The kitchen becomes the main photo. Staging in the empty third bedroom, labelled and compared. A thirty-second vertical video opens on the lot. The page goes live Thursday with your contact card, the unbranded link goes in the MLS, the FSBO listing comes down, and the seller texts the branded link to the eleven people who had asked about the house. Seven enquiries in the first week, two from the seller's own list. Nothing the buyers saw was something they had seen before.

Traps

  • Uploading the seller's photos to the MLS to save a week, with their number cropped out. Buyers recognise them; so does the previous photographer, if there was one.
  • Leaving the FSBO listing live on a portal at a different price.
  • Keeping the front exterior as the main photo because that is the convention, when it is the one image everyone has seen.
  • Mentioning FSBO in the new listing's text or video. Buyers read it as a problem house.
  • Skipping the first conversation and losing the only useful thing the seller learned: what buyers said.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Can I use the seller's FSBO photos to launch faster?

No. Buyers who passed on those photos will pass again, phone photos are usually why the house looked like a rental, and the seller may not own photos that came from a previous listing. A reshoot is the first week's work, and it is worth it.

What is actually worth keeping from the FSBO attempt?

What buyers said at showings, the list of people who enquired, and the seller's knowledge of the house. Ask for all three at the first meeting; the showing feedback tells you what the new photos and video should lead with.

Should the new listing mention that it was FSBO?

No. Buyers read it as a problem house. The new listing is a new listing: new main photo, new video, new page, your contact details, and a first line about the home.

What about the seller's FSBO listing on portals?

It comes down the day yours goes up. Two listings for one address at two prices confuse buyers and give their agents a reason to wait.
