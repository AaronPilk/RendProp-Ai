January listings: beating the slow month

January has fewer listings and fewer casual buyers. Launch complete, make the house look warm and lit, and let the page and video do the showing.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. January works for a listing because the people looking are serious and the competition is thin. Launch complete on day one, make every photo warm and lit, use the early dusk for a real twilight exterior, and put the video and property page to work for buyers who are searching from a sofa rather than driving past.

January is a slow month for casual browsers and a good month for a listing, because the buyers who are out looking in the second week of January need to buy and there are fewer homes for them to compare yours with. The job is to launch complete, make the house look warm and lit in every frame, and let the video and the property page carry the first showing for buyers who are searching from the sofa rather than from the car.

Who is looking in January

Relocation buyers with a start date. People whose lease ends in March. Couples who decided over the holidays. Investors doing the year's first round. None of them are browsing; all of them are comparing what is actually available, which in January is not much. A complete listing with a good video stands out more in January than the same listing would in May, simply because the field is thinner.

That changes what you optimise for. Reach matters less; completeness matters more. The buyer who finds you will read everything, so everything should be there.

Complete on day one means this

  1. Every detail typed in, including the ones that answer January questions: heating type, age of the furnace if known, whether the driveway is plowed by the association.
  2. A gallery where every room is lit and warm, exterior at twilight if at all possible.
  3. A listing video on the page so the out-of-town buyer can do a first pass without a flight.
  4. Both links in place the morning it goes live: the unbranded one in the MLS virtual tour field, the branded one in the email to your database.
  5. An enquiry form you have tested, and a plan to answer within the hour, because a January buyer who enquires is a buyer who is ready.

Light is most of the job

January interiors are shot in low, grey light with short days. Schedule the shoot for late morning, open every blind, and turn on every lamp and overhead light before the photographer arrives. Replace the dim bulbs with matching warm ones the week before. A room that reads warm in the photo is a room the buyer wants to be in; a room that reads grey is a room they scroll past.

Outside, January hands you the easiest twilight of the year. Dusk comes before dinner, so the photographer can shoot the lit house at the end of the same visit. Clear the walk and the drive, turn on the porch light, and put lamps in any front room that does not have one. A lit winter twilight is often the best exterior a house will have, and it is real. If the lawn is dormant and there is no snow, the twilight frame hides nothing and flatters everything.

A January week with the condo on Harbor Street

A two-bed condo on the third floor of a mid-rise, sellers moving for work, listing date Thursday, January 8. The shoot was Monday at eleven. Every lamp on, blinds open, the gas fireplace lit. The photographer took landscape photos of each room for the listing video and portrait frames of the living room, the galley kitchen and the balcony view for the social cut. At dusk he shot the building entrance with the lobby lit.

Tuesday the photos were reviewed and one edit tried: a tidy the room pass on the second bedroom, which had been a home office with cables everywhere. The edit removed the cables but also blurred the baseboard heater; it was rejected and the original kept, with the cables tucked by hand and reshot on the sellers' phone later that day. Wednesday the video was built, the page published and checked on a phone. Thursday morning it went live with the unbranded link in the tour field and the branded link in an email to the agent's database.

The first enquiry came Thursday evening from a buyer relocating in March who had watched the video twice and asked for a video call walkthrough before flying in. Two showings on Saturday, both from people who had already seen the page. In May that condo would have been one of a dozen in the building's price range. In January it was one of two.

The January mistakes

Waiting for spring. Sellers hear that spring is the time to list and sit on their hands until April, when they launch alongside everyone else who waited. If the house is ready and the sellers are motivated, January is a good month precisely because others are waiting.

Shooting on a grey afternoon. A three o'clock January shoot runs out of interior light before it finishes the bedrooms. Late morning for interiors, dusk for the exterior.

A cold house for showings. Sellers keeping the thermostat low to save on heating while they are away. A buyer who is cold in the kitchen is not imagining dinner there. Ask for the heat up before every showing.

Treating the slow month as a reason to do less. It is the opposite. Fewer buyers means each one is worth more of your attention, and the page they find should be the most complete one they see that week.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Is January a bad month to list a house?

It is a slow month for browsers and a reasonable month for a listing. The buyers who are active in January tend to have a deadline, and there are fewer competing homes. A complete, well-lit listing with a video stands out more in January than the same listing would in spring.

What time of day should I shoot a January listing?

Late morning for the interiors, when the sun is as high as it gets, with every lamp on and every blind open. Then dusk for the exterior, which in January arrives early enough to shoot at the end of the same visit. A lit winter twilight is usually the best exterior the house will have.

Should I show the dormant lawn or edit it green?

Show it, or shoot at twilight where the lawn is not the subject. A green lawn edit in January describes a condition the buyer will not find for months. If you use one, it carries a disclosure, and the better move is a lit exterior where the lawn does not matter.

How fast should I answer a January enquiry?

As fast as you can, ideally within the hour. January enquiries come from people with a reason to buy soon, often relocating, and a prompt reply with the property page link and an offer of a video call is often the difference between a showing and a lost buyer.
