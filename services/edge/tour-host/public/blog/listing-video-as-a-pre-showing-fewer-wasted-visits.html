Listing video as a pre-showing: fewer wasted visits

A listing video sent before the showing is booked lets the wrong buyers screen themselves out and the right ones arrive with questions. What it must show.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A listing video works as a pre-showing when it is sent before the showing is booked and when it shows the layout honestly, including the things people complain about. Buyers who would have left in four minutes screen themselves out after watching; the ones who still book arrive knowing the house and asking real questions. The video has to be a tour, not a reel.

A listing video, sent before a showing is booked, does the work of a first visit without anyone opening the door. The buyer who would have walked in, seen the narrow kitchen and left in four minutes watches the video and does not book. The buyer who likes the layout books anyway, and arrives having already walked the house in their head. Fewer showings, better showings, a seller whose counter is cleared less often.

This only works if the video is a tour rather than an advertisement. A thirty-second reel with a hook and music sells the idea of the house and filters nobody. A two-to-three-minute video that walks the rooms in order, shows the ceiling height and the street and the small third bedroom, filters everyone who needed filtering.

Hawthorn Drive, a Saturday with eight requests

A 4-bed on Hawthorn Drive, well priced, with one known issue: the fourth bedroom is small and the house backs onto a secondary road. Eight showing requests for the first Saturday.

Each request gets the same reply on Thursday: the property page link and one line, “the video walks the whole house including the fourth bedroom and the back garden; worth two minutes before Saturday.” The video does exactly that. It does not hide the road; one scene is the garden with the fence and the voiceover says “a secondary road behind the fence, quieter in the evening.” The fourth bedroom gets its scene, with the voiceover saying what it is: a single bed and a desk.

By Friday evening, two of the eight have cancelled, politely. One wanted four real bedrooms; one did not want the road. Six come on Saturday. All six know about the road and the small room before they park, so neither is a surprise and neither ends the visit. Two ask for second showings. The seller cleared the house six times instead of eight, and the six were the right six.

Without the video, eight people come, two leave within minutes looking disappointed, and the other six spend the first part of the visit discovering what the video would have told them.

What a pre-showing video has to include

  • The full route, in order. Street, front, entry, main living spaces, kitchen, every bedroom, every bathroom, the garden, the garage. Nothing skipped because it is unglamorous.
  • Scale. Scenes long enough to read the size of a room. The voiceover can say it: “the main bedroom takes a king with room either side.”
  • The thing people complain about. The road, the slope, the small room, the shared drive. In the video, said plainly, with context.
  • The thing people miss. The utility room, the loft hatch, the side access. Buyers ask about these at every showing.
  • A voiceover with facts. Ages of roof, boiler and windows; which way the garden faces; what is walkable. Captions for the people watching on mute.

What to send, and when

Send the branded property page link, not a video file. The page holds the video, the gallery and the details together, and the enquiry form is there if the buyer wants a different time. Send it the moment a showing is requested, before it is confirmed, with the one line about what to watch. Then confirm the time after they have had a chance to look.

For listings with a lot of showing demand, make the video part of the request: “please watch the video on the page before booking; it shows the whole house.” Most agents appreciate it. Their buyers' Saturdays are finite too.

Why some videos filter nothing

  • It is a reel. Three rooms, a hook, music. Beautiful, and useless as a pre-showing.
  • It hides the flaw. The road is cropped out, the small bedroom is missing. The buyer arrives primed, finds the flaw, and the showing is a letdown instead of a confirmation.
  • Music only. No voiceover, no facts. The buyer still has to come to learn the roof's age.
  • Sent as a file. Big, slow, no details beneath it, nowhere to ask for a different time.
  • Sent after booking. The filtering happens before the booking or not at all.
  • A different cut from what is on the page. Send the page; keep one video as the tour so what the buyer watches is what everyone watches.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Does a listing video reduce the number of showings?

It reduces the number of wasted ones. Buyers who watch a full tour and do not like the layout stop before booking, and the buyers who still book already know the house. For that to happen the video has to show every room, including the small one, and be sent before the booking is confirmed.

Should the pre-showing video show the house's flaws?

Yes, plainly and with context. A road behind the fence or a small fourth bedroom shown in the video becomes a known fact rather than a surprise, and the showing turns into a confirmation. A video that hides the flaw produces a disappointed buyer at the door and a shorter visit.

Should I send the video file or the property page link?

The page link. It holds the video, the gallery and the details together, loads quickly on a phone, and has an enquiry form for a different time. A video file is large, carries no details and gives the buyer nowhere to ask.

How long should a pre-showing listing video be?

Two to three minutes, walking every room in order with a voiceover that gives the facts buyers ask for: ages of roof and boiler, which way the garden faces, what is nearby. A thirty-second social reel is a different piece and does not filter anyone.
