Posting schedule for a listing from launch to sold

Five moments earn a post: coming soon if your MLS allows it, launch, the open house, a status or price change, and sold. A 45-day listing mapped out.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A listing earns a post at five moments: coming soon if your MLS's rules allow it, launch day, the open house, any price or status change, and sold. Each moment gets one reel or post and a short story run, and between moments the account says nothing about that home. A 45-day listing ends up with six or seven posts, not forty-five.

A listing earns a post at five moments and no others: coming soon, if your MLS's current rules allow it; launch day; the open house; any price or status change; and sold. Each moment gets one reel or grid post and a short run of stories. Between moments, the account says nothing about that home. Over a 45-day listing that comes to six or seven posts, each one with a reason to exist.

The five moments

Coming soon

Only where your MLS and brokerage permit pre-marketing, and in the form they permit. The rules differ by MLS and by brokerage, so ask your broker and read the current rules before you post a single frame. Where it is allowed, one story frame or one grid post with a detail photo and the week it goes live is enough.

Launch

The reel. Vertical video from the portrait photos, voiceover, word-by-word captions, the price in the first line, the open house date in the caption, the property page link in the bio. This is the post that carries the listing and the one worth getting right.

The open house

A story sequence across the three days before, and one reel or grid post two days out. The five-frame story sequence covers it.

A price or status change

A price reduction gets one post framed around what the new price buys, not around the cut. Under contract gets one story and a change to the bio link. A relaunch after a failed contract gets a fresh reel with a new opening frame.

Sold

One post the week it closes, about the home and the people. See just sold posts that are not cringe.

A 45-day listing, day by day

A three-bed, two-bath ranch with a sunroom, listed on day one, a Tuesday.

  • Day minus 4. Coming soon story frame, allowed in this market: the sunroom window, no address, the words Tuesday, this street. One frame.
  • Day 1, Tuesday. Launch reel. Bio link set to the property page.
  • Day 3, Thursday. Open house announcement story, Saturday 1 to 3.
  • Day 4, Friday. Grid post, the sunroom. Story: one room and a question.
  • Day 5, Saturday. Morning reminder story with the link. Afternoon thank-you story.
  • Days 6 to 17. Nothing about this listing. Other content.
  • Day 18. Second open house, announced with one story two days out and a reminder on the morning. No new reel; the first one still stands.
  • Day 24. Price reduced. One grid post: the kitchen photo, which has not been in the grid yet, and a caption about what the new price compares with on this street. No apology, no slash-through graphic.
  • Days 25 to 33. Nothing.
  • Day 34. Under contract. One story: Under contract on the ranch with the sunroom, thanks to everyone who came through. Bio link moves to the next listing.
  • Day 45. Closed. One grid post about the home and the sellers, with their permission. Property page marked sold.

Seven posts and roughly ten story frames across six and a half weeks. Twenty-nine of the forty-five days had nothing about this home, and that silence is what made the seven posts readable.

The two days that matter most

Launch day and the morning of the first open house. If you only have time to do two things well, do those two: a launch reel with a strong first line and a morning-of story with the link. The rest of the schedule supports them.

Where the schedule breaks

  • Coming soon that breaks the rules. A teaser with the address in a market that forbids pre-marketing is a complaint waiting to be filed. Check first.
  • Daily posts in the gaps. The gaps are the plan. Filling them teaches followers to scroll past the home.
  • A price reduction post that leads with the reduction. Reduced by $15,000 tells buyers the home was overpriced. Now $414,000, which buys a sunroom and a quarter acre on this street, tells them what they get.
  • Silence after launch until sold. The open house and the status changes are the moments that bring people back. Skipping them leaves the launch reel doing all the work.
  • The bio link never moving. Under contract day is the day it changes, before the story goes up.
  • A sold post with the price, unasked. Seller permission and MLS rules first.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Can I post a coming soon teaser before the listing is in the MLS?

Only if your MLS's current rules and your brokerage allow pre-marketing, and only in the form they allow. The rules differ by MLS and by brokerage. Ask your broker before posting.

How should I post a price reduction?

One post that leads with the new price and what it buys compared with other homes on the street. Use a photo that has not been in the grid yet. Do not lead with the size of the cut or use a slash-through graphic.

What do I post when the listing goes under contract?

One story saying so and thanking the people who came through, then move the bio link to the next listing. Save the grid post for the closing.

Is it a problem to go two weeks without posting about an active listing?

No. The gaps between the five moments are what keep the posts readable. Fill the gaps with content that is not about this home, or with your other listings.
