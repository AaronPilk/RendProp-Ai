AI voice or your voice: which to use on a listing video

Use your own voice when you are the face of the listing and post often. Use an AI voice for volume, consistency across agents, or a bad recording room.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Record your own voice when you are the face of your business, the video goes on your own profile, and you can get a quiet room. Use an AI voice when you are producing video for several agents, when you need every listing to sound the same, when the only room available echoes, or when you simply will not post if you have to record. The script is the same either way, and the script matters more than the voice.

Use your own voice when you are the person the buyer will meet, the video is going on your own profile, and you can find a quiet room. Use an AI voice when you are making video for several agents, when you want every listing to sound the same, when your only recording spot echoes, or when the honest truth is that you will not post the video if you have to record it first. The script is identical in both cases, and the script decides more than the voice does.

The case for your own voice

A buyer who watches three of your reels and then meets you at the open house should recognise you. Your voice is the thread. It also carries things a synthetic voice does not: you know which word to lean on, you know the street is pronounced the local way, and you can sound like you have been in the house, because you have.

Your own voice also makes a seller feel marketed. When they hear you on their listing, they hear effort. That is worth something at the next listing appointment, when they tell a neighbour who did the video.

The cost is time and a room. A clean take needs a quiet, soft space and about six minutes. If you post weekly, that is a small habit. The setup is in recording your own voiceover on an iPhone.

The case for an AI voice

A photographer delivering reels to twelve agents cannot record twelve voiceovers in twelve voices, and should not record one in theirs. An AI voice reading each agent's script keeps the delivery consistent and the agent's words intact.

A team with four listings a week has the same problem at a smaller scale. The marketing coordinator writes the scripts, the AI voice reads them, and the videos sound like one brand rather than four people with four microphones.

There is also the plain practical case: a cold, a loud office, a deadline. An AI voice reading a good script beats a hoarse agent reading it in a kitchen, every time.

A decision rule you can apply in ten seconds

  • Is this going on a profile with your face on it, and will you meet the buyers it brings in. Your voice.
  • Are you producing this for someone else, or for more than two agents a week. AI voice.
  • Can you get a quiet, soft room in the next hour. If no, AI voice for this video, your voice next time.
  • Will the video go out today if you have to record it. If no, AI voice, because a posted video beats a perfect one in drafts.

What stays the same

The script. Place line, three things a buyer cannot see in a photo, one ask, 65 to 80 words for a reel. Write it as if you will read it, even if the AI voice reads it, because scripts written to be read aloud sound better from any voice. Short sentences, local spellings, no listing-sheet adjectives. The method is in writing a 30-second voiceover script.

The captions also stay the same. Word-by-word captions carry whichever voice you chose to the people watching muted, which on social is a lot of them.

A worked example: one listing, two reasonable choices

A two-bed condo near the train. The listing agent posts to her own Instagram three times a week and has a quiet car. She records it herself in the parking garage, 24 seconds, second take. Her voice, her listing, her followers.

The photographer who shot the condo also delivers a reel to the agent as part of the package, and one to each of three other agents that day. He uses an AI voice on all four, each reading the script the agent sent back. Four reels, four scripts, one consistent sound, delivered before dinner.

Both choices are right. They are answering different questions.

Where each choice goes wrong

  • Your voice, bad room. Echo and a fridge hum make the home sound cheap. If the room is bad, switch to AI for that video.
  • AI voice, listing-sheet script. A synthetic voice reading spacious and must-see sounds like an advert. Write for a person and the AI voice sounds like one.
  • Switching every video. If your profile alternates between your voice and an AI voice at random, nobody learns your sound. Pick a default and keep exceptions rare.
  • Pretending the AI voice is you. It is not necessary and it ages badly. Nobody minds a narrated reel; people mind being misled.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Do buyers mind an AI voice on a listing video?

Most do not notice if the script is written for a person and the facts are right. What people react to is a script full of listing-sheet adjectives, and that sounds bad in any voice. Do not present an AI voice as your own voice.

Should a photographer record voiceovers for agents' reels?

Usually not. The reel is the agent's marketing and should carry the agent's words. Have the agent send a short script and let an AI voice read it, so every delivery is consistent and no photographer's voice ends up on twelve brands.

Is my own voice worth the extra time?

If you post to your own profile and meet the buyers your reels bring in, yes. Recognition is the point of being on camera or on audio. If you are recording once a quarter and dreading it, an AI voice that gets the video posted is the better outcome.

Does the script change for an AI voice?

No. Write it as if you will read it: short sentences, local spellings, no clauses that need commas to make sense. Scripts written to be spoken sound better from any voice, including a synthetic one.
