9:16 or 16:9: the format for Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, MLS

Use 9:16 for Instagram Reels, TikTok and Shorts, 16:9 for YouTube and the property page, and send the MLS a link to the page rather than a video file.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Post 9:16 vertical video on Instagram Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts. Use 16:9 horizontal video for a regular YouTube upload and for the property website. For the MLS, do not upload a video file at all: put the unbranded property page link in the virtual tour field and let the 16:9 video play there. Build both formats from photos shot in the matching orientation rather than cropping one into the other.

Vertical 9:16 is the format for Instagram Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts. Horizontal 16:9 is the format for a standard YouTube upload and for the video on the property website. For the MLS, the answer is neither file: the virtual tour field is built for a link, so you point it at the unbranded property page and the 16:9 video plays there. The practical rule is to make both, from photos shot in the matching orientation, and send each one where it belongs.

What the two ratios are for

9:16 fills a phone held upright. It is the shape of a reel, a TikTok, a Short and a story. The viewer is thumbing through a feed and your video takes the whole screen or none of it.

16:9 fills a laptop, a TV, a tablet turned sideways, and the video player on a web page. It is the shape of a tour someone chose to watch rather than scrolled into.

The content changes with the shape. A 9:16 listing video is short, leads with the strongest frame, and sells one or two ideas. A 16:9 listing video is longer, walks the home in order, and carries the full script.

Channel by channel

  • Instagram. Reels are 9:16. Post the vertical video natively and put the property page link wherever Instagram currently lets you place one. Details in posting a listing video on Instagram.
  • TikTok. 9:16, short, and the first three seconds do the work. A 16:9 video posted here sits as a thin strip with black bars above and below.
  • YouTube. Both. A 16:9 upload becomes a searchable listing video with a title and description. A 9:16 upload becomes a Short. They are different audiences and the same photos feed both.
  • The property website. 16:9. The page is a tour the buyer chose to open, and a horizontal video shows rooms at their full width.
  • The MLS. A link, not a file. Put the unbranded property page link in the virtual tour field, check your own MLS rules on what the page may contain, and let the page carry the video.
  • Text message and email. A link again. The preview card from the property page does more than an attached video file that may not play.

Why you want both and not one cropped

Cropping a 16:9 video to 9:16 removes the left and right thirds of every frame. In a kitchen that means the island or the window. In a living room it means the width that made the room feel large. Cropping a 9:16 video to 16:9 is worse: you get a narrow column with blurred or black sides.

The fix is upstream. Shoot the five or six rooms that sell the home in both orientations on the day, and build each video from the frames made for it. That takes a photographer ten extra minutes and saves you a video that looks wrong on half the channels.

A worked example: the townhouse with the roof deck

A three-storey townhouse, the roof deck is the feature. The photographer delivers 26 landscape photos and, on request, seven portrait frames: roof deck at sunset, kitchen, living room, main bedroom, the stair, the front door, the street.

The 16:9 listing video uses 13 landscape photos in walking order, a 70-second script, and goes on the property page and YouTube. The 9:16 reel opens on the portrait roof deck frame, uses six photos, carries a 22-second version of the script, and goes to Instagram, TikTok and Shorts. The MLS virtual tour field gets the unbranded page link. One shoot, three channels, no crops.

Format mistakes that cost views

  • Posting the horizontal listing video as a reel. It plays small with bars and people scroll past.
  • Posting the vertical reel on the property page. On a laptop it is a tall sliver in the middle of a wide player.
  • Uploading a video file to an MLS field that expects a link, or linking the branded page where the rules ask for unbranded.
  • Letting the same 70-second script run on the 9:16 version. The vertical cut needs its own shorter script.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Can I upload a video file directly to the MLS?

The virtual tour field is built for a link rather than a file, and boards differ on what that link may carry. Check your own MLS's current rules, then use the unbranded property page link and let the 16:9 video play on the page.

Which format should go on the property website?

16:9. The page is opened on purpose, often on a laptop or tablet, and a horizontal video shows each room at full width. Save the 9:16 version for feeds.

Is it fine to crop one video into the other ratio?

It works in an emergency but it costs you the sides of every room or leaves black bars. The better fix is to shoot the key rooms in both orientations and build each video from the matching frames.

Should the vertical and horizontal videos use the same script?

Use the same facts and a different length. The 16:9 tour can carry a 60 to 90 second script. The 9:16 reel needs a 20 to 30 second cut that leads with the strongest point.
