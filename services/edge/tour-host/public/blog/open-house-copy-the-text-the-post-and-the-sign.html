Open house copy: the text, the post and the sign

Three pieces, three lengths: a text under thirty words, a post with the day and time first, and a sign readable from a moving car. All point at one page.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Open house copy is three pieces at three lengths. The text: day, time, address, link, under thirty words. The post: the day and time in the first line, one reason to come, the link. The sign: the words readable from a car at thirty miles an hour, which is OPEN, the day, the time and an arrow. All three carry the same details and point at the same property page.

Open house copy is three pieces at three lengths, and each has one job. The text message: day, time, address and link in under thirty words. The post: the day and time in the first line, one reason to come, and the link. The sign: only the words a driver can read at thirty miles an hour, which is OPEN, the day, the hours and an arrow. All three must agree on the details and point at the same property page.

The pieces fail in different ways. The text runs long and gets skimmed. The post buries the time in the third sentence, below the fold. The sign tries to carry the address, the price and a tagline, and a driver reads none of it. Writing each for the way it is read fixes all three.

The text: thirty words, one tap

The text goes to the buyers you have spoken to and the segment of your database near the home. It is read on a lock screen in a glance, so the day and time lead and the link ends it.

“Open house Saturday 1 to 3 at the Alder Street 3-bed, $478,000. Gallery and video here: [branded link]. Reply if you want a time before then.”

Twenty-seven words. Check the link preview on your own phone first; if it shows the main photo, the text does not have to carry more. The last sentence gives the people who cannot make Saturday a way to ask for something else, which is often the better lead.

The post: day and time first

Feeds truncate. If the first line of the post does not say when, the post is a photo of a house with a “more” link. Put the day and the hours in the first six words, then one reason to come that the photo cannot show, then the link or where to find it.

“Open Saturday 1 to 3: the Alder Street 3-bed. Come see the deck that looks over the creek. Address and full tour in the link in bio.”

One reason, not five. The reason should be something a visitor can only confirm by coming: the view from the deck, the size of the garage, the quiet. If the post has a video, the first line still says when. A reel made for the open house is covered in open house announcement video from the photos you already have.

The sign: four words and an arrow

Directional signs are read by a driver who has about two seconds. That is four words. OPEN, the day, the hours, the arrow. “OPEN SAT 1–3” and an arrow is the whole sign. No address (the arrow replaces it), no price, no tagline, no logo larger than the word OPEN. Save the QR code for the yard sign at the house, where a visitor is standing still; see directional signs, QR codes and the property page.

The yard sign at the house can carry more: the hours, a QR code to the property page, and your name. Even there, large type and few words win. Someone walking up should see the hours from the sidewalk.

A before and after: the post

Before: “So excited to share this one. Beautiful 3 bedroom home on Alder Street with an updated kitchen, hardwood floors throughout, finished basement and a huge yard. We will be holding it open this weekend so come by and check it out. DM me for the address and details.”

After: “Open Saturday 1 to 3: the Alder Street 3-bed, $478,000. Come see the deck that looks over the creek. Address and full tour in the link in bio.”

The first version never says the day or the hours. The second says them in six words.

The Monday text

The fourth piece is the one most agents skip. The morning after, a text to everyone who came: “Thanks for coming Saturday. The gallery and video are here if you want a second look: [branded link]. Showings are open this week; tell me a day that works.” Short, useful, and it sends people back to the page where the enquiry form is.

Where open house copy goes wrong

  • The date without the day. “Open 11/14” makes the reader do math. “Open Saturday” does not.
  • “This weekend” with no hours. Buyers plan around hours.
  • Details that disagree. The post says 1 to 3, the sign says 12 to 2 because the time changed and only one got updated. Keep a single note with the current details and write every piece from it.
  • The sign with a paragraph. Nothing on a directional sign beyond four words gets read.
  • Different links in different places. One page, one link, everywhere. The post on one link for the flyer, the sign, the MLS and the post explains why.
  • Describing who should come. “Great for first-time buyers” describes people rather than the property. Say what the house is; the reader decides whether to come.

How this works in Rendprop

Every piece above points at the listing's branded property page, which shows the details you typed, the story section, the gallery, the listing video and your contact card, with an enquiry form that asks the visitor for the date they want. That form is what catches the people who cannot make Saturday: they pick a different day and the enquiry lands in the app's inbox. Rendprop does not write the text, the post or the sign; it gives all three the same link to point at.

Write the note first

Before you write any of the three pieces, make one note with the day, the hours, the address, the price and the branded link. Write the text, the post and the sign from that note, in that order, and update the note first if anything changes.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What should an open house text message say?

The day, the hours, the home in a few words, and the link to the property page, in under thirty words. End with a line inviting a reply from people who cannot make it. Check the link preview on your own phone first, so the text itself can stay short.

Where should the open house time go in a social post?

In the first line, within the first six words, because feeds cut posts off and many readers never tap to expand. Day and hours first, one reason to come second, the link or where to find it third.

What should a directional open house sign say?

OPEN, the day, the hours and an arrow. A driver has about two seconds, which is four words. Leave the address, the price, the tagline and the QR code for the yard sign at the house, where a visitor is standing still. Check local sign rules with your broker or municipality.

Should the open house post include the address?

Many agents put the address on the property page and the yard sign rather than in the post, and send readers to the link. It keeps the post short and keeps the page as the single place with the full details. Ask your broker about any policy on publishing addresses, and check your MLS's current rules on advertising.
