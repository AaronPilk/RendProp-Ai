How to review a batch of AI photo edits in ten minutes

Sort first, compare second, label last. Two minutes to sort, six to count the ones that need a look, two to confirm disclosures. An 18-edit example.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Sort first, compare second, label last. Two minutes to sort the batch into obvious keeps, obvious rejects and the ones that need a look. Six minutes to run a fixed count on those. Two minutes to confirm every kept edit carries the right disclosure and the originals are where they should be.

Sort first, compare second, label last. Two minutes to sort the batch into obvious keeps, obvious rejects and the ones that need a look. Six minutes to run a fixed count on the ones that need a look. Two minutes to confirm every kept edit carries the right disclosure and that the originals are where they should be. Twenty edits fits in ten minutes once the routine is set, and the routine is what stops a missing outlet reaching the MLS.

Minute 0 to 2: the sort

Open the edits at thumbnail size, side by side with their originals if the tool allows it. Three piles.

  • Obvious keeps. A sky swap where the house and the shadows match. A lawn where only the colour changed. You will still glance at these in the last two minutes, but they do not need the count.
  • Obvious rejects. A twilight with lit windows on a wall that has none. A staging with a sofa through the fireplace. A tidy edit that emptied the room. Bin them now; do not spend compare time on a frame you can see is wrong.
  • Needs a look. Everything else. Usually a third to a half of the batch.

Minute 2 to 8: the count

For each frame in the needs-a-look pile, at full size, edit next to original, run the same count in the same order every time: windows and doors, outlets and switches, fixtures, flooring and counters, text, edges, reflections. Say the numbers under your breath if it helps. Six before, six after. Three outlets, three outlets. Fan, fan.

At about thirty seconds a frame, that is twelve frames in six minutes. If the pile is bigger than twelve, the batch needs two sessions, not a faster count.

Any frame that fails the count goes to one of two places: the reject pile, or a note that says what to change in the prompt for a second run. Do not try to fix it in the ten minutes. Note it and move on.

Minute 8 to 10: labels and originals

  1. Every kept staging edit is labelled Virtually staged.
  2. Every other kept edit carries an AI-alteration disclosure.
  3. The original of every kept edit is saved and findable.
  4. Whatever remarks or caption wording your board's rules call for is ready to paste.
  5. Nothing in the keep pile is a frame you only glanced at in the sort.

A worked example: Sunday night, 18 edits, Monday launch

A photographer has three listings launching Monday and 18 edits across them: four skies, three twilights, six tidy-the-room, five staged interiors.

Sort: three skies and two tidies are obvious keeps. One twilight is an obvious reject, every window lit. One staging is an obvious reject, the sofa is bigger than the wall. Eleven need a look.

Count: two of the tidies removed an outlet; noted for a re-run with a keep list. One staging swapped the ceiling fan for a flush light; rejected. One sky has the bright side wrong; rejected, original used. Seven pass.

Labels: twelve keeps, four of them staged and labelled as such, eight with the alteration disclosure. Originals confirmed. Two re-runs queued for the morning. Eleven minutes, and the three listings launch with edits that have been checked rather than admired.

What makes the ten minutes slip

Fixing during review. Rewriting a prompt mid-count breaks the rhythm and the other frames wait. Note it, finish the batch, then fix.

Reviewing at thumbnail size. Thumbnails are for sorting. The count needs full size or you will pass an edit that changed the grout.

Skipping the obvious keeps entirely. They get a glance in the last two minutes. A sky swap that looked fine at thumbnail can have chewed the tree line.

Mixing batches. One listing at a time. Three listings' edits in one pile means you lose track of which original goes with which edit.

Set a timer the first few times

Ten minutes on the clock, one listing's edits, the count in the same order every time. After five or six batches the routine runs without the timer. FAQ

Questions on this topic

How many AI photo edits can I review properly in ten minutes?

About twenty, if a third to a half of them need the full count and the rest are obvious keeps or rejects at thumbnail size. At thirty seconds per counted frame, twelve counts fit in six minutes. A bigger batch needs two sessions.

What should I do with an edit that fails the count?

Reject it or write a one-line note on what the next prompt should say to keep, then move on. Do not rewrite the prompt during the review. Fixing mid-batch breaks the rhythm and the other frames wait.

Do obvious keeps need any review at all?

A glance at full size in the last two minutes. A sky swap that looked fine at thumbnail can have chewed the tree line or lit the wrong side. The glance takes five seconds and catches the ones that would otherwise get through.

Does Rendprop label edits automatically?

Yes. Staging from the add furniture edit is labelled Virtually staged and other AI edits carry an AI-alteration disclosure, with the original kept in the photo history. Your MLS remarks wording is still yours to prepare, so keep it ready to paste.
