Can I use the same photos for Instagram and the MLS

Yes, the same shoot serves both. The MLS wants landscape photos; Instagram favours vertical. Ask for a few portrait frames and carry every AI label across.

Yes. One shoot can serve both, and it should. The MLS gallery and the listing video want landscape photos; Instagram and TikTok favour vertical, so the only real adjustment is asking the photographer for a handful of portrait frames of the key rooms during the same visit. Two other things carry across unchanged: the AI disclosures on any edited photo, and the usage terms in your photographer agreement.

Why the orientation matters more than the platform

A landscape photo cropped to 9:16 loses two thirds of the frame. The kitchen that read as open and bright becomes a slice of cabinet and a window. That is why vertical listing content built from landscape photos looks cramped, and why the fix is upstream: shoot the portrait frames on the day.

You do not need many. Five to eight portrait photos cover the front exterior, the kitchen, the main living space, the primary bedroom and whatever the home's one unusual feature is. The landscape photos stay the main deliverable; the portrait set exists for the vertical video and the Stories.

The MLS side is the simpler half. Landscape photos, in the order a buyer would walk the home, within whatever photo limit your MLS's current rules set. Nothing about that set changes because you also post on Instagram; the social set is a small addition shot in the same light, on the same day, by the same person.

A worked example: one shoot, two channels

A photographer shoots a three-bed on a Wednesday: 26 landscape photos and 7 portrait photos. The agent gets both sets that evening.

The 26 landscape photos go to the MLS in the order a buyer would walk the house, and 14 of them become the landscape video that sits on the property page and in the MLS virtual-tour field as an unbranded link. The 7 portrait photos become a 30-second vertical reel for Instagram and TikTok with a voiceover and captions. The main exterior photo is the same image in both places. The twilight edit of the front, applied once, carries its AI-alteration note in the MLS and a one-line disclosure in the reel's caption.

Nothing was shot twice. The difference between the channels was decided on the day, in the photographer's framing, not afterwards in a crop tool.

Where reuse goes wrong

  • Cropping a landscape photo to vertical. It cuts the room in half. Use portrait photos for vertical video and keep landscape photos in landscape video.
  • Dropping the disclosure on social. A virtually staged photo labelled in the MLS and posted bare on Instagram is the same photo with half the honesty. The label travels with the image.
  • Posting before the listing is live. Depending on your MLS, marketing a property publicly before it is entered can breach the rules on pre-marketing. Check your MLS's current rules on timing.
  • Ignoring the photographer's licence. Some agreements cover the MLS and the agent's own marketing; some are silent on social. Read the invoice, and ask if it is unclear.
  • Different edits on different channels. A green lawn on Instagram and a brown one in the MLS invites the question of which is true. Pick the versions once and use them everywhere.
  • Heavy filters on social. The warm, high-contrast look that performs on a feed makes the home look different from the MLS photos and from the showing. Keep the colour consistent.

Add one line to your photographer booking: “plus six portrait frames of the front, kitchen, living room and primary bedroom for social.” That line turns one shoot into both channels without a crop in sight.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Do I need separate photos for Instagram?

Not a separate shoot, but a few portrait-orientation frames shot during the same visit. Vertical video and Stories built from landscape photos end up cropped to a sliver of the room; portrait photos of the key rooms fix that.

Do I need to disclose AI edits on Instagram as well as the MLS?

Treat the disclosure as attached to the photo rather than to the channel. If a photo is virtually staged or AI-altered in the MLS, say so in the caption when you post it on social. The label should survive a screenshot.

Can I post the listing photos on social before the MLS listing is live?

That depends on your MLS's current rules on pre-marketing, which vary and change. Check them and your brokerage policy before posting anything about a property that has not been entered.

Does my photographer's licence cover social media?

Only if the agreement says so. Many cover the MLS and the agent's marketing for that listing; some are silent on social or on video. Read the invoice or contract and ask in writing if it is unclear.
