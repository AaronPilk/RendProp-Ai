What is a just sold post

A just sold post announces a closed sale on social or by email, credits the people involved and reuses the listing media as proof for future sellers.

October 28, 2026 · 3 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A just sold post is a social post, reel or email you publish after closing to announce that a home has sold. It reuses the listing's photos or video, credits the people involved, and points to the property page now marked sold. Its audience is not buyers; it is future sellers in your area deciding who to call, which is why the tone matters more than the numbers.

A just sold post is a social post, reel or email published after closing that announces a home has sold. It reuses the listing's photos or video, credits the people involved, and points to the property page, which now says sold. Its audience is not buyers. It is the homeowners in your area who will sell in the next two years and are quietly deciding who to call.

Done plainly, it is proof of work. Done loudly, it is the post people mute you for.

How one is put together

Take the best exterior or the hero room from the listing, the same photo set you used at launch. Add the word Sold, the neighbourhood, and one line of substance: how long it took, how many showings, what the sellers were trying to do and whether it worked. Credit the buyer's agent if there was one, the photographer if the photos carried the listing, and the sellers if they are happy to be mentioned. End with a quiet invitation: thinking about selling in this area, send a message.

As a reel, it is the just listed cut with a different first frame and a shorter script. As an email, it is one image, three sentences and the page link.

Timing a sold announcement

  • After closing, not after the offer is accepted. Deals fall through, and a sold post on a home that comes back to market is a problem.
  • Within the week, while the listing is still recognisable to neighbours who saw the sign.
  • Again, months later, as part of a round-up of homes sold on that street or in that school zone.

What a sold post is not

A just sold post is not a just listed video run backwards, and it is not a sales pitch. It is also not a place to publish the sale price unless the sellers are comfortable with that and your MLS's current rules and local norms allow it. Check both before you put a number on a graphic.

The nearest confusable thing is the under contract post. That one is a status update during the deal; the sold post is the result. Mixing them up, or posting sold too early, is how an agent ends up explaining a failed contract in the comments.

Four weeks, two offers, one email

A 2-bed bungalow two streets from the park closes on a Friday, four weeks after it listed, with two offers after the first open house. The following Tuesday the agent posts a 15-second vertical cut: the front exterior with Sold over it, the kitchen, the deck, then a frame that reads Four weeks, two offers, and the neighbourhood name. The caption thanks the sellers for trusting her with a home they had lived in for nineteen years, credits the buyer's agent by name, and tags the photographer.

She sends the same image and three sentences to her database with the link to the property page, which now says sold at the top. Two neighbours reply to the email. One asks what her place might be worth. That is the entire purpose of the post, and it worked because it read like a note from a person rather than a billboard.

Where sold posts go wrong

Posting before closing. Leading with your own photo and a trophy pose instead of the home. Publishing the price without asking the sellers. Forgetting to credit the other agent, who will notice. Writing in all capitals with a row of celebration icons. And leaving the property page live and unchanged, so the link under the sold post still invites enquiries for a home that is gone. Close first, home first, credit everyone, mark the page sold.

Next step: open your last closed listing's page and check what it says at the top. If it still looks for sale, fix that before you post about the sale.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

When should I publish a just sold post?

After closing, within the week. Posting at offer acceptance risks announcing a sale that falls through. The home should still be recognisable to neighbours who saw the sign.

Should a just sold post include the sale price?

Only if the sellers are comfortable and your MLS's current rules and local norms allow it. Many agents share the outcome in words instead: four weeks, two offers, or over asking, which tells future sellers what they want to know.

Who should a just sold post credit?

The sellers, if they are happy to be mentioned, the buyer's agent by name, and the photographer if the media carried the listing. Credit costs nothing and other agents notice when it is missing.

What happens to the property page after the sale?

It should stay up and say sold, so the link in your old posts and emails still works and now shows the result. In Rendprop, marking a listing sold or archived updates the page to say so.
