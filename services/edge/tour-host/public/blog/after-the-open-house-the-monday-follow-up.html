After the open house: the Monday follow-up

Three messages before 10 a.m. Monday: a reply to every enquiry from the page, a personal text to everyone who signed in, and a short report to the seller.

The Monday follow-up is three kinds of message, all sent before 10 a.m. Replies to every enquiry that came through the property page, confirming the visit date they asked for. A personal text to everyone who signed the sheet, with one specific thing they said and the page link. And a short report to the seller: how many groups, what they said, what happens this week.

The order matters. The enquiries come first because those people asked a question and are waiting. The sheet comes second because those people are warm but did not ask. The seller comes third because the seller's report is better once you know who wants to come back.

Sunday at Hillcrest, Monday at the desk

A 3-bed on Hillcrest Road, open Sunday 1 to 3. Eleven groups through. Nine signed the sheet. Two enquiries came through the page: one on Sunday at 2:40 from a couple who were standing in the kitchen, asking for Tuesday evening; one on Sunday night at 9 from someone who had the link forwarded by a friend who came, asking for Saturday morning.

Monday 8:30. The two enquiries get replies first: “Tuesday at 6 works, I will confirm with the seller and text you the details this afternoon.” Both done in five minutes. Then the sheet. Nine texts, each with one line that belongs to that person: “You asked about the age of the boiler; it was replaced in 2021; the date is on the page.” “Your daughter liked the back bedroom; here is the page so she can look again.” Each one ends with the branded link. Twenty-five minutes.

Then the seller, by 9:30: “Eleven groups, nine signed in, two second visits requested (Tuesday evening and Saturday morning). Three people asked about the boiler; it is now on the page. Two comments on the main bedroom carpet. I will confirm both visits with you today.” Short, specific, with the next step at the end.

What goes in the text to the sheet

  • Their name. Obvious, and skipped more than you would think when nine texts are being sent.
  • One thing they said or did. This is the whole message. It proves the text was written for them. If you cannot remember anything, you were not paying attention on Sunday, and the fix is a note on the sheet beside each name as they leave.
  • The branded page link. So they can look again, show someone else, and ask for a visit through the form without calling.
  • One line about the week. “Showings Tuesday to Thursday evenings, and I will let you know if offers come in.” Honest, no pressure, gives them a reason to reply.

Keep it under fifty words. A short text gets answered; a long one gets read later, which means never.

The seller's report

Four lines. The count of groups, not people. What came up more than once. Who wants to come back and when. What you are changing this week, if anything. Sellers who get this on Monday morning stop calling on Monday afternoon. Sellers who get “it went well” call at 4.

If feedback pointed at something fixable, say what and when: “Three people mentioned the smell in the utility room; can you run the washer empty with a cleaner today?” If feedback pointed at price, do not raise it in the Monday text. That is a call, later in the week, with more than one Sunday behind it.

Where Monday goes wrong

  • A blast email to the sheet. Same words to nine people. They know, and they file it.
  • Replying to enquiries after the texts. The person who asked for Tuesday evening at 2:40 on Sunday should not wait while you write nine texts to people who did not ask.
  • Waiting until Wednesday. By Wednesday they have been to two other open houses and yours has blurred.
  • Sending the unbranded link. It has no contact card and no form. The branded link is the one that lets them come back to you.
  • “It went well.” Not a report. The seller wants numbers and the next step.
  • No note on the sheet. Without a word beside each name, Monday's texts have nothing personal in them. Write the word as they leave.

Set up Sunday so Monday is easy

Put a column on the sheet for one word per visitor and write it as they leave. On Monday, open the inbox first, the sheet second, the seller third, and be done by 10. For the words that go in the first reply to an enquiry, read follow-up from a listing enquiry: the first reply and the first call.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Who do I follow up with first after an open house?

The people who sent an enquiry through the property page, because they asked a question and gave a preferred date. Then everyone who signed the sheet, with a personal text. Then the seller, with a short report. Replies to enquiries take five minutes and should be done before anything else.

What should the follow-up text to open house visitors say?

Their name, one specific thing they said or did on Sunday, the branded property page link, and one line about what happens this week. Under fifty words. The specific thing is what makes it a message rather than a broadcast, so write a word beside each name on the sheet as they leave.

What should I tell the seller after an open house?

Four lines, Monday morning: the number of groups, what came up more than once, who wants to come back and when, and what you are changing this week. Leave price out of the Monday text; if feedback pointed at price, that is a call later in the week with more than one Sunday behind it.

Is Monday too late to follow up after a Sunday open house?

Monday before 10 a.m. is fine for the sheet and the seller. Enquiries that arrived during the open house itself are better answered Sunday evening if you can. Wednesday is too late for any of it; by then the visitor has seen other homes and yours has blurred.
