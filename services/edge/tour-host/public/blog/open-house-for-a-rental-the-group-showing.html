Open house for a rental: the group showing

Replace a week of individual rental showings with one or two group showings in a fixed window, with rent, date and how to apply stated up front.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. For a rental, hold one or two group showings in a fixed window, say Saturday 10 to 11, instead of a week of individual visits. Put the rent, the deposit, the move-in date, the pet and parking rules and how to apply on the property page before anyone arrives, so the people who come already fit. Reply to everyone afterwards, including the ones who did not get it.

Show a rental in one or two group showings inside a fixed window rather than a week of individual visits. Saturday 10 to 11, announced on the listing and on the property page, with the rent, the deposit, the move-in date, the pet and parking rules and the application steps stated before anyone arrives. The people who turn up already fit the basics, the current tenant is disturbed once instead of nine times, and the unit is seen by everyone in the same light on the same morning.

Individual rental showings are a false kindness. Each one costs the tenant an hour of tidying and you an hour of driving, and half of them end in “I did not realise there was no parking.” The group showing moves that sentence to before the visit, where the page can answer it.

Grant Street, Saturday 10 to 11

A 2-bed on the second floor at 310 Grant Street, available on the first of next month, current tenant moving out in three weeks. The listing goes up on a Tuesday with the page link. The page says: the rent, the deposit, the date, no pets, one parking space, laundry in the basement, how to apply and what the application asks for. Showing Saturday 10 to 11, no appointment needed, come to the side door.

Enquiries come in through the week. Most are “is it still available” and “can I see it before Saturday.” The reply to both is the same: yes, Saturday 10 to 11, here is the page with everything. A few ask about the pet rule after reading it; those are honest conversations by text that save them a trip.

Saturday 9:40 you arrive. The tenant has tidied and gone out for an hour, by arrangement, with thanks and a coffee voucher. One sign on the side door. From 10 to 11 you stand in the kitchen and groups come through, three or four at a time, with a short loop: living room, kitchen, both bedrooms, bathroom, a look at the parking from the back window, the basement laundry for anyone who asks. The questions are the same ones every time; you answer them out loud so the next group hears.

By 11:05 you have met fourteen people, handed out nothing, and pointed everyone to the page for the application. Monday there are five applications. You reply to all fourteen by Tuesday, including the nine who did not apply or were not chosen, because they will be looking again and so will you.

What the page states before the showing

  • Rent, deposit, move-in date. Non-negotiable facts, at the top.
  • Pets, smoking, parking, laundry, utilities included or not. The five questions every rental enquiry asks.
  • Who the lease is for. Minimum term, whether sharers are considered.
  • How to apply. The steps, what documents, any fee and what it covers, how long a decision takes. Check your local rules on application fees and screening and follow them; they vary by place.
  • The showing window. Day, time, where to come, whether to book or just arrive.

A page that answers these filters the Saturday crowd down to people who can take the unit. A page that does not answer them produces a hallway of people asking.

Running the hour

One person at the door, one inside if you can manage it. Groups of three or four at a time; the next group waits on the landing or outside, which is fine for a few minutes and not fine for fifteen. A short loop and the same facts said aloud. Keep the conversation on the unit and the process; it is not the place for small talk about who people are or how they live, and it is not fair to the people in the room who hear it. Questions about the application go to the page.

Tell the current tenant exactly when and for how long, arrange for them to be out if they are willing, and thank them. A cooperative tenant is worth more than a second showing slot.

Where group showings go wrong

  • Fifteen people in a hallway. Stagger entry. Three or four at a time, a few minutes each.
  • No-shows. Expect half of the “I will be there” replies not to come. The window is the window; do not hold it open for them.
  • Not telling the tenant. They find out when the first group arrives. Once.
  • Questions the page should have answered. “Are pets allowed” asked eleven times is a page problem.
  • No reply to the people who did not get it. They are next month's applicants for another unit.
  • A video that shows the tenant's belongings. If you make one, make it from photos taken after the tenant's agreement and with their things cleared, or leave the video for the vacant period.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Why hold a group showing for a rental instead of individual visits?

It disturbs the current tenant once instead of many times, it shows every applicant the unit in the same light on the same morning, and it moves the basic questions about pets, parking and rent to the page, where they are answered before anyone travels. Individual showings mostly end with a question the page could have answered.

What should be on the page before a rental open house?

Rent, deposit and move-in date; pets, smoking, parking, laundry and utilities; the lease term and whether sharers are considered; how to apply, what documents are needed, any fee and the decision time; and the showing window with where to come. Check your local rules on fees and screening and follow them.

How many people should be in the unit at once during a group showing?

Three or four, for a few minutes each, with the next group waiting outside or on the landing. One person at the door and one inside if you can. Say the same facts aloud to each group and keep the conversation on the unit and the process.

Should I reply to applicants who did not get the rental?

Yes, by Tuesday. They are looking, you will have other units, and a short honest reply is remembered. Marking the listing archived so the page says it has gone also stops the enquiries that otherwise keep arriving for a unit that is let.
