Features and finishes: listing them without the brochure voice

List features the way a buyer checks them off: the item, the spec or material, the year. Grouped by room or system, plain, with no adjectives attached.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. List features the way a buyer would check them off: the item, the maker or material, and the date, grouped by room or by system, with no adjectives attached. “Gas range, 36-inch, 2022” tells a buyer more than “gourmet kitchen with premium appliances”, and it survives the inspection.

List features and finishes the way a buyer would check them off: the item, the maker or material, and the year, grouped by room or by system, with no adjectives attached. “Gas range, 36-inch, 2022” tells a buyer more than “gourmet kitchen with premium appliances”, and it is still true after the inspection. The brochure voice adds praise to every line; the useful voice adds a spec.

The brochure voice creeps in because feature lists feel like marketing, and marketing feels like it should sell. But a feature list is reference material. Buyers read it twice: once to decide whether to visit, once after the showing to remember what they saw. Praise helps with neither. Specifics help with both.

The three-part line

Each feature gets up to three parts, in this order: what it is, what it is made of or who made it, and when it went in. Not every feature has all three. A line never needs a fourth part.

  • Roof, architectural shingle, 2021.
  • Furnace, high-efficiency gas, 2019; serviced spring 2026.
  • Kitchen counters, honed quartz, 2022.
  • Flooring, 5-inch white oak, main floor, refinished 2023.
  • Windows, double-pane vinyl, replaced throughout 2018.
  • Fence, cedar, 6-foot, three sides, 2020.

Notice what is missing: “beautiful”, “premium”, “designer”, “top-of-the-line”. None of those words would help a buyer, and all of them would make the line longer than the fact deserves.

Group by where a buyer would look

A long unbroken list is hard to use. Group it the way a buyer walks the house or the way an inspector works through it. Four groups cover most homes: kitchen, baths, systems and structure, outside. A fifth, “stays with the house”, is worth its own group because it answers a question that otherwise arrives by text the night before closing.

Inside each group, order by what matters most to a buyer at that price point. In a starter home, the roof and furnace lead the systems group. In a renovated colonial, the kitchen group leads the whole list. Keep the groups short; eight lines is plenty for a kitchen.

What counts as a feature

A feature is something a buyer would pay for, ask about, or be glad to know the age of. Windows are not a feature; windows replaced in 2018 are. A garage is not a feature; a 24 by 24 heated garage with a 240-volt outlet is. If you cannot add a spec or a date to a line, ask whether the line is telling the buyer anything.

Standard items pad the list and lower the register of the real features around them. Central air in a region where every house has it, a dishwasher, closets. Leave them out unless there is a date worth stating.

A before and after, as a list

Before: “Chef's kitchen with premium stainless appliances, gorgeous granite and custom cabinetry. Spa-inspired primary bath. Newer roof and mechanicals. Professionally finished yard.”

After:

  • Kitchen: gas range, 36-inch, 2022; counter-depth refrigerator, 2022; granite counters; maple cabinets, painted 2022; under-cabinet lighting.
  • Primary bath: walk-in tiled shower, double vanity, heated floor, 2020.
  • Systems: roof, architectural shingle, 2021; furnace and central air, 2019; water heater, 50-gallon gas, 2023; 200-amp panel.
  • Outside: cedar fence, 2020; paver patio, 16 by 20, 2021; irrigation, front and back.

The second version is a list a buyer can carry into the showing and tick off. The first is a paragraph they will forget by the car.

How the brochure voice gets back in

  • Stacking adjectives on a noun. “Gleaming, newly refinished hardwood” is “white oak, refinished 2023” wearing a costume.
  • “New” without a year. New to whom. Write the year; if the seller does not know it, write what they do know (“replaced by previous owner, before 2018”).
  • Hope mixed with fact. “Could be a fourth bedroom” is not a feature. Put it in the description if it is physically true, and keep the list to what exists.
  • Brand names the seller cannot confirm. Check the label before you write it. A wrong brand is a small thing that makes a buyer check everything else.
  • Listing the edits. If a photo is virtually staged, the furniture is not a feature. If a lawn is a green-lawn edit, the lawn is not “professionally maintained”. The list describes the house.

How this works in Rendprop

A Rendprop property page shows the headline details you typed (price, beds, baths, square feet) in their own fields, and a story section for your longer text. A grouped feature list fits in the story section as plain lines, and it sits next to the gallery, the listing video, your contact card and the enquiry form. If you read the top features aloud in the video, you can record the voiceover or have an AI voice read your script, and the captions appear word by word, so a line like “roof, 2021” lands on screen as you say it. Rendprop does not write the list for you. What appears is what you typed, which is why the three-part line matters.

Make the list before the description

Walk the house with the seller and a notes app, and write every feature as item, material or maker, and year. Group it. Then write the description from the list, not the other way round; the description borrows the top three lines, and the list holds the rest. FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should a feature list include brand names?

Include them when they mean something to a buyer and the seller has confirmed them from the label or the paperwork. A named range in a high-end kitchen is useful. In an ordinary kitchen, the size and the fuel say enough. A wrong brand name is a small error that makes buyers doubt the whole list.

How many features should a listing name?

As many as have a spec or a date worth stating, grouped so the list is usable. For most homes that is fifteen to thirty lines across kitchen, baths, systems and outside. Standard items with no date, like closets or a dishwasher, pad the list and should be left out.

Where should the feature list go: MLS remarks, the property page, or a separate sheet?

The top three lines go in the remarks, the full grouped list goes on the property page where there is room, and a printed version works at the open house. Check your MLS's current rules on feature fields, since some have structured fields that should hold certain items rather than the remarks.

What if the seller does not know when something was replaced?

Write what is known: replaced by the previous owner, before 2018. Do not guess a year and do not write new. An honest approximate date is more useful to a buyer and safer for you than a confident wrong one.
