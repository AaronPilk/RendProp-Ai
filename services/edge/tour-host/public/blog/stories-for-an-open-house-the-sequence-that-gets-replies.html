Stories for an open house: the sequence that gets replies

A five-frame story sequence across three days: the announcement, one room, a question, the morning-of reminder with the link, and a thank-you after.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Run five story frames across three days: the announcement with date and time, one room with a specific fact, a question frame that invites a reply, the morning-of reminder with the property page link, and a thank-you after. Replies come from the question frame and the reminder, and each reply is a conversation you can move toward a showing.

The open house story sequence that gets replies is five frames across three days: an announcement with the date and time, one room with one fact, a question frame people can answer, a morning-of reminder with the property page link, and a thank-you frame afterwards. The replies come from the question and the reminder, and every reply is a person you can now talk to directly.

Why stories and not another reel

A reel is for people who do not know the home yet. Stories are for people who already follow you and have half an eye on what you are doing this weekend. They are quick to make, they come and go, and they have a reply box attached. For an open house, that reply box is the point. The reel announces; the story converts the people who were already warm.

The five frames for a Saturday split-level

Here is the sequence for a four-bed split-level with a finished lower level, open Saturday from 1 to 3.

  1. Thursday evening, the announcement. The front exterior photo. Text on the frame: Open Saturday 1 to 3, the street name and town, the price. Nothing else. One frame, readable in two seconds.
  2. Friday morning, the room. The lower level. Text: 600 square feet down here that the listing sheet calls a rec room and the seller used as a workshop and a guest suite. One fact that a photo alone would not carry.
  3. Friday evening, the question. A kitchen photo and a question people can actually answer: Island or no island, which side are you on? Or: What would you use the lower level for? A poll or a question sticker if the platform currently offers one, otherwise plain text and the reply box. This frame is where most replies come from, because it asks for an opinion rather than a commitment.
  4. Saturday 9 a.m., the reminder. The exterior again, or the best interior. Text: Today 1 to 3, parking on the street, the full gallery and video here, with the property page link wherever the platform currently lets you place one. The people who reply to this frame are the ones who are coming or wish they could.
  5. Saturday 4 p.m., the thank-you. A photo of the house from the sidewalk with the sign. Text: Thanks to everyone who came through. Still taking private showings this week, message me. No visitor photos unless each person agreed.

Five frames, three days, roughly ten minutes of work in total. Every reply gets a real answer the same day.

What to do with the replies

A reply to the question frame is an opening, not a lead yet. Answer the opinion with your own, then ask one thing: Are you around Saturday? A reply to the reminder frame is closer. Answer it with a specific: Great, parking is easiest on the north side, and the lower level is the room to see first. If someone cannot make it, offer the link and a private showing time. The pattern is the same as in turning a reel into a lead: public reply, private conversation, then the page.

Ways the sequence fails

  • Fifteen frames on Friday night. People tap through quickly or skip the whole set. Five frames across three days gives each one a chance.
  • No text on the frames. A photo of a kitchen with no words is a photo of a kitchen. The date, the time and the one fact have to be on the frame itself.
  • No link on the reminder. The morning-of frame is the one people act on. If it has no link, the action is a screenshot at best.
  • All exteriors. The front of the house is the announcement. The rooms are the reason to come.
  • Replies left until Monday. A Friday night reply answered on Monday is a reply to an open house that already happened.
  • A thank-you frame that overstates the turnout. If four people came, do not imply forty. The people who were there know.

Write the five frames now

For your next open house, write the five frames' text now, before the weekend: date and time, one room fact, one question, the reminder line, the thank-you line. Then the only work on the day is posting and replying.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How many stories should I post for an open house?

Five frames across three days: the announcement, one room, a question, the morning-of reminder with the link, and a thank-you after. That is enough to be seen and not enough to be skipped.

Which story frame gets the most replies?

The question frame, because it asks for an opinion rather than a commitment. Island or no island, what would you do with the lower level. The morning-of reminder gets fewer replies, but they are from people who are closer to coming.

Should the reminder story have the property page link or the address?

Both if the frame stays readable, but the link matters more. The page carries the address, the gallery, the video and a form for people who cannot make it. Place the link wherever the platform currently allows.

Can I post photos of visitors in the thank-you story?

Only if each person in the frame agreed to it. A sidewalk shot of the house and the sign does the job without the question. Never post the sign-in sheet.
