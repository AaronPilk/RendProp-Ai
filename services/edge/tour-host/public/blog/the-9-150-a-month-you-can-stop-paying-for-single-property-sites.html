The $9–150 a month you can stop paying for single-property sites

Single-property site tools cost $9–150 a month at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026. What the fee buys, what it leaves you doing, and the alternative.

Single-property site tools charge $9–150 per month at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026, and what the fee buys is a template, hosting and a contact form that you still have to fill in, design around and remember to cancel. If the property page can be published as part of building the listing's photos and video, that monthly line disappears from the budget and so does the cancellation chore. Here is what the fee covers, what it leaves on your desk, and when a monthly tool is still the right answer.

What the monthly fee buys

  • A template. A hero image, a gallery block, a details block, a map, a form. Usually attractive, usually the same as the agent down the street.
  • Hosting and a web address. Sometimes a subdomain, sometimes a custom domain you buy separately and renew yearly.
  • A form. It emails you. Where that email lands, and whether it is separate from the enquiries that come from portals and texts, is your problem.
  • Analytics of some kind. Views and visitors, which feel like information and rarely change a decision.

What it still leaves you doing

Uploading the photos, in the right order, again. Typing the price, beds, baths and square feet, again. Embedding a video from somewhere else. Writing a description that is now the third version of the same paragraph. Checking the page on a phone because the template was built for a desktop. Setting a reminder to cancel. And then, when the price changes, doing the details again in a third place. The fee is small; the duplicate work is not.

The arithmetic over a year

Take an agent who lists twenty homes a year and keeps each page live for about two months. That is forty page-months a year, billed somewhere in the $9–150 range each at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026, if every page is cancelled on time. In practice some pages run for six months because nobody cancelled, and the real figure creeps up. At the top of the range the same twenty listings cost a serious sum for pages that exist to hold photos you already paid for. None of this is a scandal. It is just a line that does not need to be a line.

When a monthly site tool is still the right call

There are honest cases. A single high-value listing where the seller wants a custom domain and a bespoke design, and is paying for it. A new-construction community with twenty units on one site that changes weekly. A brokerage that wants every page on its own domain for brand reasons and has someone whose job is to maintain them. In those cases, the fee buys something specific. For a 3-bed in a cul-de-sac that will sell in three weeks, it buys a template and a chore.

A worked example: the Tuesday listing

A 3-bed goes live on a Tuesday. In the old shape, Monday evening is spent on the site tool: upload thirty photos, drag them into order, type the details, paste a video embed, preview on a phone, fix the hero crop, publish, copy the link, set a cancel reminder for July. Forty minutes, if nothing breaks. In the other shape, the page is published from the listing after the video is reviewed, the details are already there because they were typed when the listing was started, and the links are copied from the Share sheet. The seller gets the branded link that evening either way. One of those Mondays has forty minutes more in it.

Traps with page fees

  • Letting a page bill after closing. If you keep a monthly tool, cancel on the day the sale closes, not when you notice the charge.
  • Different details on the page and the MLS. Whichever tool you use, type the numbers once and copy them.
  • A page nobody can find. The link has to be on the sign, the flyer, the posts and in the MLS tour field, or the fee buys an empty room.
  • Choosing a tool because the template is pretty and the form goes to an email you do not check.

Next step: open your card statement and count the single-property site charges from the last twelve months, including the ones for homes that closed. That number is the budget you are deciding about.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Is $9–150 a month a lot for a property website?

Per page, no. Across twenty listings a year, with pages that run two to six months each, it adds up to a line worth questioning, especially since the page holds photos and details you have already produced elsewhere.

What does a single-property site do that a portal listing does not?

It gives the listing a home that is not shared with other agents, holds the video and the full gallery in your order, and sends enquiries to you with the date the buyer wants. That is worth having; the question is whether it needs its own subscription.

When is a dedicated site tool still worth paying for?

A bespoke high-value listing with a custom domain the seller is paying for, a multi-unit development site, or a brokerage that maintains every page on its own domain. For a typical home that sells in weeks, a page that comes with the listing is enough.

What happens to the page when the home sells?

With a monthly tool, it keeps billing until you cancel it. With a page that comes with the listing, you mark the listing sold and the page says so, and the branded link on old flyers still tells the truth.
