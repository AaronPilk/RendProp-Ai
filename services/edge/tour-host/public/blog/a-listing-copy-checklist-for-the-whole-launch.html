A listing copy checklist for the whole launch

One listing needs about nine pieces of text. Write them in order from one source note, and run the same five checks on each before anything goes live.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. One listing needs about nine pieces of text, and they should all come from one source note. The MLS remarks, the property page details and story section, the voiceover script, the reel caption, the just-listed email, the open house text and post, the sign, and the disclosure line. Write them in that order, each cut from the one before where that makes sense, and run the same five checks on every piece before it goes live: numbers against the source, adjectives against facts, people versus property, the disclosure present, and the link correct.

Most launch copy problems are not writing problems. They are version problems: the price changed and three of nine pieces still have the old one; the den became a family room halfway through the week; the staging note is on the page and nowhere else. One source note and one order of work prevent most of it.

The source note

Before writing anything, make one note that holds the facts every piece will draw on. Keep it open all week and change it first when anything changes.

  • Price, beds, baths, square feet and which measure, lot size, year built, taxes, any fee.
  • The dates of every update, with the source (invoice, permit, the seller's receipt).
  • The one thing: the sentence you would say first in the car.
  • The room names, one per room, as you will use them everywhere.
  • The list of edited photos by number and type, and the one-line disclosure written out.
  • The open house day and hours.
  • The branded link and the unbranded link.

The nine pieces, in order

  1. MLS remarks. From the photos in gallery order, opener first, within your MLS's current character limit. The disclosure line near the top.
  2. Property page details. The numbers from the source note, typed into the fields once and checked against the note.
  3. Property page story section. The remarks rewritten in plain sentences for a page without a character limit: the one thing, a day in the home, what was done and when, what to do next. The disclosure line at the top if the gallery has edited photos.
  4. Voiceover script. Cut from the description in scene order, shortened for speech, numbers as they are said. Read aloud with a timer.
  5. Reel caption. Three lines cut from the script: the fact that made you post, one detail the video shows, one call to action. The disclosure line if a staged or edited photo is in the reel. Hashtags after a break.
  6. Just-listed email. Subject with the type and the town, one photo, the four facts, three lines, one link to the branded page.
  7. Open house text and post. Day and hours first, one reason to come, the link.
  8. Sign copy. OPEN, the day, the hours, an arrow. The yard sign adds the QR code to the page.
  9. Disclosure line. Already written in the source note; this step is confirming it appears, in the same words, in every piece that shows or uses an edited photo. Check your MLS's current rules on placement and ask your broker about office policy.

The five checks on every piece

  • Numbers. Each one matches the source note, and the source note matches a document.
  • Adjectives. Each one replaced by a fact or cut.
  • People versus property. No sentence about who the home suits or who lives nearby. Not legal advice; ask your broker about anything borderline.
  • Disclosure. Present where edited photos appear, in the same words.
  • Link. The branded link in anything with your name on it; the unbranded link in the MLS virtual tour field if your MLS's current rules call for an unbranded tour there. Tap each link once from the published piece.

A before and after: the disclosure line across three places

Before: the MLS remarks say “some photos virtually staged”; the page story section says nothing; the reel caption says “furniture for illustration”. Three wordings, one missing.

After, in all three: “Photos 2, 3 and 6 are virtually staged; the home is vacant and the furniture shown is not included. Unstaged photos are available on request.” One line from the source note, pasted.

A four-day timeline

  • Monday: source note; photos chosen and ordered; remarks drafted; page details typed.
  • Tuesday: story section; script cut from the remarks; video created and reviewed; caption cut from the script.
  • Wednesday: five checks on everything; page published; links tested; listing live; just-listed email sent.
  • Thursday: reel posted; open house text and post out; sign copy to the printer or the marker pen.

Where launches go wrong on the copy side

  • Writing the remarks and generating the rest by copy-paste. The story section ends up in abbreviations and the caption is 200 words.
  • Changing the price in two places out of nine. Change the source note, then walk the list.
  • The sign and the post disagreeing on the hours. Both came from memory instead of the note.
  • Nobody owning the text on a team. One person owns the source note and the five checks, even if several people write pieces.
  • The script written at the video step from scratch. It drifts from the description every time.
  • Disclosure in one place. It needs to be wherever the edited photo is.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How many pieces of text does a listing launch need?

About nine: MLS remarks, the page details, the page story section, the voiceover script, the reel caption, the just-listed email, the open house text and post, the sign, and the disclosure line. Some listings skip the open house pieces; most need the rest. All of them should come from one source note.

What goes in the source note?

Every number with its source, the dates of updates, the one sentence you would say first about the home, one agreed name per room, the list of edited photos with the disclosure line written out, the open house day and hours, and both links. Change the note first when anything changes, then walk the nine pieces.

In what order should the pieces be written?

Remarks first from the photos, then the page details and story section, then the script cut from the description, then the caption cut from the script, then the email, the open house pieces and the sign. Each piece borrows from the one before, which keeps the facts and the names identical.

Who should own the copy on a team listing?

One person owns the source note and runs the five checks, even if the assistant writes the email and the agent writes the remarks. Without a single owner, the price changes in two places out of nine and the sign disagrees with the post.
