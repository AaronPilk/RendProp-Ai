Putting a property website link in your MLS listing the right way

Use the unbranded link, put it in the virtual-tour field only, test it from a portal after syndication, and keep the branded link for your own channels.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Put the unbranded link in the MLS virtual-tour field, not in the public remarks and not in the photo captions. Test it in a private window before you enter it, then open the listing on a portal after it syndicates and click through to confirm the tour works. Put the unbranded link in the MLS virtual-tour field and nowhere else in the listing: not in the public remarks, not in a photo caption, not in the agent-to-agent notes. Test the link in a private browser window before you enter it, then open the listing on a portal after it syndicates and click through to confirm the tour loads. Keep the branded link for texts, email, social and the yard sign.

This sounds like a small procedural thing. It is the difference between a tour that reaches every buyer looking at your listing and a link that gets stripped out in week one.

The four places people try to put a link, and the one that works

The virtual-tour field. This is the only field built for it. It syndicates to portals, it shows up as a tour button on many buyer-facing sites, and it is the field built to hold a link. Use the unbranded link here.

Public remarks. Many boards do not want URLs, email addresses or phone numbers in public remarks; check your own MLS's current rules. Even where a link is permitted, portals often strip it or render it as plain text nobody can tap.

Photo captions. Same problem. Captions syndicate, and a URL in a caption may be treated as branding or contact information; check your board's rules.

Private or broker remarks. Only other agents see these, so check your board's rules and, if a link is fine there, use it for sending the branded link to the buyer's agent so they know who to call, but it does nothing for the public.

A step-by-step for a Monday morning entry

  1. Publish the property page and copy the unbranded link. Open it in a private window on your phone. Confirm there is no name, phone, email or logo on the page or in the video.
  2. Enter the listing in the MLS. In the virtual-tour field (your board may call it "virtual tour URL", "tour link", "unbranded tour" or similar), paste the unbranded link. Some boards have separate branded and unbranded tour fields; if yours does, use both correctly rather than putting the unbranded link in the branded slot.
  3. Do not add the link anywhere else. If you want the buyer's agent to have the branded page, put it in the broker-only remarks.
  4. Save and wait for the listing to syndicate. Then open the listing on a portal, find the tour button, and tap it. Confirm it opens the right property and the page loads on a phone.
  5. Check your enquiry inbox the next morning. If the unbranded page has a form, enquiries from portal traffic will start here.

A worked example with a price change

A 4-bed in a newer subdivision lists at $629,000 on Monday. The unbranded link goes into the tour field. Two weeks later the seller agrees to drop to $599,000. You update the MLS price. If the property page is tied to the same listing, you update the price in the app and the tour shows $599,000 the next time a buyer opens it from a portal. If the page is a separate site, you now have to remember to update it too, and a buyer who sees $629,000 on the tour and $599,000 on the portal will wonder which is true.

This is the strongest argument for using one page with two links rather than two pages.

Mistakes that cost you the tour

  • Pasting the branded link. The most common error. It looks fine to you because you expect to see your name there.
  • A link that redirects. Shortened or redirecting URLs may be blocked by your board. Use the direct link.
  • A page that autoplays video with sound. Not a rules question, but buyers on portals tap tours from waiting rooms and buses. A page that starts playing audio gets closed.
  • Forgetting to update the link when the page changes. If you rebuild the page and the slug changes, the MLS still points at the old one. Keep the slug stable or update the field.
  • Leaving the tour link live after the home sells. The listing may stay visible as sold after closing. Make sure the page says sold too, so a buyer who finds it in a month is not confused.

How this works in Rendprop

After you publish a listing, the Share sheet gives you both links. The habit that keeps this clean

Write a two-line note in your listing entry checklist: "Tour field: unbranded link. Everything else: branded link." Anyone on your team entering listings follows it. When a photographer or assistant builds the page, ask them to send both links labelled so the wrong one never gets pasted.

The next step: open your most recent active listing on a portal, tap the tour button, and look at what a stranger sees. If your name is on it, swap the link today. FAQ

Questions on this topic

Which MLS field should the property website link go in?

The virtual-tour field, whatever your board calls it. It is the one field designed to carry a link and the one that syndicates to portals as a tour button. Avoid putting links in public remarks or photo captions; check your own MLS's current rules, and expect portals to strip them anyway.

My MLS has both a branded and an unbranded tour field. What goes where?

Put the branded link in the branded field and the unbranded link in the unbranded field. The unbranded one is what syndicates to the public. Do not put the unbranded link in both slots; the branded field exists so other agents can see who to contact.

How do I know the link worked after I entered it?

Wait for syndication, then open the listing on a portal and tap the tour button. If it opens the right property on your phone without your name showing, it worked. Do this once per listing rather than assuming.

Can I use a shortened URL in the tour field?

Shortened or redirecting links may be blocked by your board, so use the direct link to the page. It is also easier for a buyer to trust.
