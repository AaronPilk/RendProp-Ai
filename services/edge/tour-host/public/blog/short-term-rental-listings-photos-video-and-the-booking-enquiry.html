Short-term rentals: photos, video and the booking enquiry

A short-term rental needs photos that match the stay, a short video that proves the layout, and a page of your own where a guest can ask about dates.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A short-term rental listing needs three things: photos that match what the guest will find, a short video that proves the layout and the view, and a page of your own where a guest can ask about dates directly. The platforms bring the traffic; your own link is where repeat guests and referrals book without the platform in between.

A short-term rental is marketed well when the photos match the stay, a short video proves the layout and the view, and there is a page of your own where a guest can ask about dates directly. The booking platforms bring first-time guests. Your own link, with the same photos and a form that asks for dates, is where repeat guests, referrals and the people who found you on Instagram book without a platform in between.

Photos that match the stay

Guests arrive with the photos in their head. The gap between the photos and the door is the review. So the standard for a rental photo is not 'as good as it can look' but 'as good as it will look when they walk in'. Make the bed the way the cleaner makes it. Put out the towels you actually provide. Open the blinds the guest will open. Then shoot.

  • Exterior and the door. With the house number visible if you can; guests use it to find the place at 11pm.
  • Living room from the entry. Wide, level, every lamp on.
  • Each bedroom from the door, bed in the centre. One per room. Note the bed size in the voiceover or details.
  • Each bathroom. Shower visible. Guests count them.
  • Kitchen with the counters clear. Coffee machine in frame if you have one; it is the most asked-about item in any rental.
  • The outside space. Deck, balcony, garden, pool, with the furniture set out.
  • The view, from the window where it is best. If the view is the reason to book, this is the main photo.

A blue sky edit on a grey-day exterior is fair if it is disclosed and the house looks the same otherwise. Adding furniture, removing a neighbouring building or changing the view is not; the guest will stand in that room. Compare every edit with the original before it goes up.

The video that proves the layout

Photos can make a studio look like a suite. A short video, either a photo-based one that moves through the rooms in the order a guest would walk them, or a recorded handheld walkthrough from the door to the view, settles the question of size. Keep it under a minute. End on the best thing: the terrace, the view, the pool. For a vertical social cut, use portrait photos of the same spots and let the captions name the sleeps and the location.

Worked example: a three-bedroom lake house that sleeps eight

A lake house with three bedrooms, two bathrooms, a dock and a deck facing west is listed on two platforms and gets most of its summer bookings from returning families. The owner shoots fourteen landscape photos on a clear morning with every bed made and the deck furniture out, plus the deck at sunset on a second trip. The listing video runs from the drive to the front door, through the living room to the kitchen, each bedroom, the bathrooms, out to the deck and down to the dock. Fifty seconds, with a voiceover giving sleeps eight, the bed sizes, and that the dock is included. The page shows beds, baths and square feet, the gallery, the video on request and a form that asks for the date the visitor wants. The link goes in the owner's reply to every past guest who emails asking about next summer. The first enquiry through it is a family who stayed two years ago asking for the same week in August.

The booking enquiry on your own page

Your own page is not a replacement for the platforms; it is the place that handles the enquiries that do not need them. A past guest asking about dates. A friend of a past guest. Someone who saw the reel. The form asks for the date they want, which is the only thing you need to start the conversation, and the message lands in your inbox with the listing it belongs to. If you manage several properties, each has its own page and the enquiries from all of them arrive together.

Rental listing mistakes that cost bookings

  • Ultra-wide from the corner. The 0.5x lens from a corner turns a 12-foot bedroom into a ballroom and the guest into a critic. Shoot from the door at the normal lens.
  • Staged with things you do not provide. Flowers, wine, a breakfast spread. Shoot what is there on arrival.
  • No bathroom photos. The guest assumes the worst.
  • The view photo from the wrong window. Shoot the view from where a guest will actually see it, not from the roof.
  • One photo set for summer used all winter. If the deck is under snow in January, say so in the details or shoot a winter exterior.
  • A page with no way to ask. If the guest has to find your email, half of them go back to the platform.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What is the most important photo for a short-term rental?

The one that shows the reason to book: the view from the window where it is best, the deck, the pool. If the rental has no standout feature, the living room from the entry with every lamp on is the main photo. Every photo should match what the guest finds on arrival.

Do I need a video for a rental listing?

A short one helps, because photos can make a small place look large and guests have learned to doubt them. A photo-based video that moves through the rooms in the order a guest walks them, or a handheld walkthrough from the door to the view, proves the layout in under a minute.

Why have my own page if I list on platforms?

For the bookings that do not need a platform: past guests, their friends, and people who found you on social. Your page has the same photos, a video and a form that asks for dates, and the enquiries land in your inbox with the property they belong to.

Which Rendprop mode is right for a short-term rental?

The real estate mode. A rental is a property with beds, baths and square feet, and the enquiry form's date question is exactly the first question a guest asks.
