Checking a property page before you share it: a two-minute review

Open the page on your phone and run eight checks: main photo, price, facts, gallery, video, edit labels, the number dials, the form arrives.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Before you share a property page, open it on your phone and run eight checks in two minutes: the main photo is the one you chose, the price matches the MLS, the beds, baths and square feet match, the gallery is in order with no stray photo, the video plays only when tapped, every AI-edited photo is labelled, the phone number dials when tapped, and a test enquiry arrives in your inbox. Send the link to the seller before anyone else.

Before a property page goes to anyone, open it on your phone, not your desktop, and run eight checks: the main photo is the one you chose, the price and the beds, baths and square feet match the MLS, the gallery is in the right order with no stray or duplicate photo, the video plays when tapped and not before, every AI-edited photo carries its label, the phone number dials when tapped, and a test enquiry from the form arrives in your inbox. It takes two minutes. Then the seller gets the link before anyone else sees it.

Every one of these has failed on a real listing. Most of them failed because nobody looked at the page the way a buyer would.

The eight checks, in order

  1. Main photo. Is it the one you meant. A wrong main photo is the most common error after a batch upload, and it is the first thing a buyer sees.
  2. Price. Read it against the MLS entry. One digit off is a different home.
  3. Beds, baths, square feet. Read all three against the MLS. A 3-bed listed as a 4-bed on the page costs you a buyer at the showing.
  4. Gallery. Swipe the whole thing. Every room present, no duplicates, no photo from another listing, no test shot of the floor. First five are the strongest.
  5. Video. It did not start on its own. Tap play; it plays with sound. Watch the first ten seconds and the last five for a wrong price or a stray frame.
  6. Labels. Any virtually staged photo says so. Any other AI-altered photo carries its disclosure. Compare each against the original once more; look for an invented window, a changed floor, a missing outlet.
  7. Contact card. Tap the number. The dialler opens with your number. Tap the email. A draft opens addressed to you.
  8. Form. Fill it in with your own details and a date. Submit. Open your inbox and confirm it arrived with the date intact and the listing attached.

A worked example: the Tuesday night launch

Photos arrived at five. By eight the page is built. You open it on your phone. Check one: the main photo is the kitchen, not the exterior you chose. Fix it. Check two: price reads $437,500, MLS says $437,500. Check three: 3, 2.5, 1,620. Matches. Check four: swipe. Photo 14 is the primary bedroom twice. Remove one. Check five: tap play. Plays. First line is right. Last frame is the exterior. Check six: the living room was staged; the label is there. The twilight exterior carries its disclosure. Compare the twilight to the original: the porch light is on in the edit and there is no porch light in the original. Swap the edit for the daytime original. Check seven: number dials. Check eight: form submitted, arrives with Saturday as the date.

Three fixes in two minutes. Any one of them would have been noticed by a buyer or the seller first.

Then the seller

Text the seller the branded link with one line: "Your home's page. Have a look and tell me if anything needs changing before it goes out." Sellers catch things you cannot: a wrong year on the roof, a photo of a room they repainted since, a neighbour's car in the driveway. Give them until the morning, then publish everywhere.

What the two minutes catch

  • The wrong main photo after a re-upload or an edit.
  • A stale price after a last-minute change with the seller.
  • A photo from another listing that landed in the wrong album.
  • A video that autoplays because a setting changed.
  • An unlabelled staging edit that your MLS may flag.
  • An AI edit that invented a detail: a window, a fireplace, a different floor.
  • A number with a typo that dials a stranger.
  • A form that goes nowhere because of an unverified email or a wrong setting.

Make it a fixed habit

Put the eight checks in your launch checklist as a single line: "Page review on phone, eight checks, seller first." Do it for every listing, including the ones where you are sure. The ones where you are sure are the ones with the duplicate bedroom photo.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Why review the page on a phone rather than a desktop?

Because buyers open it on phones, and a page that looks right on a wide screen can hide a wrong main photo, an autoplaying video or a tap target that is too small. The phone shows you what the buyer gets.

Should I send a test enquiry through my own form every time?

Yes. It takes twenty seconds and it is the only way to know the form reaches you with the date intact. A form that silently fails costs you every enquiry until someone mentions they never heard back.

What should I look for when comparing an AI edit against the original?

Anything the edit added, removed or changed that is not the thing you asked for: a window, a light fixture, a floor material, a missing outlet, a different view through the glass. If the edit invented something, use the original or re-run the edit.

Why send the page to the seller before publishing?

Sellers notice things you cannot: a room they repainted since the shoot, a wrong detail, a neighbour's car in the driveway. One night of their review prevents a correction after the listing is live on every channel.
