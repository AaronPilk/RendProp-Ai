Why a photographer should keep the originals and the versions

Keep the untouched original and every edited version of each listing photo. It settles disputes, protects you with the MLS and makes re-edits quick.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Keep the untouched original and every edited version of each photo, labelled, for every listing you shoot. The original is your proof of what the home looked like; the versions are your record of what you changed. Together they answer any question from an agent, a buyer or an MLS without an argument.

Keep the untouched original of every listing photo and every edited version you made from it, labelled so you can tell them apart. The original is your evidence of what the home looked like on the day. The versions are your record of what was changed and how. A photographer who can produce both, quickly, ends disputes before they start.

This used to be a backup question. With AI edits it is a professional one, because an edit can now change a detail nobody asked to change, and the only way to prove what the room really contained is the frame you shot.

The three questions you will be asked

Over a year of listings, three questions come up, and each is answered by the same folder:

  • A buyer at a showing says the photos were misleading. The agent calls you. You send the original and the edited version with its disclosure. Either the edit was faithful and labelled, and the matter closes, or it was not and you fix it. Both outcomes beat not knowing.
  • An MLS compliance desk asks about an altered image. They may ask for the original and a note of what was changed. If you have both, it is an email. If you only have the edited export, it is a problem.
  • The agent wants a different edit two weeks later. The seller has changed their mind about the twilight. If you kept the original, the new version takes a minute. If you only kept the twilight, you are driving back.

What a version record should contain

At minimum: the original, each edited version, what kind of edit it was, and the disclosure it carries. For staging that label is Virtually staged. For sky, lawn, decluttering and custom edits it is an AI-alteration disclosure. The label is not decoration. It is the line a buyer sees that tells them this frame has been changed, and it is the line you point to when someone asks whether you disclosed.

A staged living room, questioned in May

You shoot a vacant three-bed in March. The agent asks for staging in the living room and primary bedroom and a green lawn on the exterior. You deliver eighteen photos, three of them edited, each edited one labelled. In May a buyer under contract walks through with their parents and says the living room looked bigger in the photos. The agent is nervous and asks you what was done.

You open the listing. The living room original shows an empty room. The staged version shows the same room, same walls, same windows, with a sofa and a rug, and it is labelled Virtually staged. The room dimensions did not change. You send both frames to the agent in two minutes. The buyer's parents were reacting to furniture scale, not a misrepresentation. Done. Without the original, that conversation runs for a week.

Keeping your own archive as well

An app's photo history is useful, but it is not your only copy. Keep your own copy of the originals on your own storage, organised by address and date, the way you always have. The app record tells you what was edited and how; your archive is the thing you control if you change tools or close an account. Both are cheap compared with one dispute.

Mistakes that leave you exposed

  • Overwriting originals with edited exports. If the file with the original name is now the twilight, you have no original.
  • Stripping the disclosure before delivery. Some agents ask for a clean version. Explain why the label stays, and point them to their MLS rules.
  • Keeping one version per photo. If the seller changes their mind, you want the alternatives you already made.
  • Not comparing before you publish. AI can change or invent details. The version record is only worth keeping if you looked at it first.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Why keep the original if the edited photo is the one the agent uses?

Because the original is proof of what the home looked like. Buyers, agents and MLS compliance desks ask about altered images, and the original answers the question in one email. It also lets you re-edit without a return visit.

What label should an edited photo carry?

Staged photos are labelled Virtually staged. Sky, lawn, decluttering and custom edits carry an AI-alteration disclosure. Rendprop applies these to each saved version, and the original stays in photo history unlabelled.

Should I rely on the app's history or keep my own files?

Both. The app's photo history records what was edited and how. Your own archive of originals on your own storage is the copy you control if you ever change tools.

Can an agent ask me to remove the disclosure?

They can ask, and you should decline. The disclosure is what makes the edit honest and defensible. Point the agent to their own MLS rules on altered images and let those settle it.
