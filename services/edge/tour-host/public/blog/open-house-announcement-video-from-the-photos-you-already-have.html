Open house announcement video from the photos you already have

An open house video needs three or four photos you already have, the day and time on screen in the first two seconds, and a 15 to 20 second script.

An open house announcement video is a 15 to 20 second reminder built from three or four photos you already have. The day and time go on screen within the first two seconds, the script says where the home is, when the doors open and one reason to come, and the only ask is the property page link. It is not a tour; the tour already exists. Post it two or three days before the open house and again the morning of, and it will do its job.

What an open house video has to say

Three things, in this order.

  • When. Day and time, spoken and captioned, in the first line. Saturday, one to three. This is the one fact the video exists to deliver.
  • Where. The neighbourhood or street and the home type. Enough for a local to picture it, not so much that it needs a map.
  • Why. One reason to come in person. The yard, the kitchen, the light at that hour, the price. One.

Then the ask: the link for the full tour and the address. Nothing else. No history of the house, no bedroom count, no square footage. Those are on the page.

Picking three or four photos

You are not walking the home; you are reminding people it exists. Choose the three frames that stop a scroll.

  1. The lead. The best photo of the listing, the one you led the original reel with. Kitchen, view, pool, living room.
  2. The exterior. So people recognise it when they drive up.
  3. The reason. Whatever the why line is about. If the why is the yard, the yard.
  4. Optional fourth. The room buyers asked about most at showings so far.

Portrait frames for the 9:16 version, since this is going to feeds. If you only have landscape photos of the reason room, use three frames instead of four rather than cropping.

The 15 to 20 second script

Forty words at most. Here is one for a three-bed on a cul-de-sac:

Open house Saturday, one to three. The three-bed on Hollis Lane with the renovated kitchen. Come see the back yard in the afternoon light. Address and the full tour at the link.

Thirty-two words. Read it with a timer; it lands around 16 seconds. If you are over 20, cut the why sentence to five words before you cut the when.

Timing the posts

Two posts, same video. The first goes out two or three days ahead, when people are planning the weekend. The second goes out the morning of, when people are deciding what to do after lunch. Change the caption on the second one to say today and repeat the time. If the open house is Sunday as well, a third post Sunday morning is fine; a fourth is noise.

Put the branded property page link wherever the platform currently lets you place a tappable one, and name that spot in the caption. The page has the address, the gallery, the full video and the enquiry form, which asks the visitor what date they want; someone who cannot make Saturday can ask for Tuesday without a phone call.

A worked example: Thursday to Saturday

Listing went live Tuesday with a full reel and a page video. Open house is Saturday, one to three. Thursday morning: kitchen frame, front exterior, back yard, 32-word script read in the car, captions on, light music under, 17 seconds. Posted Thursday at lunch with the link in the profile. Saturday at 8 a.m.: same video, caption now reads Today, 1 to 3. Two posts, one video, eleven minutes of work in total.

Where open house videos go wrong

  • Burying the time. If Saturday one to three arrives in the last three seconds, the people who scrolled at second four will not be there.
  • Turning it into a tour. Eight photos and a 40-second script is the listing reel again. People who watched that one scroll past this one.
  • A date in the video that changes. If the open house moves, the video is wrong everywhere it was posted. Confirm the slot before you build, and keep the exact address on the page rather than in the voiceover so a change there is a page edit.
  • Posting once, a week out. Nobody remembers a Saturday open house they saw on the previous Friday. Post close to the day.
  • Forgetting the second post. The morning-of post is the one that moves people off the couch.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How long should an open house announcement video be?

Fifteen to twenty seconds. Three or four photos, a script of forty words or fewer, the day and time on screen in the first two seconds. It is a reminder, not a tour, and the full tour already lives on the property page.

Which photos should I use for an open house video?

The best single frame of the home, the exterior so people recognise it when they arrive, and the room or feature your one reason to come is about. A fourth frame is optional. Use portrait photos for the 9:16 version.

When should I post the open house video?

Twice: two or three days before, when people plan their weekend, and the morning of, when they decide what to do after lunch. Change the caption on the second post to say today and repeat the time.

Should the address be in the video?

Put the street or neighbourhood in the script and keep the exact address on the property page. If anything changes, the page is a quick edit and the video stays correct everywhere it was posted.
