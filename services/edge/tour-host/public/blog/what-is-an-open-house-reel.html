What is an open house reel

An open house reel is a short vertical video that invites people to a specific open house: the day, the time, three scenes of the home and the page link.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. An open house reel is a short vertical video whose one job is to get people to a specific open house. It puts the day and time on the first frame, shows three or four scenes of the home, repeats the time at the end and points to the property page. An open house reel is a short vertical video whose one job is to get people to a specific open house. It puts the day and the time on the first frame, shows three or four scenes of the home, repeats the time at the end and points to the property page for the address and the details. It is built from the same photos as the listing video and remade, with a new first frame, for every open house the listing has.

It is an invitation, not a tour. The viewer should know when to show up before they know how many bathrooms there are.

How one is assembled

Start with the time. Sunday 1 to 3 is the headline, over the front exterior or the room that sells the house. Then three scenes chosen for a feed: the kitchen, the living space, the yard or the view. A short voiceover says the day, the time, the neighbourhood and the price, and the captions carry the same words for viewers on mute. The last frame repeats the day and time and says link in bio, where the branded page link lives. Fifteen to twenty-five seconds is plenty.

Because the photos already exist, the whole reel is a script and a first frame away from the just listed cut.

When to post the invitation

  • Three or four days before the open house, so it has time to circulate and be saved.
  • Again the morning of, as a reminder, with the same cut or a shorter one.
  • As a story the day before and the day of, if you use stories.

It does not work when posted a week early, when the date is lost under newer posts, or after the fact, when it becomes a different kind of post entirely.

What an open house reel is not

An open house reel is not the just listed video, which announces the home exists, and not the general listing reel, which shows the home without a date. It is also not the behind-the-scenes video from the open house itself, the kitchen table, the sign, the people arriving; that is content from the event, not an invitation to it.

The nearest confusable thing is an open house post with a single photo and the time in the caption. It carries the same message. The reel puts the time on the image, which survives the caption being collapsed, and shows the home moving, which earns a longer look in the feed.

Saturday 1 to 3, posted Wednesday

A 4-bed with a pool lists on a Tuesday with an open house set for Saturday 1 to 3. Wednesday morning the agent builds a 19-second vertical cut from the portrait photos: Saturday 1 to 3 over the front exterior, then the kitchen, the pool and the primary bedroom, then Saturday 1 to 3 again with the neighbourhood name and Link in bio. The voiceover says the day, the time, the price and that the kitchen opens to the pool terrace.

It posts Wednesday at noon. Saturday at 8 am she reposts the same cut as a story and a reel with the words Today in the caption. Fourteen people come through between one and three; four of them mention the reel at the door. The enquiry form on the page has two requests for private showings from people who could not make Saturday. The reel's first frame did most of that work.

Where open house reels go wrong

The time is in the caption but not on the screen. The time is on the screen but at the very top, under the platform's interface. The reel runs forty seconds and the time comes at the end. The link points to a portal page that does not mention the open house. Or the reel is posted after the open house, with the past-tense date still on it. Time first, time in the middle of the frame, short, and the page link.

Next step: for your next open house, make one cut with the day and time as the first frame, and post it three days before. Then count how many people at the door mention it.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How long should an open house reel be?

Fifteen to twenty-five seconds. Its job is to say when and where, show the home briefly and send people to the page. A longer reel buries the time, which is the only fact that matters here.

When should I post an open house reel?

Three or four days before, and again the morning of as a reminder. A week early gets lost; after the event it is no longer an invitation.

Should the open house time be in the video or the caption?

Both, but on the first frame of the video above all. Captions collapse and many viewers watch on mute. The day and time should be readable on the screen in the first second and repeated at the end.

Can I reuse the open house reel for the next open house?

Yes. Change the first frame and the voiceover line with the new day and time and keep the scenes. The photos do not age between weekends.
