Posting a listing video on Instagram: caption, hashtags, link

Post the 9:16 reel natively, write a caption with the facts the video left out, use a few local hashtags, and put the property page link where taps work.

Post the 9:16 reel natively on Instagram, write a caption that carries the facts the video deliberately left out, use a few specific local hashtags, and put the property page link wherever Instagram currently lets you place a tappable link, then tell people in the caption where to find it. The reel's job is to stop the scroll; the caption's job is to give the numbers; the link's job is to turn interest into an enquiry on a page you control.

Which version to post

The vertical one. A 16:9 listing video posted as a reel sits as a thin strip with bars above and below, and it gets scrolled. The 9:16 social video built from portrait photos fills the screen. If you only have the horizontal video, make the vertical one before posting rather than cropping.

Post it as a reel, not a story only. A story is gone in a day; a reel stays on your profile and keeps working while the listing is live.

The caption carries what the video left out

The reel script says where the home is and three things a buyer cannot see in a photo. It does not read out square footage or the price, because spoken numbers are hard to hold. The caption is where those go.

  • First line. Neighbourhood or street character and the home type. This is the line people see before they tap more.
  • The numbers. Price, beds, baths, square feet, lot if it matters. One line, plain.
  • One or two things from the script, written down for the people who watched muted and skimmed the captions.
  • The open house, if there is one, with the day and time.
  • Where to go. Name the place the link lives. Full tour, photos and the enquiry form are on the property page, link in profile, or wherever you put it.

Keep it short enough to read in the time it takes the reel to loop once. Nobody reads a caption longer than the video.

Hashtags that do something

A wall of thirty generic tags looks like spam and reaches nobody who is buying. Use a handful that a local buyer or a local agent might follow or search: the town, the neighbourhood, the home type, the county, and your own brokerage or team tag if you use one consistently. Five to eight is plenty. Check Instagram's current guidance on hashtags if you want to go further, and watch what the tags do over a month rather than guessing.

The link problem and how to handle it

Instagram controls where tappable links can go, and that changes over time. Rather than assuming, check where the platform currently lets you place a link, put the branded property page link there, and name that location in the caption. The branded link carries your name and contact card, and the page has the gallery, the 16:9 video, the details and an enquiry form that asks when the visitor wants to see the home.

Do not send Instagram traffic to a third-party listing portal. It shows the buyer six other homes and three other agents. Send them to a page where the only agent is you.

A worked example: the Thursday post

A two-bed, two-bath condo with a balcony over a park, open house Sunday.

Reel: six portrait photos, balcony view first, 24 seconds, the agent's own voice, captions on, light music. Caption: Two beds over the park, a five-minute walk to the station. $385,000, 2 bed, 2 bath, 1,050 sq ft, parking included. New kitchen, in-unit laundry, gym downstairs. Open Sunday 1 to 3. Full tour and the enquiry form are at the link in my profile. Six hashtags: the neighbourhood, the city, the city plus condo, the county, the brokerage, and open house plus the city. Link in profile updated to the branded property page. Posted Thursday at lunch.

What goes wrong

  • Posting the horizontal video. Bars, small, scrolled.
  • A caption that repeats the script word for word. The caption should add the numbers, not restate the voiceover.
  • Thirty hashtags. Reach does not scale with tag count, and it reads as spam.
  • A link to a portal or to your homepage. The buyer wanted this home. Send them to this home.
  • A stale link in the profile. If the profile link points to last month's listing, every new reel sends people to the wrong page. Update it when you post.
  • Unlabelled staged photos. A virtually staged frame in the reel carries its label; the caption can repeat it in a few words.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should I post the listing video as a reel or a story?

As a reel, and optionally also as a story. A story disappears in a day; a reel stays on your profile and keeps working for the life of the listing. Use the vertical 9:16 version for both.

What should the caption on a listing reel say?

The facts the video left out: price, beds, baths, square feet, the open house, and where the full tour lives. Open with the neighbourhood and home type, keep it short, and name where the tappable link is so people know where to go.

How many hashtags should a listing reel use?

A handful, five to eight, chosen for a local buyer or agent: the town, the neighbourhood, the home type, the county and your brokerage or team tag. Check Instagram's current guidance and watch what the tags do over a month rather than guessing.

Where should the link in a listing post go?

To the branded property page for that listing, not to a portal or your homepage. The page has the gallery, the 16:9 video, the details and an enquiry form, and the only agent on it is you. Place the link wherever Instagram currently allows a tappable one.
