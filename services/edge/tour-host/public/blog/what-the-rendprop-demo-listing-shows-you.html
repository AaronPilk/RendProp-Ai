What the Rendprop demo listing shows you

The live demo at rendprop.com/f/estate-demo is a published property page: main photo, details, gallery, video on request, contact card and enquiry form.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. The demo listing at rendprop.com/f/estate-demo is a real published Rendprop property page. It shows you what a buyer sees: the main photo, the property details, the gallery, the listing video that plays on request, the contact card and the enquiry form that asks for a date. Open it on your phone, tap through it like a buyer, and use it to show a seller what their page will look like.

The demo listing at rendprop.com/f/estate-demo is a published Rendprop property page you can open right now, in a browser, with no app. It shows what every published listing shows: the main photo, the details, the gallery, the selected listing video that plays when you ask it to, a contact card and an enquiry form that asks for the date you want. Open it on your phone, because that is how buyers will open yours.

What to look at, section by section

  • The top of the page. The main photo and the headline details. Notice how much a buyer learns before scrolling.
  • The details. Price, beds, baths and square feet for this home. These are typed into the app in the website step; nothing is designed.
  • The gallery. Scroll it the way a buyer would, fast. Notice the order and the count; both are choices you will make for your own listing.
  • The video. It does not play until you tap it. Tap it. That is the photo-based listing video, built from the stills with scene order and camera motion planned by the app.
  • The contact card. Name, business, number, one tap. On the unbranded version of a page this section is absent.
  • The enquiry form. It asks for the date the visitor wants. That date is what lands in the agent's inbox.

How to use it in three real situations

Before you build your first listing

Open the demo, then open Rendprop and start a listing. As you move through the photo studio, the video step and the website step, you know what each decision turns into on the page. The main photo you choose is the one at the top. The video you select is the one that plays when a visitor asks for it. The details you type are the ones under the photo.

In a listing presentation

A seller asks what you will do to market the home. Hand them your phone with the demo open, or your own most recent published page if you have one. Let them scroll. Let them tap the video. Then say: "Your home will have one of these by Wednesday, with a branded link for me and an unbranded one for the MLS." The listing presentation post builds the rest of that conversation.

When a photographer pitches an agent

Send the demo link in the email with your rates. An agent who has opened a working page understands a "property website with every shoot" offer in ten seconds, where a paragraph describing it takes a minute and lands half as well.

A worked example: the Tuesday pitch

A photographer meets an agent for coffee on Tuesday. The agent lists a 3-bed in a cul-de-sac next week and asks what video would cost. The photographer opens the demo on her phone and slides it across the table. The agent scrolls the gallery, taps the video, watches twenty seconds, scrolls down to the form. "So buyers fill this in and it comes to me?" Yes, forwarded to your verified email, and it asks them for the date they want. The agent books the shoot with video and a page. The demo did the explaining.

What the demo cannot tell you

  • Your own photos. The page is only as good as the set behind it. The demo shows the structure; your shoot decides the result.
  • Your MLS rules. The unbranded link exists for the virtual-tour field, but whether and how your MLS accepts it is for you to check.
  • Plan details. Current plans and prices are on the Rendprop pricing page. Rendprop shows current plan features and included usage in the app, and Apple shows the price, billing period and any introductory offer terms before confirmation. The demo is a page, not a price list; see pricing for the current position.
  • How the app looks. The demo is the output. The input happens in the app on an iPhone running iOS 16 or later.

Reading the demo like a buyer, not an agent

Open it, count to three, and ask what you know about the home. Then scroll once and ask again. Then tap the video. Most buyers decide whether to book a showing in that sequence. A page that answers price, size, look and "who do I call" inside that minute is doing its job, and that is the standard your own pages should be held to. The phone-first post is about what buyers actually tap.

Next step: open the demo on your phone now, scroll it once fast and once slowly, and tap the video. Then start a listing in Rendprop with the photos from your most recent shoot and build the same page for a home of your own.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Where is the Rendprop demo listing?

At rendprop.com/f/estate-demo. It opens in any browser; no app is needed to view it. It is a real published property page, the same kind every listing gets.

Does the demo video autoplay?

No. Like every Rendprop property page, the listing video plays on request. Tap it to watch.

Can I use the demo in a listing presentation?

Yes. Open it on your phone and hand it to the seller, or use your own most recent published page. Seeing a working page explains the marketing faster than describing it.

What does the unbranded version of a page look like?

The same page without the name, contact details or Rendprop branding, made for the MLS virtual-tour field. The demo link is the branded form; every published listing gets both.
