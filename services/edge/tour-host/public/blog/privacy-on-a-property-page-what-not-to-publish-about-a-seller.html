Privacy on a property page: what not to publish about a seller

A property page sells the home, not the household. Keep names, faces, mail, documents and the reason for selling off the page and out of the photos.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A property page should show the home and nothing about the household. That means no seller names, no faces in photos, no mail or documents on counters, no children's names on bedroom doors, no visible medication, no security system details, and no mention of why they are selling. Check every gallery photo and the video at full size before publishing; a reflection or a fridge note gives away more than you expect.

A property page should show the home and nothing about the people who live in it. Keep the seller's name off the page, keep faces out of the photos, and before publishing go through every gallery image and the video at full size looking for mail on the counter, documents on the desk, a child's name on a bedroom door, medication on a nightstand, a security keypad with the code written beside it, or a family photo on the wall that shows faces. Never state why the seller is moving: the buyer needs the home, and the household is none of their business.

Most privacy leaks on listing pages are not decisions. They are things nobody looked for.

What stays off the page entirely

  • The seller's name. Not in the details, not in the video script, not in the URL. "The Hendersons' home" is a privacy leak and a negotiation leak.
  • The reason for selling. Divorce, job loss, a death, a relocation deadline. Any of these on the page tells a buyer how hard to push on price.
  • The seller's contact details. Enquiries go to you. If the seller wants to talk to buyers, that is a conversation to have with them first, not a line on the page.
  • Schedules. "Vacant weekdays" or "owners travel often" is information about when the home is empty, published to strangers.
  • Security details. Alarm company, camera positions, the lockbox location.

What to look for in the photos

Open each gallery photo at full size on a large screen, not a phone thumbnail, and scan the edges and surfaces.

  • Paper. Mail, bills, a calendar with appointments, a prescription label, a child's school worksheet with a name.
  • Screens. A laptop with an open inbox, a tablet on the counter, a monitor showing a document.
  • Walls and doors. Family photos with faces, diplomas with names, a child's name in letters on a door, a whiteboard with the week's plan.
  • Reflections. Mirrors, windows, oven doors and TV screens reflect the photographer and sometimes the family in the next room.
  • Nightstands and bathrooms. Medication, medical equipment, anything that says something about a person's health.
  • Fridge doors. Notes, photos, magnets from a doctor's office.
  • Exteriors. A car's licence plate, a neighbour in their yard, a house number if the seller wants the address withheld.

A worked example: the Tuesday review

The photographer delivers 29 photos of a 3-bed on a Tuesday. You open them full size. Photo 7, the kitchen: a stack of mail on the island with the seller's name readable. Photo 12, the hall: three framed family photos. Photo 19, the second bedroom: "EMMA" in wooden letters on the door. Photo 23, the primary bath: a prescription bottle on the vanity. Photo 26, the office: a monitor with a spreadsheet open.

Photo 7 is reshot or the mail is removed with a tidy-the-room edit, labelled. Photo 12 is reshot with the frames turned or removed. Photo 19 is reshot or the letters are edited out, labelled. Photo 23 is reshot. Photo 26 is reshot with the monitor off. Five photos out of 29, and none of them would have been caught on a phone screen.

The video has the same problems, moving

A photo-based video is made from the photos, so if the photos are clean, the video is clean. A recorded walkthrough is not. A handheld pass through the kitchen catches the fridge door, the hall catches the family photos, and the voiceover might say "the owners added the deck in 2019" which is harmless, or "the owners are relocating for work" which is not. Watch the walkthrough once purely for privacy, with no attention to pacing or quality.

The address question

Some sellers want the address off the page: high-profile people, people with safety concerns, people who do not want neighbours to know yet. The page can show the neighbourhood and the street without the number, and the main photo can avoid the house number. Check your MLS's current rules for the listing entry itself, which is a separate matter from what the shareable page shows. Agree with the seller in writing before publishing.

Privacy slips that get past a quick look

  • Reviewing on a phone. Thumbnails hide everything above. Use a big screen.
  • Trusting the photographer to catch it. A good photographer tidies, but the agent is responsible for what is published.
  • Editing out a privacy problem and forgetting to label the edit. Removing mail is a tidy-the-room edit, which makes the photo an altered image; check your MLS's current rules on disclosure. Remove it physically and reshoot where you can; label the edit where you cannot.
  • The seller's own social posts. A seller who shares the page with "so sad to leave after Dad passed" has told every buyer why the price is soft. Ask them to share the link without the backstory.

Make it a step, not a hope

Add a line to your pre-publish checklist: "Every photo full size, every surface, every reflection. Video watched for privacy." Ten minutes per listing. The seller never knows you did it, which is the point.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should the seller's name appear anywhere on the property page?

No. Not in the details, not in the video script, not in the link. The buyer does not need it, and a name makes the seller searchable, which can expose the reason for the sale and weaken their negotiating position.

Is it fine to edit out mail or family photos with AI instead of reshooting?

Reshoot where you can; it is cleaner and needs no label. Where you cannot, a tidy-the-room edit works but is an altered image, so label it and check your MLS rules on disclosure. Keep the original on file.

Can I leave the address off the property page?

Yes, if the seller prefers. Show the neighbourhood or the street without the number and keep the house number out of the main photo. Your MLS has its own rules for the listing entry, which are separate from what your shareable page shows.

What should I check in a recorded walkthrough for privacy?

Fridge doors, desks, nightstands, mirrors and family photos on walls, plus anything the voiceover says about the owners. Watch it once with privacy as the only focus, before you judge pacing or quality.
