Photographer branding on a property page: what is allowed

Where a photographer's name can appear on a listing page, where it cannot, and why the MLS link carries no branding at all. Rules and convention.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. On a client's listing page the branding belongs to the agent, and on the MLS link there is no branding at all. A photographer's credit lives in the delivery email, the agent's social captions where they agree to it, and your own portfolio, not on the page a buyer uses to enquire. Check the agent's MLS rules and your contract before putting your name anywhere on a listing.

On a property page you publish for an agent, the branding belongs to the agent: their name, their business, their number on the contact card. On the unbranded link that goes in the MLS virtual-tour field there is no branding at all, not the agent's and not yours. A photographer's name belongs in the delivery email, in the agent's social captions where they agree to credit you, and on your own portfolio. It does not belong on the page a buyer uses to enquire about someone else's listing, unless the agent has asked for it and their MLS allows it.

That is the convention, and it exists because the page is the agent's marketing, paid for by the agent, pointed at the agent's buyers. Your name there is a distraction at best and a compliance note at worst.

The two links and who they belong to

Every published Rendprop listing has a branded page at rendprop.com/f/ and an unbranded page at rendprop.com/u/. The branded page carries the contact card and the enquiry form. When a photographer presents a client's contact details on the listing, that card is the agent's. The unbranded page has no name, no contact details and no Rendprop branding, built for the MLS virtual-tour field; whatever your client's MLS says about agent branding in that field, assume it applies to yours as well. The two links page explains the split, and the MLS rules post covers what to check with the board.

What to check with the MLS

Boards differ, and the current rules for the MLS your client belongs to are the only ones that matter. The question to ask is whether the virtual-tour link may carry branding, contact details or calls to action, because that link is syndicated to other brokers' sites, and whether a photographer's credit counts as branding; assume it does unless the board says otherwise. The unbranded link exists for that field: no name, no contact details, no Rendprop branding, so there is nothing in it to question.

On the branded page, which the agent uses in their own email and social, the MLS has less to say, but the agent's brokerage might. Some brokerages have rules about third-party names on listing marketing. Ask.

Where your credit does belong

  • The delivery email. Your name and business are on the message that carries the links. Agents forward it.
  • The agent's social posts. Many agents are happy to tag the photographer on a reel. Ask once per client, and they will usually say yes.
  • Your own portfolio listings. A listing you publish for yourself, in your own workspace, with your own details on the contact card. That is your showcase, and it is the page you send to prospective agents.
  • Your contract and invoice. Which is where the licence terms live anyway.

You own your photos and videos. Rendprop does not use your content for its own marketing or AI training without written permission. What the agent may do with the files is between you and the agent, and the contract is the place to set it, not a watermark on the page.

A lakefront listing, credited the right way

A photographer publishes a page for an agent's lakefront listing. The page shows the agent's name, brokerage and mobile on the contact card. The unbranded link goes to the MLS. The agent posts the reel on Instagram and tags the photographer in the caption, as agreed at the first booking. A second agent at the same brokerage sees the tag, opens the photographer's own portfolio page, which carries the photographer's contact card, and books a shoot. The credit did its job without touching the client's page.

Now the counter-example. A photographer adds their logo to the main photo of a client's listing. The agent pastes the unbranded link into the MLS, but the main photo with the logo goes into the MLS photo upload, where the agent's board does not want it. The agent has to ask for a clean set and does not rebook.

If the agent wants you credited on the page

Some agents, especially on high-end listings, want the photographer named. If so, agree the wording and the placement in writing, confirm with the agent that their brokerage and MLS allow it on the branded page, and keep it off the unbranded link and off the photos themselves. Treat it as the agent's decision about the agent's page. The contact details post covers how the page presents whoever is meant to be contacted.

Mistakes that cost bookings

  • Watermarking delivered photos. They end up in the MLS photo upload, and the agent has to come back to you for a clean set.
  • Your contact card on the client's page. Buyers call you. Check the live page before sending links.
  • Sending the branded link for the MLS. Even with the agent's card and not yours, it is the wrong link for that field.
  • Crediting yourself in the listing description. That is the agent's copy, and their MLS rules may apply to it.
  • Assuming last year's rules. Boards update. Check the current rules each time a client joins a new MLS.

Next step: publish one portfolio listing of your own with your details on the card, and make that the page you send to new agents. Keep client pages clean.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Can I put my logo on a client's property page?

Not by default. The page is the agent's marketing and carries their contact card. If the agent wants you credited on the branded page, agree it in writing and confirm their brokerage and MLS allow it. Keep the unbranded MLS link free of any branding.

Why does the unbranded link have no branding at all?

Because the virtual-tour field is syndicated to other brokers' sites, and the link that goes there needs to work without anyone's name on it. The unbranded link has no name, no contact details and no Rendprop branding for that reason. Check your client's MLS's current rules on the field.

Where should a photographer be credited?

In the delivery email, in the agent's social captions where they agree to tag you, and on your own portfolio listings published with your own contact card. Those reach prospective agents without touching the client's page.

Who owns the photos on the page?

You do. Rendprop does not use your content for its own marketing or AI training without written permission. What the agent may do with the files is set by your contract with them.
