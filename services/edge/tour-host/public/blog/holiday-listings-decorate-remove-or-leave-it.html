Holiday listings: decorate, remove or leave it

Listing photos outlast the holiday, so shoot the house undecorated and let the sellers decorate for showings. Here is the rule and the exceptions.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Shoot the listing photos without holiday decorations, because the photos will still be online in January and February. Let the sellers decorate for showings if they want to, within reason. The one exception is a tasteful exterior twilight with white lights, and even that should be a second version, not the only one.

Shoot the listing photos with the decorations down, because the photos will still be the listing in January and February when the tree is long gone. Let the sellers decorate for showings if they want to, modestly, because a warm house in December shows well in person. The exception is a lit exterior at twilight with plain white lights, and even that belongs in the gallery as a second exterior, not the main photo.

The rule: photos outlast the season

A listing that goes live on December 2 has a real chance of still being active in late January. A gallery full of garland and a tree in the bay window tells every buyer who finds it in February exactly how long it has been sitting.

The same applies to the video. A listing video that pans past a decorated mantel is a video with an expiry date. If the sellers insist on decorations in the photos, shoot two sets of the affected rooms and keep the undecorated set as the one on the page and in the video.

Decorate, remove or leave it: a working list

  • Remove for the photos. The tree, stockings on the mantel, garland on the stair rail, anything inflatable, window clings, holiday towels and bedding, the countertop village. Anything that names a specific holiday or a specific week.
  • Leave it. A plain evergreen wreath on the front door, a bowl of pinecones on the table, white candles, a throw on the sofa. Seasonal without being dated. A wreath in a January photo looks like winter, not like a missed deadline.
  • Decorate for showings only. Once the photos are done, the sellers can put the tree back up. A buyer walking into a warm, lightly decorated house in December has a good experience and does not compare it against the undecorated photos. Keep the paths clear and keep the tree out of the main walkway.
  • The lit exterior exception. A twilight photo of the house with plain white lights along the roofline and lit windows can be a beautiful second exterior. Colored lights, animated displays and lawn figures do not qualify. Keep the daytime exterior as the main photo and put the lit one a few frames in.

Showings are not photos

Where sellers push back is on the idea that the house should be bare for the whole holiday. It does not need to be. The photos take a morning. After that, the decorations can go back up and stay up until the sellers would normally take them down. The conversation is easier when you say it that way: one morning without the tree, not a whole December.

For showings, ask for two things. No scent plug-ins or candles burning, because strong scent makes buyers wonder what it covers. And a clear path through every room, because a tree in a small living room makes the room read small and the buyer will remember the size, not the tree.

The split-level on December 2

A three-bed split-level, listing planned for Tuesday, December 2, sellers with a tree already up in the lower family room and garland on the stair rail. They wanted both in the photos. The compromise took five minutes to agree. Saturday morning the garland came down and the tree went into the garage for the day. The photographer shot at ten, interiors first, then the front exterior at midday under an overcast sky. He came back at dusk and shot the house with the white roofline lights on and every window lit, which the sellers loved. The tree went back up Saturday evening.

The gallery led with the daytime exterior, then the kitchen, the living room and the rest in walking order, with the lit twilight as the fifth frame. The video used the undecorated interiors and finished on the lit house. The listing was still active on January 20, and nothing in it said December. The sellers hosted a showing on December 23 with the tree up and the stair rail bare, and the buyer who came through that evening had seen a clean, season-neutral gallery and found a warm house.

If the house is going to be lived in through the listing, the broader approach is in marketing a listing while the owner still lives there.

Where holiday listings go wrong

Removing decorations with an AI edit instead of by hand. It is tempting to shoot the tree and ask the tidy the room edit to remove it. Do not rely on it. The edit has to invent whatever was behind the tree, which means a window, a corner or a section of floor the model guessed at. If you do try it, compare the edit against the original frame by frame and expect to reject it. Moving the tree to the garage for a morning is cheaper and honest. The limits of that edit are covered in tidy the room: decluttering without misleading buyers.

Shooting only the lit exterior. It is the prettiest frame of the day and sellers want it as the main photo. In February it is a decorated house. Daytime main photo, lit twilight as the supporting frame.

Letting the holiday set the launch date. A listing that launches on December 23 launches into a week when buyers are travelling. Either launch early in the month or hold for the first full week of January, and decide that with the seller rather than drifting into it.

Forgetting the video has the same problem. A reel posted in December with garland in every scene cannot be reposted in January. Build it from the undecorated set.

How this works in Rendprop

Add the undecorated photos to the listing as landscape photos for the listing video and portrait photos for the social cut, and add the lit twilight exterior alongside the daytime one. When you publish the property website, choose the daytime exterior as the main photo; the gallery and the video are built from whichever saved versions you select. If you experiment with a tidy the room edit on a decorated frame, the photo studio stores it as a separate version with an AI-alteration disclosure and keeps the untouched original in photo history, so comparing the two is built in and rejecting the edit costs nothing. In January, nothing on the page needs to change.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should the Christmas tree be in the listing photos?

No. The photos will still be live in January and a tree dates them to one week of the year. Move the tree out for the morning of the shoot and let the sellers put it back for showings. Shooting the room twice, with and without, is the fallback if they insist.

Can sellers keep decorations up for showings?

Yes, within reason. A lightly decorated, warm house shows well in December. Keep walkways clear, keep the tree out of the main path through the living room, and skip burning candles and scent plug-ins.

Is a photo of the house with holiday lights a good main photo?

It is a good supporting photo. A twilight exterior with plain white lights and lit windows can sit a few frames into the gallery, but the main photo should be the daytime exterior that still looks right in February. Colored or animated displays should not be in the photos at all.

Can I use an AI edit to remove decorations from a photo?

You can try, but the edit has to invent whatever was behind the decorations, and it carries an AI-alteration disclosure. Compare it carefully against the original and expect to reject it. Taking the decorations down for one morning is simpler and honest.

Should I list in late December or wait until January?

Decide it deliberately with the seller. Launching early in December gives you the serious buyers who are still looking; launching the week of the holiday puts the listing in front of people who are travelling. If early December is not possible, the first full week of January is usually the better launch.
