Writing the virtually staged note that keeps everyone honest

The note says which photos are staged, that the furniture is not there or included, and that unstaged photos exist. Two sentences, same words everywhere.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. The virtually staged note should say which photos are staged, that the furniture shown is not present and not included, and that unstaged photos are available. Two sentences, in the same words everywhere the photos appear. It protects the buyer from a surprise, the seller from a complaint, and you from the conversation that follows both.

A virtually staged note needs to say three things: which photos are staged, that the furniture shown is not in the home and is not included, and that unstaged photos are available. Two sentences cover it. “Photos 2, 3 and 6 are virtually staged; the home is vacant and the furniture shown is not included. Unstaged photos are available on request.” Write it once and use the same words everywhere the photos go.

The note does more than satisfy a rule. It sets the buyer's expectation before they drive over, so the empty living room is what they expected rather than a let-down. It protects the seller from a buyer who feels misled. And it saves you the conversation at the door that starts with “where's the furniture”. Check your MLS's current rules on where the note must appear and whether particular wording is required, and ask your broker; the shape below is a starting point, not a legal standard.

The three things the note must cover

  • Which photos. By number, or by room if the gallery is not numbered: “the living room, dining room and primary bedroom photos”. A note that says “some photos” leaves the buyer guessing which rooms are real.
  • What is true in the room. That it is vacant, or that the furniture shown replaces what is there, and that none of it is included in the sale.
  • Where the real ones are. That unstaged photos are available on request, or that they follow in the gallery, if you include both versions.

Three lengths for three places

For the MLS remarks, where characters are limited: “Photos 2, 3 and 6 virtually staged; home is vacant, furniture not included. Unstaged photos on request.”

For the property page story section, where there is room to be plain: “The living room, dining room and primary bedroom photos are virtually staged to show the scale of the rooms. The home is vacant and none of the furniture shown is included. Unstaged versions of each photo are available on request.”

For a social caption, one line: “Living, dining and primary bedroom photos virtually staged; the home is vacant.”

The facts are the same in all three. Only the length changes.

The seller conversation that comes first

Write the note with the seller, not after. Show them the staged and the unstaged versions side by side, explain that the note will say the home is vacant, and agree on which rooms to stage. Sellers who understand why the note exists stop asking for it to be smaller. The post on sellers and AI staging covers that conversation in full.

The same note, two ways

Before: “Gorgeous, fully furnished living spaces throughout. Furniture shown for illustration purposes.”

After: “Photos 2, 3 and 6 are virtually staged; the home is vacant and the furniture shown is not included. Unstaged photos are available on request. The living room is 15 by 20 with oak floors and a wood-burning fireplace.”

The first version calls the home furnished and then half-retracts it in a phrase nobody reads. The second says what was done and then describes the room that exists.

Where the note goes wrong

  • “Staged” without “virtually”. Staged on its own means a stager brought real furniture. The buyer expects to see it.
  • The note on photo 1 when photos 4 to 9 are the staged ones. Put it where the staged photos are and in the text.
  • “For illustration purposes.” It does not say the room is empty or that the furniture is not included. Say both.
  • Different notes in different places. The remarks say one thing, the caption another, the flyer nothing. One note, pasted.
  • Staging a room and then describing what it is “perfect as”. The staged nursery makes the point without the sentence, and the sentence describes people rather than the room; it is the kind of line to run past your broker.
  • Describing the staged furniture. “Elegant dining set” is not in the house. The description belongs to the room's dimensions and windows.
  • Staging that changes the room. If an edit moved a wall, removed a radiator or added a window, that is not staging and that version should not be published at all. Compare every staged photo against the original before it goes anywhere.

How this works in Rendprop

In Rendprop's photo studio, an add-furniture edit is saved as a separate version labelled “Virtually staged”, and the untouched original stays in the listing's photo history, so “unstaged photos available on request” is a promise you can keep. AI can change or invent details, so compare each staged version against its original before choosing which to publish. The note itself goes in the story section on the property page, which shows it with the details you typed, the gallery and your contact card; the same line can go in the caption of any social video built from those photos. Rendprop labels the version; the note in the text is yours to write.

Write the note now, with numbers

Open the gallery, write down the numbers of the staged photos, and write the two sentences with those numbers in them. Paste the sentences into the remarks, the story section and the caption before the listing goes live, and keep the unstaged versions ready to send.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What should a virtually staged note say?

Which photos are staged, that the home is vacant or the furniture shown replaces what is there, that none of it is included in the sale, and that unstaged photos are available. Two sentences cover it. Check your MLS's current rules for required wording or placement and ask your broker about office policy.

Is staged the same as virtually staged?

No. Staged on its own means a stager placed real furniture in the home, and the buyer expects to see it at the showing. Virtually staged means the furniture exists only in the photo. Always include the word virtually.

Should I include the unstaged photos in the gallery too?

Many agents do, placing each unstaged photo right after its staged version, so the buyer sees both without asking. If you do, the note can say the unstaged photo follows. If not, keep the unstaged versions ready to send on request.

Does the note need to be on the photo as well as in the text?

Often yes. A label on the staged photo and a note in the text cover the buyer who only looks at pictures and the buyer who only reads. Check your MLS's current rules on where the disclosure must appear, and use the same wording in both places.
