Photo mistakes that make a $900,000 home look like a rental

Tilted verticals, flash, bathroom clutter, the seller's life in frame, two colours of light, the doorway at 0.5x and HDR glow. Seven fixes before any edit.

Seven mistakes do most of the damage: tilted verticals, the phone flash, toilet lids up and towels on the floor, personal clutter, mixed light colours, rooms shot from the doorway at 0.5x, and over-processed HDR with halos around every window. Each one reads as a rental inspection photo, and a buyer looking at a $900,000 listing is pricing the care that went into the house by the care that went into the photos. Fix all seven before you think about edits.

1. Verticals that lean

A door frame that tilts, a wall that leans in, a fridge that looks like it is falling over. It comes from tilting the phone up to get more ceiling or down to get more floor. Hold the phone level and step back instead. This is the single biggest difference between a phone shoot and a professional one, and it is free.

2. The flash

A hot spot on the far wall, hard shadows behind the furniture, a reflection in every window and a dark corner. Turn on the lights in the room and leave the flash off. Fixing dark rooms without flash covers what to do instead.

3. Bathroom signals

Toilet lid up. Bath mat on the floor. Toothbrushes on the vanity. A towel over the shower door. A plunger. Each one says someone lives here and did not expect company. Lid down, mats up, surfaces clear, one folded towel, and shoot from the doorway at the height of the vanity.

4. The seller's life in the frame

Photos on the fridge, a calendar on the wall, shoes by the door, the dog bed, the charging cables on the nightstand. None of it is wrong in a home. All of it is wrong in a listing, because the buyer is trying to picture their own life in the room and the seller's is in the way. Clear it before the shoot. A tidy-the-room edit can clear one frame afterward, with a disclosure, but a whole house of clutter needs a weekend, not an edit.

5. Two colours of light

Warm lamps and a cool overhead in the same room make half the wall orange and half blue. Daylight from the window and tungsten from a lamp do the same. Pick one: either all the lamps on with the overhead off, or swap the bulbs so they match. A room with one colour of light looks finished. A room with two looks like a rental.

6. The doorway at 0.5x

Standing in the doorway and shooting the whole room on the wide lens is the fastest way to photograph a house and the fastest way to make every room look the same. The foreground stretches, the walls bend, and the frame has no subject. Walk to the corner, switch to 1x, find the thing in the room worth looking at, and put it in the middle third. 0.5x wide listing photos covers the few rooms where the wide lens is right.

7. The HDR glow

Over-processed HDR gives every window a grey halo, every shadow a lifted muddy tone and every surface the same flat brightness. It was meant to fix the dark room and the bright window at once, and pushed too far it fixes neither. Keep the processing light. A window that is a little bright is fine. A room that glows is not.

A worked example: the $900,000 colonial, two shoots

The first set: shot by the seller's nephew on a Sunday evening, flash on, from each doorway at 0.5x, lids up, family photos on every surface, the kitchen lit by one warm pendant and one cool ceiling panel. It looks like the photos on a rental listing at a third of the price, and the showings reflect that.

The second set, a week later: Tuesday at 10am, every light on and every blind open, lens wiped, 1x from the corners, level, bathrooms cleared, one bowl on the counter, mismatched bulbs swapped the night before. Same house. The second set gets the showings the first should have.

Where edits come in, and where they do not

An AI edit can swap a grey sky, green a dormant lawn, clear one counter or stage an empty room. It cannot straighten a tilted frame without cropping the room away, remove flash shadows convincingly, or fix two colours of light without changing the room's materials. In Rendprop's photo studio, each edit is a separate version with a disclosure and the untouched original stays in the photo history, which is useful for the first four jobs and no help for the seven mistakes above. Shoot it right, then edit the one frame that still needs it.

Run the list before the shoot

Level, no flash, bathrooms cleared, clutter gone, one colour of light, 1x from the corner, light processing. Read it once before you lift the phone and the set will look like the price. For the room where the money shows, kitchen photos that sell goes angle by angle.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What is the most common mistake in listing photos?

Tilted verticals. Tilting the phone up for more ceiling or down for more floor makes walls lean and doors look like they are falling. Hold the phone level and step back. It costs nothing and is the biggest single difference between an amateur and a professional set.

Can an AI edit fix a photo taken with the flash?

Not convincingly. Flash shadows, hot spots and window reflections are baked into the exposure. An edit that lifts the dark corner invents what is there. Reshoot with the lights on and the flash off; it takes two minutes.

Why does my expensive listing look cheap in photos?

Usually a combination of the seven mistakes: leaning walls, flash, bathroom clutter, personal items, two colours of light, wide-lens doorway shots and heavy HDR. Buyers price the care in the house by the care in the photos. Fix the shooting before the editing.

How do I fix two colours of light in a room?

Turn off one source or swap the bulbs so they match. All lamps on with the overhead off usually works. A room with one colour of light reads as finished; a room with warm and cool mixed reads as a rental.
