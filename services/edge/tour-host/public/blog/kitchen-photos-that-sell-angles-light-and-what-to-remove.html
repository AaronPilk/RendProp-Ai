Kitchen photos that sell: angles, light and what to remove

Three frames: from the entry, down the island, and from the far corner. Every light on, no flash, counters cleared to one bowl. A galley kitchen example.

Three frames cover most kitchens: one from the entry showing the whole room, one down the island or counter toward the window, and one from the far corner looking back toward the living space. Shoot with the overhead lights on and the under-cabinet lights on, at a time when the window is not blowing out. Clear the counters of everything that is not a single bowl of fruit or a single plant, and take the magnets off the fridge.

Why the kitchen gets three frames when a bedroom gets one

Buyers rank the kitchen first or second in almost every walk-through, and it is the room where the money shows. Cabinets, counters, appliances and the layout are all decisions a buyer is pricing in their head. One frame cannot show the layout and the finish at the same time. Three can.

The three angles

From the entry. Stand where a buyer stands when they first see the kitchen. This frame shows the layout: where the island is, how the work triangle sits, how the room connects to dining or living. Shoot at the 1x lens if the room allows it, 0.5x only if you cannot step back far enough.

Down the island. Stand at one end of the island or the main run of counter and shoot along it toward the window or the range. The counter leads the eye, the finish is in sharp focus in the foreground, and the depth of the room reads. This is often the best kitchen frame in the set.

From the far corner. Turn around and shoot back toward where you came in. This frame shows the kitchen as part of the home rather than a room on its own, and it picks up the dining table or the sofa through the opening.

A fourth frame, a detail of the range and hood or a tiled backsplash, earns its place only when that detail is the upgrade the seller paid for.

Light

  • Turn on everything: overhead, pendants, under-cabinet, the range hood light. Kitchens look dead with only daylight.
  • Shoot when the kitchen window is bright but not direct. Morning for an east window, afternoon for a west one. Direct sun through the window burns a white patch into the frame.
  • If the pendants are warm and the overhead is cool, turn one set off. Two colours of light in one frame makes the counter look two colours.
  • Do not use the phone flash. It flattens the cabinets and puts a hot spot on the fridge.

What to remove before the shot

Everything on the counter except one bowl or one plant. The drying rack, the dish soap, the paper towels, the knife block, the toaster, the mail. Everything on the fridge: magnets, photos, the school calendar. The mat in front of the sink. The towel on the oven handle. The bin, even a nice one. The dog bowls.

Leave: a bowl of lemons or green apples, a single plant, the stools at the island pushed in. A kitchen with nothing on the counter reads as a show kitchen and buyers accept that.

A worked example: the galley kitchen in a 1990s townhouse

Narrow room, counters on both sides, window at the end. There is no island and the entry frame at 1x shows two walls of cabinets and not much else. The photographer skips the entry frame and shoots the galley end-on from the doorway at 1x, so both counters lead to the window. Second frame from the window end looking back toward the dining room. Third, a detail of the new quartz counter where it meets the backsplash, because that is what the seller spent on. Three frames, two of them usable in the listing video, one for the gallery.

Kitchen mistakes that cost the listing

A shot from the doorway at 0.5x that bends the cabinets. A frame with the dishwasher open. The seller's reflection in the oven door. A frame where the brightest thing is the window and the darkest thing is the kitchen. A tidy-the-room edit that removed the clutter and also removed the outlets along the backsplash, which is why you compare every edit against the original before it goes out. Shoot the island frame first

If you only have time for one careful kitchen frame, make it the one down the island or the main counter toward the window. It shows finish and depth in one shot, and it is the frame that gets the kitchen into the main gallery order and the video. FAQ

Questions on this topic

What is the best angle to photograph a kitchen for a listing?

Down the island or the main counter toward the window. The counter leads the eye, the finish is sharp in the foreground and the depth of the room reads. Add a frame from the entry for layout and one from the far corner looking back toward the living space.

Should the kitchen lights be on for listing photos?

Yes, all of them: overhead, pendants, under-cabinet and the range hood light. Kitchens look flat with daylight alone. If the pendants are warm and the overhead is cool, turn one set off so the counter is one colour.

What should I take off the counters before a kitchen photo?

Everything except one bowl of fruit or one plant. Drying rack, soap, paper towels, knife block, toaster, mail, the mat by the sink, the towel on the oven handle and the bin. Clear the fridge of magnets and photos too.

Can I fix a cluttered kitchen photo with an AI edit?

A tidy-the-room edit can clear one counter with a disclosure, but compare it against the original, because the edit can remove outlets along the backsplash along with the clutter. Clearing the counters before the shoot is faster and needs no label.
