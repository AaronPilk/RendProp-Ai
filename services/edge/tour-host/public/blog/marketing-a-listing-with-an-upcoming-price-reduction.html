Marketing a listing with an upcoming price reduction

A price reduction is a relaunch. Use the week before to fix what the first launch got wrong, then update price, page, video and posts in one morning.

A price reduction is a relaunch, and it deserves the same week of preparation the first launch got. Use the days before the change to fix whatever the first launch got wrong, which is usually the main photo, the opening of the video or a detail that was never filled in, so that the new price arrives on a better listing rather than the same one with a smaller number. A price change is a launch; treat it like one

When the price changes, the listing surfaces again: it reappears in saved searches, agents who dismissed it look again, and buyers who liked it at the old number recalculate. That is a second first impression, and most agents waste it by changing one field and sending one text.

The better plan is to ask why the first number did not work. Sometimes it was only the number. Often the listing underperformed too: the main photo was the one the seller liked rather than the one buyers click, the video opened on the hallway, the square footage field was blank. Fix those in the week before, and the reduction lands with momentum.

The week before: media and page prep

  • Look at the listing the way a returning buyer will. Open the page on your phone. Is the main photo the strongest frame? Does the video open on the exterior? Is anything missing from the details that a buyer would have to call about?
  • Change the main photo if a better one exists in the set. A new main photo is the cheapest signal that something is different. If the season has turned since the shoot, a new exterior is worth the photographer's short visit.
  • Re-cut the video if the opening is weak or the new main photo should lead it. A changed first scene makes the video read as new to everyone who already watched it.
  • Fill the gaps. Every empty detail field, the roof year, the lot size, the heating. The returning buyer should find more than they found last time.
  • Agree the number and the date with the seller in writing, and agree that nobody mentions it until the day. A reduction that leaks a week early is a week of buyers waiting.

Whether the reduction needs new photos or a new video is a judgement about what went wrong the first time; it is worked through in relisting after a price cut: new photos or new video.

Thursday: the update sequence

Do it in this order, in one sitting, so no buyer sees two different numbers.

  1. The MLS. The source of truth first, so the portals that pull from it update.
  2. The property page. The price typed into the listing, the new main photo set, the re-cut video published. Open it on your phone and check the number.
  3. Anything static with the old price on it. Flyers in the box on the sign, the brochure at the house, a pinned social post. Replace or remove.
  4. The buyers and agents who already saw it, by phone or text, with the one sentence.
  5. The email list, same sentence.
  6. The social post, last, with the new main photo or the re-cut video.

The cape on Weaver Street: five weeks in

A three-bed cape, listed in late April, five weeks on market with steady showings and no offers. The feedback had a pattern: buyers liked the house and thought it was priced against the renovated capes two streets over. The seller agreed a reduction for Thursday of week six.

The agent spent the weekend before on the listing rather than the number. The main photo was the front exterior the seller had chosen; the frame buyers had been clicking in the gallery was the back garden with the deck, so that became the main photo. The video had opened on the front door; she re-cut it to open on the garden and finish on the front at twilight. The roof year and the furnace year, both recent, had never been typed into the details; they went in.

Thursday at nine she changed the MLS. By ten the page showed the new price, the garden main photo and the re-cut video. She pulled the old flyers from the sign box and put new ones in. By eleven the sentence had gone to the six agents who had shown it and the two buyers who had come twice, by text. The email went at noon. The social post with the re-cut video went at one. The buyer who had come twice at the old price booked a third showing for Saturday, and the offer came Monday.

Price-reduction mistakes

Changing only the number. The returning buyer sees the same listing that did not work.

Leaking the date. Buyers who hear a reduction is coming wait for it, and sometimes wait for the next one.

Updating out of order. A flyer with the old price next to a page with the new one is a conversation you do not want at the open house.

Apologetic copy. Motivated seller, bring offers, priced to sell. State the new price and the showing time. The house has not changed; the number has.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What should I do before a price reduction goes live?

Use the week before to fix what the first launch got wrong: swap in a stronger main photo, re-cut the video so it opens on it, fill any empty detail fields, and write the one-sentence announcement in advance. Agree the number and the date with the seller in writing and keep it quiet until the day.

In what order should I update the price?

The MLS first, then the property page, then anything static with the old price such as flyers and pinned posts, then the buyers and agents who already saw the house, then the email list, then social. Doing it in one sitting avoids a buyer seeing two different numbers.

Should I announce a price reduction on social media?

Yes, last in the sequence, with the new main photo or the re-cut video and a plain sentence that gives the new price and the next showing time. Avoid phrases like motivated seller or priced to sell; the house has not changed, only the number.

Does a price reduction need new photos?

Not necessarily new ones, but usually a different main photo from the existing set and a re-cut video that opens on it. If the season has turned since the shoot, a short photographer visit for a new exterior is worth it so the listing does not look like it has been sitting.
