Marketing a listing in 48 hours: a realistic timeline

A realistic 48-hour timeline from signed agreement to live listing with photos, video, a property page and a first post, hour by hour, and what breaks it.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Forty-eight hours from signed agreement to a live listing with photos, video, page and first post is realistic if the shoot happens in the first morning and you do the review, video and page work the same afternoon and evening. It is not realistic if you wait on separate vendors for each piece.

Forty-eight hours from a signed listing agreement to a live listing with photos, a video, a property page and a first post is realistic on one condition: the shoot happens in the first morning and you do the review, the video and the page yourself that same afternoon and evening. It is not realistic if the video comes from one vendor, the staging from another and the site from a third, because each of those adds a delivery day you do not control. Here is the timeline that works, hour by hour.

Hour 0 to 4: decisions before anyone shoots

The agreement is signed at 10 a.m. Monday. Before the photographer is booked, settle three things with the seller: what gets shown and what stays out of frame, which rooms are empty and whether they will be virtually staged, and the price that goes on the page. Then book the photographer for Tuesday at 9 a.m. and tell the seller the house needs to be ready at 8:30, with lights on and counters clear. Send them a one-page prep list by text, not email.

Hour 23 to 26: the shoot

Tuesday morning. Be there. Not to direct the photographer, but to decide on the spot which angle of the living room is the main photo and whether the backyard needs a second pass once the sun moves. Ask for the delivery as a link to full-resolution files, landscape photos for the listing video and page, plus a handful of portrait photos for social if the photographer is willing. Agree a delivery time before they leave; same day is normal for a straightforward set.

Hour 28 to 31: review, edits, video

Tuesday afternoon, the photos arrive. This is the block most agents hand to a vendor and then wait on. Do it yourself:

  1. Cull to about thirty. Order them the way a buyer walks the home.
  2. Choose the main photo and lock it. It goes on the MLS, the page and the first post.
  3. Apply only the edits that fix a real problem: blue sky over the overcast front, staging in the two empty bedrooms. Compare each with the original. Keep the ones that look true, reject the ones that moved a wall or invented a window.
  4. Build the landscape listing video. Write a short script, record it or use an AI voice, captions on. Watch it once for wrong details.

Hour 31 to 33: the page

Tuesday evening. Publish the property page with the main photo, the gallery in order, the video selected, the details typed, your contact card and the enquiry form. Put both links, branded and unbranded, in a note you can reach from anywhere. Open the page on your own phone as if you were a buyer and tap everything.

Hour 46 to 48: MLS, links, first post

Wednesday morning. Enter the listing in the MLS with the same main photo and the unbranded link in the virtual-tour field. Post the vertical social video with the branded link. Send the email to your database. Text the handful of buyers who asked for this kind of home. By 10 a.m. Wednesday, forty-eight hours after the signature, the listing is live everywhere with consistent media and a page that collects enquiries.

What the seller sees

From the seller's side the sequence is: Monday a prep list, Tuesday a photographer, Tuesday night a link to their own home's page to check, Wednesday morning a text saying it is live. That last text should include the branded link so they can send it to their family. A seller who can see their home online within two days of signing stops worrying about whether you are doing anything.

What breaks the timeline

  • Waiting on a video vendor. A separately shot and edited video rarely lands inside forty-eight hours. Build the video from the photos instead and add a recorded walkthrough later if the home needs one.
  • A seller who is not ready at the shoot. A reshoot of one room costs a day. The prep list by text on Monday is what prevents this.
  • Reviewing edits late at night. Tired eyes approve a staged sofa that floats. If the review slips past 9 p.m., do it first thing Wednesday instead and push the launch to Wednesday noon.
  • Different details in different places. Type the price and the counts once and copy them. Fixing a wrong square footage after the email has gone out is a bad Wednesday.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Is 48 hours too fast to do the marketing well?

Not if the photos are good and you do the review yourself. The parts that take time in a slow launch are vendor handoffs, not the work. A listing that goes live within two days of signing with consistent media and a working enquiry form is better marketed than one that waits a week for a separate video.

What if the photographer cannot shoot the next morning?

Then the clock starts at the shoot, not the signature. Keep the rest of the timeline the same: review and video the afternoon of the shoot, page that evening, MLS and post the next morning.

Should the recorded walkthrough be part of the 48 hours?

Usually no. A photo-based listing video covers the launch. Record a handheld walkthrough on the next visit if the home's flow between rooms is a selling point, and add it to the page then.

When should the seller see the page?

The evening it is published, before the MLS entry and the first post. A seller who spots a wrong detail on Tuesday night saves you a correction on Wednesday after the email has gone out.
