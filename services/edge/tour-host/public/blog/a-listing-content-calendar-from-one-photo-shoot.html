A listing content calendar from one photo shoot

One photo shoot gives you three weeks of posts if you plan them before the first goes out: launch reel, room posts, twilight, open house, status changes.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. One good photo shoot of 25 to 35 photos gives you two to three weeks of posts if you plan them before you post the first: a just-listed reel, three or four single-room posts, a twilight exterior, an open-house announcement, a feature detail, an under-contract post and eventually the sold post.

One photo shoot of twenty-five to thirty-five photos gives you two to three weeks of posts, if you plan them before the first one goes out: a just-listed reel, three or four single-room posts, a twilight exterior, an open-house announcement, one detail, an under-contract post and, eventually, the sold post. The calendar is the point. Agents who post from the same shoot without one post three times in launch week and then nothing, which looks to a buyer like the listing went quiet.

Week one: the launch

  • Day 1, Tuesday. The just-listed reel: a vertical video from five or six photos, a thirty-second voiceover that says what the home is and who it suits, captions on, the branded link in the caption. The landscape listing video goes on the property page the same morning.
  • Day 2. The database email, with the main photo, three lines and the branded link. Not a post; an email.
  • Day 3. One room: the kitchen, usually. Three photos, one line about one thing in it. The branded link.
  • Day 5, Friday. The twilight exterior, if there is one, as a single still or a short clip. Evenings are when people imagine living somewhere.

Week two: rooms and details

  • Day 8. The main living space or the room with the best light, as a short vertical cut.
  • Day 10. A detail: the stone on the fireplace, the view from the back door, the built-in shelves. One photo, one sentence. Details are what people send to each other.
  • Day 11, Friday. The open-house announcement video, if there is one that weekend: three photos, the hours, one reason to come.
  • Day 12 or 13. Nothing. The open house is the post.

Week three: status and the second look

  • Day 15. A second-look post for people who missed the launch: a different room as the cover, a different first line. New buyers arrive every week.
  • Day 17. The outdoor space, or the bedroom, whichever has not been shown.
  • When it happens. Under contract: the main photo, a short line, the day count if it is good. Later, sold: the same main photo so people recognise it, a line about what happened, and the page marked sold so the link still tells the truth.

The rule behind the calendar

Each post shows one thing and says one thing. The launch reel is the exception, because it has to say what the home is. After that, a post about the kitchen is about the kitchen. This keeps the shoot going for three weeks instead of three days, and it means every post has a reason for someone to stop. The link is the same every time, the branded one, so the whole calendar points at one page.

A 3-bed in a cul-de-sac, planned on Monday night

The photographer delivers thirty-one photos on Monday. That evening, before anything is posted, the calendar is written in a note: Tuesday the launch reel (front, living room, kitchen, main bedroom, deck); Wednesday the email; Thursday the kitchen (three photos, the line is about the window over the sink); Friday the twilight front. Week two: Monday the living room clip; Wednesday the deck railing detail the photographer caught; Friday the open-house announcement for Saturday 1 to 3. Week three: Monday a second-look post with the deck as the cover; Wednesday the main bedroom. Under contract lands on day 19; the post goes out that afternoon. Eleven posts and one email from one shoot, each built in a few minutes because the plan already existed.

Where calendars fall apart

  • Posting everything in week one. Three posts on launch day, then silence. Space them.
  • The same cover photo every time. People scroll past what they have seen. A different first frame each post.
  • A different link in different posts. Portal link here, page link there. One branded link, every time.
  • Posting an edited photo without its label. If the staged bedroom goes out on day 17, the staged label goes with it, and the original stays in the set.
  • Forgetting the status posts. Under contract and sold are the posts other sellers notice most.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How many posts can one listing photo shoot support?

Ten to twelve over three weeks, plus the status posts, if each one shows one room or one detail. A thirty-photo shoot has more than enough frames; the limit is the plan, not the photos.

Should every post use video?

No. The launch reel, the room clips and the announcement are short vertical videos; a detail or a twilight exterior works as a single still. Mixing keeps the feed from looking automated.

What if the listing goes under contract in week one?

Post the under-contract update and stop the calendar. A sold post follows at closing. The rest of the plan rolls over to the next listing; the structure is the same for every home.

Which link goes in the posts?

The branded property page link, every time. One link means every post points at the gallery, the video, the details and the enquiry form, and you can see enquiries arrive from it in one place.
