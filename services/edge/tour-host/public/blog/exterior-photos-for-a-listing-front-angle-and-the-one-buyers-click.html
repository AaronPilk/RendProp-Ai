Exterior photos: front, angle and the one buyers click

Three exterior frames: a three-quarter angle from the lot corner, a straight-on front, and the other corner. Shoot when the sun is on the front.

Shoot three exterior frames: a three-quarter angle from the corner of the lot, a straight-on front elevation, and a frame from the other corner. The three-quarter angle is the one buyers click, because it shows depth and the house reads as a building rather than a facade. The straight-on frame goes second in the gallery. Shoot when the sun is on the front of the house, move the cars, and get the whole roofline in.

The three-quarter angle, and why it wins

From the corner of the lot you see the front and one side. The house gets a shape: a porch that sticks out, a garage that steps back, a roofline that changes. Straight on, every house is a rectangle with a door in it. At thumbnail size, which is where the main photo lives, the three-quarter frame is the one that looks like a home.

Pick the corner that shows the better side. If the garage dominates one side, shoot from the other so the front door and the windows lead.

Time of day

The sun should be on the front of the house. That means: a north-facing front is lit evenly all day and can be shot any time. An east-facing front wants morning. A west-facing front wants afternoon. A south-facing front wants late morning or mid-afternoon, not noon, when the eaves throw a hard shadow across the windows.

If you cannot pick the time, a bright overcast day is the next best thing, and the grey sky can be swapped with a disclosure. Blue sky replacement covers how to do that without the shadows giving it away.

What to move before you shoot

  • Cars out of the drive and off the street in front
  • Bins to the back
  • Hose rolled, kids' bikes gone, the basketball hoop folded if it folds
  • Porch furniture straightened, cushions on
  • Garage door closed
  • Any for-sale sign out of the frame, because the photo will be used after the sign changes

Ask the seller to do this the night before. On a Tuesday shoot, the text goes out Monday evening.

Camera position

Stand far enough back that the whole house and some lawn are in frame at the 1x lens. If the lot is tight and 1x will not get the house in, step into the street or the neighbour's drive before reaching for 0.5x, which bends the corners of the house and makes a flat lot look like a hill. Keep the phone level. Tilting up to get the roof makes the house lean back.

Hold the phone at chest height. Getting down low makes a small house look big and the buyer notices when they arrive. A step-ladder in the drive is a legitimate trick for a house set below the road, because it gets the camera back to level.

A worked example: the corner-lot 4-bed

West-facing front, so the shoot is booked for 3pm. The photographer shoots the three-quarter from the south corner, because the north side is all garage. Straight-on from the middle of the street, standing on the far kerb to get the full roofline at 1x. A third frame from the north corner anyway, in case the agent wants it. Then the rear from the back fence, with the deck and the yard.

Main photo: the south three-quarter. Gallery order: three-quarter, straight-on, then straight into the entry and living room. The rear exterior goes after the kitchen so the buyer sees the yard once they have seen the inside, and listing photo order explains why.

Exterior frames that lose the click

A frame with the sun behind the house, so the front is in shadow and the sky is white. A frame with a car in the drive. A 0.5x shot from the kerb that makes the garage bulge. A twilight edit on a house with no exterior fixtures. A frame that cuts the chimney off at the top. A frame shot in winter with a green lawn edit and bare trees.

The exterior on the property page and in the video

The front exterior is usually the main photo at the top of a published property page and the opening scene of the listing video. Book the exterior for the light

Everything else in a shoot can be done at any hour with the lights on. The exterior cannot. Check which way the front faces before you book, and put the exterior at the time of day that suits it, even if that means shooting it first or last. FAQ

Questions on this topic

What is the best angle for the front exterior photo of a house?

A three-quarter angle from the corner of the lot, showing the front and one side. It gives the house depth and shape, which is what survives at thumbnail size. Shoot the straight-on frame too, but run it second.

What time of day should I photograph the front of a house?

When the sun is on the front. East-facing fronts want morning, west-facing fronts want afternoon, south-facing fronts want late morning or mid-afternoon rather than noon. North-facing fronts are lit evenly and can be shot any time.

Should I use the wide lens for exterior photos?

Only if you cannot get the house in at 1x after stepping back to the street. The 0.5x lens bends the corners of the house and makes the garage bulge. Crossing the street at 1x almost always looks better.

What should be moved before an exterior listing photo?

Cars, bins, hoses, bikes and anything on the lawn. Close the garage door, straighten the porch furniture and keep any for-sale sign out of frame. Text the seller the night before with the list.
