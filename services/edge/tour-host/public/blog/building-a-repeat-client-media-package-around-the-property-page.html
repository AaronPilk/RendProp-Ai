Building a repeat-client media package around the property page

A package agents rebook is built around one deliverable they share: the property page. How to structure it, what to include and what to add.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Make the published property page the centre of every package, with the photo set, the landscape video and the agent's contact card included by default, and sell the vertical reel, twilight and staging as add-ons per listing. Agents rebook the photographer whose delivery they can share in a minute, not the one with the longest shot list.

A media package that agents rebook is built around the one deliverable they share: the property page. Put the edited photo set, the landscape video and the agent's own contact card on that page by default, send two links with every delivery, and sell the vertical reel, twilight and staging as per-listing add-ons on top. The page is what the agent forwards to the seller, pastes into the MLS and drops in the listing email, so it is the thing they remember you for.

Packages built on shot counts compete on price. Packages built on what the agent can do with the delivery compete on convenience, and convenience is what gets you the next booking without a quote.

What goes in by default

  • The photo set, shot along the path through the home so it works as a gallery and as a video.
  • The landscape video, built from the set, for the property page, the MLS link and YouTube.
  • The property page, with the main photo, price, beds, baths, square feet, the gallery, the video and an enquiry form that asks for the visitor's preferred date.
  • The agent's contact card on the page, with enquiries forwarded to them once their email is verified.
  • Two links: the branded page for their marketing and the unbranded page for the MLS virtual-tour field.

That is one price, one delivery message, no decisions for the agent on a standard listing. The property website page shows what the page includes.

What goes on top

Add-ons are the per-listing choices the agent makes on the booking form:

  1. Vertical social reel. From the portrait pass. The one most agents tick.
  2. Twilight conversion. One front exterior, labelled, with the daytime original.
  3. Virtual staging per room. For vacant listings, labelled Virtually staged, with the empty original.
  4. Recorded walkthrough. Handheld footage for the listings that justify a separate filmed product.

Typical U.S. rates as of August 2026 give the reference points an agent has in mind: listing video $250–600, social cutdowns $75–150, twilight $5–25 per photo, staging $5–15 per photo, single-property sites $9–150 per month. Your package replaces several of those lines with one, which is the argument for it. The pricing post goes through the numbers.

A worked package in use

An agent who lists two homes a month books you for a four-bed in April. She ticks the reel and the twilight. You shoot the landscape photos along the path plus five portrait frames, convert the front exterior, review the conversion against the original, build both videos, put her details on the page, publish, and send the branded link, the unbranded link, the 9:16 export and a suggested caption. She posts the reel the morning the listing goes live and forwards the page to the seller, who shares it with the neighbours. In May she books you again without asking the price. In June she refers a colleague, who asks what the package includes, and the answer is the page.

Why the page does the retention work

Three reasons agents come back for a page rather than a folder:

  • It is the seller-facing proof. An agent forwards the page to the seller on launch day. It shows the seller that their home has a video, a gallery and a site, and it makes the agent look good. That is a listing-presentation asset for the agent's next pitch.
  • It carries their leads. The enquiry form sends buyers to the agent, not to a portal. Enquiries land in the inbox and are forwarded when the agent's email is verified.
  • It stays consistent. Every listing you deliver looks the same to their clients, which is what a brand is.

Setting the package up

Write a one-page package sheet: what is included, the add-ons with prices, the one-line AI edit policy (every edit labelled, originals kept, nothing structural changed), and what the agent needs to send at booking: price, beds, baths, square feet, their name, business, mobile and enquiry email. Put the booking form online with the add-ons as tick boxes. The what agents ask for post covers what to anticipate on the form.

What breaks a repeat-client package

  • Inconsistent delivery. A page on one listing and a folder on the next. Make the page the default on every shoot.
  • The agent's details missing. A page with your card on it is a page the agent cannot share. Collect details at booking.
  • Add-ons that need a conversation. If the agent has to email you to ask about twilight, they will not. Tick boxes.
  • Unlabelled AI edits. One question from the agent's MLS about an unlabelled edit and the agent trusts the whole package less. Compare each edit with its original, leave the disclosure on, and tell agents to check their MLS rules.
  • Pages left live after closing. Mark listings sold or archived when the agent tells you so the page says so.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should the property page be an add-on or included?

Included. The page is what the agent shares and what makes your delivery different from a folder. Keep add-ons for the per-listing choices: reel, twilight, staging, walkthrough.

How do I present the package to agents who already have a site tool?

Send a page from a listing you have shot and let them compare it with what they pay for. Typical U.S. rates as of August 2026 put single-property sites at $9–150 per month, and your page is included with the shoot.

What details do I need from the agent to build the page?

Price, beds, baths and square feet for the home, plus the agent's name, business, mobile and the email enquiries should reach. Ask at booking so the page is complete when you publish.

Can one package work for both residential and business clients?

The structure does. Rendprop has modes for real estate, event venues, restaurants and bars, gyms and studios, retail and other businesses, and the page fields change with the mode, so a venue client gets hours, capacity and menu fields instead of beds and baths.
