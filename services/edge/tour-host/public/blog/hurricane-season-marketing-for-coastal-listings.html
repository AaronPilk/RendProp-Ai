Hurricane season marketing for coastal listings

Shoot a coastal listing before the forecast turns, keep the originals, say what you can verify about storms, and have a pause plan ready for storm week.

Market a coastal listing in hurricane season by shooting early, in the first calm and clear window, and keeping every original photo because you may not get a second shoot. Say only what you can verify about the house and storms, send insurance and elevation questions to the buyer's insurer and inspector, and have a written pause plan for the week a storm shows up in the forecast. The listings that go wrong in storm season are the ones that overpromise in the copy and go silent when the weather turns.

Shoot at the first good window, not the convenient one

Coastal light in summer is at its best early in the morning and in the hour before sunset, and the weather can close on you for a week at a time. When the forecast shows a clear morning, take it, even if it is three days earlier than planned and the sellers have not finished the prep. A clean set of exterior photos taken before the season turns is an asset you cannot recreate in October if the dune walk is covered in debris.

Shoot more than you need: the front from the street, the back from the water side, the view from the deck, the dock or beach access, the ground-level garage or storage, the stairs. Landscape photos of each for the listing video, portrait frames of the view and the deck for the social cut. If the house has shutters or storm panels, photograph them stored neatly rather than deployed; a buyer wants to know they exist, not to see the house boarded up.

What to say about storms, and what to leave to others

Buyers will ask. The honest position is to describe what you can point to and to send the rest to the right professional.

  • You can describe: features you can see and the seller can document. Impact windows installed in a given year with the receipt. A metal roof. A generator and its transfer switch. A sump pump. The elevation certificate, if the seller has one, handed over as a document rather than summarised.
  • You should not say: that the house is safe in a storm, that it has never flooded, that insurance will be affordable, or anything about what a flood zone designation means for the buyer. Those are questions for the insurer, the inspector and the buyer's own research.
  • Disclosure: disclose what your state and your broker require about past damage and flood history, in the form they require. Ask your broker what that is before the listing goes live rather than after a buyer asks.

The pause plan, written before you need it

When a storm enters the forecast, the listing needs to go quiet in an orderly way, and then come back the same way. Write it down now.

  1. Showings. Decide the point at which showings stop, agree it with the seller, and tell any buyer with a scheduled visit the same day.
  2. Posting. Scheduled social posts about the listing come down while the storm is in the news. A reel of a beach house playing while the beach is being evacuated reads badly, however good the reel is.
  3. The page. The property page stays up; it is where out-of-town buyers go to check the listing is still active. Enquiries that come in during the week get a short, calm reply: still available, showings resume when it is safe, here is the video in the meantime.
  4. After. Walk the property with the seller before showings resume. If anything changed, reshoot that part and replace those photos. Do not leave a pre-storm photo of a deck that is no longer there.

The stilt house on Pelican Lane: late August

A three-bed on pilings on a barrier island, listing planned for the last week of August. The forecast showed a clear Tuesday and unsettled weather from Thursday. The photographer moved the shoot to Tuesday at seven in the morning, shot the exterior from the street and the beach side, every interior, the deck and the view, then came back for the evening deck and sunset frames. The whole set was in hand by Tuesday night.

The listing went live Thursday. The following week a storm appeared in the extended forecast. The agent texted the two buyers with weekend showings on Monday, pulled the scheduled reels, and left the page live with the video. One out-of-state buyer enquired on Wednesday through the page, got a reply within the hour and watched the video that evening. The storm turned away; showings resumed the next Saturday with no changes to the photos, and that buyer flew in the week after.

Mistakes that come back after the storm

Copy that promises safety. Any phrase that reads as a promise about storms becomes a problem the moment a storm arrives. Describe features, not outcomes.

Editing the sky to hide the season. A blue sky edit over a grey morning is defensible with a disclosure. An edit that removes the deployed shutters, the sandbags or the high-water stain on the garage wall is not. Compare every edit against the original and reject anything that changes the condition of the property.

Going silent. A listing that stops answering during storm week loses the buyers who were most committed. A short reply with the video link keeps them.

Reusing pre-storm photos of something that changed. Walk the property first, every time.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

When should I shoot a coastal listing in hurricane season?

At the first calm, clear window, even if it is earlier than planned. Weather can close in for a week or more, and a clean exterior set taken early is an asset you may not be able to recreate later in the season. Shoot more than you need.

What can I say about storms in the listing?

Describe features you can see and the seller can document: impact windows with a receipt, a metal roof, a generator, an elevation certificate handed over as a document. Do not say the house is safe, has never flooded, or that insurance will be affordable. Send those questions to the insurer and the inspector, and disclose what your state and broker require.

Should I take the listing down during a storm?

Pause showings and scheduled social posts, but leave the property page up. Out-of-town buyers use it to check the listing is still active, and a calm reply to enquiries with the video link keeps committed buyers through the week. Walk the property with the seller before showings resume.

Can I edit storm damage or shutters out of a photo?

No. A sky edit over a grey morning with a disclosure is defensible; removing deployed shutters, sandbags or a water stain changes the condition of the property and will be found at the showing or the inspection. Compare every edit against the original and reject anything that alters condition.
