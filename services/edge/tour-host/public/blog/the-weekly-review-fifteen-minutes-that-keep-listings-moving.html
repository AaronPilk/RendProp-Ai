The weekly review: fifteen minutes that keep listings moving

Fifteen minutes every Monday, one listing at a time: enquiries, showings and feedback, what changed, and one media action for the week.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Fifteen minutes every Monday, one listing at a time: enquiries received and answered, showings held and what they said, what changed in the market or the home, and the one media action for the week. Listings go stale when nobody looks at them on a schedule, not when the market turns.

Fifteen minutes every Monday morning, one listing at a time, four questions each: what enquiries came in and were they all answered, what showings happened and what did they say, what changed in the market or the home since last week, and what is the one media action for this week. Then a two-line text to each seller. That is the whole review. Listings go stale when nobody looks at them on a schedule, not when the market turns, and the schedule is the part most agents skip.

The four questions, per listing

  1. Enquiries. How many came in last week, from where, and is every one of them answered. An unanswered enquiry from Thursday is the first thing to fix on Monday, before anything else in the review.
  2. Showings and feedback. How many, and what was said. Write the feedback down in the buyer's words, not yours. Three showings that mention the same small room is information; three showings with no feedback is a call to make.
  3. What changed. A new comparable listing on the street, a price cut on the one across town, a seller who finally cleared the garage, a season that turned since the photos. Any of these can mean the media or the price needs to move.
  4. The one action. One thing for this listing this week: a new vertical cut from a different room, a database email, flyers to the street, a reshoot of one exterior, a price conversation. One, not five. Write it down with a day.

The two-line text to the seller

After the four questions, text the seller: last week's enquiries and showings as numbers, and the one action with its day. Two lines. Sellers who hear real numbers every Monday stop calling on Wednesday to ask whether anything is happening, and when the price conversation comes, they have seen the numbers that lead to it for weeks. The text takes a minute and saves a phone call.

Four listings in fifteen minutes

Monday, 8:30 a.m., coffee, phone, the inbox and the showing log open.

  • The townhouse, week two. Six enquiries, all answered; four showings; feedback likes the kitchen, two mention street parking. Nothing changed. Action: a vertical cut of the kitchen Wednesday with the branded link. Text the seller. Three minutes.
  • The split-level, week five. One enquiry, answered; one showing, no feedback yet. A comparable two streets over cut its price Friday. Action: call the showing agent for feedback today, and if the feedback is price, book the price conversation Thursday. Text the seller the numbers and that a call is coming. Four minutes.
  • The condo, week one. Nine enquiries, two from Sunday evening unanswered. Fix that first, now, two replies with dates. Five showings booked. Action: the open-house announcement video Friday. Text the seller. Five minutes.
  • The farmhouse, week eight. No enquiries, no showings. The exterior photos are from February and it is now April. Action: photographer for an evening exterior this week, new main photo and a new video from it next Monday. Text the seller that new photos are coming. Three minutes.

Fifteen minutes, four listings, four actions with days, four sellers who know what is happening. The farmhouse would have gone another month on February photos without the question about what changed.

Keeping the review honest

  • Same time, same day. A review that floats to any free slot is a review that stops.
  • Numbers, not impressions. Enquiries, showings, offers. Views and likes stay out.
  • One action per listing. Five actions is a list that gets ignored. One gets done.
  • Feedback in the buyer's words. Felt small is different from the third bedroom is small. Write what they said.
  • Do not skip the listing that is going well. It is the one that goes under contract this week and needs its status post.
  • Close the loop. A listing under contract gets its post; a listing that closed gets marked sold so the links tell the truth.

What the review prevents

Three things, mostly. The unanswered enquiry that sat for four days because it came in on a Thursday night. The listing that quietly went eight weeks on winter photos while the trees came into leaf. And the seller who called on a Wednesday afternoon, upset, because they had heard nothing for two weeks and assumed that meant nothing was happening. Each of those costs more than fifteen minutes a week, and each of them is a question on the list.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Why Monday morning for the weekly review?

Because the weekend is when enquiries and showings pile up and when a Thursday enquiry can sit unanswered. Monday morning catches those first, and the one action for each listing has the whole week to happen.

Is fifteen minutes really enough for several listings?

For four or five, yes, if the four questions are the whole agenda and each listing gets one action. If a listing needs more than four minutes, that is the week's action: a feedback call, a price conversation or a reshoot.

What should the weekly text to the seller say?

Two lines: last week's enquiries and showings as numbers, and the one action for this week with its day. Sellers who get that every Monday stop calling midweek to ask what is happening.

What is the most common thing the review catches?

An unanswered enquiry from the weekend, and a listing whose photos no longer match the season. Both are invisible unless someone looks on a schedule, and both cost showings every week they go unnoticed.
