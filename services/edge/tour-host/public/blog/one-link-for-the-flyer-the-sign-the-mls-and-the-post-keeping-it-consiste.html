One link for the flyer, the sign, the MLS and the post

One branded link on every channel you control, the unbranded twin in the MLS. A buyer who meets the home three times lands on one page.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Use one branded link for everything you control: the flyer, the sign rider, the social posts, the email, the texts. Use its unbranded twin in the MLS virtual-tour field. A buyer who meets the home on a sign, then a reel, then a flyer should land on the same page every time, with the same price and the same form. Two or three different destinations means two or three places that go out of date separately.

Use one branded link on every channel you control (the flyer, the sign rider's QR code, the social posts, the database email, the texts to buyers) and put its unbranded twin, the same listing with the identity stripped, in the MLS virtual-tour field. A buyer who meets the home on a sign Thursday, a reel Friday and a flyer at the open house Saturday should land on the same page all three times, with the same price, the same gallery and the same form. Every additional destination is one more place to update when the price moves and one more place to forget.

Why one link beats a link per channel

It stays current. One page, one price. When the seller agrees to a cut, you change it once and the sign, the flyer, the posts and the MLS tour all show the new figure.

It is recognisable. A buyer who has opened the page once knows what they are tapping the second time. That familiarity is what gets the third tap, the one from the flyer on the kitchen counter a week later.

It collects enquiries in one place. Every path ends at the same form, so every enquiry lands in the same inbox with the same listing attached. You can tell the seller how many people asked, without adding up four systems.

It survives forwarding. Buyers forward links. If each channel had its own link, the forwarded copies would diverge. One link means every copy out in the world is the same page.

The channel map

  • Sign rider QR code: branded link.
  • Flyer and open house stand: branded link.
  • Instagram, Facebook, TikTok posts and bio: branded link.
  • Email to the database: branded link.
  • Texts to buyers and the seller: branded link.
  • Broker-only MLS remarks: branded link, so the buyer's agent knows who to call.
  • MLS virtual-tour field: unbranded link.

Six places get the same link. One place gets its twin. Nothing else.

A worked example: the price cut in week three

A 3-bed lists at $529,000. Sign up Thursday with a QR code, flyers printed, three social posts over the first week, one email, the MLS tour link live. Week three, the seller agrees to $512,000.

With one link: you change the price on the listing. The page shows $512,000. The sign's code, the flyers in the drawer, the posts from two weeks ago, the email in a thousand inboxes and the MLS tour all now open on $512,000. Ten minutes, including updating the MLS price itself.

With a link per channel: the sign's code opens a page you built in a site tool, the posts link to a video platform with the old price in the description, the email linked to a portal, and the MLS tour is a separate page. Four edits, and one of them gets missed. A buyer sees $529,000 on the tour and $512,000 on the sign, and asks which is right.

Where link sprawl starts

  • Linking to the video instead of the page. The video is on the page. Link to the page.
  • Linking to a portal from social. The portal page has other listings and may route enquiries to another agent.
  • Shortened links. Each shortener is one more thing that can break or expire. Use the real link.
  • A new link when the page is edited. If rebuilding the page changes the address, every printed code and old post breaks. Edit the listing; do not replace it.
  • The branded link in the MLS. Check your MLS's current rules on the tour field; it is built for the unbranded twin.
  • No link on the flyer because "they can search the address". They search, find a portal, and enquire with someone else.

Write the rule down

If anyone else touches your marketing, a photographer, an assistant, a sign company, a teammate, give them the channel map above with the two links filled in. The rule is short enough to fit on a sticky note: branded everywhere, unbranded in the MLS tour field, nothing else anywhere.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Why not use a different link per channel so I can track which one works?

Tracking by link costs you consistency: four pages that drift apart on price and status. Ask buyers on the call how they found the home instead. If you need tracking, use the same destination with a tag rather than separate pages.

Does the MLS tour link count as a different link?

It is the unbranded twin of the same listing, not a separate page. It shows the same photos, video and price with the agent identity removed, which is what the virtual-tour field is for; check your own MLS's current rules for the detail. One listing, two addresses.

What if I need to rebuild the page mid-listing?

Edit the existing listing rather than creating a new one, so the address stays the same and every printed code and old post keeps working. If you must start over, reprint the sign rider and update every channel the same day.

Should the flyer have the full link printed or just a QR code?

Both, if there is room. The code is for scanning; the printed link is for the person who photographs the flyer or types it later. Keep the printed link short enough to type correctly.
