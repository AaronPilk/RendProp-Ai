What does an AI aerial opening actually show

Generated footage built from one reviewed front exterior photo: the house as photographed, with motion added. It is not a drone recording of the property.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. It shows the front of the house as it appears in one reviewed exterior photo, with generated camera motion that gives the impression of approaching from above. It is generated footage, not a drone recording. Anything beyond the edges of that photo, including the roof, the back garden, the neighbours and the street layout, is the model's guess, which is why it is an opening shot and not evidence of the property.

It shows the front of the house as it appears in one reviewed exterior photo, with generated camera motion that gives the impression of descending or approaching from above. It is generated footage, not a drone recording. Everything the camera seems to reveal beyond the edges of the original photo, including the roof, the back garden, the neighbours and the shape of the street, is the model's guess at what is probably there. That is why it works as an opening shot and should not be treated as a record of the property.

What the model has to work with

A single front exterior photo contains the facade, part of the roofline, the front garden or drive, and whatever sky and neighbouring edges the frame caught. From that, the model generates a few seconds of motion that give the sense of arriving at the house from above; the exact move varies from one photo to the next, and results vary in quality. Where it has information, which is the face of the house, the result can look convincing. Where it does not, which is everything above, behind and beside the frame, it fills in plausible roofs, trees and lawns.

Plausible is the key word. The generated roof may have the right pitch and the wrong material. The generated garden may be larger or greener than the real one. A chimney may appear or vanish. None of this is malicious; it is what generation means. A buyer who takes the opening as a drone survey of the lot has been shown something that was not surveyed.

What it is good for

  • Opening a video with movement. The first two seconds of a listing reel decide whether anyone keeps watching. A descent onto the front of the house is a stronger start than a static photo.
  • Homes where a real drone shot would show nothing useful. A subdivision three-bed where the aerial would be roofs and the neighbour's trampoline. The opening gives the feel of arrival without pretending the lot is the story.
  • No operator and no weather window. There is nobody to book and no clear day to wait for.

What it is not for

  • Showing the lot, the boundaries or the setting. If a buyer needs to understand acreage, water frontage or what is behind the house, that needs a real drone session or a walk of the property.
  • Standing in for a drone recording in the MLS or the remarks. Do not describe it as aerial photography of the property.
  • Proving anything. Roof condition, solar panels, outbuildings, the state of the neighbour's fence: the generated version is not evidence of any of it.

A worked example: a three-bed on a corner lot

An agent has a clean, level front exterior photo of a three-bed on a corner lot, shot mid-morning with the sun on the facade. With eligible paid access, a remaining Aerial allowance, available processing capacity and separate processor permission, she generates an aerial opening from it, knowing results vary and she will review it before use. The result is a short move that starts above the roofline and settles on the front door. The facade matches the photo closely. The roof, which the photo barely showed, has been rendered as plain grey shingle; the real roof is grey with two skylights. The side garden, out of frame in the original, appears as lawn; in reality it is gravel with bins.

She reviews it against the original and against what she knows of the house. The facade is right, so it works as an opening. The missing skylights are not a selling point she is relying on, and nothing invented is being claimed in the remarks. She uses it as the first few seconds of the social reel, follows it immediately with the real front photo and the interiors, and does not use it on the property page video where the buyer is studying rather than scrolling. If the skylights had been a feature she was promoting, or the side garden a selling point, she would have left the opening out.

Where an AI aerial opening goes wrong

  1. Calling it a drone shot. In the remarks, in the caption, or to the seller. It is generated. Say so if asked and do not imply otherwise.
  2. Not reviewing it. The opening is footage like any other AI output: compare it with the original photo and with the house as you know it before anyone sees it.
  3. Starting from a weak photo. A tilted, shaded or cluttered front exterior produces a tilted, shaded, cluttered opening with invented surroundings. Use the best reviewed original you have.
  4. Letting it carry the lot. A buyer who books a showing because the opening made the garden look large will notice the gravel.
  5. Using it on every listing. It is a device. When every reel opens the same way, it stops working.

The question to answer before you use one

Before using an aerial opening on a listing, write down the one thing a buyer might believe about the property after watching it that is not in the original photo. If that thing matters to the sale, leave the opening out or book a drone. If it does not, review the result against the photo and use it as the first few seconds of the reel.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Is an AI aerial opening the same as drone footage?

No. It is generated footage built from one reviewed front exterior photo, with camera motion added by a model. A drone recording captures the real property from the air; the AI opening shows the facade from the photo and invents whatever lies beyond its edges.

Can buyers tell the difference?

Often not at a glance, which is the reason to be straightforward about it. Describe it as a generated opening if asked, do not call it a drone shot in the remarks or captions, and follow it with real photos of the home.

Should I use an AI aerial opening on the property page video?

It fits the first seconds of a social reel better than a page video a buyer is studying for detail. If you do use it on the page, review it carefully and make sure nothing it suggests about the lot or the roof is being relied on.

What photo should the opening be generated from?

The best reviewed original front exterior you have: level, well lit, uncluttered, with the facade clear. The opening inherits every flaw in the photo and adds invented surroundings around it.
