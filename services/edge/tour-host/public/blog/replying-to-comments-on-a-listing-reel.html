Replying to comments on a listing reel

Answer every genuine comment the same day with a specific fact and the next step. Six common comment types on a listing reel and the reply to each.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Reply to every genuine comment on a listing reel the same day, with a specific fact rather than a DM me, and offer the next step where it fits. Price questions, is it still available and what are the taxes are leads. Jokes get a short reply or none. Never argue about the price in public, and never answer with a fact you have not checked.

Questions about price, availability, taxes or the HOA are leads and should be treated like a phone call. Jokes and sniping get a short reply or none. The one thing never to do is argue about the price in public.

Why the thread is part of the listing

Everyone who watches the reel after the first few hours also reads the comments. An unanswered question looks like something you do not know or do not want to say. A sharp, factual reply does two jobs: it answers the person who asked, and it shows every later viewer that this agent knows the home and answers quickly. That is the whole reason to answer the easy ones.

Six comments on a condo reel, and the replies

A two-bed, two-bath condo on the fourth floor of a 1980s building with a pool, listed at $329,000. Here is what landed in the thread in the first two days, and what went back.

  1. How much is the HOA? Reply: $410 a month, covers water, trash, the pool and exterior maintenance. Reserve study is from 2024 and I can send it, message me. The number, what it covers, and the next step.
  2. Is this still available? Reply: Yes, as of today. Showings this week are Tuesday evening and Thursday afternoon; the full gallery and a form to pick a day are at the link in my bio. Confirmed, dated, with a path.
  3. Overpriced for that building. Reply: Fair to ask. The last two sales on this stack were $305,000 in 2024 and $318,000 earlier this year; this one has the renovated kitchen and the corner exposure. Happy to walk through the comparison if you want to message me. One factual reply, no second round. If they come back swinging, you are done in public.
  4. What are the taxes? Reply: $3,140 last year, from the county record. That is a lead; it is the question a buyer asks when they are running numbers.
  5. Love the kitchen, who did the cabinets? Reply: The seller had them done locally in 2022, I will find out the shop and reply here. Then actually come back and reply. Small, but it shows you follow through.
  6. My cousin lives in that building and says the pool was closed all summer. Reply: Thanks, I will ask the management company about the pool and post what I find. Then do it, and if the record is bad, say so in the thread. Hiding it costs more later than saying it now.

Six replies, maybe ten minutes in total, and three of them turned into private conversations. The pattern for turning those into a form submission is in the comment, the DM, the form.

What a reply should contain

  • The fact. A number, a date, a yes or a no. Not a thank you for your interest.
  • The source, when it matters. From the county record, from the HOA's reserve study, from the seller's disclosure. It makes the fact credible and it tells the next reader where facts come from.
  • The next step, when it fits. Message me, the link in my bio, Tuesday evening. One step, not three.
  • Nothing you have not checked. A tax figure from memory that is wrong is a comment thread that stays up with the wrong number in it.

Replies that cause trouble

  • DM me for details, to every question. It reads as evasive, and it moves a question the whole thread wanted answered into a private box.
  • Arguing about the price. One factual reply. If they want a second round, they can have it in a private message or not at all.
  • Deleting critical comments. A deleted comment about the road noise becomes a screenshot. Answer it with the truth.
  • Sharing the seller's situation. Why they are selling, when they are moving, what they will accept. None of it belongs in a thread.
  • Describing the neighborhood by who lives there. A comment asks what the neighbors are like. Reply about the street, the park and the distances, not the people, and ask your broker about fair housing language if you are unsure.
  • Replying to the same question six times. If the HOA question keeps coming, pin your reply or edit the caption to include the figure.
  • Leaving it until the weekend. By then the reel is old and so is the question.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How fast should I reply to comments on a listing reel?

The same day, and sooner in the first few hours when most of the viewing happens. A question left overnight looks like a question you did not want to answer.

Should I reply to a comment that says the home is overpriced?

Once, with facts: recent sales on the same street or in the same building and what this home has that they did not. Offer to continue privately. Do not go a second round in public.

Is it better to answer in the thread or in a private message?

Answer the fact in the thread, because everyone else reading has the same question. Move to a private message for the next step, such as arranging a showing or sending documents.

What should I do with a negative comment from a neighbor?

Thank them, say you will check, and then check. If they are right, say so in the thread and address it. Deleting the comment turns a fixable issue into a screenshot.
