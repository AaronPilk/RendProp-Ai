Switching modes: using one app for homes, venues and businesses

One app covers a home on Monday, a wedding barn on Tuesday and a gym on Wednesday: the mode changes the fields on the page, the workflow stays the same.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. One app can market a home, a wedding barn and a gym in the same week because the mode changes what the page asks for, not how the work is done. Real estate asks for price, beds, baths and square feet; the business modes ask for hours, menu, capacity or membership. Photos, AI edits, video, the published page, the two links and the inbox are the same in every mode.

One app covers a three-bed on Monday, a wedding barn on Tuesday and a gym on Wednesday because the mode changes the page, not the work. In real estate mode the page asks for price, beds, baths and square feet; in the business modes it asks for details such as hours, menu, capacity or membership. Everything else is the same: add photos, review edits, create the video, publish the page, share the two links, answer enquiries in one inbox.

You choose the mode when you create the listing, and the words and fields follow.

Who actually needs this

A photographer who shoots homes for agents on weekdays and a restaurant or a venue on Fridays. An agent whose client also owns the pub on the corner. A marketing freelancer with a gym, a salon and two rental properties on the books. A commercial broker who lists offices and also handles the owner's event space. None of them want three apps with three logins and three places enquiries arrive. One app with a mode chooser means one workflow learned once.

What each mode asks the page for

  • Real estate. The primary mode. Price, beds, baths, square feet. The page reads as a listing and the unbranded link is made for an MLS virtual-tour field.
  • Event venues. Details such as capacity and hours. The enquiry form's request for a date becomes the main question, because every venue enquiry is a date enquiry.
  • Restaurants and bars. Details such as hours, menu and capacity, typed so they read on a phone.
  • Gyms and studios. Details such as hours and membership.
  • Retail. Details such as hours, with the store itself in the photos.
  • Other businesses. Offices, studios, workshops, and the service businesses that do not fit the others. The general case.

In every mode the page has the same shape: main photo, the details you typed, the gallery, the selected video on request, a contact card and an enquiry form that asks for the date the visitor wants. Sections appear when filled in and hide when empty, so a venue without a menu does not show a menu line and an office without hours does not show hours.

What stays the same across modes

  1. Photos. Landscape photos for the listing video, portrait photos for the vertical social video, in any mode.
  2. AI edits. Blue sky, twilight, green lawn, tidy the room, add furniture and custom prompts work on a barn exterior the same as on a house. Every edit is a separate version with a disclosure; staging is labelled virtually staged; the original stays in photo history. Compare before publishing in every mode, and be more conservative with businesses, where the customer walks into the exact room.
  3. Video. Scene order and camera motion planned automatically, a recorded or AI voiceover, word-by-word captions, optional music, 9:16 and 16:9 exports.
  4. The page and the two links. A branded link and an unbranded one for every listing, whatever the mode.
  5. The inbox. Enquiries from a home, a venue and a gym arrive in the same place, per listing and all together.
  6. Teams and the web Studio. The same people can work across all modes, on the phone or at studio.rendprop.com.

Worked example: a photographer's three-day week

A photographer shoots a four-bed colonial on Monday for a listing agent, a converted mill that hosts weddings on Tuesday for its owner, and a boutique gym on Wednesday for its manager. Monday's listing is created in real estate mode: twenty-two landscape photos, a blue sky edit on the front exterior, a listing video with the agent's voiceover, beds, baths, square feet and price typed in, published with the unbranded link handed to the agent for the MLS. Tuesday's listing is created in event venues mode: the mill empty and dressed, capacity typed as 150 seated or 200 standing, hours with the music cut-off, the same video workflow, the branded link for the venue's Instagram. Wednesday's listing is created in gyms and studios mode: the floor at the quiet hour, hours and membership typed in, a vertical cut for the gym's accounts. Three listings, three modes, one app, and on Thursday the photographer opens one inbox and sees an enquiry on each.

Choosing the mode when it is not obvious

Choose by the details you want the page to show. A short-term rental is a property with beds and baths, so real estate. A coworking space that sells memberships fits gyms and studios; one that leases suites fits other businesses. A hotel's rooms are real estate; its meeting rooms are event venues. A salon is other businesses. A dealership is retail. If you are unsure, pick the mode whose details match what a customer asks first, and remember that empty sections hide, so an unused field costs nothing.

What goes wrong when people skip the mode

  • A restaurant in real estate mode. The page asks for beds and baths and the menu has nowhere to go.
  • A venue without capacity. The planner's first question, unanswered.
  • Deciding the mode after the photos. The mode is set when the listing is created; if you realise it is wrong early, start a new listing in the right mode before adding much.
  • Treating business photos like home photos. Staging a dining room with AI furniture misleads a diner who will sit in it. Keep business edits light and disclosed.
  • Three separate tools after all. If the enquiries from the venue go somewhere different from the enquiries from the homes, the point of one app is lost.

The next non-home job

Take the next non-home job you have, a venue, a restaurant or a gym, and create it in its own mode rather than forcing it into a home listing. Type in the hours and the capacity, build the video the same way you would for a house, and notice how little else changes.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What changes between modes?

The fields the page asks for, the wording on the page and the sample content. A home asks for price, beds, baths and square feet; a business asks for hours, menu, capacity or membership depending on the mode. Photos, edits, video, the two links and the inbox are the same.

When do I choose the mode?

When you create the listing or the workspace, in the mode chooser. The mode is set at creation, so if you picked the wrong one and have not added much, start a new listing in the right mode.

Which mode for something that is not on the list?

Other businesses, which covers offices, studios, workshops and service businesses. Choose by the details you want the page to show, and remember that sections you leave empty are hidden.

Do enquiries from different modes go to different places?

No. Enquiries from a home, a venue and a gym all land in the same inbox, per listing and all together. That is the main reason to keep every kind of listing in one app.
