Open house metrics worth tracking

Five counts per open house: groups through, unrepresented buyers, page enquiries within 48 hours, second-showing requests, offers. Plus one comment.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Track five numbers for every open house: groups through the door, how many were unrepresented, enquiries that arrived through the property page in the following 48 hours, second-showing requests, and offers. Add one sentence: the comment that repeated. Count groups rather than people, write it down the same way every Sunday, and compare week to week on the same house before you change anything.

Track five numbers for every open house and one sentence. The numbers: groups through the door, how many of those were unrepresented, enquiries that came through the property page in the forty-eight hours after, second-showing requests, and offers. The sentence: the comment that came up more than once. Written the same way every Sunday, these tell you whether the marketing is working, whether the price is working, and what to tell the seller on Monday.

Most open house reporting is “good turnout.” That is a feeling. Five counts and a sentence are a record, and after two or three Sundays on the same house, the record says something.

Elmwood Avenue, three Sundays

A 3-bed on Elmwood Avenue, open three Sundays running, 1 to 3. The log:

  • Sunday 1. Groups: 16. Unrepresented: 9. Page enquiries by Tuesday: 3. Second-showing requests: 1. Offers: 0. Comment: “smaller than the photos,” four times, about the living room.
  • Sunday 2 (living room photo replaced with a plainer frame, the garden moved up the gallery, video re-cut to lead with the kitchen). Groups: 11. Unrepresented: 5. Page enquiries: 4. Second-showing requests: 3. Offers: 0. Comment: “nice garden,” three times. Nobody mentioned the living room.
  • Sunday 3 (no changes). Groups: 8. Unrepresented: 3. Page enquiries: 2. Second-showing requests: 2. Offers: 1. Comment: “price is a bit high for the street,” twice.

Read it. The first Sunday's crowd was large and shallow: lots of people, one second look, a repeated complaint about a photo. The fix was marketing, and the second Sunday shows it worked: fewer groups, more second showings, the complaint gone. The third Sunday's falling numbers are normal for a third week; the comment has moved to price, which with an offer in hand is a negotiation, not a relaunch.

Without the log, Sunday 1 feels like a success (sixteen groups) and Sunday 3 feels like a failure (eight). The log says the opposite.

The five counts, and how to take them

  • Groups, not people. A couple with two children is one buyer. Count the decision units. Tick marks on the back of the sheet as each group leaves.
  • Unrepresented. Ask at the door, as part of the sign-in: “working with an agent?” It tells you how much of the crowd is potential business beyond this house.
  • Page enquiries in 48 hours. The enquiry form on the property page, Sunday afternoon to Tuesday afternoon. This is the warm count; these people asked for something and gave a date.
  • Second-showing requests. By any route: the form, a text, an agent's call. The clearest signal that the house, not just the event, worked.
  • Offers. Obvious, and attributable: did the offer come from someone who was at the open house, from a private showing, or from the page without a visit at all.

The sentence

Write down what people said, and after the open house, pick the thing said most often. One sentence. “Smaller than the photos.” “Lovely kitchen, no storage.” “Why so cheap?” It is the qualitative number, and it is usually the one that tells you what to change.

Reading the numbers without fooling yourself

  • Compare the same house across Sundays, not different houses on the same Sunday. A condo and a farmhouse will never have the same counts.
  • Falling groups is normal. Week one has the neighbours and the curious. Week three has the buyers. Watch second showings and enquiries, not footfall.
  • Change one thing between Sundays. If you change the photos and the price in the same week, the log cannot tell you which one moved the numbers.
  • Attribute offers honestly. An offer from a private showing booked through the page is the page's offer, not the open house's.

What to tell the seller

The five counts and the sentence, Monday morning, and after the second Sunday, the comparison. Sellers are reassured by a record even when the numbers are modest, because a record means someone is paying attention. “Eleven groups, five unrepresented, four enquiries through the page, three second-showing requests, no offers yet; the comment this week was the garden, and nobody mentioned the living room after the photo was changed.” That is a report a seller can repeat to their partner.

Where tracking goes wrong

  • Counting people. Forty people and twelve buyers is twelve buyers.
  • “Leads” with no definition. Decide what counts before Sunday. A signed sheet is attendance. An enquiry with a date is a lead.
  • Reporting scans as interest. If your QR tool reports scans, that is curiosity, not intent. Enquiries are intent.
  • No baseline. One Sunday's numbers mean little. Three mean something.
  • Not writing the sentence. The comment is where the fix lives, and it is forgotten by Tuesday if not written on Sunday.
  • Tracking without changing anything. The log is for decisions. If three Sundays say “smaller than the photos,” change the photos.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What should I track at an open house?

Five counts and one sentence: groups through the door, how many were unrepresented, enquiries through the property page in the following 48 hours, second-showing requests, and offers; plus the comment that came up most. Write them the same way every Sunday so the second and third open houses on the same home can be compared.

Should I count people or groups at an open house?

Groups. A couple with two children is one buyer making one decision. Tick marks as each group leaves gives you the number that matters, and it stops forty visitors being reported as forty buyers when twelve of them were deciding anything.

Is a falling open house turnout a bad sign?

Not by itself. The first Sunday brings the neighbours and the curious; the third brings the buyers. Watch second-showing requests and page enquiries rather than footfall: if those hold or rise while groups fall, the listing is working. If everything falls and the repeated comment is price, that is a different conversation.

Does Rendprop track open house attendance?

No. Rendprop shows enquiries sent from the property page in the app's inbox, per listing, each with a preferred date, which gives you the page-enquiry and second-showing counts. Groups, unrepresented buyers and offers are yours to log on paper. There is no sign-in sheet feature.
