Marketing a new construction home

New construction sells on the finished kitchen, the completion date and what is included. Label model photos, stage honestly and keep the page current.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A new construction home sells on three facts: what the finished house looks like, when it will be ready and what is included at the price. Lead with the finished kitchen and great room, state the builder's completion estimate and the included-versus-upgrade list plainly, label any model home photo as the model, and keep the page current as the house moves from framing to finished.

A new construction buyer cannot judge a muddy lot, so the marketing has to answer three questions in the first screen: what will the finished house look like, when will it be ready, and what is included at this price. Lead with the finished kitchen and great room, or the model's if the house is not done, and say which is which. State the builder's completion estimate as the builder gives it, and list what is standard and what is an upgrade. Then keep the page current, because a new build changes every week and a page from framing day looks abandoned by drywall.

Start with the page, because it changes

On a resale listing the page is written once. On a new build it is a living document. Set it up so that updating it is a two-minute job.

  • Status and date. The stage the house is at and the builder's current completion estimate, with the date you last updated it. Change the main photo as the house changes.
  • Plan, lot and orientation. The plan name, the lot number, which way the back yard faces, and what is behind it.
  • Included versus upgrade. A short list of what comes at the listed price and what the photos show that does not. If the model has the upgraded tile, say so in the text.
  • Builder warranty, as the builder describes it. Quote the builder's own summary and point to their document. Do not restate terms in your own words.
  • Yard, driveway, fencing. What is installed at completion and what is not. A buyer assumes grass unless you say otherwise.
  • Price, beds, baths and square feet in the details fields, with square feet from the plan until the house is measured.

The earlier post on progress photos and the finished home covers how the gallery should evolve; this one is about the rest of the marketing.

The shot list, in two passes

You usually shoot a new build twice: once before completion, from the model or a finished sister home, and once when the actual house is done.

  1. Pass one, the model. Kitchen wide from the great room, kitchen island detail, great room toward the kitchen, primary bedroom, primary bath, one secondary bath, the covered patio if the plan has one, the front from the street. Every frame identified as the model in the text.
  2. Pass one, the actual lot. One honest frame of the house at its current stage, from the same spot you will shoot the finished front from. Buyers like seeing the comparison later.
  3. Pass two, the finished house. Front exterior once the driveway is poured and the yard is in or clearly not in. Kitchen, great room, dining, primary suite, each secondary bedroom, each bath, laundry, garage interior, back patio and yard, street view showing the neighbors' stage of completion.

Shoot pass two with every light on and the blinds up; new builds have good glass and bad bare bulbs. The landscape listing video comes from pass two. For pass one, a shorter video from the model photos works if the voiceover and its captions say it is the model. Portrait frames of the kitchen island and the primary bath make the vertical social video.

Worked example: lot 14, eight weeks out

A 4-bed, 2.5-bath two-story spec home on lot 14 of a sixty-home phase, at drywall, with the builder estimating completion in eight weeks. The model, two doors down, is the same plan with upgraded cabinets and a tiled shower. The agent shoots the model on a Wednesday morning, nine landscape frames, and one frame of lot 14 from the curb. The page goes live with the model kitchen as the main photo, a first line that reads 'Photos show the model home; this home is at drywall, builder estimates completion in eight weeks, updated March 3,' then the plan name, the north-facing back yard that backs onto the greenbelt, and a list: standard cabinets in this home, model shows the upgrade; sod and irrigation included in front only; builder warranty summary attached. The short video from the model photos opens with a caption that says model home. Seven weeks later the house is painted and the counters are in; the agent reshoots, swaps the main photo for the real kitchen, replaces the gallery, creates the full listing video and changes the first line to the walkthrough date.

What goes wrong on new builds

  • Model photos presented as the house. The buyer walks in and the cabinets are different. Caption every model frame.
  • A green lawn edit on a dirt lot. If sod is not installed, the lawn does not exist. Say what is included instead of painting it in.
  • Virtual staging that adds a fireplace or a window. An add-furniture edit can invent architecture. Compare each staged version against the original and keep the Virtually staged label on anything you publish. The post on staging an empty living room covers what reads as real.
  • A stale page. A completion estimate from two months ago, unchanged, tells buyers nobody is watching the listing.
  • Overstating readiness. Use the builder's words for the stage the house is at; do not promise a move-in date the builder has not given.

How this works in Rendprop for a new construction listing

Start the listing with the model photos, and when pass two is shot add the finished photos, choose a new main photo, create the listing video and publish again. Add-furniture edits in the photo studio are labelled Virtually staged and saved as separate versions beside the original. The property website shows the details you type, the gallery and the selected video, and an enquiry form that asks for the visitor's preferred date, which on a pre-completion home is a useful signal of who is ready. The branded link goes on the builder's sign and your posts; the unbranded link goes in the MLS virtual tour field.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Can I use model home photos for a new construction listing?

Yes, if the page says the photos show the model and which finishes in the model are upgrades. Replace them with photos of the actual house as soon as it is finished.

Should I virtually stage an empty new build?

Staging helps buyers read room sizes, and it is fine with the Virtually staged label. Compare the staged version with the original so the edit has not added windows, fireplaces or trim that are not in the house.

What should the page say about the completion date?

The builder's current estimate, in the builder's words, with the date you last updated it. Change it whenever the estimate changes. A stale date makes the listing look unattended.

How do I describe the builder warranty?

Quote the builder's own summary and point to their document. Do not restate the terms yourself. If the buyer has questions, the builder answers them.
