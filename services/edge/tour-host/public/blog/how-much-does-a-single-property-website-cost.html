How much does a single-property website cost

Dedicated single-property site tools run $9–150 per month at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026. Agencies cost far more. Some apps include the page.

October 28, 2026 · 3 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. At typical U.S. rates as of August 2026, a dedicated single-property website tool costs $9–150 per month, with a custom domain and design work on top if you want them. A marketing agency that builds pages as part of a package runs $2,500–5,000 per month. Some listing apps publish the page as part of the workflow, so the cost sits inside the subscription instead of per site.

At typical U.S. rates as of August 2026, a dedicated single-property website tool costs $9–150 per month, with a custom domain and any design work on top. A marketing agency that produces pages as part of a monthly package runs $2,500–5,000 per month. Some listing apps publish the property page as part of the normal workflow, so the cost sits inside the app subscription rather than per site.

The spread from $9 to $150 is not random. It tracks what you are paying for: a template and hosting at the low end, custom domains, lead routing, analytics and more templates in the middle, and white-glove setup at the top.

What the monthly fee covers, and what it does not

  • Hosting and a URL. The page is online and reachable. Usually a subdomain of the tool unless you add your own domain, which is a separate annual cost.
  • A template. Hero photo, gallery, details, map, agent card, form. Most tools offer a few looks. Few let you change much beyond colours and logo.
  • A contact form. Sometimes routed to your email, sometimes to the tool's own dashboard, sometimes to a CRM at an extra tier.
  • Video embedding. Usually a link to a hosted video elsewhere rather than the video itself.

What it rarely covers is the work: uploading the photos, typing the details, choosing the order, writing the description, testing the form, building the QR code, updating the page when the price changes and marking it sold. That is your time, and on a per-listing basis it is often the larger cost.

A year of pages for an agent with twelve listings

Take an agent who lists twelve homes a year, roughly one a month, each on the market for about two months. With a per-site tool at $29 per month and sites kept live for three months each, that is around $1,000 a year, before a custom domain. The agent also spends an hour per listing building and checking the page, and another twenty minutes later marking it sold or taking it down.

If the page is produced from the same listing where the photos and video already live, the per-site charge disappears into the subscription and the build time falls to the few minutes it takes to confirm the details and publish. The agent still owns the decisions: which photo leads, whether to show the video, what the form asks. The page itself is no longer a project.

Where the money leaks

  1. Paying for pages that are sitting. A site for a home that sold in March and is still billing in September is pure waste. Set a reminder or use a tool where sold pages cost nothing extra.
  2. Cancelling and breaking every link. When the subscription ends, the QR code on the old flyer, the link in the just-sold post and the URL in the MLS record all die. Dead links look careless to the next seller who clicks them.
  3. Buying a domain per listing. 123MainStreet.com reads nicely on a sign and costs an annual fee that outlives the sale. A consistent path on one domain does the same job.
  4. Paying an agency rate for a template page. At $2,500–5,000 a month, the agency should be running campaigns, not filling in a form you could fill in yourself.

Work out your own number

Add up what you paid for single-property pages over the last twelve months, including the domains, and divide by the number of listings. If that number is more than a few minutes of your time per listing, the page should come with the listing rather than as a separate bill.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What is the cheapest way to get a single-property website?

A template tool at the low end of the $9–150 per month range, typical U.S. rates as of August 2026, or a listing app that publishes the page as part of the workflow so the cost sits inside one subscription. Free page builders exist but usually carry their own branding and ads.

Do I need a custom domain for each listing?

No. A memorable address on a sign is nice but the domain costs an annual fee and dies with the listing. A consistent link on one domain works for the QR code, the MLS field and the post, and keeps working after the sale.

Should the seller pay for the property website?

Most agents treat the page as part of their marketing service rather than a seller expense, especially when the cost is inside a subscription. Showing the seller a live example in the listing presentation is worth more than billing them for it.

What happens to the page when I stop paying?

With most per-site tools the page goes offline and every link to it breaks. Check the cancellation terms before you print a QR code, and prefer a setup where sold pages stay reachable and say sold.
