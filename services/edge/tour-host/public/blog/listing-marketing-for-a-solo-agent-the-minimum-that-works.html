Listing marketing for a solo agent: the minimum that works

The four pieces of listing marketing a solo agent needs (photos, a video, a page with a form, one link), what to skip, and a two-listing week.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. The minimum that works for a solo agent is four pieces: a proper photo set, one video built from it, one property page with a form that reaches you, and one link used everywhere. Everything else is optional and most of it is noise.

The minimum that works for a solo agent is four pieces: a proper photo set, one video, one property page with a form that reaches you directly, and one link you use everywhere. Everything else, from the printed brochure to the third social platform, is optional, and most of it costs more time than it returns. Here is why those four, in what order, and how to run them without a coordinator.

Why these four and not ten

Each piece does a job the others cannot.

  • The photo set is the listing. It is what buyers scroll on the portals, what the seller judges you on, and the raw material for the other three pieces. This is where the money goes, $150–300 per shoot at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026, and it is the one line not to cut.
  • One video shows flow and scale in a way thirty stills cannot. A landscape listing video built from the photos covers the page and the MLS; a vertical cut from the same photos covers social. You do not need a separately filmed production for most homes.
  • One page with a form gives the listing a home that is not a portal. A buyer who finds it there can enquire with you, not with whichever other agents a portal may show beside your listing.
  • One link keeps it simple. The branded link goes on the sign, the flyer, the posts, the email and your texts. The unbranded link goes in the MLS tour field. Two links, two jobs, nothing else to remember.

What you can skip without losing a buyer

Drone sessions on lots that are not the story. Printed brochures beyond a one-page flyer with a QR code. A separate videographer. Posting to five platforms when two carry your audience. Paid promotion before the organic post has shown whether the hook works. A monthly agency retainer, which at $2,500–5,000 per month at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026 buys brand presence, not the speed a single listing needs. None of these will lose you a buyer if the four pieces are done well.

A solo week with two listings

Monday: photographer at the split-level at 9 a.m., at the townhouse at 1 p.m. Monday evening: cull and order both sets, pick the main photo for each, review the one blue-sky edit on the split-level's front and the staging on the townhouse's empty office, each compared with its original. Tuesday morning: build one landscape video per listing, publish both pages, copy the links into a note. Tuesday noon: both MLS entries with the unbranded links, both branded links in a single email to the database, one reel per listing posted a day apart so they do not compete. Wednesday to Friday: showings and replies. Saturday: open house at the split-level with the branded link on the sign-in. Total marketing time across the week, excluding the shoots themselves: about four hours.

Where solo agents lose the week

  • Doing the review in pieces. Ten minutes here and there means the main photo is chosen three times. Block an hour and finish both sets.
  • Letting the page be the last thing. The page is where enquiries come from. If it is published after the MLS, the first week of interest goes through the portal.
  • Replying from four inboxes. Portal messages, texts, email, the form. Pick the form as the place you point everyone, and check the others once a day.
  • Perfectionism on the video. One clean thirty-second cut with captions beats a day spent on music choices.

When to add a fifth piece

Add a recorded handheld walkthrough when the flow of the home is the selling point and the stills do not carry it. Add a twilight exterior when the home is on a street that looks better at dusk. Add paid promotion when an organic post has already shown that the hook works. Each of these is a response to a specific listing, not a default.

Next step: write the four pieces on a card and tape it where you work. On the next listing, do not add a fifth thing until the four are live and the first enquiry has been answered.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Can a solo agent really skip a videographer?

For most homes, yes. A listing video built from the photo set shows flow and scale well enough for the page, the MLS and social. Add a recorded handheld walkthrough only when the home's layout is the story and stills do not tell it.

Which social platform should a solo agent post on?

The one or two where your past clients and their friends actually are. One vertical video per listing with the branded link in the caption, posted where people already follow you, beats the same video on five platforms nobody checks.

Is a property page worth it for a modest listing?

Yes, because it is where the enquiry form lives. On a portal the enquiry may go to other agents shown beside your listing. On the page it comes to you, with the date the buyer wants to visit.

How much time should listing marketing take a solo agent each week?

With the four-piece minimum and the review done in one block, about two hours per listing in launch week, excluding the shoot. After launch, the time is replies and the weekly review, not new media.
