Listing marketing for relocation buyers

Relocation buyers have a start date and a job address but no neighborhood. Anchor the listing to the commute, the calendar and facts a newcomer can use.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A relocation buyer has a start date and a job address and no neighborhood knowledge, so the search is anchored to the commute and the calendar rather than to a street they already like. The listing should state the drive to the major employers at commute hours, describe the location in terms a newcomer can use, and make it easy to book a showing inside the one or two trips they will make before the move.

A relocation buyer has three things a local buyer does not: a start date, a job address and no neighborhood knowledge. They are not shopping a street they already like; they are shopping a commute and a calendar. Market the listing by anchoring it to the facts a newcomer can use (drive times to the major employers at commute hours, what the area is like in plain terms, the practical logistics of arriving) and by making it easy to see the home inside the one or two short trips they will make before the move. A relocation buyer who misses a listing on their trip does not come back for it later; they have already bought something else.

The couple with a start date six weeks out

She starts at the hospital on the first of the month. They are coming for four days in two weeks to find a house, and then they are not coming again until the moving truck does. They have a list of twelve homes from a portal and a relocation agent on the other end of a phone. They do not know the town. They know the hospital's address and that they want a yard.

On Tuesday night they open twelve tour links. The ones with a video and a page that states the drive to the hospital at 7am get watched twice. The ones that say nothing about where the house is relative to anything get cut. They fill in enquiry forms on five with the dates of the trip. The agent on the three-bed near the park replies on Wednesday with a time and a line about the drive and the nearest grocery store. That house goes first on the Saturday schedule, and it is the one they buy, partly because it was the one they understood before they arrived.

The relocation buyer's calendar

Everything about this buyer is compressed. They are researching on weeknights in another time zone, booking one trip with a fixed number of days, and making a decision that would normally take a season in a long weekend. Your marketing has to fit into that calendar. Reply to enquiries the same day, because a Monday reply to a Thursday enquiry is a reply to someone whose trip is already full. Offer showing times across the days they named. Send the page link again the night before so they can re-watch the video on the way to the showing. After the trip, send the page link one more time with the video, because the decision is often made from the airport with a partner who saw it once.

What to put on the page for a newcomer

Write the location as if the reader has never been to the town, because they have not. Name the employers within commuting range and give the drive at commute hours, measured. Give the drive to the airport, because they will be using it. Name the grocery store and the pharmacy with distances. Describe the street in facts: sidewalks, through traffic or not, what the neighbouring homes are like, distance to a park. Leave out who lives there and who the home suits; describe the home and the location and let the buyer decide. Keep adjectives about schools out of it; name the district as a fact if your MLS and brokerage allow it, and leave the judgement to them.

Put the practical facts of moving in too: when the home can be vacant, whether the seller can be flexible on the closing date, what stays. A relocation buyer with a start date reads the closing timeline before they read the kitchen finishes.

What goes wrong with relocation listings

  • Location written for locals. Near the old mill, close to everything, minutes from downtown. A newcomer cannot use any of that. Give named places and measured times.
  • No video. The buyer has one trip. A gallery makes them spend part of it finding out whether the house is worth the visit; a video lets them spend it deciding.
  • Slow replies. The trip has a fixed number of hours. Every hour you take to reply is an hour that goes to another listing.
  • Nothing about the move itself. Closing flexibility and vacancy dates are the first practical questions for a buyer with a start date.
  • Edits the one visit will expose. This buyer sees the house once, and they compare the photos against reality on that one visit. Compare every edit with the original before you publish, keep the disclosure, and avoid anything that changes what they will find at the door.

Next step: write one paragraph about the location for someone who has never been to your town, with three named places and three measured drive times at commute hours. Put it on the page above the kitchen description.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What do relocation buyers look for first in a listing?

The drive to their new workplace at commute hours, the drive to the airport, and a description of the location that a newcomer can use: named places and measured times rather than phrases like close to everything. After that, the closing timeline and when the home can be vacant.

How fast should I reply to a relocation buyer's enquiry?

The same day. They are filling a short trip with a fixed number of showings, and the agents who reply first with a time are the ones who get on the schedule. When the enquiry form asks for a date, the answer tells you the days of their trip before you have spoken.

Should I mention schools for relocation buyers?

Name the district as a fact if your MLS and brokerage allow it, and leave the judgement to the buyer. Avoid adjectives about schools or about who the home suits; describe the home and the location and let them decide.

Is a video necessary for a listing aimed at relocation buyers?

Yes. The buyer has one trip and a gallery makes them spend part of it discovering whether the house is worth the visit. A video that walks the home in order lets them arrive already knowing and spend the showing deciding.
