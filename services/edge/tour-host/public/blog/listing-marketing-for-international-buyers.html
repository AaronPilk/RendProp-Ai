Listing marketing for international buyers

An international buyer views at midnight, in a second language, through an adviser. Build a page that works on mute, in both units, with a date field.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. An international buyer is viewing your listing at an odd hour, often in a second language, often through a relative or an adviser who is doing the looking for them. The listing has to work without sound, state sizes and distances in both unit systems, show the whole property and its surroundings in one video, and offer a written way to ask for a viewing date, because a phone call across time zones is not going to happen first.

An international buyer is looking at your listing at midnight their time, often in a second language, and often through someone else: a relative in the country, an adviser, a buyer's agent who is doing the first pass. They will not call. They may not visit until they are close to a decision, and some will buy on the strength of a trusted person's visit plus what you published. Market the listing by making the page work without sound and without local knowledge: sizes and distances in both unit systems, a video that shows the property and its surroundings in one pass, captions that carry the facts, and a written way to ask for a viewing date.

Distance, time zones and an unfamiliar process

Three things stack up for this buyer. Distance means the page replaces the first and often the second showing. Time zones mean every reply you send lands while they are asleep, so the page has to anticipate the next question rather than wait for it. And the buying process is unfamiliar to them; that part belongs to their advisers and their lawyers, not to you, so the listing should stay on the property and leave process questions to the people they have hired. Your job is to make the property itself completely understood from the other side of the world.

That means more context than a local listing needs. A buyer from abroad does not know what your suburb is, how far your city is from the airport they would fly into, what a typical lot looks like in your area or whether your square footage is large or small. Write the location as if to a stranger: the airport and the drive time, the nearest city, the neighbourhood in plain facts. Give the floor area in square feet and square metres, the lot in both, and distances in miles and kilometres. It costs you one line and saves the buyer a calculator.

What goes on the page

Lead with the video, because it crosses language better than text. A calm walk through the property in order, captioned word by word with the room names and the key facts, watched on mute on a phone in a different country, tells the buyer more than any description. Then the gallery, complete, including the street and the surroundings. Then the facts: year built, floor area both ways, lot both ways, the systems, the parking, what is included. Then the location paragraph for a stranger. Then the enquiry form, which asks for a date and lets the buyer or their representative write at 2am and get an answer in your morning.

Keep the written copy plain and short-sentenced. Idioms, local jokes and brochure adjectives do not translate and can mislead. A sentence like the kitchen was replaced in a named year with stone counters and a gas range survives any translation. A sentence about a chef's dream does not.

Example: a buyer viewing at 2am with her brother's help

A four-bed in a suburb thirty minutes from an international airport. The seller's agent builds the page with the video first, captioned, and the facts list with areas and distances in both units. A buyer in another country finds it through a portal and sends the branded link to her brother, who lives an hour away from the house. She watches the video at 2am her time, twice, with the sound off. He fills in the enquiry form asking for Saturday and writes that he is viewing on his sister's behalf.

The agent replies that morning with a time and a line about the airport drive. The brother visits, records his own phone walkthrough for her, and compares it against the listing video on the spot. They match. She instructs her advisers that week. The agent spoke to the buyer once, by message, and the listing had done the rest.

What trips up listings for overseas buyers

  • Copy that only a local understands. Neighbourhood nicknames, local landmarks without context, distances with no reference point. Write for a stranger.
  • One unit system. Square feet alone makes the buyer guess. Give both.
  • A video that needs sound. A voiceover-only video, watched on mute in another language, says nothing. Captions carry the facts.
  • Edits that will not survive the representative's visit. The brother, the adviser or the buyer's agent will stand in the room and compare. Sky, lawn, declutter and staging edits all get checked by someone whose job is to check. Keep the disclosure on each edit and compare it with the original before you publish.
  • Waiting for a phone call. It is not coming. The enquiry form is the phone call.
  • Explaining the buying process on the page. That is their advisers' job and it changes by country. Stay on the property.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What should a listing include for international buyers?

A captioned video that works without sound and a complete gallery that includes the street and the surroundings. Then the facts a stranger needs: floor and lot areas in both square feet and square metres, distances in miles and kilometres, and the drive time from the nearest international airport. Finish with an enquiry form that asks for a viewing date, because the buyer or their representative will write rather than call.

Should the listing explain the buying process for foreign buyers?

No. The process differs by country and belongs to the buyer's advisers and lawyers. Keep the page on the property and the location; that is the part only you can describe accurately.

How do international buyers usually view a property?

Online first, at odd hours, often through a relative, an adviser or a buyer's agent who visits on their behalf and compares the real rooms with the listing. That representative will check every photo, so edits that change what they find in the room cost you the sale.

Is a voiceover useful for an international audience?

It can help, but the captions are what travel. Many viewers will watch on mute in a second language, so make sure the word-by-word captions carry the room names and the key facts on their own.
