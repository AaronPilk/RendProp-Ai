Property website links for open houses: a sign-in that follows up

Replace the clipboard with the property page. Visitors scan a code, see the home and fill in a form that asks when they want to come back.

Use the property page as the open house sign-in. A QR code on a stand at the door opens the branded page; the visitor sees the home they are standing in, swipes the gallery, and fills in the enquiry form, which asks for a name, a way to reach them, and the date they would like to come back. By the time the last visitor leaves, you have a follow-up list that tells you who wants a second look and when, which is the one thing a clipboard never captures.

Paper sign-ins produce illegible emails and a Sunday evening of guessing who was serious. A page sign-in produces a list sorted by intent.

Why the property page beats a sign-in sheet

It is legible. The visitor types their own details into their own phone. No deciphering handwriting.

It asks the question that matters. "When would you like to come back?" A visitor who picks a date has told you they are interested. A visitor who leaves it blank has told you they were browsing. Paper does not sort that.

The visitor leaves with the page. They opened it on their phone. They can forward it to a partner who could not make it, watch the video in the car, and show their parents at dinner.

It is the same link as everywhere else. The sign, the flyer, the Instagram post and the sign-in all go to one page. The visitor who scanned the sign on Thursday and comes to the open house on Saturday lands on a page they have already seen.

Setting it up for Saturday

  1. Make sure the page is live and correct: main photo, price, gallery, video, form. Test the form from your own phone and confirm the submission arrives.
  2. Generate a QR code from the branded link. Print it large on a card or a small stand with a line underneath: "Scan to see photos and video, and let us know if you would like to come back."
  3. Put the stand at the door, where you greet people, not in the kitchen where they are already distracted. Point to it as they arrive: "If you scan that, you get the whole listing on your phone and you can tell me if you want a second look."
  4. Keep a paper sheet as a backup for the visitor whose phone is dead or who prefers paper. Transfer those names after.
  5. During the open house, glance at your inbox between visitors. A submission that says "today at four" is a conversation to have before they leave.

A worked example: fourteen visitors, three follow-ups

A 3-bed townhouse is open one to three on Saturday. Fourteen people come through. Nine scan the code. Of the nine, six fill in the form. Three pick a date: one for Sunday, one for Tuesday evening, one for "next weekend". Three leave the date blank with messages like "nice kitchen". The five who did not scan include two neighbours and a couple who said they were just starting.

Saturday at four you call the Sunday person and confirm eleven. You text the Tuesday person. You email the next-weekend person the link again with the video. You send the three blank-date people a short note with the page link. The neighbours get nothing, which is right. The couple who were just starting get a text with the page and an offer to send similar homes.

Total follow-up time: twenty minutes. On a clipboard, the same afternoon produces fourteen names, half unreadable, and no idea who wanted Sunday.

Open house sign-in mistakes

  • The code opens the wrong thing. Your homepage, a PDF, a portal listing. The visitor is standing in the home and wants the home. Use the branded property page.
  • The form is too long. A visitor at a door with people behind them will not fill in eight fields. Name, contact, message, date.
  • Nobody mentions the code. A stand nobody points to gets scanned by nobody. Say one sentence to each arrival.
  • No follow-up the same day. The form told you who wants Sunday. If you call them Monday, they went to another open house Sunday.
  • The unbranded link on the stand. No name, no number. The visitor who wants to call you on the drive home cannot.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Do visitors actually scan a QR code at an open house?

Most will if you point to it and say what it does in one sentence. A stand nobody mentions gets ignored. Keep a paper sheet for the few who prefer it and transfer those names afterward.

What should the open house sign-in form ask for?

Name, one contact method, an optional message, and the date they would like to come back. The date is the field that sorts serious visitors from browsers. Avoid anything about pre-approval or agent representation; ask that on the follow-up call.

Should I still collect names on paper?

Keep a paper backup for visitors with a dead phone or a preference for paper, and for any local rule on physical sign-ins; ask your broker what applies where you are. The page form is the primary list; paper fills the gaps.

How soon should I follow up after the open house?

The same day for anyone who picked a date, ideally before dinner. A visitor who asked for Sunday and hears nothing Saturday evening will book another home. Everyone else gets a short note with the page link within a day.
