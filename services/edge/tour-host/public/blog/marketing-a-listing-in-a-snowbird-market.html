Marketing a listing in a snowbird market

In a snowbird market the seller leaves for the season and the buyer arrives with it. Shoot before the move, run it from afar, refresh for the return.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. In a snowbird market the seller and the buyer run on two different calendars: the seller leaves for the season just as the listing starts, and the buyers arrive with the season months later. In a snowbird market the seller and the buyer are on two different calendars. The seller often decides to sell at the end of the season and leaves within weeks, so the listing starts with an empty home and an owner two thousand miles away. The buyers arrive with the next season, months later, and shop while they are in town. Market the listing by shooting before the seller leaves and while the furniture is still there, by running the listing from a distance with the property page as the one place everyone looks, by keeping the empty home showing well through the quiet months, and by planning a refresh for the week the seasonal buyers come back.

Two calendars

The seller's calendar says shoot now, before the moving truck, and then go. The buyer's calendar says look in the busy season, see three or four places in a week, decide before the flight home, and often buy a place they first saw online during the summer from a long way away. Between those two calendars sits a stretch of months where the home is empty, the seller is remote and the local buyers are few. The listing has to survive that stretch without looking abandoned, which means a page that stays current, a video that still does the first showing, and a schedule of small refreshes rather than a single launch.

The out-of-season period is also when the year-round local buyer and the investor shop, with less competition. Do not switch the marketing off; aim it at them, with the facts they want (association, systems, rental rules with a verification note) and a page that answers without a phone call.

A condo whose owner left in April

A two-bed, two-bath condo on the second floor of a low-rise building near a golf course. The owner decides to sell in late March and flies home on the twelfth of April. The agent books the photographer for the fifth, while the furniture is in place and the lanai has its chairs. The shoot covers the unit, the lanai at 6pm, the pool and clubhouse with the association's permission, and a portrait walk from the parking space to the front door and on to the pool with the minutes in the captions. The owner approves the page by opening the branded link on his phone at the airport.

Through the summer the listing is quiet but not dead. The agent posts a new social video in June opening on the pool, answers two investor enquiries with the association facts, and has the cleaner open the blinds and run the air before each of the three showings. In October, two weeks before the seasonal residents return, the agent reshoots the lanai in the softer light, makes it the new main photo, and sends the database the branded link with a subject line about the evening. The buyer who had saved the listing in July fills in the enquiry form for the first week she is back. She views it twice and offers before Thanksgiving. The owner signs from his kitchen two time zones away, having never flown back.

Marketing an empty home from far away

  • Shoot furnished, sell empty. The photos from the week before the seller left are the ones that do the work. If the home is already empty, virtual staging sized to the real rooms and labelled as virtually staged is the honest substitute; compare it with the empty original before publishing.
  • Make the page the single source of truth. The remote seller, the cleaner, the association and every buyer look at the same link. Price, details and status live there.
  • Keep the home showing well. A cleaner or a neighbour who opens the blinds, runs the water and sets the thermostat an hour before each showing is worth more than any edit.
  • Send the seller the link, not the files. A seller two thousand miles away approves a page on a phone in a minute; a folder of files takes a week.
  • Refresh for the return. A new main photo and a new video opening in the week the seasonal buyers arrive gives the listing a second launch to the people most likely to buy.

Where snowbird listings go wrong

  • Shooting after the furniture left. An empty condo in harsh light, shot in a hurry after the truck, is the listing for the next six months. Book the photographer before the move.
  • Letting the page go stale. A summer open house date still showing in November, a price that does not match the MLS, a video that still says just listed. The seasonal buyer who returns to a stale page assumes the home has problems.
  • Switching the marketing off in the quiet months. The local and investor buyers are shopping with less competition. Give them facts.
  • Edits that overpromise the season. A blue sky over the summer storm season, a twilight the lanai does not actually get. The returning buyer visits in person and compares. Keep the disclosure and compare each edit with its original.
  • Nobody local to open the blinds. A dark, stuffy showing undoes a good page. Arrange the person before the seller leaves.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

When should I photograph a home in a snowbird market?

Before the seller leaves and while the furniture is still in place, even if the listing will not launch for a few weeks. The furnished photos carry the listing through the empty months. If the home is already empty, use virtual staging sized to the real rooms and labelled as virtually staged.

Should I keep marketing a seasonal home during the off-season?

Yes, aimed at year-round locals and investors, who shop with less competition in the quiet months. Keep the page current, post a fresh social video, and answer enquiries with the association, systems and rental-rule facts those buyers want.

How do I run a listing when the seller is two thousand miles away?

Make the property page the single source of truth for price, details and status, send the seller the branded link rather than files, and arrange a cleaner or neighbour to open the blinds and set the thermostat before each showing. The seller can review and approve from a phone.

What should change when the seasonal buyers come back?

A new main photo shot in the season's light, a new social video opening on a different space, and an email to the database with the same branded link. It gives the listing a second launch to the people most likely to buy.
