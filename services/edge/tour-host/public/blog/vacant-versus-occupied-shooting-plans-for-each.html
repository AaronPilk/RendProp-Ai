Vacant versus occupied: shooting plans for each

A vacant home and an occupied home need different shoot plans: one is about scale and staging, the other about editing what is there. Both plans.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A vacant home is shot for scale and for staging: consistent frames that show the floor and two walls so added furniture sits right, and the staged versions labelled. An occupied home is shot for what is there: a pre-shoot tidy with the seller, frames that work around the furniture, and a decluttering edit used sparingly and labelled. The plan changes before you arrive, not on the doorstep.

A vacant home and an occupied home are two different shoots. The vacant one is about scale: frames that show the floor and the walls clearly enough that a buyer can read the room and virtual staging can sit in it convincingly. The occupied one is about editing what is there: a tidy agreed with the seller before you arrive, frames composed around the furniture, and a decluttering edit used only where it is honest. Decide which plan you are on when the booking comes in, because the preparation is different.

Most mistakes in either case come from treating one like the other: shooting a vacant home from the corner so the staging has nowhere to go, or shooting an occupied home as if the sofa were not there.

The vacant plan

Empty rooms photograph flat. There is nothing to give scale, and a wide lens makes them look either enormous or like a cell. The plan:

  1. Tripod at one height, every room. Staging is only convincing if the staged frames share a horizon.
  2. Frame for the furniture that is not there. Stand where the room would be viewed from with a sofa in it: usually the doorway, showing the floor and the two walls the furniture would sit against. Leave floor in the bottom third.
  3. Verticals straight. Staging on a tilted frame produces furniture that leans.
  4. Light every room evenly. No pools of light on bare floor; staging will not hide them.
  5. Shoot the defects honestly. A worn patch of carpet stays visible. Staging may not cover it.

Then stage the rooms that need scale: the main living space, the primary bedroom, any oddly shaped room. Two or three frames. Each staged version is labelled Virtually staged and delivered with the empty original. The vacant home post goes through the frame in detail, and the staging service post covers the labels and limits.

The occupied plan

The seller's furniture is the subject whether you like it or not. The plan starts before the shoot:

  1. Send the seller a prep list two days ahead. Counters clear, beds made, personal photos down, pet things away, cars off the drive, lights working. The agent usually sends this; confirm it went.
  2. Walk the home before you unpack. Decide which rooms are shootable as they are, which need five minutes of moving things, and which should be left out of the set.
  3. Compose around the furniture. Shoot from the position that shows the room's shape with the furniture in a natural place, not the widest possible angle with a chair in the corner.
  4. Move what moves, not what the buyer will see. A pile of mail, yes. A wall-mounted unit that conveys, no.
  5. Keep the frames honest about size. Furniture gives scale, which is useful. Do not go wider to shrink it.

Decluttering as an edit, and where it stops

A tidy-the-room edit removes movable items from a frame. On an occupied home it is tempting to use it everywhere, and that is where it goes wrong. Use it for the things the seller would have removed if they had time: the laundry, the counter clutter, the toys. Do not use it to remove fixtures, fittings, visible damage or anything that stays with the home. The edit carries an AI-alteration disclosure, and the original stays in the set. The tidy the room post draws the line with examples.

In Rendprop both edits live in the photo studio: add furniture for vacant rooms, labelled Virtually staged, and tidy the room for occupied ones, with an AI-alteration disclosure. Every edit is a separate version, the untouched original stays in photo history, and you compare each version against the original before choosing which to use. AI can change or invent details, so look at the edges and count the fixed items. The photo studio page describes each edit. Check the agent's MLS rules before posting altered images.

Two worked shoots

Vacant three-bed, Thursday morning. Tripod at chest height. Living room from the hall doorway with the floor and both long walls in frame; primary bedroom from the foot of where the bed would go; the small third bedroom from the door. Fourteen landscape photos on the path, five portrait frames for the reel. Living room and primary staged, labelled, originals delivered. The third bedroom left empty, because staging it would hide how small it is.

Occupied four-bed, Saturday afternoon. Seller prepped most rooms; the home office is a mess and the garage is full. Office shot after five minutes moving boxes to the hall, with a tidy edit for the papers on the desk, labelled. Garage left out of the set. Living room composed from the far side so the sectional reads as part of the room rather than the whole of it. Sixteen landscape photos, five portrait. One edit in the whole set.

What goes wrong on each

  • Vacant, shot from corners. Rooms look huge, staging looks wrong, buyers feel misled.
  • Vacant, every room staged. Looks like a showroom and multiplies review time.
  • Occupied, no prep. You spend the shoot moving things and the set still looks lived-in.
  • Occupied, decluttered to vacant. Removing all furniture by edit is a staging decision dressed as a tidy. Do not.
  • Either, edits not compared. A staged frame with a moved wall or a tidy that removed a vent gets caught at the showing, not before.

Next step: write two short shoot plans, one per case, and attach the right one to the booking confirmation the moment the agent tells you whether the home is empty.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Which rooms should be staged in a vacant home?

The main living space, the primary bedroom, and any room whose scale is hard to read empty. Two or three frames. Leave small rooms unstaged if staging would make them look larger than they are.

How much should I move in an occupied home?

Movable clutter the seller would have cleared given time: mail, laundry, counter items, toys. Not fixtures, fittings or anything that conveys with the home. If a room needs more than five minutes of moving, consider leaving it out of the set.

Can a decluttering edit remove furniture?

Removing all the furniture turns a tidy into a staging decision and misrepresents the room. Use tidy-the-room for small movable items only, keep the disclosure on, and compare with the original before using it.

Do both staged and decluttered photos need labels?

Yes. Rendprop labels staged versions Virtually staged and gives decluttering and other edits an AI-alteration disclosure. Deliver the original alongside and check the agent's MLS rules on altered images.
