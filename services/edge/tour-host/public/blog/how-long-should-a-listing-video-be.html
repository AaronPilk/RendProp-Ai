How long should a listing video be

A social reel should run 20 to 35 seconds. A property page or YouTube listing video should run 60 to 120 seconds. Length follows photos and script.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A listing reel for Instagram, TikTok or Shorts should run 20 to 35 seconds. The 16:9 listing video for the property page or YouTube should run 60 to 120 seconds, closer to 60 for a condo and closer to 120 for a large home. Length is an outcome of how many photos you include and how long the script is, so set those first and the duration follows.

A listing reel for social should run 20 to 35 seconds. The horizontal listing video for the property page or YouTube should run 60 to 120 seconds, with a condo near the bottom of that range and a five-bedroom on acreage near the top. Length is not a setting you pick; it is the result of how many photos you include and how many words are in the script. Decide those two things and the duration takes care of itself.

Length by where the video plays

  • Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts: 20 to 35 seconds. The viewer did not choose to watch. Every second has to earn the next one. Six to ten photos, a 65 to 80 word script.
  • Property page: 60 to 120 seconds. The viewer opened the page on purpose. They want the tour. Ten to fifteen photos, a script that walks every main room.
  • YouTube main upload: 60 to 120 seconds. Same as the page video; it usually is the page video. A longer cut is fine if the home warrants it, but the second minute has to keep showing new rooms.
  • The MLS: not a video length question. The virtual tour field is built for a link. Put the unbranded property page link there and the page carries the 16:9 video.
  • Text message: the reel or the link. If you send a video by text, send the short one. Better, send the property page link and let the preview do the work.

What sets the length

Photo count. A photo-based video gives each frame a few seconds of motion. Six photos make a short reel; fourteen make a tour. The quickest way to shorten a video that runs long is to take photos out, and the quickest way to make a thin one fuller is to add the rooms you skipped.

Script length. A 70-word script fills about 30 seconds. A 180-word script fills about 80. The voice sets the pace, and the photos should match it: one sentence per frame on a reel, one or two per frame on the tour.

The home. A studio does not need 90 seconds. A six-bedroom with a pool house and a workshop cannot be done in 30. Let the size of the place set the size of the tour, within the ranges above.

A length test you can run in a minute

Watch the video and notice the first moment you want to scroll. On a reel that moment should not arrive before the end card. On the page video it should not arrive before the yard. If it does, the fix is nearly always to cut frames, not to speed up the motion. A second angle of the hall bath, the third bedroom from the doorway, the laundry room: these are the frames that make a viewer leave.

Then watch it again with the sound off. A video that holds with captions alone is the right length. One that only holds with the voice is carrying a frame or two it does not need.

A worked example: the same home, three lengths

A four-bed, three-bath on a quarter acre with a renovated kitchen and a pool.

The reel: pool, kitchen, living room, main bedroom, back of the house from the pool, front exterior. Six photos, 68 words, 28 seconds. Ends on the open house date.

The page video: front, entry, living from two angles, kitchen from two angles, dining, main bedroom, main bath, three bedrooms, hall bath, office, deck, pool, yard. Fifteen photos, 190 words, about 85 seconds. Ends on the pool at dusk.

A 15-second open house cut: pool, kitchen, front, with the date and time in the caption. Three photos, 30 words. Same photo set, three durations, each sized for its job.

Signs a listing video is too long

  • Two frames of the same room from similar angles.
  • Any frame the script has nothing to say about.
  • A spoken introduction of you or your brokerage before the first room.
  • A reel over 40 seconds. If it needs that long, it is a page video, not a reel.
  • Square footage, lot size and year built read aloud. Those live in the caption and on the page.

Signs it is too short

  • A page video that skips a bedroom. Buyers count them.
  • A reel that ends before saying where the home is or what to do next.
  • A tour that shows the kitchen for two seconds on a listing where the kitchen is the point.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How long should a real estate reel be?

Between 20 and 35 seconds. The viewer has not chosen to watch and will scroll the moment the video stops showing them something new. Six to ten photos and a 65 to 80 word script land in that range.

How long should the video on the property page be?

Between 60 and 120 seconds. The buyer opened the page on purpose and wants the full walk, so include every main room once. A condo sits near 60 seconds; a large home on land can use the full two minutes.

How do I make a listing video shorter?

Remove photos. Second angles of small rooms, hallways, closets and laundry rooms go first. Then shorten the script by cutting adjectives and any number that already appears on the listing page. Speeding up the camera motion is the wrong fix.

Is there a right length for the MLS?

The virtual tour field is built for a link rather than a video file, so length is not the question. Put the unbranded property page link there and let the 16:9 video on the page carry the tour. Check your own MLS rules on what the page may contain.
