Follow-up from a listing enquiry: first reply, first call

The first reply to a listing enquiry goes out within the hour, confirms their date and offers one alternative. The first call has one job: book it.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

The first reply to a listing enquiry should go out the same hour, confirm the date the visitor asked for, and offer one alternative in case that date fails. The first call, ideally the same day, has one job: find out what they want to see and book it. Whether they are pre-approved, whether they have a home to sell, whether they are working with another agent, all of that can wait until you are standing in the kitchen together. Buyers enquire about a house, not about a process.

The first reply: four lines

Write it by text if they gave a mobile number, by email if not. Four lines, no more.

  1. Their name and the address, so they know which enquiry this is. People enquire about several homes in one evening.
  2. A yes to the date they asked for, or the nearest you can do, with a time.
  3. One alternative date and time.
  4. Your name and the branded link to the page, so they can look again while they decide.

That is it. No paragraph about the neighbourhood, no questions about financing, no attachment. The reply exists to turn a date they typed into a date on your calendar. The link at the end is there because a buyer who re-opens the page after your reply is a buyer who is still thinking about it.

The first call: what to ask and what to skip

Call after the reply, not instead of it. Some people do not answer unknown numbers, and the reply means your call is expected. On the call, ask three things:

  • What made them stop on this one. The answer tells you what to show first at the viewing and what the video should have led with.
  • Who is coming to the showing. Partner, parent, children. It sets the length and the tone.
  • Whether the date in the form still works, and confirm it out loud.

Skip, for now: pre-approval, the home they would sell, their agent situation, their timeline. Those are real questions and you will ask them, after they have seen the house and want it. Asked before, they sound like a gate.

A Thursday evening enquiry, handled

At 7:40 p.m. on a Thursday an enquiry comes in for a 3-bed in a cul-de-sac: a first name, a mobile number, a date of Saturday, and a one-line question about whether the garage is heated. At 7:52 the reply goes out by text: their name, the address, Saturday at 11 works, or Sunday at 2 if that is easier, the garage is not heated but has power, your name, the link. At 8:05 you call. They answer because they just got the text. They stopped on the listing because of the back deck in the video. It is the two of them and a dog. Saturday at 11 is confirmed. The call lasted four minutes. At the showing on Saturday you start on the deck, and only afterwards, back in the kitchen, do you ask about their situation. They are pre-approved and renting month to month. That question would have landed badly on Thursday night.

Replies that lose the buyer

  • The canned paragraph. A reply that could have gone to anyone reads as one that did.
  • Questions before a date. Asking whether they are pre-approved in the first message converts a buyer into someone who did not reply.
  • The morning-after reply. An enquiry at 7:40 p.m. answered at 9 a.m. has competed overnight with every other listing they saved.
  • No alternative date. If Saturday fails and you offered nothing else, the thread ends.
  • Replying from the wrong place. Form enquiries in one inbox, portal messages in another, texts in a third. Whichever you check least is where the next good buyer is waiting.

After the showing

The second message is where the process questions belong, framed as help: if they want to move on it, what do they need from you. If they do not want it, ask what was off; the answer improves the listing's video or page for the next buyer, and sometimes the price conversation with the seller.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How fast should I reply to a listing enquiry?

Within the hour if it arrives during waking hours, and first thing in the morning if it came in overnight. The buyer enquired about several homes; the one that answers first is the one they visit first.

Should I ask about pre-approval in the first reply?

No. The first reply confirms a date and offers one alternative. Ask about financing, their current home and their timeline at or after the showing, when they have a reason to answer.

Text or email for the first reply?

Text if they gave a mobile number, because it gets read. Email otherwise. Either way, four lines: name and address, a yes to their date with a time, one alternative, your name and the page link.

What if the buyer does not answer the call?

Leave a short message that repeats the date in your text, and stop. They have your reply and the link. Two more attempts over two days is enough; after that, the next enquiry deserves the time.
