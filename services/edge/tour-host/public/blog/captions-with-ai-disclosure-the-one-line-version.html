Captions with AI disclosure: the one-line version

One plain line at the end of the caption, naming the edit: sky replaced, lawn edited, living room virtually staged. Templates for the three common cases.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Put one plain line at the end of the caption that names the edit: Sky replaced in the exterior photo. Living room virtually staged; the room is empty. Kitchen counters tidied in the photo. It is nine words, it matches the label on the photo itself, and it ends the argument in the comments before it starts.

The AI disclosure in a caption is one plain line at the end that names the edit, such as sky replaced in the exterior photo, living room virtually staged and empty in person, or kitchen counters tidied in the photo. It runs nine words or so, sits after the ask and before any hashtags, and matches whatever label the photo itself carries. It costs nothing to write and it settles the question before anyone in the comments asks it.

Why one line and not a paragraph

A disclosure that runs three sentences reads like a legal notice and makes the edit sound like a bigger deal than it is. A disclosure that is missing invites the one commenter who notices the sky to say so publicly, and then the thread is about the edit rather than the home. One specific line does the job: it says what changed, it says nothing else, and the reader moves on.

Specific matters. Photos enhanced tells the reader nothing and sounds like a hedge. Sky replaced tells them exactly what to discount. The more precise the line, the less it sounds like you are hiding something.

Three captions, three disclosures

The twilight exterior

A two-storey brick home photographed at two in the afternoon, with the sky converted to dusk and the windows warmed.

Caption: The front of this one faces west, which is the whole reason for the porch. Four beds, two baths, 2,100 square feet, on a quiet loop. $468,000. Open Saturday 11 to 1, link in bio. Exterior photo is a twilight edit; the house was shot in daylight.

The disclosure says what the edit did and what the original was. A reader who shows up at noon knows why the house does not look like the photo.

The staged living room

A vacant home with furniture added to the living room photo.

Caption: Vacant, which means you can see the floors. Oak throughout, refinished in 2024. Three beds, one and a half baths, $312,000. Showings from Tuesday, link in bio. Living room virtually staged; the home is empty.

Virtually staged is the standard phrase and the one to use, so that it matches the label on the photo and is the phrase to check against your MLS's current rules. Add that the home is empty so nobody expects the sofa.

The tidied kitchen

An occupied home where the counters in the kitchen photo were cleared with an edit.

Caption: The seller cooks every night and the kitchen shows it, in a good way: gas range, a real pantry, a window over the sink. Three beds, two baths, $389,000. Open Sunday 1 to 3, link in bio. Kitchen counters tidied in the photo.

Tidying is the edit people forget to disclose because it feels like cleaning. It is still a change to what the buyer will see, and the line costs five words.

Where the line goes

  1. After the ask, so the caption still ends with what you want the reader to do, followed by the honest note.
  2. Before the hashtags, so it is read as part of the caption and not buried in a list.
  3. On every post that uses the edited frame, not only the first one. A twilight exterior in a sold post two months later still gets the line.
  4. Matching the photo. If the photo carries a Virtually staged label, the caption says virtually staged. If the photo carries an AI-alteration note, the caption names the alteration.

Lines that do not work

  • Photos may be enhanced. Vague, and it reads as covering for something.
  • AI was used in this post. Overstates it. The reader now wonders whether the house exists.
  • The disclosure in a hashtag. Nobody reads it, and it looks like you hoped nobody would.
  • A disclosure on the photo but not in the caption. The reel plays at thumb speed; the caption is where people read.
  • Disclosing the staging and not the other edit. If the staged living room also had a ceiling fan removed, say both. The edit a buyer notices at the showing is the one you did not mention.
  • No disclosure because the edit was small. Small is the reader's call, not yours. Name it and let them decide.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What should an AI disclosure line in a caption say?

Name the edit and nothing else: sky replaced in the exterior photo, living room virtually staged and the home is empty, kitchen counters tidied in the photo. One line, after the ask, before the hashtags.

Do I need the disclosure on every post that uses the photo?

Yes. The edit does not expire. A twilight exterior used again in a sold post two months later carries the same line.

Is photos enhanced an acceptable disclosure?

It is vague enough to read as a hedge. Say what changed. A specific line sounds more honest than a general one and gives the reader something they can actually discount.

What if the photo is labelled on the image itself?

Keep the label and add the caption line too. Reels play fast and labels get missed; the caption is where people read. The two should say the same thing.
