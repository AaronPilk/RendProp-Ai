Can I use one app for homes and for my gym

Yes, if the app has modes. A home needs price, beds and baths; a gym needs hours, classes and membership. Photos, video and page work the same.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Yes, provided the app has a mode for each. A home page needs price, beds, baths and square feet; a gym page needs hours, classes, capacity and membership. The parts underneath are identical: photos in, a short video out, a page with a gallery, details, a contact card and an enquiry form. Why the same workflow fits both

A buyer deciding whether to view a house and a person deciding whether to visit a gym are doing the same thing: trying to see a space before they commit to going there. Both want to know what it looks like inside, what it costs, where it is and how to get in touch. Both will open the link on a phone, scroll the photos, maybe play the video, and either fill in a form or close the tab.

The photographer's skills transfer too. Wide, level, bright, tidy. The reception desk is the front door; the main floor is the living room; the changing rooms are the bathrooms, and they matter just as much.

What a mode actually changes

  • The fields. Price, beds, baths, square feet for a home. Hours, menu, capacity or membership for a business.
  • The words. A viewing becomes a visit, a trial or a tour; the listing becomes the venue, the restaurant, the studio.
  • The sample content. Placeholder text and example prompts that make sense for that kind of space, so you are not editing house copy into gym copy.
  • What the enquiry means. A showing request for a home; a trial class, a tour or a membership question for a gym; a date and headcount for a venue.

What a mode should not change is the quality bar on photos, the comparison of AI edits against originals, or where the enquiries land.

A worked example: an agent who owns a boxing gym

An agent lists eight to ten homes a year and co-owns a boxing gym with her brother. She has been using one app for the listings and paying a web designer for the gym.

In the same app, she creates a workspace in the gym mode. The fields ask for hours, class types, capacity and membership options instead of beds and baths. She shoots the gym on a Sunday morning when it is empty and clean: the ring from the door, the bag line, the strength corner, the changing rooms, the front desk. Fourteen landscape photos, six portrait. The video is 30 seconds with her brother's voice saying what a first visit looks like and that the first session is a trial. The page has the hours, the three membership options, the address and a form that asks when the visitor wants to come in.

Her home listings sit in the real estate workspace with their own fields. Nothing bleeds across. The gym page replaced the designer; the listings carried on as before. One app, one inbox, two kinds of enquiry she can tell apart at a glance because each arrives under its own listing.

What goes wrong when homes and a gym share an app

  1. Using the home mode for a business. A gym page with a price field and a beds field looks like a mistake, because it is.
  2. Mixing them in one workspace. Keep homes and businesses in separate workspaces or clearly separate listings so the enquiries and the share links do not get confused.
  3. Shooting the gym at 6 p.m. Full of people, half of whom did not agree to be photographed. Shoot it empty and clean, then add one or two portrait frames with consenting members for life.
  4. Same voiceover tone for both. The gym video can be warmer and more direct than the listing video. Write each for its visitor.
  5. Forgetting the business page needs updating. A home listing ends. A gym's hours and classes change. Put a date in the calendar to check the page each quarter.

If you already have an app and a business

If you already use an app for listings and you have a business on the side, check whether it has a mode for that business. If it does, shoot the space empty this weekend and publish the page from the same app. If it does not, the designer invoice is the price of the missing mode.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What is a mode in a listing marketing app?

A setting, chosen when you create a listing or workspace, that changes the fields, the words and the sample content to fit the kind of space: homes, venues, restaurants, gyms, retail or offices. The photo, video and page workflow stays the same underneath.

Do I need separate accounts for homes and a business?

No, if the app supports modes. Keep them in separate workspaces or clearly separate listings so enquiries and share links stay distinct, but one account and one inbox is the point.

Does a gym page need a video?

A short one helps more than you would expect. Thirty seconds of the space, empty and clean, with a voice saying what a first visit involves answers the question people are nervous about: what it is like to walk in.

Can a photographer use the same kit for homes and businesses?

Yes. Wide, level, bright and tidy applies to a gym floor as much as a living room. The differences are timing, shooting when the space is empty, and consent if any people appear in frame.
