Luxury open house: by appointment, with a page to match

Replace the public open house with appointments inside a window, qualified by a short call, and let a restrained property page do the public work.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. For a luxury listing, swap the public Sunday for appointments inside a window, say Saturday 11 to 3 in forty-five minute slots, each preceded by a short call to confirm the buyer is real. The property page does the public part: a full gallery, a quiet video, the details, and an enquiry form that asks when they want to come. The page should be as restrained as the house.

A luxury listing does not need a public open house; it needs a window of appointments and a page that does the public work. Saturday 11 to 3, forty-five minute slots, each one booked after a short call that confirms the buyer is real and the timing suits them. Everyone else, which is most of the people who would have walked through a public Sunday, gets the property page: the full gallery, a quiet video, the details, and an enquiry form that asks when they want to visit.

The public open house at this level produces a crowd of neighbours and a seller who is upset about their rug. The appointment window produces four conversations with people who can buy the house. The page is how you make sure the four find you.

Lakeshore Drive, Saturday 11 to 3

An estate on Lakeshore Drive, priced in the low millions, with a dock, a guest house and a kitchen the seller spent a year on. Listed on a Tuesday. The marketing:

  • Tuesday. The page goes live with thirty photos, a three-minute video with a calm voiceover and no music, and the facts: beds, baths, square feet, lot, year built, price. The description is short. The enquiry form is at the bottom.
  • Wednesday. The link goes to the twenty agents who sell at this price on this lake, with “appointments Saturday 11 to 3; call me to book.” It goes to your database as a short email with one photo and the link. It goes on your social accounts with the video and the words “private viewings Saturday.”
  • Thursday and Friday. Calls come in. Each gets five minutes: who they are, who their agent is, what they are looking for, whether Saturday works. Five slots fill. One caller is a neighbour; you offer the page instead.
  • Saturday. Five appointments, forty-five minutes each, fifteen minutes between. You walk each one from the gate, not the door, because the approach is part of the house. The seller is away for the day.
  • Sunday. Two enquiries came through the page overnight asking for the following week; you reply with times.

No sign-in sheet, no crowd, no rug.

The qualifying call

It is five minutes and it is not an interrogation. Three questions. Are you working with an agent. What are you looking for in a home at this level. Does Saturday between 11 and 3 suit you. The answers tell you whether to book a slot, suggest a different time, or offer the page and a follow-up in a month. Nobody who can buy the house objects to the call; they expect it.

Agents calling for their clients get the same three questions and the same courtesy. The page link goes to them so they can prepare their buyer, and the slot is confirmed by text.

A page as restrained as the house

At this price the page is the first showing and it is judged like one. Restraint reads as confidence.

  • The gallery. Thirty photos is fine; forty is fine. In order, exterior to interior to grounds. No duplicates, no three angles of the same bathroom.
  • The video. Longer than a reel, slower than a reel, no music or music low enough to forget. A voiceover that says what the photos cannot: the dock depth, the age of the roof, what is across the water.
  • The description. Short, specific, no adjectives that need a straight face. The kitchen's maker, the architect if there is one, the year of the renovation.
  • The form. Present and plain, with its date field, so a request for a private viewing arrives with a day attached.
  • The unbranded link. For the MLS virtual tour field only. It has no contact card and no form, so it is not the one an agent should forward to a buyer.

Where luxury open houses fail

  • Public Sunday, public crowd. Forty people, three buyers, one broken vase.
  • A page that shouts. Music, captions in capitals, “once in a lifetime.” The buyers at this level have seen a hundred pages and trust the quiet ones.
  • Treating an appointment like a tour. Forty-five minutes is for conversation. Walk ahead, open the doors, then stop talking and let them stand in the kitchen.
  • Valuables left out. Appointments or not, the seller clears art, jewellery and papers. Ask on Tuesday.
  • Slots back to back. Fifteen minutes between, so one buyer never meets the next in the drive.
  • No one at the gate. The approach is the first room. Meet them there.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should a luxury listing have a public open house?

Usually not. A public Sunday at this price brings neighbours and curious visitors, and a seller worried about their belongings. A window of forty-five minute appointments, each booked after a short call, produces a handful of conversations with people who can buy the house, and the property page serves everyone else.

What should I ask on the qualifying call before a luxury showing?

Three things, in five minutes: whether they are working with an agent, what they are looking for in a home at this level, and whether the appointment window suits them. Buyers at this price expect the call. The answers tell you whether to book, suggest another time, or send the page and follow up later.

What should a luxury property page look like?

Restrained. A full gallery in order, a slower video with a calm voiceover and little or no music, a short specific description, the facts, and a plain enquiry form that asks when the buyer wants to come. Music, capitals and superlatives read as insecurity to buyers who have seen a hundred pages.

How long should each appointment be?

About forty-five minutes, with fifteen minutes between so one buyer never meets the next in the drive. Walk ahead and open the doors, then stop talking. At this level the appointment is a conversation in the kitchen more than a tour.
