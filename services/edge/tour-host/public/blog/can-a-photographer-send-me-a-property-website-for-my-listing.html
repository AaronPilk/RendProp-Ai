Can a photographer send me a property website for my listing

Yes, if their tool puts your contact details on the page and sends enquiries to you. Ask for both links and a plan for after the sale.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Yes, and a growing number of photographers deliver one with the photos. The conditions that make it useful to you: your name and number on the page rather than theirs, enquiries routed to you, a branded link for your marketing and an unbranded link for the MLS, and a clear answer to what happens to the page when the home sells or the photographer's account changes.

Yes, and more photographers are delivering one with the photo set. The conditions that make it worth having: your name and number on the page rather than the photographer's, enquiries routed to you, a branded link for your marketing plus an unbranded link for the MLS, and a straight answer about what happens to the page when the home sells or when the photographer stops using that tool. If those four are in place, a photographer-built page saves you an evening per listing.

Why photographers have started doing this

The photographer already has the best version of every photo, in the right order, before anyone else. Building the video and the page from those files is a small step for them and a large one for you, and it turns a photo delivery into a finished launch kit. For the photographer it is a reason to be rebooked; for you it is a listing that goes live with a video and a page the same day the photos arrive.

The thing to watch is whose listing it is. The photographer is doing you a service, not taking over your marketing. The page has to present you.

What to ask for, in the order it matters

  1. Your contact card on the page. Your name, your business, your number. Not the photographer's, and not a generic form that goes to them.
  2. Enquiries to you. When a buyer fills in the form, you should get it, directly. Ask how that is set up and test it with a fake enquiry before the listing goes live.
  3. Both links. A branded link for your texts, emails and posts, and an unbranded link for the MLS virtual-tour field. Check your MLS's current rules on what that field may contain.
  4. The video as well as the page. A landscape video for the page and a vertical cut for social, built from the same shoot.
  5. A plan for after the sale. Who marks the page sold, and does it stay reachable.
  6. Clarity on AI edits. If the photographer has applied twilight, sky or staging edits, each should be labelled and the originals should be in the delivery too.

A worked example: four shoots on Saturday, links on Sunday

A photographer shoots four homes on a Saturday for three different agents. Sunday morning, from the kitchen table, they build each listing: photos in, main photo chosen, a video from fourteen frames per home, and the agent's contact details on each page. By 11 a.m. each agent has a text with two links.

Agent one opens the branded link, scrolls the page, sees her own name and number on the contact card, and submits a test enquiry with Monday's date. It arrives in her own inbox. She pastes the unbranded link into the MLS entry Monday morning and sends the branded one to her database Monday afternoon. She has not opened a laptop, and the listing launched with a video.

Agent two gets the same delivery but notices the voiceover on the video mentions the photographer's studio name. He asks for a version without it before the video goes anywhere unbranded. Small catch, easy fix, and worth looking for.

Where a photographer-built page lets the agent down

  • The photographer's branding on your listing. A logo, a credit line in the description, a studio name in the voiceover. Fine on their portfolio, wrong on your MLS link. Check the content, not just the contact card.
  • Enquiries going to the photographer. They are not set up to handle buyers, and a lead that takes two days to reach you is a lead gone. Test the form before launch.
  • The page dies with the photographer's account. If they stop paying, switch tools or close the business, the QR code on your sign points at nothing. Ask what happens, in writing.
  • Unlabelled edits in the set. You are the one answering to the buyer and the MLS. Insist on labels and originals.
  • Nobody owns the sold step. The home closes and the page keeps saying for sale because each party thought the other would change it. Agree who does it.

For photographers reading this from the other side, delivering a property website with every shoot and presenting the agent's contact details cover the setup.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Whose name should be on a photographer-built property page?

Yours. The page is marketing for your listing, so the contact card should show your name, business and number, and enquiries should come to you. The photographer's credit belongs on their own portfolio, not on your MLS link.

How do I check that enquiries reach me?

Submit a test enquiry from the published page with a real date before the listing goes live, and confirm it arrives where you expect. Do this every time, not just the first time, because forwarding setups change.

What if the photographer stops using the tool?

Ask in advance what happens to published pages if their account changes or closes, and get the answer in writing. Avoid printing QR codes that point at a page whose future you do not know.

Should the photographer apply AI edits before sending the page?

Only with your agreement, with each edit labelled, and with the untouched originals included in the delivery. You answer to the buyer and the MLS for what is on the page, so you need to be able to compare every edit against its original.
