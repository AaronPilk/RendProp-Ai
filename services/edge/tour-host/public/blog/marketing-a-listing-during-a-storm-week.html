Marketing a listing during a storm week

When a storm hits launch week, decide on Monday: launch, delay, or split. Shoot interiors now, exterior when it clears, and let the page carry showings.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. When a storm lands on launch week, make one decision on Monday: launch as planned, delay a week, or split the launch. Splitting usually wins: shoot the interiors now, shoot the exterior the first clear morning after, and let the property page and video carry buyers through the days when nobody wants to drive. Never fake the exterior.

When a storm lands on the week you planned to launch, make one decision on Monday and hold to it: launch as planned, delay by a week, or split the launch. Splitting is usually the right call. Shoot the interiors while it is wet or snowing outside, shoot the exterior on the first clear morning after, and let the property page and the video carry buyers through the days when nobody wants to drive to a showing. The one thing not to do is fake the exterior.

Monday: three options and how to choose

The forecast is in. Here is how the choice usually falls.

  • Launch as planned if the photos are already done and the storm is a day or two of rain. Buyers browse more, not less, when they are stuck inside. Showings slip to the weekend; the page does the work until then.
  • Delay a week if the photos are not done, the storm covers the whole week, and the seller can wait. Launching onto a wet, grey exterior and a half-finished gallery wastes the first week of attention, which is the week you get the most of.
  • Split the launch if the photos are not done but the seller cannot wait, or the market is moving. Interiors shot indoors this week, the listing goes live on the interiors with a plain exterior, and the proper exterior and the video get added the first clear morning. This is the one most agents should pick most of the time.

Whatever you choose, tell the seller the same day and tell them why. A seller who hears on Thursday that the Thursday launch is not happening has a worse week than one who heard on Monday.

The split launch in practice

Interiors on the storm days

Rain and snow make soft, even light indoors, which suits most rooms. Lamps on, blinds open, and accept that the windows will show grey. The photographer shoots every room as landscape photos for the listing video and the best three or four as portrait frames for the social cut. Nothing outside is shot except a straight, honest frame of the front from the street, which goes on the page as a placeholder until the real one exists.

Exterior on the first clear morning

Book the photographer for the first morning the forecast shows clear, before you know the exact day. Shoot the front, the back, the yard and, if it is late in the day, a twilight. Then rebuild the video with the real exterior as the opening scene and swap the main photo on the page. Buyers who saw the placeholder see the upgrade; buyers who arrive later never knew there was one.

Showings during the week

Expect cancellations and do not chase them. Send every buyer who cancels the property page link and the video, and offer the first clear day. Rescheduling without losing the buyer is its own skill, covered in rescheduling showings around the weather.

The townhouse on Quarry Row, Tuesday to Sunday

A three-bed end-unit townhouse, launch planned for Thursday, photographer booked for Tuesday. Monday afternoon the forecast firmed up: rain and wind from Tuesday morning through Thursday night. The agent called the seller Monday at five and they agreed to split.

Tuesday. Photographer arrived at eleven in the rain. Interiors only, every lamp on. One honest frame of the front door and the covered entry from under the neighbour's porch, dry and sharp, as the placeholder exterior. Wednesday. Photos reviewed, page built with the entry frame as the main photo, details typed in, enquiry form tested. Thursday. Live on the MLS as planned, unbranded link in the tour field, branded link in the just-listed email. Two enquiries Thursday evening from people at home watching the rain. Friday. Clear by mid-morning. Photographer back at ten for the front, the back patio and the common green, then a twilight at dusk. Saturday. Video rebuilt with the twilight as the opening, main photo swapped, first reel posted. Four showings Saturday afternoon, three of them rescheduled from Wednesday. Sunday. Open house, dry.

The listing never looked unfinished to anyone who saw it after Saturday morning, and it was live two days before the sky cleared.

Where storm-week launches go wrong

A fake exterior. Shooting the house in sheeting rain and replacing the sky is the tempting shortcut. The sky might be fixable with a disclosure, but the wet pavement, the dripping gutters, the bare wet lawn and the dark windows are not, and an edit that tries to fix all of them produces a house that does not exist. Shoot the real one on Friday. The honest version of rain-day exteriors is in rainy-day listing photos and the edits that fix them.

Posting the reel on the worst day. A bright reel of a back garden going out while the street is flooding is a poor look. Hold the social cut until the exterior is real.

Letting the seller decide by mood. On Wednesday, in the rain, every seller wants to delay. The decision belongs on Monday with the forecast in front of you both.

Forgetting the placeholder. If you split, swap the main photo and rebuild the video the day the real exterior exists. A listing that still leads with the covered-entry frame two weeks later looks like nobody is paying attention.

How this works in Rendprop

Start the listing with the interior set on Tuesday and publish the property website on Wednesday with the entry frame as the main photo; sections of the page appear as you fill them in, so a page without a video yet is still a complete page. On Friday, add the exterior photos, choose the twilight as the main photo and build the video in the video step, where scene order and camera motion are planned automatically, and watch it once to see where the new exterior lands. Publish the property website again from the same listing so the page shows the new main photo and the video, and check that the links you sent on Thursday still open on it. Enquiries from the rainy evenings land in the app, per listing, ready for the Friday reply that offers Saturday.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should I delay a listing launch because of a storm?

Only if the photos are not done, the storm covers the whole week and the seller can wait. If the photos exist, launch; buyers browse more when they are indoors. If the photos are not done and the seller cannot wait, split the launch: interiors now, exterior on the first clear morning.

Can I shoot listing interiors while it is raining?

Yes, and they often look good. Rain gives soft, even light indoors. Turn on every lamp, open the blinds, and accept grey windows. Shoot the exterior separately on the first clear morning rather than trying to fix a wet facade afterwards.

What do I put on the page before the real exterior exists?

One honest, sharp frame of the entry or the front, shot from somewhere dry, as a placeholder main photo. Swap it for the real exterior and rebuild the video the day the weather clears. Do not leave the placeholder up for weeks.

What should I say to buyers who cancel showings for the weather?

Send the property page link and the video the same day and offer the first clear day. Most buyers who cancel for weather still want to see the house; a buyer who has watched the video is more likely to keep the rescheduled appointment.
