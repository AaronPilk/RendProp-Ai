QR codes on the yard sign and the flyer: send people to the page

A QR code on the sign rider and the flyer should open the branded property page, not your homepage or a PDF. Size it for a car window and test it first.

A QR code is a shortcut for someone who is already standing in front of the home. The page it opens has to answer the question they have right then: what is this, what does it cost, can I see inside.

Where the code should point

The branded property page. The person scanning is a buyer or a neighbour who will tell a buyer. Either way you want them to see your name and be able to call you. The unbranded link is for the MLS, not for the sign.

Avoid these destinations:

  • Your homepage or a search page. They have to find the home again. Most will not.
  • A PDF flyer. Slow to load, hard to read on a phone, no form, no video.
  • A portal listing. The contact button may route to a different agent.
  • A video on a social platform. Autoplays, shows unrelated content afterward, no enquiry path.
  • A shortened URL that redirects. One more hop to fail, and one more thing for a wary scanner to distrust.

Sizing and placement for a sign rider

A driver sees the sign from the road, slows, and scans from the car or walks up. The code needs to be legible from three to four feet for a walk-up and ideally scannable from the driveway. On a standard sign rider, that means the code should be the largest element on the rider, with high contrast (black on white, never white on a colour photo), a quiet margin around it, and a short line of text: "Scan for photos, video and showings."

Put it at eye level for someone standing, not at the top of the sign where glare hits. If the rider is double-sided, both sides get the code.

The flyer version

Flyers get picked up at open houses and left on kitchen counters. The code here can be smaller because it is scanned from hand distance, but the text under it matters more because the flyer will be looked at days later. Say what the page has: "Full gallery, listing video, book a showing." A flyer with a code and no explanation is a flyer with a decorative square.

A worked example: the sign goes up Thursday

A 4-bed two-storey lists Tuesday. The page is live Wednesday. Wednesday afternoon you generate a QR code from the branded link, drop it onto the sign rider artwork at full width, add the one-line caption, and send it to the sign company. Thursday the sign goes in. Before you drive away, you stand at the curb and scan it with your own phone. The page opens on the main photo with the price visible. You tap the video; it plays. You scroll to the form; it loads. Then you scan it from the driver's seat with the window down. If that works, it works.

Saturday, a neighbour walking a dog scans it and texts the link to her sister, who fills in the form asking for Sunday. That is the whole point of the code.

QR code mistakes that waste the sign

  • The code is too small or printed over a photo. It does not scan from the road and nobody walks up to a sign to try again.
  • The link changes after printing. If the page's address changes, the sign now opens nothing. The link has to stay the same for the life of the listing.
  • No test on the physical sign. A code that scanned on your screen can fail on vinyl in sunlight. Scan the real thing.
  • Generic code reused across listings. A code to your homepage on every sign means every scan starts with a search. Each listing gets its own code.
  • The page is not marked sold. Signs come down late. A buyer who scans a sold sign should see "sold" on the page, not a form.

One code, one listing, one link

Use the same branded link on the sign, the flyer, the social post and the email so that a buyer who meets the home in three places lands on one page each time. That consistency is what makes a QR code trustworthy: it opens what the caption says, every time.

The next step: scan the code on your current sign from the curb. If it opens anything other than that home's page with the price visible, reprint the rider. FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should the QR code open the branded or unbranded property page?

Branded. The person scanning a sign or flyer is standing in front of the home and may want to call you right then. The unbranded page, with no name or number, is for the MLS tour field only.

How big should a QR code be on a yard sign rider?

Large enough to scan from the driveway and comfortably from three to four feet on foot. In practice it should be the biggest element on the rider, black on white, with clear space around it and a short caption underneath.

Can I reuse one QR code across all my listings?

Not if it points to your homepage. Every scan then starts with a search and most people give up. Generate a separate code for each listing's page. If you want one physical rider reused, the link it points to must be changeable, which adds a redirect and a point of failure.

What should happen when someone scans the sign after the home sells?

The page should say sold. Signs often stay up for days after closing, and a buyer who scans one and finds a working enquiry form will waste their time and yours. Mark the listing sold the day it closes.
