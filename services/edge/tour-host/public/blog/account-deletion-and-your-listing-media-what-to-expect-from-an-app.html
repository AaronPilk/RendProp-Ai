Account deletion and your listing media: what to expect

Before deleting an account that holds listing media: what a fair app provides, what to download first, who to tell and what to ask support.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Expect a deletion path you can find yourself, a plain statement of what is removed and when, and time to download your originals, versions and videos first. Before you request deletion, save your media, note which property pages are live and tell anyone who relies on those links. Ask support anything the app does not state plainly.

Expect three things from any app that holds your listing media: a deletion path you can find yourself without emailing anyone, a plain statement of what gets removed and roughly when, and enough time to download your originals, edited versions and videos before it happens. Before you request deletion, save your media somewhere you control, note which property pages are still live and who is using those links, and ask support anything the app does not state plainly. Deletion is the one action you cannot review afterwards.

Why listing media makes deletion different

Deleting a notes app loses your notes. Deleting a property marketing app can lose a seller's photos, the only labelled copy of a staged image, a video that is embedded in a live MLS listing, and a property page whose link is printed on a yard sign. Some of those belong to other people. Some are in use by a buyer right now. The question is not only “what happens to my account” but “what happens to everything attached to it that other people are relying on.”

What a fair app provides

A path you can find

Account deletion should be in the app's settings, not hidden behind a support ticket. If you have to ask how to delete, that is a signal about how the app thinks of your data.

A clear statement of scope

What is removed: the account, the media, the published pages, the enquiries. What may be kept and why: billing records, for instance, often have to be kept for a period. The app should say this in plain words. If it does not, ask support and keep the reply.

Time and a way to export

You should be able to get your originals, your edited versions with their labels, and your videos out before the account goes. An app that keeps your untouched originals in a history is only useful if you can take them with you.

Ownership that survives deletion

Your content should be yours before, during and after. An app whose terms grant it a lasting licence to content you uploaded is one to read carefully before you delete, because deletion may not end that licence.

The pre-deletion checklist

  1. Download everything. Originals, each edited version, the videos in both 16:9 and 9:16, the voiceover audio if separate. Put them in a folder per listing. Check the folder opens before you go further.
  2. List the live pages. Every published property page and its links. For each one: is it on a yard sign, a flyer, a QR code, an MLS virtual tour field, a social bio? Anything still active needs a plan.
  3. Tell the people who rely on the links. A seller whose listing is active, a photographer who delivered through the app, a client whose contact details appear on a page you published. Nobody should learn about the deletion from a dead link.
  4. Mark listings sold or archived first where that is the honest status, so anyone reaching the page in the meantime sees the right thing.
  5. Ask support what you cannot see. Whether published pages come down, whether enquiries are retained, whether deletion is immediate or delayed. Keep the reply.
  6. Then request deletion.

A worked example: the photographer changing tools

A photographer has delivered forty listings through one app over a year and is moving to another. Twelve of those listings are still active, and six agents have the property page links in their MLS virtual tour fields. She exports all forty listings' originals, versions and videos over an evening, two folders per listing. She emails the six agents with active links, tells them the pages will come down on a date two weeks out, and sends each a copy of their media. On that date she marks the active listings archived, requests deletion, and moves on. No agent discovers a dead link in their MLS entry.

Skip the second and third steps and the same photographer gets six phone calls over the following month, each starting with “the tour link on my listing is broken.”

Deletion mistakes people make

  • Deleting to fix a billing problem. Subscriptions and accounts are different things. Deleting the account may not cancel the subscription, and cancelling the subscription does not require deleting the account. Sort the subscription first.
  • Assuming deletion is reversible. It usually is not. Treat it as final.
  • Forgetting the links. The property page link on the sign in the yard does not know you deleted your account.
  • Only exporting edits. If the originals are left behind, you have lost your evidence of what was changed. The post on originals explains why that matters even after the sale.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What should I download before deleting an account that holds listing media?

The untouched originals, every edited version with its label, and the videos in each format you used. Put them in a folder per listing and check the files open before you request deletion.

Will deleting my account cancel my subscription?

Not always. Subscriptions and accounts are managed separately in many apps, including those billed through Apple. Sort the subscription first, then handle the account.

What happens to property pages whose links are already in the MLS or on a sign?

That depends on the app, so ask support before you delete and keep the reply. Either way, list every live link, tell the people relying on them and mark listings sold or archived first so nobody discovers a dead link by accident.
