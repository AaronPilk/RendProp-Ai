Hashtags for listing reels: a short, local set

Use a short hashtag set built from the town, the neighborhood, the metro and the home type. Skip the giant generic tags and test it on your own account.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Use a short set of hashtags, most of them local: the town, the neighborhood, the county or metro, plus two or three that describe the home type. Skip the enormous generic tags, keep the set at the end of the caption, and test what works on your own account rather than copying a list from someone else's market.

The hashtag set for a listing reel should be short and mostly local: the town, the neighborhood, the county or metro area, and two or three tags that describe the kind of home. Leave out the giant generic tags that every agent in the country uses, put the set at the end of the caption, and check what the platform currently allows before you decide on a number.

Why local beats big

A tag like the name of your town or neighborhood is used by people who live there, want to live there, or sell things there. That is your audience for a listing. A tag like realestate or dreamhome is used by millions of accounts in every market, and a reel about a three-bed in your county is one grain of sand in it.

Nobody can tell you how a given platform weighs tags this month. What you can observe is who finds your reel and comments, and local tags are the ones that bring in people who recognise the street.

Building the set for a townhouse

Take a two-storey townhouse in a district called Riverside, in a city called Fairmont, in Harlan County. The set might look like this.

  • Place, from small to large. RiversideFairmont, FairmontHomes, FairmontRealEstate, HarlanCounty. Four tags, each one a place a person would type.
  • Home type. Townhouse, TownhouseLiving. Two tags for people who are specifically looking for that kind of home.
  • Moment, optional. JustListed or OpenHouse, if that is what the reel is. One tag, and only when it is true.

That is six or seven tags. If your market has a well-known nickname for the area, or a local tag that community accounts use, add it. If a tag returns nothing but spam when you search it, drop it.

Build one set per market you work, save it somewhere you can copy from, and change the neighborhood line for each listing. The rest stays the same from one reel to the next.

Check the tags before you use them

Search each tag on the platform before it goes in the caption. You are looking for three things: that the tag is actually used in your area and not for a town with the same name elsewhere, that it is not full of content you would not want next to a listing, and that it is not a tag the platform has restricted. The last one changes without notice, so repeat the check every few months.

Mistakes that make the set work against you

  • Thirty tags copied from a list. A wall of tags at the bottom of a caption looks like a listing from a lead-generation farm. Short and local reads like a person.
  • Tags that are not about the listing. Luxury on a starter home, waterfront on a home that is a ten-minute drive from the lake. The viewer who came for that tag leaves, and leaves annoyed.
  • Tags instead of a caption. The tags are the last line, not the first. The caption still needs an observation, specifics and one ask.
  • The same set on every platform without checking. Each platform has its own current rules about tags and how many it tolerates. Check them with your own account and adjust.
  • Tags that describe the buyer. A tag that names a group of people rather than a place or a home type is a fair housing question. Ask your broker before you use it.
  • Expecting tags to do the work of the first three seconds. Tags might bring someone to the reel. The opening frame and the first line keep them there. See the first three seconds.

Build the set for your market

Write the set for the market you list in most: three or four place tags from small to large, two home-type tags, and one moment tag. Search each one before you save it. That set is now the last line of every listing reel caption until your own account tells you to change it.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How many hashtags should a listing reel have?

Six to eight is a workable set: three or four place tags, two home-type tags and one for the moment such as open house. Check what the platform currently allows and test against your own account. More tags do not make the home more local.

Should I use hashtags like realestate or dreamhome?

They put your reel next to millions of others in markets nowhere near yours. A tag for your town, neighborhood or county reaches people who might actually come to the open house. Use the big tags only if your own results show they bring real enquiries.

Do hashtags go in the caption or in a comment?

At the end of the caption is the simplest place and it keeps the post readable. Some agents put them in a first comment; the platform's current behaviour decides whether that works, so test it rather than assume.

Can I use the same hashtag set on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube Shorts?

Start with the same local set and then check each platform's current rules and your own results. Platforms differ in how many tags they tolerate and how they treat them, and that changes. Keep one saved set per market and adjust per platform.
