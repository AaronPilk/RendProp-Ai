The listing page gallery: how many photos and in what order

Put every MLS photo in the page gallery, in the order a buyer would walk the home, with the five strongest first. Cut duplicates, not rooms.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. The page gallery should hold every photo you would put on the MLS, usually 20 to 35 for a typical home, in the order a buyer would walk through it: exterior, entry, main living space, kitchen, primary suite, other bedrooms and baths, outdoor space, garage and extras. The first five slots carry the five strongest images, because many buyers stop there. Cut near-duplicates and bad angles, never whole rooms.

How many is enough

Enough to show every room once and the important rooms twice. A 3-bed, 2-bath with a yard usually lands around 25. A 1-bed condo can be done in 14. A 5-bed with a pool and a finished basement might need 40. The number follows the home, not a target.

Too few, and the buyer wonders what you are not showing. Too many, and they lose the thread by photo thirty: three angles of the same bedroom, two of the laundry, a close-up of a tap. Each extra photo past the point of usefulness costs attention the buyer would have spent on the form.

The order that keeps a buyer moving

  1. Front exterior. The hero, same as the main photo.
  2. The best interior. Usually the kitchen or the main living room. This is the second-most-seen image on the page.
  3. The next best interior or the view. If the home has a view, it goes here.
  4. Kitchen, if it was not slot two.
  5. Primary bedroom.

Those five do the selling. After them, the gallery follows the walk: entry and hallway if they matter, living and dining, kitchen details, primary bath, other bedrooms in order of size, other baths, laundry and utility only if they are good, backyard and patio, garage, then any extras (shed, workshop, basement). Finish on a strong exterior or the view, not on the furnace.

A worked example: 31 photos from a Saturday shoot

The photographer delivers 31 frames of a 4-bed farmhouse. You sort: front exterior at golden hour (1), kitchen wide (2), family room with the fireplace (3), the back deck looking at the trees (4), primary bedroom (5). Then entry, formal living, dining, kitchen second angle, breakfast nook, primary bath, bedrooms two through four, hall bath, upstairs laundry, finished basement, basement bath, garage, front exterior straight-on, rear exterior.

You cut: a second angle of bedroom three that adds nothing, a close-up of the stove, the powder room shot with the toilet in the centre, a blurry basement frame. 27 remain. Every room is present. Nothing repeats without a reason.

Gallery mistakes that lose the swipe

  • Portal order copied blindly. Some portals re-order photos by their own rules. Set the order you want on the page and the MLS entry separately if you have to.
  • Starting with a rendering or the yard sign. The first slot is the most-seen photo on the page after the main one. Spend it on the home.
  • Hiding the weak room. Skipping the small third bedroom or the dated bath. Buyers notice the omission and assume the worst; one honest photo is better.
  • Vertical photos in a horizontal gallery. They shrink and leave bars. Shoot the gallery set in landscape orientation and keep portrait frames for social video.
  • Edited and original versions of the same photo. A twilight edit and the daytime original of the same exterior, side by side, invites the question of which is real. Pick one for the gallery and label the edit.
  • No final image. A gallery that ends on the water heater leaves the buyer there. End on the exterior or the view.

Where the video fits

The gallery and the video show the same rooms in roughly the same order, which is not a problem; it is the point. A buyer swipes the gallery, then taps the video to feel the flow. If the video's scene order matches the gallery's, the home feels coherent. If the video starts in the basement and the gallery starts outside, it feels like two different houses.

The ten-second check

Open the gallery on your phone and swipe through at the speed a buyer would. Does the fifth photo still make you want to see the sixth. Does every room appear. Does any photo appear twice in effect. Does it end well. Fix anything that fails, then send the link.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How many photos should a property page gallery have?

Enough to show every room once and the key rooms twice, which is usually 20 to 35 for a typical house and fewer for a small condo. Let the home set the number. Cut duplicates and weak frames, never rooms.

Should the page gallery match the MLS photo order?

Ideally yes, so a buyer who sees both does not feel they are looking at two different homes. Some portals re-order photos on their own, which you cannot control; set the page order the way you want a buyer to walk the home.

Should I include a photo of a room that is in poor condition?

Yes, one honest frame. A missing room reads as a hidden problem and comes up at the showing anyway. One straightforward photo, shot as well as the room allows, is better than an omission.

Where should AI-edited photos go in the gallery?

Pick one version of each view for the gallery and label the edited ones. Showing the twilight edit and the daytime original of the same exterior side by side invites the buyer to ask which is real. Keep the original in your history, not in the gallery.
