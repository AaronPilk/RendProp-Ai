Do listing videos help a home sell faster

Nobody can promise that, and distrust anyone who quotes a number. What a video reliably does is get more of the right buyers to the door, sooner.

Nobody can honestly promise that a video shortens days on market, and the figures that get quoted are usually unsourced, out of date, or come from someone selling video. What a video reliably does is narrower and more useful: it gets the listing in front of more people, it helps the right ones decide to visit, and it reduces the showings that were not going to turn into offers. Price and condition still decide whether and when a home sells. Measure the video on your own listings rather than trusting a headline.

What video can and cannot do

A video cannot fix an overpriced home. It cannot make a dark north-facing living room bright at the showing. It cannot create buyers who do not exist in that price band this month. If the fundamentals are wrong, a good video brings more people to discover that faster.

What it can do is change who shows up and how prepared they are. A buyer who has watched 45 seconds of the home with a voice explaining the kitchen, the garden and the price arrives knowing what they are walking into. The ones for whom it was wrong did not come, which saves the seller a Saturday. The ones who did come are closer to a decision. That is not “sells faster” as a slogan; it is fewer wasted showings and better ones, which is what tends to lead to offers.

It also widens reach. A video travels on social, in a text, in an email and on the property page in a way a gallery does not. More of the right people seeing the listing in the first week is the mechanism, if there is one.

How to measure it yourself

You do not need a study. You need two or three listings with video and two or three without, in a similar price band and season, and four numbers for each.

  1. Enquiries in the first seven days. Form submissions, calls and texts about the listing.
  2. Showings in the first fourteen days. Booked and attended.
  3. Showings per offer. How many visits it took to produce each offer. A lower number means the visitors were better matched.
  4. Where enquiries came from. Ask, or look at which link they used. If the video posts and the page are producing them, that is your answer.

Keep this in a note on your phone. After six listings you will know what video does in your market, in your price band, with your photographer, which is more useful than any national claim.

A worked example: two listings in the same subdivision

Two three-beds in the same subdivision list a month apart at similar prices. The first goes live with 24 photos and no video. The second goes live with the same photographer's 24 photos plus a 45-second photo-based video on the property page, in the MLS virtual-tour field as an unbranded link, and as a vertical reel on Instagram.

The agent tracks the four numbers. The first listing gets its enquiries mostly from the portal and the sign; showings are steady but several visitors say the living room is smaller than they expected. The second gets more enquiries through the page's form in week one, and visitors mention the video unprompted; fewer of them are surprised by the rooms. Whether the second sells faster depends on a buyer being ready that week. What the agent can say for certain is that it generated more enquiries from better-prepared buyers, and that is the claim worth making to the next seller.

The claims that come back to bite

  • Promising the seller a timeline. “Video sells homes in half the time” is a sentence you will be reminded of in week six.
  • Quoting a statistic you cannot source. If a seller asks where the number came from and you do not know, you have lost credibility over a video.
  • Making the video and posting it once. Reach is the mechanism. A video that is not on the page, in the MLS link, in the email and on social has not been given the chance to do anything.
  • Blaming the video for a pricing problem. If showings are good and offers are not coming, the video did its job and the price did not.
  • Not tracking anything. Then the question stays a matter of opinion forever.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Is there proof that listing videos sell homes faster?

Figures circulate, but most are unsourced or come from people selling video, and market conditions dominate days on market anyway. The defensible claim is that video widens reach and brings better-prepared buyers to showings. Track your own listings to see the effect in your market.

What should I tell a seller who asks if video will sell their home faster?

That price and condition decide the sale, and that video gets the home in front of more of the right buyers and reduces wasted showings. Show them your own numbers from past listings rather than a national statistic.

What is the best way to measure whether video is working?

Track four numbers per listing: enquiries in the first seven days, showings in the first fourteen, showings per offer, and where enquiries came from. Compare listings with and without video in a similar price band and season.

Does the video need to be professional to have an effect?

It needs to be clear, honest and watchable: good photos, slow motion, a plain voiceover and captions. A photo-based video made carefully from a strong photo set does the job for a typical home; a filmed piece matters more at the top of the market.
