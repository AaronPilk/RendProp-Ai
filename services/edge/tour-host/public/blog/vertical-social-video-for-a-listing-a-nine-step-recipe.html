Vertical social video for a listing: a nine-step recipe

Nine steps from portrait photos to a 9:16 listing reel you can post today: photo pick, lead frame, script, voice, captions, music, build, review, post.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Pick six to ten portrait photos, choose the strongest as the lead, write a script you can read in 25 seconds, decide between your voice and an AI voice, keep word-by-word captions on, decide on music, build the 9:16 video, review it muted and with sound, then post it with the property page link. That is the whole recipe.

A vertical social video for a listing takes nine steps: choose six to ten portrait photos, pick the lead frame, write a script you can read aloud in about 25 seconds, decide whether you or an AI voice reads it, keep word-by-word captions on, make a call on music, build the 9:16 video, review it twice, and post it with the property page link in the place the platform allows. Most of the work is in steps one and three. The rest is quick if those two are done well.

Here is each step with the decisions that matter.

Step 1: choose six to ten portrait photos

Vertical video wants portrait frames. A landscape photo cropped to 9:16 loses both sides of the room and usually the thing you wanted to show. If the photographer only shot in landscape orientation, ask for portrait frames of the five rooms that sell the home on the next shoot; until then, shoot them yourself on the phone at the same time of day.

Six to ten is the range because a social reel is short. Ten photos at three seconds each is already 30 seconds. Choose the exterior, the kitchen, the main living space, the main bedroom, one bathroom if it is a good one, and the yard or view. Leave the laundry room for the property page gallery.

Step 2: pick the lead frame

The first frame is the one people see while deciding whether to keep scrolling. It is rarely the front of the house. More often it is the kitchen island, the view from the living room, or the pool. Pick the single photo that would make a stranger stop, and let the exterior come second.

Step 3: write a 25-second script

Write it before you touch the video. Say where the home is, what kind of home it is, three things a buyer cannot read off a listing sheet, and what to do next. Read it aloud with a timer. If it runs past 30 seconds, cut adjectives first and features second. There is a fuller method in writing a 30-second voiceover script.

Step 4: your voice or an AI voice

If you are the face of your business and you post often, record it yourself. A quiet room and the phone held near your mouth is enough. If you are producing reels for several agents, or you want every listing to sound the same, an AI voice reading your script is the consistent choice. The script does not change either way.

Step 5: captions on, word by word

Many people scroll with the sound off. Word-by-word captions carry the script to them and give the eye something to follow while the photo drifts. Keep them on. Check the spelling of the street name and the neighbourhood, because a caption typo is the one thing a local will comment on.

Step 6: decide on music

Music is a choice. For a bright starter home or a condo near the action, a light track under the voice helps. For a quiet luxury listing or a rental, the voice alone can be stronger. Whatever you choose, the voice stays on top and the music stays under it. Check the platform's current rules on what audio you may use before you post.

Step 7: build the 9:16 video

With photos, script, voice and music decided, building is the fast part. Scene order and camera motion are handled for you in Rendprop; your job was the selection. If you also need a horizontal version for the property page or YouTube, build that from landscape photos rather than stretching this one.

Step 8: review it twice

Watch it once with the sound off. Can you follow the home from the captions and photos alone. Watch it again with the sound on. Does the voice land on the right room, is the music under the voice, is any virtually staged photo carrying its label. Then check the facts: beds, baths, price point, street. The checklist in reviewing a listing video before you post it covers the rest.

Step 9: post it with the link

Post the reel natively on each platform rather than linking out to a video. Put the property page link wherever the platform currently lets you place a tappable link, and name the destination in the caption so people know where to go. The branded link from Rendprop carries your name and contact card; the enquiry form on the page is where the lead lands.

A Thursday example

Photos arrive Wednesday night for a two-bed condo with a balcony over a park. Thursday morning: eight portrait frames chosen, balcony view as the lead. Script written at the kitchen table in ten minutes, read in 23 seconds. Recorded in the car. Captions on, light music under. Built, reviewed muted on the walk to the office, reviewed with sound at the desk. Posted at lunch with the link. The open house is Saturday, and the reel has two days to work.

Where the recipe breaks

  • Starting with the video and writing the script to fit. You end up describing photos instead of selling a home.
  • Using landscape photos. The crop takes out the island, the window, the width of the room.
  • Twelve photos and a 50-second script. Social video is short. Save the full tour for the 16:9 listing video.
  • Skipping the muted watch. If it only works with sound on, half the audience gets a silent slideshow.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How many photos should a vertical listing reel use?

Six to ten portrait photos is the practical range. Fewer than six feels thin; more than ten pushes the reel past 30 seconds and loses viewers. Pick the rooms that sell the home and leave the rest for the property page gallery.

Can I make a vertical video from landscape photos?

You can, but the crop removes the sides of every room and usually the feature you wanted to show. Shoot or request portrait frames of the five best rooms and use those for the 9:16 version. Use the landscape photos for the 16:9 listing video instead.

Should the first frame be the front of the house?

Usually not. The first frame has to stop a scroll, and a kitchen island, a view or a pool does that better than a facade. Put the exterior second so the viewer knows what kind of home it is once they have stopped.

Do I need music on a listing reel?

No. Music is optional and depends on the home and the audience. If you use it, keep it under the voice and check the platform's current rules on which audio you may use.
