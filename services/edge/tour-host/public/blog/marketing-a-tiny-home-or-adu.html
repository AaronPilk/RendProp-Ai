Marketing a tiny home or ADU

Tiny homes and ADUs sell on honest scale, a clear statement of what is for sale, and separate access. Shoot at 1x, give dimensions, show the entrance.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A tiny home or an ADU sells on honest scale and a clear statement of what is actually for sale: the unit alone, the unit with land, or a main house with a second dwelling. Shoot small rooms at 1x rather than the wide lens so nothing looks bigger than it is, give dimensions and loft height on the page, and show the separate entrance, parking and utilities, because that is what makes a second unit worth paying for.

Small dwellings sell on honest scale and a clear answer to what is for sale. A tiny home might be sold with land, on a leased pad or on wheels; an ADU is almost always sold as part of a main house, and the question is whether it is for family, for income or for an office. Say which in the first line. Then shoot the small rooms at 1x instead of the wide lens, give dimensions and loft height on the page, and photograph the separate entrance, the parking and the utility arrangement, because those are what turn a shed into a second dwelling in a buyer's mind.

Shot list for a small space

  1. Exterior with something for scale. The front door, a chair on the porch, the main house behind an ADU. A tiny home photographed alone against sky could be any size.
  2. The entrance and the path to it. For an ADU, the gate or side path from the street, so a buyer sees that a tenant or a parent could come and go without walking through the main house.
  3. Main room from the door, at 1x. Then one frame at the wide lens, noted as the wide one in the text if you use it at all, so the buyer has both.
  4. Kitchen, close. Counter length, cooktop size, fridge height. These are the questions.
  5. Bathroom, showing the shower size and whether there is a tub.
  6. Sleeping area or loft, with the ladder or stair in frame and, if it is a loft, a frame that shows the headroom.
  7. Storage. Every closet, under-stair cupboard and built-in. Small-space buyers count them.
  8. Utilities. The meter, the mini-split, the water heater, the hookups for a home on wheels.
  9. Parking and outdoor space that belongs to the unit.
  10. The main house, in full, if the ADU is part of a house sale, placed after the ADU or before it depending on which is the headline.

For the video, the 1x frames matter more here than anywhere: camera motion across a wide frame of a 300-square-foot room makes it feel like a hallway, so give the video the honest frames. A short handheld walkthrough from the gate to the loft gives the buyer the real distances; the wide 0.5x lens the app defaults to for walkthroughs stretches a small room, so keep the camera level and the pace slow and let viewers scroll it.

How small-space listings mislead without meaning to

  • Everything at 0.5x. A 10-foot room reads as 16. The buyer walks in and feels tricked. One honest 1x frame per room is the fix.
  • Staging with furniture that would not fit. An add-furniture edit can drop a full sofa into a space that takes a loveseat. If you stage, compare against the original, check the scale against the door, and keep the Virtually staged label.
  • Not saying what is for sale. A tiny home on a leased pad is not the same purchase as one on an owned lot. State it in line one.
  • Claiming rental income or legal status. Whether an ADU may be rented, and on what terms, varies by place and changes. Say what the seller's permits and documents show, and ask your broker how to word it. Do not quote a rent the unit does not currently earn.
  • No loft height. A loft a tall adult cannot sit up in is a storage space. Give the number.

The page: what it is, how big it is, how separate it is

  • What is for sale: the unit, the unit and land, or a main house with an ADU.
  • Dimensions of the unit and each room, loft height, ceiling height.
  • Access: separate entrance, path from the street, mail, parking.
  • Utilities: separately metered or shared, heating and cooling type, laundry.
  • Permits and documents as the seller has them, described as the seller's documents.
  • Current use: occupied by family, rented at a stated current rent, used as an office, vacant.
  • For a home on wheels: trailer, hitch, hookups, and where it currently sits.
  • The details fields hold price, beds, baths and square feet. For a main house with an ADU, give the main house figures in the fields and the ADU figures in the text so the two are not added together by accident.

If the second unit's main appeal is income, the post on marketing to investors covers how to present the numbers; if it is for guests, the post on a home with a guest house is the closer match.

Worked example: a 3-bed with a 480-square-foot ADU

A 3-bed, 2-bath main house with a detached 480-square-foot ADU over the garage: one bedroom, a full bath, a kitchen with a two-burner cooktop, a mini-split, its own electric meter, an exterior stair from the side yard and one parking space on the driveway apron. It is currently rented. The agent shoots the main house first on a Friday afternoon, then the ADU: the side gate, the stair, the main room at 1x and once at wide, the kitchen close, the bath with the shower visible, the bedroom with the window, the two closets, the meter and the mini-split head, and the parking space. The page's first line: 3-bed main house with a permitted detached ADU per the seller's documents, separately metered, currently rented at the stated rent on a lease ending in the stated month. Main house figures in the details; ADU dimensions in the text. The main house exterior is the main photo. Eighteen landscape frames make the listing video, with the ADU frames in the set.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should I use the wide lens in a tiny home?

Shoot every room at 1x first. A wide frame can be added if it is clearly the wide one, but a set made only at 0.5x makes a 10-foot room look like 16 and the buyer feels misled on arrival.

How do I describe an ADU's rental potential?

State the current rent if it is rented and what the seller's permits and documents show. Whether and how it may be rented varies and changes; ask your broker how to word it and do not quote income the unit does not currently earn.

Where do the ADU's beds, baths and square feet go on the page?

Put the main house figures in the details fields and the ADU's dimensions in the text, so the two are never added together by accident. State the ADU's own bed and bath count in the same sentence as its square footage.

Can I virtually stage a tiny home?

Yes, with care. Compare the staged version against the original, check the furniture scale against the door and windows, and keep the Virtually staged label on the version you publish.
