Marketing a listing in a college town

In a college town three buyer types shop the same house on the academic calendar. State the walk to campus as a fact and time the launch to the term.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. In a college town the same house is shopped by three different buyers: parents buying for a student, investors buying for tenants, and faculty or staff buying to live in it. They shop on the academic calendar, not the usual spring one, and the first fact all three want is the walk or drive to campus. The same house is shopped by three buyers at once: a parent buying a place for a student, an investor buying it for tenants, and a faculty or staff member buying it to live in. They all want the same first fact, the walk or drive to campus, and they all shop on the academic calendar rather than the spring one.

Who buys in a college town

The parent buyer is shopping from another city, often another state, and will see the house once, during a campus visit or move-in weekend. They want the walk to campus, the parking, the number of bedrooms and bathrooms, the condition of the systems and whether the house can be managed from a distance. The investor is local or regional, buys on facts, and wants the lease history as the seller states it, the permitted occupancy with a note to verify, the meters, the parking count and the systems. The faculty buyer is a relocation buyer with a start date, wants the commute to a specific building, the neighbourhood described for a newcomer, and a home that does not feel like a rental.

One page serves all three if it is built on facts rather than on a pitch. The walk to campus in minutes, the drive to the main gate, the parking, the bedroom and bath count, the systems and their ages, the permitted use with a verification note, and what stays. Each buyer reads the lines that matter to them. None of them needs to be told who the house suits, and telling them raises fair housing questions you do not need.

Timing the launch to the academic year

Parent buyers shop in the weeks around admitted-student visits and again before move-in. Investors shop before leases turn over, which in most college towns is a fixed month. Faculty buyers shop after hiring decisions, usually late spring, with a start date in late summer. A listing that launches in the dead middle of a term, or in the week of final exams, meets a town that is not paying attention. A listing that launches two weeks before the campus visit weekend meets all three buyers at once.

Shoot the house when the town looks like itself. A street that is empty in July and lined with cars in September is two different streets, and the parent buyer will see the September one. Shoot the campus-facing context too: the walk, the bus stop, the route to the main gate, in portrait, for the social video.

A three-bed a ten-minute walk from campus

A three-bed, two-bath with off-street parking for three, two blocks from a bus line and a ten-minute walk to the main quad. It has been a rental for six years and the lease ends in July. The agent lists it in late March, two weeks before the admitted-student weekend. The shoot covers the exterior on a weekday morning when the street is full, the interiors with the tenants' consent and their belongings tidied but present, the parking, the basement with the furnace and water heater visible, and a portrait walk from the front door to the quad with the time on screen.

The page gives the walk time, the bus line, the parking count, the bedroom and bath count, the lease end date and the rent as stated by the seller, the ages of the roof and systems, and the permitted occupancy with a note to verify with the city. The listing video walks the house; the social video is the walk to campus. A parent in another state finds it during the visit weekend, watches the walk, and books a showing through the form for the Sunday they are in town. An investor emails about the lease. A new hire in the chemistry department asks about the drive to her building. Three enquiries, three buyers, one page.

What goes wrong in college-town listings

  • Saying who it is for. Perfect for students, great investment, ideal for a professor. Each one narrows the buyer pool and the first two can raise fair housing and occupancy issues. State facts and let each buyer place themselves.
  • Launching on the wrong calendar. A May launch after the faculty hires are settled and the parents have gone home meets an empty town.
  • Hiding the rental history. The investor wants it, the parent will find out, and the faculty buyer will see the wear anyway. State it.
  • Skipping the walk. Campus distance is the first question from all three buyers. A measured walk, shown in portrait with the time, answers it better than a line of text.
  • Edits that erase wear. A tidy-the-room edit on a six-year rental sets up a showing where every scuff is a surprise. Keep edits to sky and weather, and compare each one with the original before publishing.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

When is the best time to list a house in a college town?

Two weeks before the buyers arrive: ahead of admitted-student visit weekends for parent buyers, ahead of the lease turnover month for investors, and in late spring for faculty with summer start dates. Avoid the middle of a term and exam weeks.

Should the listing say it is ideal for students or investors?

No. Describing who the house is for narrows the pool and can raise fair housing and occupancy questions. State the walk to campus, the parking, the bedroom count, the systems and the permitted use with a verification note, and let each buyer place themselves.

What should a college-town listing page include?

The walk and drive to campus, measured, plus the bus line and the parking count. Bedrooms and baths, the lease history and end date as the seller states them, and the ages of the roof and systems. The permitted occupancy with a note to verify, and what stays with the sale.

How do I show the distance to campus?

Walk it with the phone in portrait and show the route from the front door to the main gate or quad with the time in the captions. It becomes the social video and answers the first question every buyer type asks.
