Writing a price reduction announcement

State the new price, one sentence on why the home is a good buy at it, and the link. No apology, no seller's reasons, no calling it a price improvement.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A price reduction announcement states the new price, one sentence on why the home is a good buy at that number, and the link to the property page. No apology, no explanation of the seller's reasoning, and no calling it a price improvement. Send it to the agents who showed the home, your database, and social, each in its own length, and update the page before any of them go out.

A price reduction announcement should say the new price, give one sentence on why the home is a good buy at that number, and link to the property page. Nothing else. No apology, no account of the seller's reasoning, no “price improvement”. The reader already knows what a price change means; the only question they have is whether this number makes the home worth a second look, and the announcement's one job is to answer it.

The temptation is to soften it. Agents write “exciting news” over a cut that nobody finds exciting, or explain that the sellers are “motivated”, which tells every buyer's agent exactly how to negotiate. The plain version respects the reader and protects the seller.

Who gets it, and in what order

  1. The agents who showed it. They have a buyer who saw the home and passed, often on price. A text or short email to each, the morning the change goes live. This is the highest-value message of the three.
  2. Your database and segment. An email with the new price in the subject, one photo, the one sentence, the link.
  3. Social. A post or short reel with the new price in the first line, one reason, the link in bio.

In every case, the property page should already show the new price before the message goes out, because the first thing a reader does is tap the link and check.

The four-sentence shape

  • The new price, plainly: “Birch Road is now $459,000.”
  • What the buyer gets at that number, as a fact: “That is a 3-bed on a fenced half-acre with a 2022 roof and furnace.”
  • The practical next step: “Showings are open this week.”
  • The link: “Gallery and video: [branded link].”

For the agents who showed it, add one line in front: “Your buyers saw Birch Road on the 3rd.” That is the whole message.

The text, the email and the post

Text to a showing agent: “Your buyers saw Birch Road on the 3rd. It's now $459,000: 3-bed on a fenced half-acre, 2022 roof and furnace. Showings open this week. [branded link]”

Email to the database. Subject: “New price on Birch Road: $459,000”. Body: the main photo, the four sentences, your name and number.

Social post: “New price on the Birch Road 3-bed: $459,000. The 2022 roof and furnace are still the reason to see it. Full gallery in the link in bio.”

Should the old price appear

It depends where. In the MLS, check your MLS's current rules on how price history is recorded and displayed; it is usually not something you control. On your own page, in email and on social, it is a judgment call to make with the seller. Showing the old number makes the reduction concrete, which can bring back a buyer who was close; it also invites the reader to anchor on the gap. Many agents state only the new price and let the portals show the history. Whatever you decide, ask the seller first and keep it consistent across channels.

The same cut, two announcements

Before: “PRICE IMPROVEMENT on this beautiful home. The sellers are motivated and ready to make a deal. Don't miss your second chance at this one. Reduced to $459,000, was $485,000. Call me today.”

After: “Birch Road is now $459,000. That is a 3-bed on a fenced half-acre with a 2022 roof and furnace. Showings are open this week. Gallery and video: [branded link].”

The first version tells every buyer's agent the seller will take less. The second tells them the price.

What goes wrong with price change copy

  • “Motivated seller.” It weakens the seller's position in every negotiation that follows, and it is not yours to disclose without their agreement.
  • “Price improvement.” Everyone knows what it means, and the euphemism makes the announcement feel like it is hiding something.
  • A banner reading REDUCED across the main photo. It turns the home's best image into a discount sticker that outlives the announcement.
  • Reusing the launch copy with a new number. The reader has seen it. Write a new sentence about why the number works.
  • The page still showing the old price. The first tap after the message is a check. If it fails, the message reads as careless.
  • Explaining why. The seller's reasons are the seller's. The announcement is about the home and the number.

How this works in Rendprop

The price on a Rendprop property page is the price you typed into the listing's details, next to beds, baths and square feet, the story section, the gallery, the video and your contact card. Change it in the app and check the page before you send anything, since the link in every version of the announcement points there. Enquiries from the page land in the app's inbox, so the agent who comes back after the change reaches you, not a portal. Rendprop does not write the announcement; the four sentences are yours.

Send the agents' texts first

Pull the list of agents who showed the home, update the price on the page, and send each of them the five-line text before you touch the email or the post. Those are the buyers closest to a second look. The post on relisting after a price cut covers whether the media needs to change too.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should I mention the old price in a price reduction announcement?

On your own channels it is a decision to make with the seller: showing the gap makes the cut concrete but invites anchoring on it. Many agents state only the new price and let the portals carry the history. In the MLS, check your MLS's current rules on how price changes are recorded; that part is usually not yours to word.

Who should hear about a price reduction first?

The agents whose buyers already saw the home. They have someone who liked it and passed, often on price, and a short text with the new number can bring that buyer back the same week. Your database and social come after, and the property page should show the new price before any message goes out.

Is it a bad idea to say the seller is motivated?

Yes, in almost every case. It tells every buyer's agent the seller will accept less, which is not yours to signal without the seller's clear agreement. State the new price and what the home offers at that number, and keep the seller's reasons private.

Should I make a new video or new photos for a price change?

Usually not. If the photos and video were good at launch, they are good now; the number changed, not the house. A short reel with the new price in the first line and the existing photos is enough. New media is worth considering only if the original set had problems or the season has changed the exterior.
