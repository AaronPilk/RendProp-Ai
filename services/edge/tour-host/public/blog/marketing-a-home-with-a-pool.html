Marketing a home with a pool

A pool sells a home when it is clean, in sun and connected to the house. Shoot it from the kitchen and at twilight, show the equipment, and state the ages.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A pool sells a home when the photos show it clean, in sun and connected to the house, which means shooting it from the kitchen or family room as well as from the deck, and staying for a real twilight frame with the pool light on. On the page, give the pool's type, size and age, the equipment and heater ages, the fencing and cover as they exist, and who maintains it now, because the buyers who want a pool also want to know what it costs to keep.

A pool sells a home when the photos show it clean, lit by the sun and connected to the rooms people live in. Shoot it from the kitchen or family room, not just from the deck, so a buyer sees the pool as part of the house. Stay for a real twilight frame with the pool light on. Then be plain on the page about what the pool is and what it costs to keep, because the buyer who wants one has usually owned one and the buyer who has not is nervous about it.

The pool shot list and when to take it

  1. Prep before the camera comes out. Skimmed, vacuumed, cover off and stored out of frame, water level right, furniture set with cushions on, toys and hoses gone, the deck swept and dry.
  2. From the main living space out to the pool. Through the slider or the big window, window pull so the water reads blue. This is the frame that sells the connection.
  3. From the far end of the pool back at the house. Mid-afternoon with the sun on the water and the house lit.
  4. The pool along its length, low angle, with the spa, the steps or the sun shelf visible.
  5. Outdoor living around it: covered patio, outdoor kitchen, fire feature, shower, pool house if there is one.
  6. The equipment pad. Pump, filter, heater, with the date plates readable. The buyer who has owned a pool will zoom in.
  7. Fencing, gates and the cover as they exist. Show what is there; do not describe what is required.
  8. Twilight from the far end, pool light on, interior lights on, patio lights on, sky still blue.
  9. The rest of the house in the usual order, with pool-facing rooms shot toward the glass.

The listing video opens on the view through the slider, cuts to the pool from the far end, goes through the house, and finishes on the twilight frame. For the vertical social video, portrait frames of the pool along its length and the twilight shot do the work; shoot the water still, because a surface with ripples from the cleaner reads as plastic once the camera moves across it.

Pool photos that cause problems

  • A cover on, or a green pool. If the pool is closed for the season, say so and date any in-season photos the seller supplies. Do not use an AI edit to remove a cover or turn water blue; that is inventing the condition of the pool.
  • A blue sky edit that recolors the water. Compare against the original. If the water changed, the edit changed more than the sky.
  • Noon glare. White water and black shadows under the furniture. Mid-afternoon or early evening is better.
  • Only deck-level frames. The buyer never sees how the pool relates to the kitchen.
  • No equipment frame. The buyer assumes the pump is old.
  • Stating safety requirements. Describe the fence, gate and alarm that exist. Whether they satisfy any rule is for the buyer to verify; ask your broker how to word it.

What the page should say about the pool

  • Type (gunite, vinyl liner, fiberglass), approximate size and depth range, year built, as the seller knows it.
  • Equipment with ages: pump, filter, heater, salt system, automation, cover.
  • Heated or not, and by what.
  • Spa, sun shelf, slide, diving depth, as they exist.
  • Fencing, gates and alarms as they exist, without a statement about compliance.
  • Who maintains it now and how often, and whether a service contract transfers.
  • Season: open year-round, or opened and closed, and when the photos were taken.
  • Price, beds, baths and square feet stay in the details fields.

In hot markets the pool is the lead; in cold ones it needs the season stated. The posts on summer listings and marketing a desert home cover the pool in its two most common settings.

Worked example: a 4-bed with a gunite pool and spa

A 4-bed, 3-bath single-storey with a 16-by-34 gunite pool built in 2009, an attached spa, a gas heater from 2021, a variable-speed pump from 2022, a mesh safety fence that is currently removed and stored, and a covered patio with an outdoor kitchen. The seller's service comes weekly. The agent schedules the service for the morning and the photographer for 3:30 p.m. on a Friday in May: the family room through the slider, the pool from the far end with the house behind it, the pool along its length with the spa spilling over, the covered patio and the grill, the equipment pad with the plates readable, the stored fence and the gate as they are, then the house. The photographer comes back at 8:10 p.m. for the twilight frame with the pool and spa lights on. Sixteen landscape frames make the listing video, opening on the slider view. The page says: 16-by-34 gunite, 2009, spa, gas heater 2021, variable-speed pump 2022, mesh fence available and currently stored, weekly service by a named company that will take the new owner on request, photos taken in May. The twilight frame is the main photo.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What is the best photo of a pool for a listing?

The view from the kitchen or family room out through the glass to the pool, and a twilight frame from the far end with the pool light on. The first shows the connection to the house; the second is the one buyers save.

Can I use AI to remove a pool cover or make the water blue?

No. That invents the condition of the pool. If the pool is closed, say so and date any in-season photos the seller supplies. A blue sky edit that recolors the water has changed more than the sky; compare it against the original.

Should I say the pool fence meets code?

Describe the fence, gates and alarms that exist and leave compliance for the buyer to verify. Ask your broker how to word it.

What pool details belong on the page?

Type, size, year, equipment and heater ages, spa and features, fencing as it exists, who maintains it now, whether a service contract transfers and when the photos were taken. Buyers who have owned a pool read that list before they look at the water.
