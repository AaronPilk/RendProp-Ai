Offering social reels to agents: the pitch and the delivery

How a photographer pitches a vertical listing reel to agents, what to show them, and how to deliver it so they post the same day, from the shoot you did.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Pitch a social reel as the thing the agent posts the day the listing goes live, built from the shoot you already did. Show one sample from your own portfolio listing, price it as a small add-on, and deliver the 9:16 export with captions already on it so the agent has nothing to do but post.

The pitch for a social reel is simple: the agent gets a vertical video to post the day the listing goes live, built from the photos you already shot, with captions already on it. The delivery is a 9:16 export and a link to the property page for the caption. No editor, no second visit, nothing for the agent to do but post. Most agents want this and most photographers are not offering it.

Typical U.S. rates as of August 2026 put a social cutdown edit at $75–150 when an agent buys it separately. You can offer it as part of the shoot for less and still make it the highest-margin line on the invoice.

Why agents say yes to this and no to other add-ons

An agent's social feed is where their next seller is watching. A listing reel does two jobs: it markets the home, and it shows the seller who is deciding whether to hire them that their listings get this treatment. That second job is why agents post reels even when they do not expect buyers from them. You are selling a listing presentation asset as much as a marketing one.

The post on the listing presentation covers how agents use media to win listings, which is the argument you are making for them.

The pitch, in one message

Do not write a pitch deck. Send one reel and three sentences. Pick a listing from your own portfolio, or a past client who has agreed, and build the vertical video from it. Then message the agents you shoot for:

  • Here is a reel built from a normal shoot.
  • From your next listing you can have one of these to post on launch day, captions on, with the property page link for the caption.
  • It is on the booking form as an add-on.

That is the whole pitch. The reel does the persuading. The agent either wants it or does not, and the ones who want it book it.

What the reel needs to be

A vertical listing reel that an agent will post without editing has:

  1. A strong first frame. The best exterior or the room that sells the home. It is the thumbnail and the first second.
  2. Four to six scenes. Not the whole home. The hook rooms. The post on the nine-step recipe goes through choosing them.
  3. Word-by-word captions. Most viewers watch with sound off. The caption carries the voiceover.
  4. A voice. Yours recorded, or an AI voice reading a short script. Thirty seconds or less of script.
  5. Portrait photos, not crops. Shoot the hook rooms in portrait on site so the frame holds the room.

Delivery that gets posted the same day

The reel is only valuable if it goes up on launch day. Deliver it with everything the agent needs in one message: the 9:16 export, the branded property page link for the caption and bio, and two lines of suggested caption text. An agent who has to come back to you for the link will post on Thursday instead of Tuesday.

A bungalow on Monday, a reel posted Tuesday

You shoot a renovated bungalow on a Monday morning for an agent who ticked the reel on the booking form. On site you shoot the normal landscape photos plus portrait frames of the exterior, the kitchen, the living room, the primary bedroom and the back deck. In the car you add both sets to the listing, build the landscape video and the vertical one, and record a twenty-second voiceover: renovated bungalow, three beds, the kitchen was redone last year, open Saturday. Monday evening you publish and send the agent the two links, the 9:16 export and a suggested caption. She posts Tuesday morning when the listing hits the MLS. The seller shares it. Two of the seller's neighbours follow the agent. That is the result the agent is paying for.

What stops a reel getting posted

  • Delivering it as a file with no caption or link. Give the agent the whole post.
  • A reel that is a cropped version of the landscape video. It looks like it. Shoot portrait.
  • Too long. A thirty-second reel built from five frames beats a ninety-second one built from twenty.
  • An AI-edited photo that changed something. Compare every edit with its original before it goes in a reel. A reel travels further than a gallery photo and so does a mistake.
  • Overpromising the result. Say it is a marketing asset the agent controls. Do not promise leads.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What should the reel add-on cost?

Below what an agent would pay an editor for a separate cutdown, which at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026 is $75–150. Price it so the agent ticks it by default on any listing they care about, and make the margin on volume.

Do I need to shoot extra photos for a reel?

Yes, a few portrait frames of the four to six rooms that sell the home. Cropping landscape photos loses the room. The extra frames take a few minutes on site.

Who writes the caption and posts it?

The agent posts on their own accounts. You deliver the export, the property page link and two lines of suggested caption so they can post in a minute. Keep it their post, not yours.

Can the reel use AI-edited photos?

It can, as long as each edit has been compared with the original and carries its disclosure. Check the agent's MLS and platform rules on altered images before it is posted.
