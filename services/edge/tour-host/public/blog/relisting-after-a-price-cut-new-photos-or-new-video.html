Relisting after a price cut: new photos or new video

After a price cut, change what buyers see first: a new main photo and a new video from the existing set, or a fresh shoot if the season has turned.

After a price cut, change what buyers see first: a new main photo and a new video. Build them from the existing photo set if the photos are good, and from a fresh shoot if the season has turned or the photos were part of the reason the home sat. Reposting the same thumbnail with a lower number tells every buyer who already scrolled past that nothing changed except the price, and many of them will wait to see whether it drops again.

First decide whether the photos were the problem

Open the gallery on your phone and look at it as a buyer who has not seen it. Three questions. Is the main photo the best room in the house, or the front of the house because that is the convention. Were the interiors shot with the lamps on and the blinds open, or in flat afternoon light. Does the season in the exterior match the season outside today. If the answers are yes, yes and yes, the photos are fine and a new video plus a new main photo is the relaunch. If any answer is no, that is the reshoot list, and it is usually two or three frames, not thirty.

New video from the same photos

A new video is the cheapest relaunch and often the most effective, because the first three seconds are what buyers see in a feed. Build the new cut from different photos than the first one, open on a different room, and write a different first line. If the first video opened on the front exterior and said four bedrooms in a quiet street, the new one opens on the kitchen and says the thing showings feedback liked most. New voiceover, captions on, both the landscape video for the page and the vertical one for social. The seller sees effort, and buyers see a listing they do not recognise, which is the point.

When to reshoot

  • The season turned. A snowy front in May or bare trees in a July listing reads as sitting. Reshoot the exterior at a good hour; keep the interiors.
  • Flat light. If the interiors were shot without lamps, reshoot the two or three rooms that matter: kitchen, main living space, main bedroom.
  • The home changed. The seller painted, cleared the garage, finally moved the exercise bike. Reshoot those rooms; the before photos are now wrong.
  • Feedback said the photos oversold a room. Reshoot it honestly, without the widest lens, so the next showing is not a disappointment.

The page: refresh the details, decide about the link

Update the price on the page the same hour it changes in the MLS; a page with the old number after a cut is the first thing a sharp buyer finds. Swap the main photo. Replace the video. Re-order the gallery so the new main photo leads. Keep the same branded link if it is already on the sign and in the seller's family group chat; a working link that now shows a refreshed page is better than a new one nobody has. If the listing was pulled and relisted, a new page with a new link is reasonable, and the old one should be archived so it says so.

A split-level after a cut, worked through

A 3-bed split-level listed in February at one price, cut in April after six showings and no offers. The photos: an overcast February front exterior as the main photo, decent interiors, a staged lower level with its label. The season has turned and the front is the problem. The photographer reshoots the exterior on a bright April evening; one frame. The kitchen, which showing feedback consistently liked, becomes the main photo. A new vertical video opens on the kitchen with a first line about the window over the sink, and a new landscape video goes on the page. The price is updated on the page the same hour as the MLS. The relaunch post goes out Tuesday with the new cover, the database email says refreshed photos and a new price, and the branded link on the sign is unchanged. Nine enquiries that week against two the week before. The cut did some of that; the new first impression did the rest.

Mistakes after a price cut

  • Same main photo, lower number, a caption that says price improved. Buyers who passed will pass again.
  • Changing the price on the MLS and forgetting the page, the flyer or the email template.
  • Pulling and relisting with identical media to reset the day count. Buyer's agents read histories.
  • Applying an AI edit to make a room look larger as a substitute for a reshoot. The next showing finds the room.
  • Relaunching on a Friday evening and answering the first enquiry on Monday.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Do I need a full reshoot after a price cut?

Rarely. Reshoot the exterior if the season has changed and the two or three rooms that were shot badly or have changed. Keep the rest, pick a new main photo and build a new video from a different selection.

Should the new video use the same photos as the old one?

Use a different selection and open on a different room with a different first line. Buyers see the first three seconds; if those match the old video, the relaunch looks like a repost.

Keep the old property page link or make a new one?

Keep it if it is already on the sign, in the MLS and in the seller's group chat; a refreshed page at a known link is better than a new link nobody has. Make a new one only if the listing was pulled and relisted, and archive the old page so it says so.

When should the price change on the page?

The same hour it changes in the MLS. A page showing the old price after a cut is the first thing a careful buyer finds, and it makes the whole relaunch look careless.
