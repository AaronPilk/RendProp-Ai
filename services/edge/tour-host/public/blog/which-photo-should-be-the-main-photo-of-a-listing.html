Which photo should be the main photo of a listing

Usually the front exterior at a slight angle in good light. The exceptions: a view property, a condo in a shared building, or a kitchen renovation.

The main photo is usually the front exterior, shot at a slight angle, in good light, with the whole house and some of the lot in frame. The exceptions are specific: a waterfront or view property where the view is the reason to buy, a condo where the exterior is a shared building, or a renovation where the kitchen is the story. Pick the frame a buyer can read at thumbnail size and that answers the question what is this.

What the main photo has to do

It is seen at a size smaller than a postage stamp, in a scroll of forty other listings, by a buyer who gives it under a second. At that size it needs to say three things: what kind of property, roughly what condition, and whether it is worth the tap. Detail is wasted. Shape, colour and light are what survive.

That is why the front exterior wins so often. A house shape reads at any size. A bathroom does not.

The default, and how to get it right

  • Shoot from the corner of the lot, so you see the front and one side. Straight-on frames look flat at thumbnail size.
  • Get the whole roofline in and leave sky above it.
  • Cars out of the drive, bins out of sight, hose rolled up.
  • Light on the front of the house, not behind it. If the front faces west, shoot in the afternoon.
  • If the day was grey, this is the one frame where a sky edit or a twilight edit is worth doing, with a disclosure. See twilight photo edits for when that helps.

When something else should lead

The view is the product. A cabin on a lake, a condo over the city, a hillside lot. Lead with the view from the deck or the living room window, with enough of the house in frame that a buyer knows they are looking at a home and not a travel photo.

The exterior is not yours. A unit in a 200-unit tower shares its exterior with 199 other listings. Lead with the best interior, usually the living space with the window wall.

The kitchen is the renovation. A 1970s ranch with an untouched exterior and a new kitchen should lead with the kitchen, and the exterior goes second so the buyer is not surprised.

The land is the listing. Acreage, a barn, a long drive. Lead with the frame that shows scale, even if the house is small in it.

A worked example: three listings, three choices

A 3-bed in a cul-de-sac, tidy exterior, ordinary interior. Main photo: the three-quarter front exterior at 4pm. Nothing else would do better.

A 2-bed condo on the ninth floor with a balcony facing the water. Main photo: the living room looking out through the balcony doors, water visible, sofa in the foreground for scale.

A 4-bed colonial with peeling paint and a kitchen that cost the seller a lot last year. Main photo: the kitchen from the dining room. Second photo: the exterior, honestly, because the buyer will see it on the drive up and a surprise there costs the showing.

Main photos that lose the tap

  • A twilight edit on a house with no exterior lighting, so it glows for no reason
  • A staged interior as the lead on a vacant home, where the buyer's first impression is a room that does not exist
  • A wide-lens exterior that bends the garage
  • Any frame with a car, a bin or a person in it
  • A portrait-orientation frame, which usually gets cropped to a strip wherever it is shown

Decide it before the shoot

Tell the photographer which frame you expect to lead. If it is the exterior, they will time it for the light. If it is the view, they will wait for the haze to lift. The main photo is the one decision in the set that is worth planning in advance, and exterior photos for a listing covers how to get the default version right.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should the main listing photo always be the exterior?

Usually, but not always. Lead with the view for a waterfront or hillside property, with the best interior for a condo in a shared building, or with the kitchen when the renovation is the story. In each case the exterior goes second so the buyer is not surprised.

What makes a good main photo at thumbnail size?

Shape, colour and light. A three-quarter exterior with the whole roofline and sky above reads at any size. Detail is wasted at thumbnail size, which is why a bathroom or a close-up rarely works as the lead.

Can the main photo be a twilight or sky-edited frame?

Yes, with a disclosure and a check of your MLS rules, which sometimes treat altered main photos differently. It works when the house has real exterior lighting and the shadows match. It fails when the house glows for no reason.
