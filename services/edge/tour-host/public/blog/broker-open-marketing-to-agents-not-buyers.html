Broker open: marketing to agents, not buyers

A broker open is a showing for the people who bring buyers. Keep it short and mid-week, send the page link ahead, and ask every agent one question.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A broker open is a showing for the people who bring buyers, so market it like one: a short mid-week, mid-morning window, an invitation with the property page link so agents can match it to their buyers before they come, and one question asked of every agent who walks through: who do you have for this. Lunch is optional; the question is not.

A broker open is a showing for agents, and agents want three things from it: to see the house quickly, to know the price and the terms without asking, and to leave with a link they can forward to a buyer from the car. Market it as a forty-five minute stop on a Wednesday morning, send the property page link with the invitation so they can match it to their buyers in advance, and ask everyone who walks through the same question: who do you have for this.

The catered lunch and the raffle are habits from a different era. Agents come to a broker open because a house fits a buyer they already have, or because the house is unusual enough to be worth knowing about. The invitation's job is to make one of those true before Wednesday.

Ridgeview Drive, Wednesday 11 to 1

A 5-bed on Ridgeview Drive, priced for move-up buyers, in a market where the agents who sell that price band know each other. Listed on Monday. The broker open is Wednesday 11 to 1.

Monday afternoon the invitation goes out by text and email to a list of roughly forty agents: the ones who have sold in that price band in that area in the last year, not the whole board. It reads:

Broker open Wednesday 11 to 1, 9 Ridgeview Drive. 5 bed, 3.5 bath, half-acre, priced at [price]. Page with the full gallery and video: [branded link]. If you have a move-up buyer in [area], this is worth ten minutes. Coffee on.

The link matters more than the coffee. An agent reads the text in a car park, opens the page, watches the video, and thinks of the couple they showed three houses to last month. On Wednesday they come, and they come with a buyer in mind.

During the two hours you stand in the kitchen, and as each agent finishes the loop you ask it: “Who do you have for this?” Some say nobody, honestly. Some name a buyer. Two say they will send the link that afternoon. You write each answer down beside the agent's name.

Thursday morning the seller gets a report with eleven agents through, three with named buyers, two showing requests for the weekend. That is a broker open that worked, and it cost a thermos.

What goes in the invitation

  • The window. Short. Two hours mid-morning mid-week, when agents are between appointments. A four-hour afternoon slot gets the same attendance spread thinner.
  • The facts. Beds, baths, lot, price. Agents will not come to a house whose price they had to look up.
  • The link. The branded property page, with the gallery and the video. This is what they forward.
  • The hook. One sentence that says who the house is for. “Move-up buyer in [area].” Not “a must-see.”
  • What is on. Coffee, or nothing. Say which.

The question, and what to do with the answers

“Who do you have for this?” is the whole return on the morning. It turns a walk-through into a conversation about a buyer, and the answer is either a lead or a piece of market information. “Nobody; my buyers at this price want a bigger garden” is useful. Five of those is a marketing note. Ten is a price note.

Write it down. By Thursday you cannot remember which agent said what, and the follow-up text, “you mentioned the Harrisons, is Saturday any good?”, only works if you can.

Why broker opens fail

  • Inviting everyone. A board-wide blast gets the agents who come for lunch. A list of forty who sell that band gets the ones with buyers.
  • No price in the invite. Agents assume it is high and stay away.
  • No link. The agent cannot forward a text that says “nice house.” They can forward a page.
  • The listing agent in the hall, talking. Agents want to walk it alone and fast. Stand in the kitchen and let them come to you.
  • Not asking the question. Eleven agents through and no idea what any of them thought.
  • No seller report. The seller cleared out for two hours; they want to know what it produced.
  • Sending the unbranded link. Agents forward whatever you send. The branded link carries your contact card so the buyer's enquiry reaches you.

Build the list before you need it

Make the list of agents who sell in each price band and area you work in, and keep it current. Then the Monday invitation is a paste, the Wednesday is a thermos and a question, and Thursday is a report. For how the link travels from the agent to the buyer, read how buyers share a property page.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

When is the best time for a broker open?

Mid-week, mid-morning, for about two hours. Tuesday to Thursday between 10 and 1 catches agents between appointments. A long afternoon window spreads the same attendance thinner, and weekends compete with the agents' own showings.

Should I provide lunch at a broker open?

Coffee is enough. Agents come because the house fits a buyer they have or is worth knowing about, and the invitation with the price and the page link is what makes that happen. A catered lunch gets you the agents who came for lunch.

Who should I invite to a broker open?

Agents who have sold in that price band and area recently, which is usually a list of thirty to fifty rather than the whole board. Send them the facts, the window and the branded property page link so they can match the house to a buyer before they arrive.

What should I ask agents at a broker open?

One question as they finish the loop: who do you have for this. The answer is either a buyer or a piece of market feedback, and both are worth writing down beside the agent's name so the Thursday follow-up can be specific.
