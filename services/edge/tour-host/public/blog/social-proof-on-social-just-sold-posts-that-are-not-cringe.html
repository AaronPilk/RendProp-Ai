Social proof on social: just sold posts that are not cringe

A just sold post works when it is about the home and the people, not about you. Show the house, say what happened plainly, credit who helped, post once.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

What social proof is actually for

The next seller who sees your just sold post is asking one thing: what would it be like to list with this person. A photo of you holding a sign answers it badly. A photo of the home, a sentence about what the sale involved, and a line of thanks to the people who made it happen answers it well. The proof is in the handling, not the celebration.

A split-level, two ways

A 1960s split-level with a dated kitchen, listed in March, three showings the first weekend, under contract after nine days, closed in May.

The version to avoid: SOLD. Multiple offers, over asking, under contract in 9 days. Another one for the books. Thinking of selling? You know who to call.

The version that works: Closed today on the split-level on Larch. The sellers had lived there 31 years and had done the hard part before I met them: cleared the garage, painted the trim, let the kitchen be a 1960s kitchen. The photographer shot it on a bright Tuesday, the photos stayed honest, and three families came through that first weekend. The buyers are a couple with a workshop habit who wanted that garage. Thanks to Marcus on the buy side for a calm transaction. Congratulations to both households.

The second version has no numbers about you and no pitch, and it does more work. It tells a seller what you noticed, what you did, and how you treat the other side.

The five lines of a just sold post

  1. The home, by street or by type. The split-level on Larch. The two-bed near the park. Enough to place it, not the full address.
  2. One thing the seller did right. With their permission. It credits them and quietly tells future sellers what helps.
  3. One thing you did. Shot it on a bright day, priced it against the last three sales, held a Saturday open house. Plain and specific.
  4. The other side. The buyer's agent by name if they are fine with it, the buyers only in general terms unless they have agreed.
  5. Congratulations. To the households, not to yourself.

The photo is the home, usually the front exterior or the room that sold it. If any photo carries an AI edit from the listing, the disclosure still applies; a twilight exterior on a sold post is still a twilight edit. See the one-line disclosure.

The sale price question

Some sellers are happy for the price to be posted; some are not. Some MLS systems have rules about publishing sold prices, and the rules vary by area. Ask the seller in writing, check your MLS's current rules, and when in doubt leave it out. A post without the price is never a problem. A post with it can be.

What makes it cringe

  • Champagne, keys, the agent's face. The reader wanted to see the home and learn something.
  • Over asking, in nine days, multiple offers. Even when true, the stats are about you. Say what happened without the scoreboard, and remember that a seller reading it next year in a slower market will wonder why their home is not doing the same.
  • Buyers' faces without consent. Never. A photo of new owners on the porch is theirs to post, not yours, unless they said yes.
  • The same template every time. If every sold post reads SOLD plus the address plus thinking of selling, followers have stopped reading before the second line.
  • Posting before closing. Under contract is not sold. Deals fall through, and a sold post that has to be deleted is worse than no post.
  • A week of sold content. One post. Maybe one story. Then back to the next listing or to content that is not about you. There is a reason just sold copy that credits the right people is short.

Two questions for the seller

Before your next closing, message the seller and ask two things: may I mention what you did to prepare the home, and may I post the price. Their answers write most of the post for you.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should a just sold post include the sale price?

Only if the seller has agreed in writing and your MLS's current rules allow it. Rules differ by area. Leaving the price out never causes a problem; including it sometimes does.

Can I post a photo of the buyers at the closing?

Only with their clear consent, and ideally let them post it and share it. A sold post about the home does not need faces to work.

When should the just sold post go up?

The week the sale closes, not when it goes under contract. Deals fall through, and a sold post that has to be deleted costs more credibility than a late one.

How many sold posts are too many?

One per sale, with perhaps one story frame. A feed that is nothing but sold signs reads as bragging and crowds out the listings you are trying to sell now.
