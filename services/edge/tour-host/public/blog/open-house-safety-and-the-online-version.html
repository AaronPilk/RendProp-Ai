Open house safety and the online version

Two people, a greeting at the door, valuables out of the house, a plan for the last twenty minutes. And a page and video so fewer strangers walk through.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. An open house is safer with two people in the house, a greeting at the door for everyone who enters, the seller's valuables and medication out of the property, your phone in your hand and a plan for the last twenty minutes when the house is quiet. The online version, a property page with the video and an enquiry form, means fewer strangers need to walk through at all, and the ones who ask for a private showing can be spoken to first.

An open house is safer when four things are true. Two people are in the house, not one. Everyone who enters is greeted at the door and asked to sign in, so nobody is anonymous. The seller's valuables, medication, mail and spare keys have left the property. And you have a plan for the last twenty minutes, when the crowd has gone and a single late visitor arrives.

The online version of the open house, the property page with the gallery, the video and an enquiry form, is the fifth thing. It does not replace the Sunday for every listing, but it means fewer people need to walk through a stranger's house to find out whether they like it, and the ones who ask for a private showing afterwards can be spoken to on the phone before a door is opened.

Dover Road, Sunday 1 to 3

A split-level at the end of a cul-de-sac on Dover Road, open Sunday 1 to 3 in late autumn, so it is getting dim by the end. The setup:

  • Tuesday. The seller gets the usual preparation page with a safety section: medication, mail, laptops, jewellery, spare keys and anything with their name and address on it leave the house for the weekend. Not into a drawer; out.
  • Saturday. You walk the house and note the exits, the rooms with one door, and where you will stand to see the front door and the stairs at once. For this house that is the dining room doorway.
  • Sunday 12:30. A colleague arrives. One of you is at the door, greeting and asking for the sheet; one of you is upstairs. Every visitor is greeted by name once they have written it. You text a third person your location and the end time.
  • During. Phone in hand, not in a bag. Visitors go ahead of you into rooms; you stay between them and the door. Nobody goes into the basement alone with you and the lights off.
  • 2:40 to 3. The crowd thins. The two of you stay together. The last visitor at 2:55 is greeted exactly as the first, and the house is locked at 3:05 with both of you outside.

Nothing about that is dramatic. It is a set of habits that cost nothing on the ninety-nine Sundays when nothing happens.

What the seller takes with them

Medication, especially anything that could be sold. Mail and documents with the address and name. Spare keys, including the ones in the kitchen drawer everyone has. Laptops and tablets. Jewellery and small valuables. Firearms, if any, out of the house entirely. Family photos that show children's names or schools. The seller will say “nobody would,” and the answer is that drawers get opened at every open house by people who are curious, and once in a while by someone who is not.

The online version, and what it changes

The property page with the video does three safety jobs.

  • Fewer walk-throughs. People who were going to walk through to see the layout watch the video instead. The Sunday crowd is smaller and more serious.
  • A screen before a private showing. Someone who asks for a visit through the enquiry form has told you when they want to come. You speak to them before anything is booked. That call is a filter that an open house does not have.
  • Less to post publicly. With the page carrying the detail, the public post does not need to say “vacant,” the exact hours and the address in the same sentence.

None of that removes the need for the four habits on the days you do hold an open house. It removes some of the days.

Where safety slips

  • Alone at dusk. A twilight open house with one agent in a house with the lights on and the street dark. Two people, always, and more so in the evening.
  • “Vacant” and the hours in one public post. You have announced an empty house and when it will be open. Keep “vacant” off public posts; the page does not need it either.
  • The recorded walkthrough that shows the alarm panel, the key hook and the back door lock. Watch any walkthrough you publish with that in mind before it goes on the page.
  • Leading visitors into rooms. Let them go first. You stay nearer the exit.
  • Phone in the car. It is in your hand or your pocket for two hours.
  • Not telling anyone where you are. A text to a colleague with the address and the end time, and a second text when you are locked up and in the car.
  • Assuming the sign-in sheet is security. It is a record and a small deterrent. It is not a lock.

Brokerages and local associations often publish their own safety guidance; read whatever yours provides, since this is one area where more habits are better than fewer.

Before the next Sunday

Book the second person, add the safety section to the seller's preparation page, and decide where you will stand. Then look at whether this listing needs a public Sunday at all, or whether the page and the video can do the first look. For what to keep off the public posts, read what not to post about a listing.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should I hold an open house alone?

No. Two people in the house means one at the door and one upstairs, nobody alone with a visitor in a room with one exit, and someone to notice if something is off. For a twilight open house, the second person matters even more. If a colleague is not available, reschedule rather than go alone.

What should the seller remove before an open house for safety?

Medication, mail and documents with their name and address, spare keys, laptops and tablets, jewellery and small valuables, firearms, and family photos that show children's names or schools. Out of the property, not into a drawer, because drawers get opened at every open house.

How does a property page make showings safer?

It lets people see the layout in the video instead of walking through, so the open house crowd is smaller and more serious. Anyone who asks for a private showing through the enquiry form has told you when they want to come, and you speak to them before anything is booked. It also means public posts can carry less detail.

What should I check in a recorded walkthrough before publishing it?

Anything a stranger could use: the alarm panel, where keys hang, the type of lock on the back door, the position of windows at ground level, and anything with the owner's name on it. If a shot shows those, cut it or do not publish the walkthrough; the photo-based video can carry the page.
