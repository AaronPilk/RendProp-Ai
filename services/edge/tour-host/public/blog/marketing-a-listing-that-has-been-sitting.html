Marketing a listing that has been sitting

A sitting listing needs a diagnosis first: price, presentation or exposure. Then change what buyers see first: main photo, video and page, not the caption.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A listing that has been sitting needs a diagnosis before it needs new marketing: price, presentation or exposure. If it is price, media will not fix it. If it is presentation or exposure, change what buyers see first, the main photo, the video and the page, rather than reposting the same set with a new caption.

A listing that has been sitting needs a diagnosis before it needs new marketing, and there are only three diagnoses: price, presentation or exposure. If it is price, new media will not fix it and you owe the seller that conversation. If it is presentation or exposure, change what buyers see first (the main photo, the video, the page) rather than reposting the same photos with a new caption. Buyers who already scrolled past need a reason to stop, and the same thumbnail is not one.

Diagnose with three questions

  • Are enquiries coming in? If the page and the portals produce almost no enquiries, the problem is presentation or exposure: the main photo is not stopping anyone, or nobody is seeing it.
  • Are enquiries turning into showings? If people enquire but do not book, the problem is usually the reply or the page: slow responses, a page with missing details, a video that raised a question it did not answer.
  • Are showings turning into offers? If people visit and leave without offering, the problem is price or the home itself, and the feedback from those showings will say which.

Answer those three with the actual counts from the last thirty days, not with a feeling. The counts point at one of the three.

If it is presentation

Change the first thing a buyer sees. Choose a new main photo, ideally the room that showings feedback says people liked. Build a new video that opens somewhere different from the old one; if the old video started on the front of the house, the new one starts in the kitchen. Re-order the gallery so the best room is second, not eighth. If the photos were shot in flat light with the lamps off, reshoot the three rooms that matter and keep the rest. If rooms are empty, stage them virtually with the label on, compared against the originals, and check your MLS rules. If the season has changed since the shoot, the exterior needs a new frame; a snowy front on a May listing says sitting louder than any day count.

If it is exposure

The media may be fine and simply finished its first run. A listing's launch post was seen by whoever was online that Tuesday; three weeks later a different group is looking. Post a new vertical cut with a different hook, send the branded link to your database again with one new detail in the subject line, drop flyers to the street with a QR code, and ask the seller to send the link to the three people who said they liked the house. Make sure the unbranded link is in the MLS tour field and works; a broken tour link on a sitting listing is more common than it should be.

If it is price

Say so to the seller with the counts in hand: this many enquiries, this many showings, zero offers, and this is what the showings said. If the price changes, treat it as a relaunch, not an edit: new main photo, new video, fresh post, email with the new number. A price cut with the same thumbnail tells buyers who passed that nothing changed except the number, and many of them will wait for the next cut.

A 60-day listing with two showings

A 3-bed, 60 days on the market, two showings, no offers, four enquiries in total. The counts say presentation and exposure, not price: too few people enquired for price to be the verdict. The main photo is an overcast front exterior shot in March; it is now May. The agent reshoots the exterior on a bright evening and the kitchen with the lamps on, makes the kitchen the main photo, builds a new vertical video that opens on the kitchen with a different first line, re-orders the gallery, confirms the unbranded link in the MLS tour field is live, and relaunches on a Tuesday with a fresh post and an email that says the home has been refreshed with new photos. Eleven enquiries in the next ten days, five showings, one offer. The price did not move. The thumbnail did.

What not to do

  • Repost the same main photo with a caption that says still available. Everyone who scrolled past will scroll past again.
  • Pull and relist to reset the day count without changing anything buyers see. The agents on the other side of the deal can read a history.
  • Add an edit that hides what showings complained about. A cramped kitchen with a wider-looking AI version is a complaint waiting for the next showing.
  • Cut the price and keep the old video. A price change deserves a new first impression.
  • Blame the market before you have counted enquiries, showings and offers.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How do I know if a sitting listing is a price problem or a marketing problem?

Count enquiries, showings and offers for the last thirty days. Few enquiries points to presentation or exposure. Enquiries without showings points to the reply or the page. Showings without offers points to price or the home itself, and the showing feedback will say which.

Should I reshoot all the photos?

Usually not. Reshoot the exterior if the season has changed and the two or three rooms that matter most if they were shot badly. Keep the rest, choose a new main photo, and build a new video that opens somewhere different.

Does a new video help if the photos are the same?

Yes, if it opens on a different room with a different first line and goes out as a fresh post and a fresh email. Buyers see the first three seconds; a new opening is a new listing to them.

Is it better to pull the listing and relist it?

Only if something buyers see has changed: photos, video, price. A relist that resets the day count with the same media fools nobody who can read the listing history, and the buyer's agent usually can.
