The photographer-agent handoff: files, links and who posts what

Four questions to settle before a listing shoot: which files the agent gets, who keeps originals, who publishes the page and posts, and where enquiries go.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A clean photographer-agent handoff settles four things before the shoot: which files the agent receives and in what orientation, who keeps the originals, who publishes the page and posts the video, and where enquiries land. Settle those and the delivery becomes a link instead of a zip file and three phone calls.

A clean photographer-agent handoff answers four questions before the shoot, not after: which files the agent gets and in what orientation, who keeps the originals, who publishes the page and posts the video, and where the enquiries go. Settle those four in one message and the delivery is a link the agent opens on their phone. Leave them open and it is a zip file, a follow-up email asking for the vertical versions, and a lead that went to the wrong inbox.

Files: what to deliver and in what shape

Agree the set before the shoot. For most listings that means landscape photos for the listing video and the property page, and a handful of portrait frames for vertical social video. If the photographer shoots only landscape photos, say so, and the agent can plan to build the vertical cut from those with tighter framing. Deliver full-resolution files by a link that survives a week, not an attachment that bounces. Name the files in walk-through order so the agent's cull starts from a sensible sequence.

If the photographer is also doing edits, twilight, sky replacement, staging, each edited photo should arrive as a separate version alongside its original, with the edit named. The agent needs the original to compare against and the label to disclose. An edited file that replaces the original is the one thing that reliably causes trouble later.

Originals: who keeps them

Unless the agreement says otherwise, assume the photographer holds the rights to the photos and licences them to the agent for the listing. Write down what the licence allows: publish on the MLS and portals, make video from them, apply edits, reuse after the sale. Both sides keep the originals. If a dispute ever comes up about an edited image, the original is the evidence, and it should not live on one phone only.

Who publishes the page

There are two workable arrangements. The agent publishes: the photographer delivers the set and the agent builds the video and the page. Or the photographer publishes: the photographer builds the video and the page as part of the delivery, with the agent's name, business and number on the contact card, and hands over the links. The second is faster for the agent and lets the photographer sell a finished product. Either is fine. What does not work is both of them publishing a page for the same address with different main photos.

Who posts what, and when

Decide the posting split by channel. The agent posts the listing on their own social and sends it to their database. The photographer may post the photos on their own channels as portfolio work, usually after the listing is live and with the agent's agreement. Nobody posts before the MLS entry. If the photographer built the vertical video, the agent gets the file or the link and posts it themselves, with their branded link in the caption.

Where the enquiry lands

If the agent publishes the page, enquiries go to the agent and this question answers itself. If the photographer publishes on the agent's behalf, the enquiry has to reach the agent, not the photographer, and that needs the agent's contact details on the page and a forwarding path that the agent has confirmed. Test it with one enquiry before the link goes anywhere.

A Saturday shoot, handed off

A photographer with four shoots on Saturday shoots a 3-bed in a cul-de-sac at 9 a.m. The agent's pre-shoot message said: landscape photos plus six portrait frames, originals and any twilight versions separately, you publish the page with my contact card, I post the reel Monday, enquiries to me. By Saturday evening the photographer has culled, built the landscape video, published the page with the agent's name and number, and sent one message: the branded link, the unbranded link, and a line confirming the twilight exterior is a separate version with its disclosure. Sunday the agent reviews the page on their phone and swaps the main photo. Monday morning the agent enters the MLS with the unbranded link and posts the reel with the branded one. The first enquiry arrives Monday afternoon and lands with the agent.

What goes wrong in the handoff

  • A zip of 180 unedited frames with no order and no indication of which three the photographer thought were best.
  • Edited files delivered in place of originals, so the agent cannot compare and does not know what changed.
  • No agreement on the licence, discovered when the agent wants to reuse the photos for a just-sold post.
  • Two pages for one address, one by each party, with different prices.
  • Enquiries routed to the photographer because the page was published from their account with their details.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should the photographer deliver edited photos or originals?

Both, as separate files. The agent needs the original to compare against and the edited version with its edit named, so the disclosure is right and nothing changed in the room goes unnoticed.

Who should publish the property page, the photographer or the agent?

Either works, as long as only one of them does it. If the photographer publishes, the agent's name, business and number go on the contact card and enquiries must reach the agent, which should be tested with one enquiry before the link is shared.

Can the photographer post the listing photos on their own channels?

Only if the agreement says so, and usually after the listing is live on the MLS. Ask the agent first and do not post before the launch.

What should the booking message include?

The file set and orientation, the request for originals and edits as separate versions, who publishes the page, who posts where, and where enquiries go. One message, sent before the shoot, saves three after it.
