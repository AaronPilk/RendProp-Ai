Listing presentation: showing the marketing before you win

Win the listing by showing the marketing, not describing it: a live property page on the seller's phone, the video playing, and where an enquiry lands.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Show the seller the marketing instead of describing it: open a live property page from a past listing on their own phone, play the video, and show them where an enquiry lands. A seller who has tapped through a page in their kitchen understands your plan better than one who has read a slide about it.

Show the seller the marketing, not a description of it. Open a live property page from a past listing on their own phone, play the video, scroll the gallery, and show them where an enquiry lands. A seller who has tapped through a page in their own kitchen understands your plan in two minutes. A seller who has read a slide titled Marketing Plan understands that you have a slide. Here is what to bring, the order to show it in, and the question that decides the room.

What to bring: three things on your phone

  1. A finished page from a comparable listing. Same price band or same kind of home, with the video selected, the gallery in order and your contact card showing. If you have nothing comparable yet, a demo page works, as long as you say it is a demo.
  2. A vertical social video from that listing. Thirty seconds, captions on, so it plays silently across the table.
  3. Your inbox with a real enquiry in it. Blur nothing, read nothing aloud; just show that a form on a page becomes a message with a name, a date and a question.

The five-minute demo order

Do this after the price conversation, not before. A seller who has not heard your number is not listening to your marketing.

  1. Hand them your phone with the page open, or send the branded link to theirs. Say: this is what buyers see when they tap the link on the sign, the flyer or the post.
  2. Let them scroll. Do not narrate. Watch where their thumb stops; it is usually the gallery or the price line.
  3. Tap the video. Say: this is built from the same photos, so what they see in the video is what they see at the showing.
  4. Show the social cut. Say: this is what goes on Instagram and Facebook on launch day, and it links to that page.
  5. Show the inbox. Say: when a buyer fills in the form, it arrives here with the date they want to visit, and I reply the same hour.

Then put the phone down and ask what they would want shown differently about their own home.

The question sellers ask, and the honest answer

Sooner or later a seller asks whether the photos are edited. The honest answer wins more often than the evasive one. Say that some photos may have a blue sky or virtual staging in an empty room, that every edited photo is labelled, that the original is always kept and compared against, and that nothing is changed about the house itself. Then show them one labelled staged photo next to its original on the demo page. Sellers who hear that from you do not hear it from a buyer's agent later.

Competing for a 4-bed against two other agents

Three agents are presenting for a 4-bed on a Thursday evening. The first brings a printed booklet with a page headed Digital Marketing. The second talks about their brokerage's reach. You bring your phone. After the price discussion you hand the seller a page from a 4-bed you sold in the spring: they scroll the gallery, tap the video, watch the social cut play silently, and see an enquiry in the inbox asking for a Saturday viewing. You show one staged bedroom beside its original and explain the label. Then you ask what they would want shown about their home, and the seller says the back garden, which the other two agents did not ask about. You explain that the garden would lead the video. The listing agreement is signed Friday morning. Nothing you showed was a promise; all of it was a thing that already existed.

Mistakes that lose the room

  • Showing the marketing before the price. They are not listening yet.
  • A demo page with a wrong price, a missing bath count or a typo in the street name. Sellers read the details first; a mistake on your example page is a mistake they expect on theirs.
  • Narrating while they scroll. Let the page do it.
  • Promising a drone, a film crew or a timeline you cannot control. Show only what you have made before.
  • Dodging the edited-photos question. A straight answer with a labelled example ends it.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should I show the marketing before or after discussing price?

After. A seller who has not heard your number is waiting for it and not taking in the page or the video. Settle price, then hand them the phone.

What if I do not have a comparable past listing to show?

Use a demo page and say so. A seller would rather see a clearly labelled demo of what their page will look like than a vague description of a plan. Replace it with your own listing as soon as you have one.

How do I answer a seller who asks whether photos will be edited?

Plainly. Some photos may have a blue sky or virtual staging in an empty room, every edited photo is labelled, the original is kept and compared, and nothing about the house itself is changed. Then show one labelled example beside its original.

Should I send the seller the link after the appointment?

Yes, the same evening, with a one-line text. A seller who scrolls the page again at home, without you there, is deciding in your favour.
