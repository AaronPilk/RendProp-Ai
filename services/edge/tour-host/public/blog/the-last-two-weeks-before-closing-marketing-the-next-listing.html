The last two weeks before closing: marketing the next listing

The fortnight before closing is when agents go quiet. Use it to prepare the just-sold post, the review ask, the neighbour cards and the next launch.

The two weeks before a closing are the quietest of the whole transaction, and they are where the next listing is won or lost. The inspection is done, the appraisal is in, and the file is with the lenders and the lawyers. Most agents wait. The quiet fortnight is where the next listing is won

The lull after the contract is signed feels like time off, and the pipeline goes cold without anyone noticing until the closing is done and the calendar is empty.

Three things are true during those two weeks. The sellers are happy and available. The neighbours have noticed the sign change and are curious about the number. The buyers who lost out are still looking. Each of those is a marketing opportunity with a short shelf life, and all of them are easier to act on now than after the closing when the sellers have moved and the neighbours have stopped thinking about it.

Day by day: what to prepare

  1. Day one. Open the listing's media and pick the frame for the just-sold post. The twilight exterior or the main photo, not a new shoot. Write the caption now: the street, the result in whatever terms your broker allows, the people to credit. Guidance on getting the credits right is in just sold copy that credits the right people.
  2. Day two. Build the short sold cut of the listing video from the same photos, with the caption's sentence as the voiceover. Leave it unposted.
  3. Day three. Draft the review ask to the sellers. Short, personal, with the link to wherever you collect reviews. Schedule it for the afternoon of closing day, when the relief is fresh.
  4. Day four. Write the neighbour letter or card. The house two doors down just sold; here is what happened; here is the link to see how it was marketed. Address the envelopes now.
  5. Day five. Go through the enquiries and showing feedback from the listing and make a list of the buyers who came twice or made an offer. Draft the message that goes to them on closing day: it closed, here is what else is coming, here is what to watch for.
  6. Week two. Spend it on the next listing. If you have a listing appointment, build the presentation around this sale: the page, the video, the enquiry count, the timeline. If you do not, the neighbour letter and the buyer follow-up are how you get one. Book the photographer provisionally for the week after closing.

Keep the sold listing working

Once it closes, the listing is a case study for as long as the page exists. Mark it sold the day of closing so the page says so, keep it live, and put the link in the listing presentation, in the neighbour letter and in the just-sold post. Closing on the 28th, listing appointment on the 19th

A three-bed on Harrow Lane, closing set for the 28th, under contract since the first week of the month. On the 14th the agent had nothing to do on the file and a listing appointment on the 19th with a couple three streets away who had seen the sign.

She spent the 14th and 15th on the Harrow Lane media: picked the twilight exterior for the sold post, wrote the caption, built the thirty-second sold cut with a one-line voiceover, drafted the sellers' review ask and scheduled it for the 28th at three. On the 16th she wrote and addressed cards to the twelve nearest houses, to go out the morning after closing. On the 17th she went through the Harrow Lane enquiries and feedback and found five buyers who had come twice; she drafted the message for them.

On the 19th the listing appointment opened with the Harrow Lane page on the couple's kitchen table: the gallery, the video, the enquiry pattern over the first ten days, and the timeline from shoot to contract. They signed on the 21st, and the photographer she had pencilled for the week after the 28th was confirmed that afternoon. On the 28th the closing happened, the page was marked sold, the post went out, the review ask landed at three, and the twelve cards went into the post the next morning with the sold page link on them.

Where agents lose the fortnight

Waiting for closing to start the just-sold work. By the time the post is written the moment has passed and the sellers have moved.

Asking for the review a week late. The hour after keys change hands is when people want to say thank you. A week later they are unpacking.

Posting sold before it has closed. Deals fall through in the last week. Prepare everything; post nothing until the money has moved.

Letting the sold page go dark. It is the best proof you have. Mark it sold and keep it live.

Walking into the listing appointment with a brochure. Open the last sold page instead.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

What should a listing agent do in the two weeks before closing?

Prepare everything that goes out on closing day: the just-sold post and sold video cut, the review ask to the sellers, the neighbour follow-up, and the message to buyers who missed out. Then spend the second week on the next listing, including a provisional photographer booking.

When should I post the just-sold announcement?

On closing day, after the money has moved, never before. Deals can fail in the last week. Have the photo, caption and short sold video ready in advance so posting takes a minute.

When is the best time to ask sellers for a review?

The hour after keys change hands, when the relief is fresh. Draft the message during the quiet fortnight and schedule it for the afternoon of closing day. A week later they are unpacking and the moment has passed.

Should I take the property page down after the sale?

No. Mark it sold so the page says so and keep it live. A sold page with the gallery and the video is the clearest proof of how you market a home, and it belongs in the neighbour cards and on the table at the next listing appointment.
