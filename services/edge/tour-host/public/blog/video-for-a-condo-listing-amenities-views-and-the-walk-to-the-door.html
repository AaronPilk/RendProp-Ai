Condo listing video: amenities, views and the walk to the door

A condo video leads with the view or the main room, shows the two amenities that matter, then walks from the street through the lobby to the unit door.

A condo listing video leads with the view or the main living space, shows the two amenities a buyer would actually use, and then takes the walk from the street through the lobby and the corridor to the unit's front door. The building exterior is not the hook, because condo buildings look alike and the viewer has seen fifty. The walk to the door is the part most condo videos skip and the part that answers what every condo buyer wants to know: what is it like to come home here.

Lead with the inside or the view

A house can open on the exterior. A condo cannot. The facade is glass and balconies, the same as the one next door, and the first frame has three seconds to stop a scroll. Open on the thing that is different: the view from the living room, the balcony over the park, the kitchen if it is the selling point, the light in the main room.

Show the building second, from the street, so the viewer knows what they are looking at. One frame.

Amenities: the two that matter, not the list

Every condo listing sheet has the same list. Gym, pool, lounge, roof deck, concierge, package room, bike storage. The video is not the place for the list. Pick the two a buyer in this unit's bracket would use on a Tuesday.

  • One-bed near the train: the gym and the package room.
  • Two-bed with a family: the pool and the playground or the park across the road.
  • Penthouse: the roof deck and the concierge.

One frame each, with one sentence each in the script. The rest go in the caption and on the property page.

The walk to the door

This is the section that makes a condo video different from a house video. A house buyer knows what arriving looks like: a driveway, a front door. A condo buyer does not. Is the lobby bright or a corridor of mailboxes. Is the elevator one of two or one of six. Is the hallway carpeted and quiet. Is the door at the end or by the elevator.

Show it in three or four frames: the entrance from the street, the lobby, the corridor, the unit door. Or film it as a short handheld walk, slowly, in one take, and keep it separate from the photo-based video for buyers who want it. Either way, the walk tells the buyer whether they would be happy coming home at ten at night with groceries, and that is the decision.

The unit itself

After the walk, the interior in order: living space, kitchen, main bedroom, bathroom if it is good, and the balcony or the view again to close. Condo units are small, so the photos are often tight; choose the wide frames from the doorway with a window at the far end, which take camera motion well and make the room read at its true size. A frame of in-unit laundry is worth more than a second bathroom angle.

The script: location does the heavy lifting

Condo buyers are buying a place in a place. The script should say where before it says what.

Two bedrooms on the fourth floor, four minutes to the train, and this is the view from the sofa. Gym and the package room downstairs. The lobby is bright, two elevators, quiet corridor, your door at the end. In-unit laundry, parking included. Open Sunday, link in the profile.

Forty-eight words. Place, view, two amenities, the walk, two unit facts, the ask. Note that the walk gets a sentence; most scripts leave it out.

A worked example: the fourth-floor two-bed

Photos: balcony view at golden hour as the lead, building from the street, gym, package room, entrance, lobby, corridor, unit door, living room from the doorway, kitchen, main bedroom, balcony again. Twelve frames for the 16:9 page video, 70 seconds. For the 9:16 reel: view, building, lobby, corridor, living room, kitchen, balcony. Seven frames, 28 seconds. The walk survives the cut to the reel, because it is the part no other listing in the building shows.

Condo video mistakes

  • Opening on the building. Every other unit in it opened on the building too.
  • Ten amenities in a row. By the pool table the viewer has gone.
  • Skipping the walk. The buyer has to imagine arriving, and imagination is where doubt lives.
  • A wide lens in a small unit that makes 700 square feet look like 1,100. The showing is where that lie gets caught. More in shooting listing photos at 0.5x wide.
  • No mention of fees or parking in the caption. They will be the first two messages.
  • Staged frames without the label. An empty condo is often staged virtually; the label stays on every frame it appears in.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should a condo video start with the building?

No. Condo buildings look alike and the first frame has to stop a scroll. Open on the view, the balcony or the main living space, and show the building second from the street so the viewer knows what it is.

How many amenities should the video show?

Two, chosen for the buyer this unit suits: the gym and the package room for a one-bed near the train, the pool and the park for a family two-bed. One frame and one sentence each. The full list belongs in the caption and on the property page.

Why include the lobby and corridor in a listing video?

Because a condo buyer does not know what coming home looks like until they see it. The entrance, lobby, corridor and unit door answer that in three or four frames and are the part most other listings in the building leave out.

Should I film the walk to the door or use photos?

Either. Three or four portrait frames work inside the photo-based reel. A short, slow handheld walk in one take works as a separate recorded walkthrough for buyers who want the full approach. Keep the two separate rather than mixing footage into the photo video.
