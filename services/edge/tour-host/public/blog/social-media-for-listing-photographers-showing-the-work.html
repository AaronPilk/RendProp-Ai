Social media for listing photographers: showing the work

A photographer's feed should show finished listing media the way agents use it: the reel, the page link, a labelled before and after. Credit the agent.

October 28, 2026 · 4 min read · by Rendprop

A listing photographer's social accounts do their job when they show the finished media the way an agent would use it: the vertical reel, the property page it lives on, a labelled before and after of a real edit, and now and then a frame of the process. Credit the agent on every post, get the agent's and the seller's permission before anything goes up, and never post a listing before the agent has launched it. The audience is agents deciding who to book, and what they want to see is what they would get.

Who is watching and what they are deciding

Agents do not follow photographers to admire photographs. They follow to answer a question: if I book this person, what lands in my inbox and how fast can I post it. A beautiful still answers half of that. A reel they could post today, a property page they could send to a seller tonight, and a before and after that shows the edits are honest answer all of it.

So the feed should be organised around deliverables, not around art. Each post is a thing an agent could receive from you.

Four shoots on Saturday, one post a week

A photographer shoots four listings on a Saturday. Not all four should become posts; one a week, chosen for what it shows, is plenty.

  • Week one: the reel. The Saturday ranch with the best light. Post the vertical reel the agent is already posting, the Tuesday after launch, with a caption: Shot Saturday, delivered Sunday with the 16:9 video, the vertical reel and the property page. Listed by the agent's name, with their handle. The agent is tagged and reposts.
  • Week two: the before and after. The split-level whose exterior had a flat grey sky. The original on the left, the blue sky version on the right, the caption: Sky replaced at the agent's request; nothing else changed; the original is in the listing's photo history and the MLS copy is labelled. A labelled comparison is the single most trust-building post a photographer can make.
  • Week three: the page. A short screen recording of the property page on a phone: the main photo, the gallery, the video playing on request, the form. Caption: Every shoot comes with this, live by Sunday night, with the agent's contact details on it. Link to the page, if the agent agrees.
  • Week four: the process. One frame of the tripod in the doorway of the fourth house, with a line about why that doorway and that hour. Craft, not chaos; the same rule as in behind-the-scenes content.

Four posts a month from sixteen shoots, each one a deliverable an agent could picture receiving, each one crediting the agent who hired you.

Permissions, every time

Two people have to say yes before a listing appears on your feed: the agent, who owns the relationship, and the seller, whose home it is. Put the request in your booking confirmation so it is answered before the shoot: May I post selected finished images from this shoot on my own accounts after the listing is live, crediting you. Some agents say yes to everything; some want approval per post; some say no. Each answer is fine. Posting without asking is not.

What photographers get wrong on social

  • Posting before launch. The agent's coming soon plan is now your post. Wait until the listing is live and the agent has posted.
  • The address in the caption or the frame. Not your call. The agent decides what identifies the home.
  • Edits with no label. A twilight conversion posted as if it were shot at dusk tells every agent watching that your edits are undisclosed. Label it, and say the MLS copy is labelled too.
  • A feed of exteriors. Exteriors are what every photographer posts. Kitchens, staircases and the reel are what agents are trying to buy.
  • No agent tag. The credit is the point: it tells other agents who already trusts you.
  • Posting the unbranded link. A page with no agent on it is the wrong showcase. Post the branded page with the agent's details, or no link.
  • Treating it as a portfolio for homeowners. Sellers rarely hire listing photographers directly. Write for the agent.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

When can a photographer post a listing they shot?

After the listing is live and the agent has posted it, and only with the agent's and the seller's permission. Ask in the booking confirmation so the answer is settled before the shoot.

Should a photographer post before and after edits?

Yes, with the original on one side, the edit on the other and the edit named in the caption. A labelled comparison shows agents that your edits are honest. An unlabelled after shot does the opposite.

Which link should a photographer share on a listing post?

The branded property page with the agent's contact details, if the agent agrees, or no link. The unbranded page is for the MLS field and shows nobody to contact.

How often should a listing photographer post?

Once a week is plenty if each post shows a different deliverable: the reel, a labelled before and after, the property page, a frame of the process. Four shoots a Saturday does not mean four posts.
