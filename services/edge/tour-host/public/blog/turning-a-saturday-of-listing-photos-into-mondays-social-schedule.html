Turning Saturday's listing photos into Monday's social schedule

Photos land Saturday. Sunday evening: select, edit, script. Monday morning: reel, page video, property page, first post. Wednesday and Friday are set.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. Photos arrive Saturday. Sunday evening takes ninety minutes: pick the frames, review any AI edits against the originals, write two scripts. Monday morning takes an hour: build the reel and the page video, publish the property page, post the reel with the link. Wednesday's and Friday's posts are stills already chosen on Sunday. The whole week is decided before Monday lunch.

Photos arrive Saturday afternoon. Sunday evening is ninety minutes of selection, review and writing: the frames for the reel, the frames for the page video, any AI edits checked against the originals, and two scripts. Monday morning is an hour of building: the 9:16 reel, the 16:9 page video, the published property page, and the first post with the link. Wednesday and Friday are stills you already picked on Sunday. The week's social is decided before Monday lunch, and the rest of the week is spent on showings.

Saturday: receive, do not react

The photographer sends the set. The temptation is to post the best one immediately. Do not. A single frame posted Saturday with no link, no page and no plan is a wasted first impression. Open the set, look for anything that needs a reshoot while the photographer still has the card, and close it. That is the Saturday job.

If you need portrait frames for the reel and the photographer only shot in landscape orientation, Saturday is the moment to ask. By Sunday it is a second trip.

Sunday evening: ninety minutes at the table

Minutes 0 to 20, the selection. From 28 landscape photos, choose twelve to fourteen for the page video in walking order: exterior, entry, living, kitchen, dining, bedrooms, baths, yard. From the portrait frames, choose six to eight for the reel with the strongest first. Mark three or four stills for the midweek posts: a detail, the yard, a room that did not make the reel.

Minutes 20 to 50, the edits. If any photo needs a blue sky, a tidied counter or virtual staging, make the edit now and compare it against the original before you decide to use it. A staged living room gets its label. An edit that invented a window gets discarded. Decide which saved versions go in the video and which stay in the gallery.

Minutes 50 to 80, the scripts. Two. A 65 to 80 word reel script with the hook first, and a 150 to 200 word page video script that walks every main room. Read both aloud with a timer. Cut adjectives.

Minutes 80 to 90, the details. Price, beds, baths, square feet, the open house date and time, and the caption for Monday's post with those numbers in it. Confirm the open house slot with the seller before you write it down anywhere.

Monday morning: one hour of building

  • Record the voice in the car or a quiet room, three takes of each script. Or hand both scripts to an AI voice if the room is bad or the week is full.
  • Build the reel from the portrait frames, captions on, music decided.
  • Build the page video from the landscape photos, captions on, music off or very low.
  • Review both, muted first, then with sound. Facts, labels, spelling, first and last three seconds.
  • Publish the property page with the main photo, the details, the gallery and the 16:9 video selected.
  • Post the reel with the caption from Sunday and the branded page link wherever the platform allows a tappable link. Update the profile link.
  • Put the unbranded link in the MLS virtual tour field, after checking your MLS rules on what the page may contain.

Done by ten.

Wednesday and Friday: already decided

Wednesday is a still from the Sunday pile: the tile detail, the built-ins, the kitchen window at the hour it looks best. One line about it, the page link. Friday is the open house announcement video, three photos and the time on screen first, if there is one, or the yard still with a line about the street if there is not. Saturday morning, the open house video again with today in the caption.

None of that takes more than ten minutes on the day because the selection and the thinking happened on Sunday.

A worked example: the four-bed on Linden

Saturday 3 p.m.: 30 landscape photos and 8 portrait frames arrive. The agent checks the set, asks for nothing, closes it.

Sunday 7 p.m.: thirteen frames for the page video, seven for the reel, lead frame the kitchen island, three stills for midweek. The empty living room gets virtual staging; the staged version is compared with the original, labelled, and used. Two scripts written and timed: 72 words and 180 words. Caption drafted with the price and Saturday's open house at one.

Monday 8 a.m.: three takes of each script in the driveway. Reel and page video built. Reviewed muted on the phone, then with sound at the desk; one caption typo fixed. Page published at 9:20. Reel posted at 9:30 with the link. Unbranded link in the MLS by 9:45.

Wednesday: the tile detail. Friday: open house video. Saturday 8 a.m.: open house video again, today in the caption. Total time across the week, under three hours, most of it Sunday.

Where the week falls apart

  • Posting a photo on Saturday with no page to link to. First impression spent, nowhere to send anyone.
  • Skipping Sunday. Monday becomes four hours of deciding instead of one hour of building.
  • Writing scripts on Monday. They come out as listing-sheet copy because you are in a hurry.
  • Using an AI edit without the side-by-side. The one week you skip it is the week the edit added a door.
  • An open house date in the reel that the seller had not confirmed. The reel is wrong on three platforms by Tuesday.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

Should I post a photo on Saturday as soon as the shoot is delivered?

No. A single frame with no property page to link to and no plan behind it spends the first impression for nothing. Check the set for anything that needs a reshoot, ask for portrait frames if you need them, and close it until Sunday.

How long does the Sunday session take?

About ninety minutes: twenty for selection, thirty for making and checking any AI edits against the originals, thirty for writing and timing two scripts, and ten for the details and Monday's caption. Everything after that in the week is building and posting.

What if I cannot record the voiceover on Monday?

Hand both scripts to an AI voice. The scripts were written on Sunday, so nothing changes except who reads them. A posted video on Monday beats a recorded one on Thursday.

What goes out on Wednesday and Friday?

Wednesday is a single still chosen on Sunday, a detail or a room that did not make the reel, with one line and the page link. Friday is the open house announcement video if there is one, repeated Saturday morning with today in the caption.
