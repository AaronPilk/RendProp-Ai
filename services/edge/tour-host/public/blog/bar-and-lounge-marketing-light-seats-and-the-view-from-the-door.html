Bar and lounge marketing: light, seats, the view from the door

Market a bar on its evening light, the seats people want and the view from the door. Shoot at opening, not noon, and put hours and capacity on the page.

October 28, 2026 · 5 min read · by Rendprop

Short answer. A bar sells on three things a photo can carry: the light it has in the evening, the seats people want to sit in, and the view from the door that decides whether a group walks in. Shoot at opening time with the bar lit as it is for guests, lead with the view from the door, and put hours and capacity on the page for the private-hire enquiries that follow.

A bar is sold by its light, its seats and the view from the door. Shoot those three at opening time with the room lit as it is for service, lead with the view from the door, and put hours and capacity on the page for the people who want to book the back room.

Light, because a bar at 9pm is a different place from the same room at noon, and the photos have to be taken in the evening version. Seats, because people choosing a bar are choosing where they will sit: the corner booth, the stools at the bar, the two-top by the window. And the view from the door, because that is the moment a group decides to stay or keep walking.

Shoot in the light you serve in

Daytime photos of a bar are the most common mistake in the category. Chairs up, daylight flattening the room, the backbar looking like a shop shelf. A bar's light is designed: the pendants over the bar, the candles on the tables, the glow from the backbar, the darkness in the corners that makes the lit parts matter. All of that only exists after dark. Arrive at opening, before the first guests, with every light set exactly as it is for service, and shoot then.

A phone handles low light if you hold it still. Brace it on the bar or a door frame. Do not use the flash; it flattens everything you lit. Do not raise the exposure until the room looks like day; the dark is the point. Skip AI brightening on bar photos for the same reason, and if you use any edit, keep the disclosure and compare it with the original.

The view from the door

Stand on the threshold, open the door, and shoot what a group sees in the first second. That is your main photo. It should show the bar itself with its light, some seating, and enough of the room to suggest where they would go. If the view from the door is a wall or a host stand, shoot from the first spot inside where the room opens up, and consider whether the layout is costing you walk-ins.

The seats people ask for

  • The bar stools, with the backbar lit behind. Shot from the room, not from behind the bar.
  • The corner booth. From the aisle, with the candle on.
  • The window two-top. With the street outside at night.
  • The big table or the back room. Set for eight or ten. This is the private-hire enquiry.
  • The terrace or the smoking area, if you have one. Lit, with heaters on if it is cold.

One shot each, level, from where a guest would stand. Keep bottles and glassware in frame; a bar with nothing on the bar looks closed.

Worked example: a 70-seat cocktail bar with a back room

A cocktail bar with 50 seats in the main room and a back room that takes 20 shoots at 5:30pm on a Tuesday, half an hour before opening, with the full lighting set and candles lit. Nine landscape photos: the view from the door, the bar with stools, the backbar close, the corner booth, the window table with the street outside, the back room set for twelve, the terrace, the restrooms, the street exterior with the sign lit. The listing video opens on the street, goes through the door to the view, moves to the bar, the booth, the window, the back room, and ends on the terrace. Forty-five seconds, with a low-mixed track and a voiceover that gives the hours and says the back room is available for private hire. On the page: hours by day, capacity shown as 50 in the main room and 20 in the back room, the gallery, the video on request, a contact card and an enquiry form that asks for the date. The first enquiry is a leaving party of eighteen asking for the back room on a Friday. They saw it set for twelve and asked whether it took eighteen; the capacity figure said yes before they wrote.

Hours and capacity on the page

Hours by day, including the late nights, in the first screen. Capacity for the main room and for any bookable space, stated separately. If the back room has a minimum spend, say it in the voiceover or leave it for the reply; do not type a number that will be out of date. The enquiry form's date question is the right first question for a bar, because almost every message is about a date.

What makes a bar look worse than it is

  • Daylight shots. Already covered, and still the number one problem.
  • Flash. Kills the lighting you paid for.
  • Faces without permission. Shoot before guests arrive.
  • A bar top with nothing on it. Set a few drinks and the menus out.
  • Vertical video on the page. Keep the vertical cut for Instagram and TikTok. The page video should be in landscape orientation so the room reads wide.
  • Music too loud on the video. If the voiceover gives the hours and the private-hire line, the music has to sit underneath it.

Half an hour before opening

Tomorrow, set the lights for service half an hour early, light the candles, set a few drinks on the bar, and shoot from the door first. Publish the page with hours and capacity typed in, and put the link in the bio and in the reply to the next private-hire enquiry.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

When should I photograph a bar?

At opening time, before guests arrive, with every light set as it is for service and the candles lit. A bar's lighting is designed for the evening and daytime photos throw it away.

What should the main photo of a bar be?

The view from the door: what a group sees in the first second when they walk in. It should show the lit bar, some seating and enough of the room to suggest where they would sit.

Should I brighten dark bar photos with an edit?

No. The dark is part of the design. Hold the phone still, brace it on the bar, and let the lit parts carry the frame. If you use any AI edit, compare it with the original and keep the disclosure.

What details belong on a bar's page?

Hours by day including late nights, capacity for the main room and for any private-hire space stated separately, your contact details and an enquiry form that asks for the date. Most bar enquiries are about a date, so that question comes first.
