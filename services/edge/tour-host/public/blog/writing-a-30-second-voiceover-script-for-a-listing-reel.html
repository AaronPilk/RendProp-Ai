Writing a 30-second voiceover script for a listing reel

A 30-second listing reel script is about 70 words: one line of place, three things a buyer cannot read on the sheet, one ask. Template and worked script.

A 30-second voiceover script for a listing reel is about 65 to 80 words, read at a calm pace. It has three parts: one line that says where the home is and what it is, a middle that gives three things a buyer cannot read on the listing sheet, and a closing line with one ask. Write it before you build the video, read it aloud with a timer running, and if it runs long cut adjectives first and facts second.

How many words fit in 30 seconds

Set a timer and read a paragraph from this page aloud at the pace you would use on the phone with a client. Count the words you got through in 30 seconds. For most people that lands somewhere between 65 and 80. That number is your budget, and it is smaller than it feels. A single sentence of listing remarks can eat a third of it.

Write to your own count, not someone else's. If you speak slowly, your script is shorter. An AI voice reading the same script will land close to the same length, so the budget holds either way.

Place, three things, one ask

Part one, the place (10 to 15 words). Neighbourhood or street character, home type, and the one headline fact. This is the hook, and it has to land while the first photo is on screen.

Part two, three things (40 to 50 words). Not beds and baths. Those are in the caption and on the page. Say the things a photo cannot: the morning light in the kitchen, the walk to the school, the quiet at the back, the new roof, the way the deck faces. One sentence each.

Part three, the ask (10 to 15 words). One action. The open house date, the property page link, a message. Not all three.

A script you can adapt

For a three-bedroom on a quiet street with a renovated kitchen, a fenced yard and a Saturday open house:

Three bedrooms on a cul-de-sac, ten minutes from downtown. The kitchen was redone last year, and the morning light comes straight across that island. Out back, a fenced yard the dog will not get out of, and a deck that faces west for the evening. Open house Saturday from one to three. Link in the profile for the full tour.

That is 60 words. Read it with a timer. If you come in at 26 seconds, you have room for one more fact. If you come in at 34, drop the deck sentence rather than the open house.

Matching the words to the photos

Each sentence in the middle should land on the photo it describes. Kitchen sentence on the kitchen frame, yard sentence on the yard frame. When you pick the photos for the reel, pick them in the order of the script, and when you write the script, write it in the order of the walk. The two should be the same list.

If a sentence has no photo, cut the sentence or add the photo. A voice describing a view over a photo of a hallway is the fastest way to make a reel feel assembled rather than made.

Words that waste the budget

  • Listing-sheet words. Spacious, charming, must-see, boasts, nestled. They cost a word each and say nothing.
  • Numbers that are on the page. Square footage, lot size, year built. Put them in the caption and on the property page, where people can read them twice.
  • Your own introduction. Your name and brokerage belong on the end card, the contact card and the branded link, not in the first five seconds.
  • Two asks. Open house and call me and link in bio is three asks. Pick one.

Writing for captions

The script becomes the word-by-word captions, so write in short sentences with no clauses that need commas to make sense. A sentence that reads well on screen three words at a time is a sentence that sounds good aloud. Spell the street and neighbourhood the way locals spell them, because the caption will show it.

Recording it or handing it to an AI voice

Record it yourself if you are the face of the listing and the reel is going on your own profile. A quiet room and the phone near your mouth will do; there is a short guide in recording your own voiceover on an iPhone. If you are producing reels for a team or you want a consistent sound across listings, an AI voice reads the same script. The choice is covered in AI voice or your voice.

FAQ

Questions on this topic

How many words is a 30-second voiceover?

For most people reading at a calm pace, 65 to 80 words. Time yourself reading a paragraph aloud and use your own count as the budget, because pace varies and the script should fit the voice reading it.

Should the script mention beds, baths and square footage?

Usually not. Those numbers sit in the caption and on the property page where people can read them. Spend the spoken words on things a buyer cannot see in a photo: light, quiet, distance, what was renovated and when.

Should I introduce myself at the start of a listing reel?

No. The first seconds belong to the home. Your name, brokerage and contact details go on the branded property page, the contact card and the end of the video if you want them there.

Can the same script work for the vertical reel and the horizontal listing video?

Use the same facts, not the same length. The 30-second script fits the 9:16 reel. The 16:9 listing video can carry a 60 to 90 second version that walks every main room in order.
