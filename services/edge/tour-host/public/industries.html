Real estate

Help someone understand
how a home lives.

For a solo agent, a small team or a brokerage's listing workflow, start with the relationship between rooms. A useful walkthrough shows where the entry leads, how the kitchen connects and what is outside the doors.

A separate unbranded tour link is available for MLS use. Check the rules of the specific MLS before publishing.

A listing-ready sequence

  1. Capture: walk the entry, living space, kitchen and outdoor areas in a clear order. Include the transitions, not just the highlight rooms.
  2. Create: select photographs for a gallery and short reel. Review optional AI edits against the property as it exists.
  3. Share: add accurate listing details and room chapters to a hosted tour. Use the branded page for enquiries and the unbranded twin where required.

Event venues

Let them picture
the whole occasion.

The ceremony view is only part of the decision. Show how guests arrive, where they gather and how one part of the venue connects to the next. A walkthrough can answer the spatial questions a single hero photo leaves open.

State capacities and accessibility details from your own verified information. A video is not an occupancy certification or an accessibility assessment.

From arrival to the main room

  1. Capture: the approach, entrance, main event space and any outdoor area guests may use. Film separate setup options as separate scenes.
  2. Create: a concise venue reel with room names and your own capacity or event information in text.
  3. Share: a tour with your venue details and an enquiry contact. Confirm dates and reservations in your existing booking process.

Restaurants and bars

Show the atmosphere.
Make the visit feel familiar.

Give a first-time guest a sense of the dining room, bar or patio before they arrive. Mix a measured room walkthrough with the details that make your place recognizable, without turning a tour into an ordering promise.

Film with permission. Keep guests, staff conversations and payment details out of the frame unless you have the appropriate consent.

A story beyond the menu

  1. Capture: the entrance, room layout and seating areas during a quiet period. Make lighting and presentation representative of a normal visit.
  2. Create: combine room footage and your own dish or service photos in a short video. Check text for current opening hours and offers.
  3. Share: enter your restaurant details and menu link on the tour page. Send viewers to your existing reservation or ordering service.

Retail and showrooms

Give the collection
a place in the story.

A storefront, a thoughtful display and a useful view through the shop can help a visitor recognize your business. Keep each sequence focused: a store introduction, one collection or one seasonal display.

A tour does not synchronize inventory or prices. Review the items, availability and promotional text before every share.

From storefront to the details

  1. Capture: the facade, entry and a route through key displays. Use close-up photos for details a walkthrough cannot explain.
  2. Create: arrange a landscape overview or vertical display reel, with a title and a clear next step.
  3. Share: add your location, hours and existing website. Keep purchases in the commerce system your business already uses.

Gyms and fitness studios

Make the first visit
less of an unknown.

Introduce the reception area, training zones and studio layout. Show what someone needs to find on arrival, with the equipment and facilities that are actually available rather than a promise of a particular result.

Avoid filming changing areas or identifiable members without consent. Keep health claims and class availability accurate and separate from the visual tour.

An introduction before the first class

  1. Capture: a quiet walkthrough from entrance to training floor. Record different class setups separately when the space changes.
  2. Create: a brief orientation reel with room names, practical arrival information and your own approved captions.
  3. Share: add your business details and membership information. Point people to your existing class schedule and signup process.

Before you publish

The tools, without the assumptions.

Is Rendprop only for real estate?

No. The iPhone app includes modes for real estate, event venues, restaurants, retail, gyms and other businesses. Choose the mode that fits your space and enter your own details. The content ideas on this page are suggested workflows, not specialized booking or commerce integrations.

Can visitors book, order or buy through a tour?

A branded tour can show your business details and contact form. Provide the links and information visitors need, and handle the enquiry through your normal process. Rendprop is not a reservation system, online store, point of sale or membership billing service.

Will these videos post automatically?

No. Review and export your content, then share it through your own channels. The Studio preview's calendar is a local planning tool with a calendar-file download, not a social-account connection or auto-posting service.

Are 3D reconstruction and measurement included in these workflows?

These workflows use walkthrough video, photos and reels. Spatial reconstruction remains experimental and is not promised here as a production-quality 3D tour or measurement tool. Supported iPhone floor-plan capture is a separate feature.
