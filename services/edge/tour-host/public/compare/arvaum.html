Arvaum Studio and Rendprop are different tools that meet at one step: editing the listing's photos. Arvaum Studio is a desktop AI photo editor for real estate photographers, with virtual staging, twilight, declutter, lighting edits and high-resolution output. Rendprop is an iPhone app that takes the same photos through optional, labelled AI edits, builds a listing video, and publishes a property website with a gallery, the video, the details, a contact form and an unbranded link for the MLS.

So the question is which job you need done. Editing stills at a desk: Arvaum Studio. Getting a listing from a folder of photos to a video, a page and two links an agent can send tonight: Rendprop. Many photographers will use one for the stills and the other for everything after.

What Arvaum Studio is

Arvaum Studio is a desktop-first AI photo editing platform for macOS and Windows, with a browser app, built by a working real estate photographer. The edits are the ones listing photographers already sell: virtual staging, twilight conversion, declutter, lighting edits, and custom prompts with AI enhancement. Output is high-resolution and files are stored locally. At last check their site described video as coming soon, so today it is a stills tool, built for someone at a desk with forty frames from a Saturday shoot.

Where the two overlap

Both will take a listing photo and offer AI versions of it: a blue sky over a flat grey one, a twilight exterior, a tidied bedroom, furniture in an empty living room. In Rendprop this happens in the photo studio. Suggest proposes edits for a photo, Improve my prompt sharpens your wording, and each edit is saved as a separate version beside the original, with staging labelled “Virtually staged” and other edits carrying an AI-alteration disclosure. The original stays in photo history; compare each edit against it before publishing, since AI can change or invent details.

That is the whole overlap. After the edit, Arvaum Studio hands you finished photos and stops. Rendprop keeps going.

Where Arvaum Studio is the better pick

  • Depth as a photo editor. Editing is the whole product, so the controls go deeper than a step inside a listing app can.
  • A desktop session. Mouse, big screen, a batch of files in a folder. Processing a whole shoot in one sitting is faster here than on a phone.
  • High-resolution output. If the deliverable is the photo file itself, for print or a full-size gallery, Arvaum Studio is built for that. Rendprop's edits exist to feed the video and the property page.
  • Local file storage. The files live on your machine.
  • Per-edit pricing. You pay for what you edit, which suits volume that rises and falls with the season.

Plainly: a photographer whose product is the photo, and who wants the strongest desktop editor available, should prefer Arvaum Studio. Rendprop is not trying to replace it.

Where Rendprop is the better pick

Rendprop wins on everything that happens after the photos are good, which for an agent is most of the job.

  • The video. From the same photos Rendprop builds a landscape video for the listing or a vertical social video. Scene order and camera motion are planned automatically, you record the voiceover or have an AI voice read your script, captions appear word by word, music is optional, and exports come in 16:9 and 9:16.
  • The property website. A hosted page with the main photo, the details you typed (price, beds, baths, square feet), the gallery, the selected video (it plays on request rather than autoplaying), a contact card and an enquiry form. Nothing to design or host. See the listing site.
  • The MLS link. Every published listing gets a branded page at rendprop.com/f/your-slug and an unbranded one at rendprop.com/u/your-slug with no name, no contact details and no Rendprop branding, made for the MLS virtual-tour field. Details at two links per listing.
  • The enquiries. The form asks for the date the visitor wants. Enquiries land in the app, per listing and all together. Mark the listing sold or archived and the page says so.
  • The iPhone workflow. On an iPhone running iOS 16 or later you add photos, review edits, create the video, publish and share without leaving the phone. The web Studio at studio.rendprop.com picks up the same listing on a desktop.

Rendprop also has modes for venues, restaurants, gyms, retail and offices, and an optional handheld walkthrough that viewers can watch or scroll.

Workflow, side by side

Take a 3-bed in a cul-de-sac going live on Thursday, with an empty living room and a front exterior shot under a flat grey sky.

With Arvaum Studio. The photographer copies the shoot to the Mac, stages the living room, converts the exterior to twilight, declutters the kids' bedroom, exports high-resolution files and sends the agent a folder. Good photos, properly done. The agent still needs a video, a page, an MLS tour link and somewhere for enquiries to land, and those come from other tools or do not happen.

With Rendprop. The agent, or the photographer for them, starts a listing in real estate mode and adds the landscape photos. Suggest proposes edits for the exterior and the living room; only the ones that hold up against the original are kept. The video step builds the landscape video with a recorded voiceover, the website step publishes the page, and from the Share sheet you copy the unbranded link for the MLS virtual-tour field and the branded link for texts and posts. Enquiries show up in the inbox. The photo editing is lighter than Arvaum's; everything after it exists.

Using both. The photographer edits in Arvaum Studio at the desk and delivers finished stills. The agent adds those to a Rendprop listing, skips most of the photo studio, and goes straight to the video and the page. One caution: Rendprop's disclosure labels apply to edits made in its own photo studio. If a room was staged in Arvaum Studio before the photo reached Rendprop, keep your own record of it and check your MLS's current rules on altered images before posting.

For context, outsourced virtual staging runs $5–15 per photo at typical U.S. rates as of August 2026, which is why per-edit AI pricing appeals to a photographer with volume.

Who should choose which

  • Choose Arvaum Studio if you are a photographer whose deliverable is the photo file, you work at a desk, and you want high-resolution output, local storage and per-edit pricing.
  • Choose Rendprop if you are an agent, or a photographer serving agents, and the listing needs a video, a hosted page, an unbranded MLS link and a place for enquiries to land, done from the phone the photos are already on.
  • Use both if you edit in Arvaum Studio and want to hand the agent a finished listing page and video along with the stills, or if your photographer already delivers edited photos.

If you are still deciding, open the demo listing on your phone and ask yourself if that is a page you would send a buyer. If it is, start there. The photo editor question stands on its own, and the answer may well be Arvaum Studio.

FAQ

Arvaum Studio or Rendprop: common questions

Is Arvaum Studio a competitor to Rendprop?

Only at the photo-editing step. Arvaum Studio is a desktop photo editor and stops at finished stills. Rendprop edits photos too, but its output is a listing video and a hosted property website with an unbranded MLS link and an enquiry form. Most people comparing the two need one, the other, or both, rather than a winner.

Can I use photos edited in Arvaum Studio in Rendprop?

Yes. Add the finished photos to a listing in Rendprop the same way you would add any photos, then go straight to the video and the website. Rendprop's disclosure labels apply to edits made in its own photo studio, so keep your own record of what was staged or altered elsewhere and check your MLS's current rules before posting.

Does Arvaum Studio make listing videos?

At last check their site described video as coming soon. Rendprop builds a landscape video or a vertical social video from the listing's photos today, with automatic scene order and camera motion, recorded or AI voiceover, captions and optional music.

Does Rendprop replace a desktop photo editor?

No. Rendprop's edits are review-first, labelled and made to feed the video and the property page. A photographer who needs high-resolution photo deliverables and a long editing session at a desk should keep a dedicated editor, and Arvaum Studio is a reasonable choice for that.

Which is cheaper?

They are priced around different units, so there is no clean answer.
