Rendprop vs Rendy: a listing page and MLS link, or six reels

Rendy renders six vertical reels per listing for photographers to resell. Rendprop gives the agent a property website, an MLS link and enquiries.

Facts about Rendy were taken from its public website in October 2026 and may have changed. Check Rendy's site for current details.

Short answer. Rendy is a web tool for real estate photographers: upload a listing's edited photos, pick effects, and it renders six vertical reels to sell on top of the shoot. Rendprop is an iPhone app and web Studio for the agent, and for photographers who deliver to agents: the photos become a listing video or social video on a hosted property website with a contact form, a branded link and an unbranded MLS link. Photographers selling reel bundles should pick Rendy; agents who want the listing to end up as a page that collects enquiries should pick Rendprop.

What Rendy is

Rendy is a web tool for real estate photographers and media companies. Upload the listing's edited photos, pick effects, render. One listing produces six vertical videos from 20 photos. Motion comes from camera moves such as push in, slide and parallax, and from more than 50 visual effects, including day to night, a helicopter reveal, and animated water and fire. There is animated virtual staging, AI actors, and text hooks for social feeds. A sales academy and marketing kits round it out for photographers selling a reels package alongside the stills. Access is by application. The agent is the client who receives the files, not the user.

Where Rendy is the better pick

  • You sell reels as a product. Six vertical videos from one upload is a bundle you can price and hand over the same evening. A photographer with five shoots on a Saturday can have thirty reels ready by Sunday. Rendprop creates a video for the listing with scene order and camera motion planned automatically; it is not built to produce a six-pack per listing to resell.
  • You want effects. Day to night, helicopter reveal, animated water and fire, AI actors, text hooks: a far wider visual toolkit than Rendprop offers. Rendprop's edits are blue sky, twilight, green lawn, tidy the room, add furniture or a custom prompt.
  • You want per-listing pricing you can quote. Rendy states its prices per listing (figures below). For a photographer building a line item, a known cost per listing is easier to work with than a monthly plan.
  • You work at a desk and deliver files. Rendy is web only, no handicap if you already edit on a desktop.
  • You want help selling. The academy and kits are aimed at photographers who have not sold video before. Rendprop is a publishing tool, not a sales course.

Where Rendprop is the better pick

Rendprop wins when the person who needs the result is the agent, and the result needs to be more than a video file.

  • The output is a page, not a folder. Rendprop publishes a hosted property website for the listing: main photo, the details typed into the app, the gallery, the selected video (plays on request, does not autoplay), a contact card and an enquiry form that asks for the date the visitor wants. Nothing to design or host.
  • Two links, one for the MLS. Every listing gets a branded page at rendprop.com/f/ and an unbranded link at rendprop.com/u/ with no name, no contact details and no Rendprop branding, made for an MLS virtual-tour field (how the two links work). Rendy does not produce an MLS tour link as a product.
  • Enquiries go somewhere. The form sends enquiries into the app, per listing and all together in the inbox (more on enquiries). A reel on a feed gets you comments; a page gets you a name and a date. Rendy has no enquiry inbox.
  • It runs on the phone. Rendprop is an iPhone app (iOS 16 or later) with a web Studio for desktop continuity. An agent can add photos, review edits, create the video, publish and share both links from the driveway. Rendy is web only.
  • Review-first edits. Every AI edit is a separate version with a disclosure, shown beside the original; staging is labelled "Virtually staged"; the untouched original stays in photo history. You compare and choose before anything is published.
  • More than homes. Modes for event venues, restaurants and bars, gyms and studios, retail and other businesses change the fields and the page. Rendy is a real estate photographer's tool.
  • Footage of the real home. An optional recorded walkthrough, handheld and not drone, can sit alongside the photo-based video; viewers can watch it or scroll through it.

One caution for both tools. A video animated from still photos is synthesized motion: the camera did not travel through that room and the pool water did not move. Watch every video through before it goes to a client, a feed or an MLS, and check your MLS's current rules before posting day-to-night or staged versions.

Workflow, side by side

Take a four-bed colonial on a corner lot, shot on a Thursday morning, 24 finished photos.

Through Rendy

  1. The photographer finishes the edit and uploads the photos to Rendy in a browser.
  2. Picks effects: camera moves, perhaps a day-to-night on the front elevation, animated staging in the empty living room, text hooks for the feed.
  3. Renders six vertical videos from 20 photos.
  4. Sends the files to the agent.

Through Rendprop

  1. Start a listing on the iPhone or in the web Studio and set the mode.
  2. Add the property photos: landscape photos for the listing video, portrait photos for social video.
  3. In the photo studio, review any AI edits, compare each against the original and choose which saved versions to use.
  4. Create the video. Scene order and camera motion are planned automatically; record a voiceover or have an AI voice read your script; captions appear word by word; music is optional.
  5. Review it, publish the property website, then use the Share sheet to copy the branded link and the unbranded MLS link.
  6. Enquiries land in the inbox. Mark the listing sold and the page says so.

Rendy's job ends at step four with files. Rendprop's ends with a live page and keeps going when a buyer fills in the form.

Who should choose which

Choose Rendy if you are a photographer or media company and the reel is something you sell: six vertical videos per listing in one pass, a wide effects library, AI actors and text hooks, per-listing pricing you can quote to a client, and training on how to sell it.

Choose Rendprop if you are the agent, or a photographer whose agents keep asking for a link rather than files: a page with the video, the details and an enquiry form; an unbranded link for the MLS; enquiries in one inbox; the whole thing done from an iPhone; edits you can check against the original; and, when it fits, a recorded walkthrough of the real home. Also if you list venues, restaurants or gyms.

Some photographers will run both: Rendy for the reels they sell, Rendprop for the page and MLS link an agent asked for, with the client's contact details on the listing (enquiry forwarding needs the client's email verified).

The quickest way to decide: open the demo listing and ask whether the agent you work with, or the agent you are, would rather receive that link or six files.

FAQ

Rendy or Rendprop: common questions

Can an agent use Rendy directly?

Rendy is built for real estate photographers and media companies, and access is by application. In the usual setup the photographer runs Rendy and hands the finished videos to the agent. If you are an agent who wants to make and publish the listing yourself, Rendprop is built for that: it runs on your iPhone and ends with a published page and two links.

Does Rendprop make six reels per listing like Rendy?

No. Rendprop creates a landscape video or a vertical social video from the photos you choose, with scene order and camera motion planned automatically, and exports in 16:9 and 9:16. If a bundle of several reels per listing is the product you sell, Rendy's six-per-listing output is the better fit.

Which tool gives me something for the MLS virtual-tour field?

Rendprop gives every listing an unbranded link at rendprop.com/u/ with no name, no contact details and no Rendprop branding, made for that field. Rendy's deliverable is a set of video files, and where they go is between you and your agent. Check your MLS's current rules on what the field accepts and on altered images.

Is the video from either tool real footage of the home?

No. Both start from still photos and synthesize the motion, so the camera move, the day-to-night change or the animated water did not happen in the room. Review each video before it goes to a client, a feed or an MLS. If you want footage of the real home, Rendprop has an optional recorded walkthrough, handheld rather than drone, that viewers can watch or scroll.

Can a photographer use Rendprop for client listings?

Yes. Teams exist, and a photographer can present a client's contact details on a listing so the enquiry form reaches the agent; enquiry forwarding needs the client's email verified. The deliverable to the agent becomes a live page plus a branded link and an unbranded MLS link, rather than files.
