# Audible original-audio WebCodecs proof — design draft

Status: external review draft only. No shipping checkout, package manifest, browser, encoder, provider, CI, deployment or account has been changed or run. An external static TypeScript copy was compiled. This is a new diagnostic after the separate three-second narrated/muted WebCodecs proof; its pass would not establish full Studio feature parity.

## Exact scope

- One 3.000 s edit: 0.500 s photo → 2.000 s video played at 2× from a 4.000 s source → 0.500 s photo. The video has a distinct 990 Hz first 150 ms of source audio and 880 Hz thereafter. It must have AAC audio, unlike the prior silent-blue source.
- `draft.audio === "original"` is explicit. Narration, music, speech tracks and cutaway overlays are absent. The prior frozen proof and its video loop stay byte-separate; a new diagnostic module imports the same model, decoder, renderer, transitions and MP4 cadence verifier.
- The video phase remains 90 explicit canvas paints and VideoFrames at 30 fps. A successor proof must use moving source footage before any feature parity claim; a constant-color source cannot establish frame-accurate seeking.
- The audio phase reuses the shipping path's HTMLVideoElement with playbackRate 2 and MediaElementAudioSourceNode → original gain. It does not synthesize speed-adjusted audio or decode/resample the original for this case. The current browser's pitch preservation is established by decoded PCM, not inferred from the playbackRate setting.
- Capture audio first in real time, without video encoding. A unity mix bus receives the zero-valued constant source and original gain; it connects to a pass-through AudioWorkletNode and then a MediaStreamDestination. No audible speaker duplicate is connected.
- After startup-clock readiness, the worklet owns a fixed 144,000-frame capture window at 48 kHz. Capture begins before source play; its first 24,000 frames are the opening photo's silence. Main thread starts video at the 0.500 s Web Audio target, stops it at 2.500 s, and captures the last 24,000 silent frames. A delayed source start or real decoder stall must fail PCM/timing or an explicit media-loss guard. Worklet sample-frame indices, not MessagePort arrival times, become AAC sample timestamps.
- The worklet emits planar stereo blocks of at most 1,024 frames. Eight reusable buffers/credits bound outstanding transfer to 8×1,024×2×4 = 65,536 bytes. Missing/reordered frame index, sequence, sample rate/channel mismatch, nonfinite PCM, exhausted credits, queue overflow or wrong exact 144,000-frame total refuses before output. The worklet cannot await the encoder. The main thread awaits each AudioSampleSource.add, then closes the sample and returns the buffer credit. No padding or invented silence repairs a gap.
- Encode AAC progressively into the same pinned Mediabunny 1.65.2 MP4 Output before offline video adds. The old proof's `BufferTarget` and `Output.finalize()` limits remain: no hard preallocation cap and finalization is not preemptible. This fixed 3 s diagnostic is not a bounded-memory or cancellable 180 s product implementation.
- Keep the original source ID/SHA/revision checks, document visibility and abort fences. On every exit, stop the video, disconnect/close the worklet port and nodes, stop the zero source, dispose media, close AudioContext, and cancel the output when possible. AudioContext suspension, hidden tab, aborted user intent and changed draft refuse with no offered download.

## Required proof oracles

1. 144,000 captured sample frames, contiguous from zero, exactly 141 sequenced blocks (140 full and one 640-frame tail); no worklet overflow, missing block or queued block after completion. Record max queue depth, maximum AudioSampleSource.add latency, audio clock readings, media-time progress and cleanup state.
2. MP4 H.264/AAC, one AAC track and exactly 90 decoded video frames with distinct presentation timestamps within 1 ms of `index / 30`; duration within 2/30 s of 3.000 s. Retain strict existing transition pixels and MP4 cadence verification.
3. Decoded 48 kHz PCM: opening photo silent; 990 Hz source opening appears within 2/30 s of 0.500 s and in adjacent bounded samples; later 880 Hz remains within ±30 Hz under 2× source playback; every 40 ms window inside active speech stays above the existing 0.03 RMS threshold; final photo is silent. Compare A/V duration gap to 2/30 s.
4. Explicit fault controls in the drafted harness: delay the **actual native play call** past the 2/30 s onset bound, inject a 100 ms actual media stall, drop the first worklet chunk, exhaust credits, suspend AudioContext, cancel before and during capture, and change revision during capture. Each must refuse with no blob/download and with disposed owned resources. Delaying only the `play()` promise's delivery while native playback runs on time is a separate positive control and must preserve the audio. A distinct out-of-order mutation is still pending and must be added before any complete protocol-fault claim.
5. Before/after SHA-256 for immutable shipping source, frozen prior v2 proof/video loop, external dependency tree and generated diagnostic files. No external request or provider use.

## Known difficulty and next stages

Live HTML playback is not scheduled at an AudioContext sample frame. The 500 ms source start is initiated from the main thread; the decoded first-tone oracle, sampled media/audio clock and two-frame bound decide whether that start was actually precise enough. A worklet message acknowledgement alone is not evidence of audible onset. The small proof must measure this; it may fail legitimately.

The worklet runs on a real-time audio thread and cannot block on AAC backpressure. The eight-credit protocol must fail closed under encoder lag, including if main-thread messages arrive too late. The MediaStreamDestination is retained as a graph sink; the proof must observe actual worklet callbacks and decoded PCM because an unconsumed graph can behave differently in a browser.

Full feature work would require normal-speed buffered speech, narration and licensed music onset/duck/fade tests, source cutaways, moving-video seek accuracy, 180 s memory/storage bounds, multi-clip capture windows, account/revision cancellation and a hard way to contain or abandon finalization. None of these are established by this first audible prototype.
